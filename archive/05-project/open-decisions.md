## Decisions requiring creator taste rather than more browsing

M10/M11 settle the first-playable scope and initial absence policy: [live AI and generated survival tools](first-playable-mvp.md) are required, with no offline catch-up; the later accepted time setting allows connected background progression when explicitly enabled. They select Jev integration direction without establishing provider access or performance. The earlier optional-Jev and bundle-only first-release interpretations are superseded; exact operating limits remain open.

The [architecture review](../../docs/maintainers/README.md) adds proposed initial policies without settling open product choices: one sector, fixed-step committed batches, finite trusted G1 templates, conservative dependency rechecks and one configured execution backend. Step size, process fallback values, automatic admission thresholds, reaction/aging policies and budget ceilings remain open. Broader task workflows, optimized dependency indexes, staged world migrations and G2 are gated extensions, not prerequisites.

The [technical architecture](../07-technical-architecture/README.md) refines D08/D14/D19/D20/D28/D30 while retaining replaceable execution and storage boundaries; the current adapter and accepted PostgreSQL target do not prove broader platform performance. Proposed defaults are explicit task-specific context contracts, required-evidence/completeness gates, separate audience projections, game-owned canonical records and a replaceable Macrofold/direct execution port. Retrieval budgets/ranking, unknown-value initialization, consistency tolerances, replay retention, schema compatibility and acceptance thresholds remain implementation decisions. The [Macrofold handoff](../07-technical-architecture/macrofold-implementation-brief.md) specifies a phased candidate implementation; adoption still requires R19/R20 evidence.

The user has chosen the wilderness premise, tolerance for meaningful NPC death, grounded modern pixel art, PlayCanvas and ownership of the simulation. Remaining consequential preferences are the still-open embodied-view/hidden-tab details in D51, whether spoken interaction is essential immediately, the exact pace of life/aging, and how dangerous human-to-human interaction should be. Research can clarify consequences, but cannot decide those values.

Resolve the remaining parts of D01–D04 through focused discussion and, after implementation authorization, pacing experiments. The accepted 60:1 base rate and 0.5×/1×/3×/8× presets anchor memory/sleep timing; future overload and aging policy remain open. This archive uses explicit provisional assumptions to make the other proposals concrete; nothing has been provisioned on those assumptions.

## D58 — Durability and storage placement

The [performance design](../../docs/performance.md) preserves PostgreSQL as the durable authority, immediate durability for explicit commands and the existing bounded routine-progress save interval. Its immediate optimizations do not require a new decision.

If measured commit latency remains unacceptable after query reduction, choose whether to move application/database placement closer, introduce a recoverable durable local/replicated journal before SQL materialization, or explicitly accept losing recently acknowledged gameplay after a crash. The recommended default is to preserve durable confirmations and improve placement before changing authority. A local disk journal protects against process failure only to the extent of its tested flush/recovery policy; it does not inherently protect against host/disk loss. This needs the user's tolerated failure/loss window before any early-acknowledgement, offline-authority or durability-policy change. The existing [journal-first requirements](../07-technical-architecture/realtime-synchronization.md#optional-journal-first-persistence) remain prerequisites; an in-memory buffer cannot satisfy them.

## D59 — Historical retention and command retry horizon

The [performance design](../../docs/performance.md#simulation-cpu-and-growing-history) permits bounded active caches and source-preserving cold storage, without authorizing historical deletion. D14 continues to own actor recall/memory tuning.

Accepted September 25: preserve important events and summaries; routine detail may age out. This does not promise permanent exact transcripts, alter actor forgetting, or select a numerical deletion window. Choose routine hot/archive periods, what qualifies as important, required summary fidelity and storage budgets, and protected retention for story jobs/diagnostics. Recommendation: retain active obligations, invention/ownership/lifecycle evidence and save dependencies; label expired detail explicitly. The planning periods in the [scale design](../07-technical-architecture/data-delivery-and-scale.md#4-retention-archives-and-deletion) remain unaccepted until this policy is selected. The ordinary-gameplay retry contract belongs to [real-time synchronization](../07-technical-architecture/realtime-synchronization.md); current implementation and legacy compatibility belong to [Architecture](../../docs/architecture.md#performance-critical-path). Provider jobs, inventions, billing/accounting and administrative workflows are not shortened by gameplay expiry.

## D60 — Gameplay save and load policy

The [save/load design](../../docs/save-and-load.md) establishes consistent world capture, safe restoration and separation from non-rewindable external authority. The initial development slice retains one pre-load recovery slot and 64 MiB per payload, without a fixed named-save count. The [active development policy](../../docs/save-and-load.md#active-development-policy) explicitly disables legacy support until the owner lifts it. Choose autosave cadence (real or simulation time), retention budgets and, only after that block is lifted, a compatibility support window. Coordinate dependency retention with D59; no new historical deletion policy is selected here.

Before shared-world or cloud saves ship, choose who may rewind a shared world, how participants are notified, how divergent saves are selected or branched, and how cross-world effects constrain restoration. These choices do not block designing serializable state or implementing personal-world saves. Exact point-in-time rewind beyond retained checkpoints is not promised by the design.

## D61 — Shared-world scale and crowd behavior

Accepted September 25: support both a shared world divided into regions and independent worlds, prioritizing the shared world. Logical regions and independently stored records come first; multiple writers/database shards follow measured need and proven transfers. [Delivery and scale](../07-technical-architecture/data-delivery-and-scale.md#shared-world-regions-and-active-state) owns the design.

Still choose an initial concurrent-player/NPC target, largest expected local crowd, action/perception/network rates and resource budget. A million-player ambition alone is not a capacity test. For an overloaded region, decide between admission queues, lower simulation/detail fidelity where rules permit it, or slowdown. Recommend bounded admission with existing admitted interactions preserved before introducing fidelity changes; that recommendation is not an accepted player-entry policy. Cross-region time skew and the first supported boundary mechanics need explicit budgets before region writers split. A single shared clock does not imply one global lock or unlimited instantaneous global interactions.

## D62 — Recall latency and search quality

The owner requested a 20 ms maximum for the full local retrieval step, excluding embedding API calls. This is a desired target, not a measured guarantee. Earlier estimates of 1,000 candidates or 15–50 ms for 5,000 candidates were speculative and are not engineering evidence or accepted caps. The [query contract](../07-technical-architecture/data-queries-and-mcp.md#relevance-retrieval-without-whole-history-preparation) defines what to measure.

Choose the qualification hardware/load and whether success means a percentile target (recommend p99 with a separately bounded timeout) or a deadline that may yield incomplete optional recall. No architecture can guarantee successful completion within 20 ms under every failure/load. Exact ranking is the initial design; adopting approximate ranking requires a chosen acceptable miss rate, representative quality checks and actor-scope filtering evidence. Until resolved, preserve exact search, direct required evidence, bounded work and explicit incomplete/unavailable outcomes; do not impose an arbitrary history cutoff while claiming complete search.
