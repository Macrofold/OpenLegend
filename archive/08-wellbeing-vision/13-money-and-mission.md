# Money and mission

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). Comments on the existing [business plan](../06-marketing/business-plan.md) and [creator economy](../06-marketing/creator-economy-and-mechanics-packs.md) directions; nothing here changes an accepted decision.

**Later DG30 product proposal — 7 October 2026:** [Creator support and contributor voice](../../docs/projects/creator-support-and-contributor-voice-feature-spec.md) develops one conditional, manually operated grant cycle and open unweighted consultation. It selects a useful game/contributor result, honest evidence, current rights/privacy, bounded cost and no funded manipulation of affection or fabricated engagement. It adopts no general Humane Creator Standard, Resident Code, binding council, corporate mission lock or universal export promise. The original ideation below remains unchanged; actual appropriation, agreements and operating authority are required through [PD10.7](../../docs/maintainers/production-deployment.md#conditional-launch-and-expansion) before the corresponding activity.

A wellbeing vision that the business model fights will lose. Free-to-play games that earn most of their revenue from a small group of heavy spenders are pulled, month after month, toward finding and monetising vulnerability. Creator economies that pay by playtime pay creators to maximise everyone's hours. The good news: much of Open Legend's existing plan already points the right way.

---

## 1. What the existing plan already gets right

- **A flat subscription for a hosted world** (the business plan's test of one owner plan) separates revenue from moment-to-moment engagement.
- **A monthly invention allowance, explicitly *not* a daily reset or a requirement to log in every day.** That instinct is exactly the anti-streak principle.
- **A creator fund that makes grants for useful work,** reviewed against milestones, rather than paying by engagement.
- **A per-subscriber allocation where visiting more worlds doesn't create more money,** and where "NPC activity, faster simulated time, idle bots, and fabricated accounts must not mint payouts."
- **Hosting as a service business** that earns by operating worlds reliably, not by harvesting attention.
- **The archive's stance on advertising:** never let a trusted resident's affection carry commercial intent ([business ideation](../04-ideation/business-and-future-directions.md#advertising)).

## 2. Where the plan could drift, and how to hold the line

### The subscriber allocation: don't split by hours
The creator-economy direction earmarks part of each subscription for the worlds a subscriber uses, split by "qualifying human participation." If that split is proportional to *time played*, it quietly becomes Fortnite's and Roblox's model — creators paid to maximise hours. Alternatives:
- **Player-directed allocation.** Once a month, the subscriber is asked which worlds were worth it, and the earmark follows their answer (with a sensible default if they skip). This pays for value as the player judges it.
- **Distinct days, capped.** Count distinct days a player meaningfully visited, not hours; cap per player per day; count nothing beyond about three hours in a day.
- **Worth-it weighting.** Weight the allocation by sampled "was this worth it?" answers.
- **Completion of finite experiences.** Reward worlds for arcs, seasons and quests players finish, not for endless loops.
- **Publish the formula** so creators can see what it rewards.

### The creator fund: resist Goodhart
- Grants chosen by a jury or creator council, not a leaderboard.
- A bonus for meeting the **Humane Creator Standard** (below).
- Weight toward experiences with measured real-world value — meetups hosted, skills practised, language villages, educational worlds — not minutes.
- Adults-only payouts; low, transparent cash-out thresholds; no in-game currency that works like company scrip (the People Make Games critique of Roblox).

### Allowances: generous, legible, never anxious
- Show allowances plainly ("12 inventions left this month") without countdown pressure or "use it or lose it" urgency.
- Never let running out of allowance degrade relationships with residents or punish the player socially. The archive's rule that quota exhaustion must leave movement, known actions and social continuity playable is exactly right.

## 3. A Humane Creator Standard

A platform layer for any world listed in the marketplace:
- No paid randomness (loot boxes, gacha, paid random crafting).
- No energy timers, daily-login rewards, FOMO timers or decay-while-away.
- Creator-made residents run under the platform's Resident Code, farewell code and crisis protocol, which creators cannot remove.
- Age labels, persona review before minors can meet residents, and takedowns.
- Worlds can be exported by their players and owners.

## 4. Proposed commercial principles

1. **Membership, not free-to-play.** Flat monthly or annual plans, family plans, and a "pause membership" button as visible as "cancel."
2. **Fixed prices in real currency.** No premium currency — or, if one is unavoidable, mapped 1:1 to a visible price.
3. **Default spending caps and a cooling-off period;** refunds for accidental purchases; a spending summary players can check.
4. **Never sell affection.** No purchasable relationship progress, no "save your friend" purchases, no resident salesmanship — residents never mention paid items.
5. **No play-to-earn, no tradeable tokens, no gambling-like markets.** (Axie Infinity is the cautionary tale.)
6. **No advertising in residents' mouths,** ever. If sponsorship ever exists, it is visible, separate and never tied to a resident's affection.
7. **Minors can't spend,** or can only with parental approval and caps.

## 5. Revenue lines that grow with wellbeing, not hours

| Line | Why it's aligned |
|---|---|
| **Private hosted worlds** for friends, families and communities | Paid for operation and reliability; bonding worlds are where connection grows |
| **Hearth and heritage worlds** | Families pay for a place to be together across distance and generations |
| **Language villages** | A real, measurable skill; comparable to paying for classes |
| **Educator and library licences** | Institutions pay flat fees for worlds with learning objectives and evaluation |
| **Care homes and intergenerational programmes** | Flat licences; staff-facilitated play |
| **Clinician-partnered worlds** | Research partnerships and, only after trials, clinical programmes |
| **The bound legend** | Printed books of players' stories: revenue from meaning, not minutes |
| **Physical bridge objects** | A starter cord kit, field book and stamp card |
| **Host kits** for venues running Moot and Market Days | Game stores, cafés and libraries as partners |
| **Mechanics packs** | Paid for creative value, not engagement |

## 6. Lock the mission into structure

Missions survive only when they are written into ownership and governance. Niantic's mission, "adventures on foot with others,"† changed hands with its games in a $3.5 billion sale in 2025.

Options, from lightest to strongest:
- **Public benefit corporation** with an explicit wellbeing purpose in the charter.
- **B Corp certification** for outside accountability.
- **A creator council** with binding votes on marketplace rules and the Humane Creator Standard.
- **A golden share or purpose trust** able to block a sale to an engagement-maximising buyer. Patagonia moved its voting stock into a purpose trust: "Earth is now our only shareholder."
- **Exit to community:** a long-term path for ownership to move to players and creators.

## 7. A sunset protocol

Players now expect to own their worlds: the European "Stop Killing Games" citizens' initiative gathered about 1.29 million verified signatures, and California is weighing notice-and-patch requirements for shut-down games. Open Legend's AGPL engine makes a generous promise credible:
- Full export of worlds, journals and inventions.
- A self-hosting path for any community that wants to keep its world.
- Long notice before any closure, and endings for residents.
- Creator buy-back or escrow clauses in any sale. (*Zombies, Run!*'s co-creator used a buy-back clause in 2025 to rescue her game from a crypto owner.)

## 8. Why this is also a competitive advantage

- **Trust.** After the 2025–26 companion-chatbot lawsuits and platform retreats, a visibly safe AI world is something parents, schools, libraries and regulators actively prefer.
- **Regulation-readiness.** Companion laws in New York and California (in force) and Oregon and Washington (from 2027), the EU AI Act and the expected Digital Fairness Act all target practices this vision already avoids.
- **Word of mouth.** Manipulative goodbyes raised engagement, but the coercive and needy ones also raised intent to quit and negative word of mouth.
- **Market signals.** Co-op games (*Peak*, *Split Fiction*), the tabletop boom, "meet strangers for dinner" services and autonomous-resident games without language models (*Tomodachi Life*) all grew in 2025–26, while an AI-friend pendant's subway ads were defaced as "exploiting loneliness." Connection sells; loneliness-exploitation increasingly doesn't.
