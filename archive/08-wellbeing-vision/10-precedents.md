# Precedents: what has already crossed from play into life

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). Evidence grades: **A** randomised or peer-reviewed quantitative; **B** peer-reviewed observational or independent records; **C** figures reported by the organisation itself (close to marketing); **D** anecdote. Full catalogue with sources: [bridge precedents brief](research/03-bridge-precedents.md).

Plenty of games, apps and play-like rituals have changed real lives or the real world. Their track record is the best guide to what Open Legend can realistically do.

---

## Ten patterns across the precedents

1. **Recurring rituals at a fixed time and place last; one-off campaigns fade.** parkrun (weekly), Geocaching clean-ups (seasonal), Friday Night Magic (weekly), Pokémon GO Community Day (monthly since 2018), Japan's Pokémon stamp rally (most summers since 1997). They share a fixed time, a known place, near-zero cost to join, and a small token for showing up.
2. **Novelty gets people moving briefly; social structure keeps them moving.** Pokémon GO's step boost was gone by week six; its raids, festivals and community days are what lasted.
3. **Real science scales when the task fits the fiction, is fun in itself, earns in-game standing, and a scientist is waiting for the results.**
4. **Results become real when an institution receives them** — a library, a planning department, a lab, a city council with a budget.
5. **Kindness mechanics work when kindness is cheap, mostly anonymous, needs no return, and flows through narrow channels.**
6. **Roles and responsibility carry over better than content does.** Volunteers, guild leaders, elected councils.
7. **Communities outlive their games** — so let them move, preserve their work, and plan for endings.
8. **Money and obligation are the most corrosive bridges.** Play-to-earn collapsed; engagement-paid creator economies exploit; streaks rely on guilt.
9. **Location-based play pushes risk onto bodies, neighbourhoods and sacred sites,** and charging for remote play excludes people.
10. **The evidence base is thin, short-term and often commissioned.** That is an opening: Open Legend can pre-register outcomes and publish null results.

## 1. Movement and place

- **Pokémon GO (2016).** Engaged players added about 1,473 steps a day over 30 days (**A**, Althoff et al.); another study found +955 steps in week one, gone by week six (**A**, BMJ). Raids require physical togetherness; Community Day has run monthly since January 2018; GO Fest 2024 drew 1.2 million+ attendees (**C**, commissioned). Players reported "a sense of belonging, linked to a sense of place" (**B**). *Lesson:* design the recurring gathering first; the step boost is novelty.
- **Niantic's 2023 remote-raid price rise.** Framed as encouraging in-person play; provoked a boycott and was partly reversed in 2025; hit disabled, rural and caregiving players hardest. *Lesson:* reward in-person play distinctively, but never tax the accessible path.
- **Ingress (2012).** Players nominated 15 million real landmarks; the largest weekend "anomaly" event drew 10,000+; clubs formed that its founder didn't expect (**C/D**). *Lesson:* a player-made map of meaningful places is a civic asset — settle data ownership first.
- **Pikmin Bloom (2021).** Flowers bloom along your real walk; a daily "Lifelog" records your day (**C**). *Lesson:* a close cousin of the Narrator's journal — tie journal entries to the real day.
- **Geocaching's Cache In Trash Out (since 2002).** 875,000+ attendees at 50,000+ clean-up events, rewarded with a seasonal digital souvenir (**C**). *Lesson:* a cheap token for real service, in fixed seasons, can last decades.
- ***Zombies, Run!* (2012).** An audio drama that advances only when you run; 10 million+ lifetime users; only 15–20% of buyers still active by 2015 (**B/C**). Co-creator Naomi Alderman bought it back from a crypto company in 2025 so it "doesn't end up being turned into AI slop." *Lesson:* story is a great pacer for real exercise; protect it from monetisation detours.
- **Japan's stamp rallies and station stamps.** Themed routes of rubber stamps at stations, descended from pilgrimage seals; "long lines formed every day" at the Pokémon rally (**C/D**). *Lesson:* a physical passport of real places is a century-old bridge that families do together.

## 2. Citizen science and real work

- **Foldit (2008).** Players solved a monkey-virus protease structure in 10 days that had resisted solution for 15 years; later designed proteins confirmed by crystallography (**A/B**). *Lesson:* give real problems and real credit; a small expert core can do frontier work.
- **EteRNA (2010).** Players design RNA molecules built in a Stanford lab; players appear as co-authors (**A/B**). *Lesson:* seeing your design built in reality motivates deeply.
- **EVE Online Project Discovery.** 41.4 million COVID-19 classifications from 157,000 players in months (**B/C**). "The accuracy of their analyses is very high." *Lesson:* make the task part of the fiction and pay in in-game standing.
- **Borderlands Science (2020).** 4.5 million players; in half a day they produced five times the data an earlier game had gathered in ten years (**A/B**, *Nature Biotechnology* 2024). *Lesson:* put the science where players already are.
- **Sea Hero Quest (2016).** 4.3 million players; showed that people carrying an Alzheimer's-risk gene navigated less efficiently before symptoms (**A/B**). *Lesson:* play can be an opt-in research instrument — with care for health-data consent.
- **Zooniverse's Anti-Slavery Manuscripts (2018–20).** 26,059 volunteers transcribed 12,247 letters for the Boston Public Library, each three times for cross-checking (**B/C**). *Lesson:* humanities work suits scribe and archive roles; community forms in the discussion forums.
- **World of Warcraft's Corrupted Blood plague (2005).** An accidental in-game epidemic that epidemiologists later studied; healers acted as first responders while griefers spread it on purpose (**B**). *Lesson:* in-world crises can teach, with consent and anti-griefing design.

## 3. Civic participation and governance

- **Block by Block (UN-Habitat, Mojang, Microsoft, since 2012).** Residents model public spaces in Minecraft; results built in Kenya's Kalobeyei settlement (solar lights, 500+ trees) and Hanoi (underpasses cleaned and lit) (**C**). *Lesson:* pair the design tool with a budget and a decision-maker, or it's consultation theatre.
- **The Uncensored Library (Reporters Without Borders, 2020).** A Minecraft library of censored journalism, 1 million+ visits (**C**); a Peabody citation called it "an innovative back door for access to censored content." *Lesson:* game worlds can carry civic information.
- **EVOKE (World Bank, 2010).** A 10-week game on social innovation; 19,324 registrants; 74 venture proposals; evaluators found "less effect on building a network of colleagues and friends" (**C**). *Lesson:* idea contests don't create ventures without sustained mentorship and money.
- **World Without Oil (2007).** "Play it — before you live it": players narrated their lives through a fictional oil shock; a handful of players dominated (**B/C**). *Lesson:* design for the quiet majority.
- **A Tale in the Desert (2003).** No combat; laws pass by petition and vote; a "clear-cutting" skill felled about 3,450 of 15,300 trees before a player law stopped it; now run by a studio its players founded (**D**). *Lesson:* shared resources plus law-making produce real deliberation — and real abuses of power.
- **EVE Online's player council and economist.** An elected Council of Stellar Management since 2008; an in-house economist publishing quarterly reports (**C**). *Lesson:* representation plus published data is civic practice.
- **Eco (Strange Loop Games).** Players share an ecology, pass laws and set taxes while stopping a meteor; over $1 million in US Department of Education support (**C**). *Lesson:* ecology and law work as mechanics; classrooms need teacher tools.

## 4. Charity and giving

- **Games Done Quick (since 2010).** About $60 million for charity; donors bid to shape the show, donations are read aloud, and the tally is public (**B/C**).
- **Extra Life (since 2008).** 24-hour marathons for local children's hospitals; $145 million+ by its own count (**C**).
- ***Sky: Children of the Light* charity seasons.** Cosmetic purchases funding causes such as Médecins Sans Frontières (**C**).
- **Forest (2014).** Your virtual tree dies if you leave the focus app; coins fund real trees — 2 million+ planted (**C**). *Lesson for all four:* frame giving as a festival and keep it transparent; don't overclaim individual impact.

## 5. Kindness and asynchronous help

- ***Kind Words* (2019).** 4 million+ anonymous letters of support; about 3% needed moderation; BAFTA for "Game Beyond Entertainment" (**C/D**).
- ***Journey* (2012).** An anonymous companion who communicates only in musical chirps; names appear only in the credits.
- ***Sky: Children of the Light* (2019).** Strangers befriend each other by gifting candles; chat unlocks as friendship grows; 62% of surveyed players were helped by veterans (**C/D**).
- ***Death Stranding* (2019).** Other players' ladders and bridges appear in your world; thanks flows as one-way "likes."
- *Lesson:* one-shot, low-cost, low-risk kindness with a way to say thanks creates community without dependency — but offline effects are barely measured.

## 6. Rituals and third places

- **parkrun (since 2004).** In 2024–25: 2.3 million participants, 309,220 volunteers; 81% report being happier, volunteers more than runners (**B/C**). *Lesson:* "not a race," plus volunteer roles, plus milestones, builds belonging.
- **Friday Night Magic.** Weekly casual tournaments in about 6,000 stores; during COVID, rewards were routed through local stores to keep them alive (**C/D**). *Lesson:* route digital rewards through local venues so the third place survives.
- **D&D Adventurers League.** A portable character across tables and stores (**D**). *Lesson:* a portable identity turns strangers into a recurring table.
- **Warhammer stores and board-game cafés.** Staff teach painting or games in person; Toronto's Snakes & Lattes deliberately offered no Wi-Fi. *Lesson:* a human teacher in a welcoming space turns beginners into regulars.
- **Wii bowling leagues (2009–).** 242 teams from 116 senior communities in 30 states by 2012 (**D**). *Lesson:* simple controls, team identity and friendly rivalry between communities.
- **Animal Crossing weddings and graduations; Berkeley's "Blockeley" commencement; a WoW memorial ambushed by a rival guild.** *Lesson:* worlds can host real rites of passage, and sacred gatherings need protection.
- **The Uru diaspora (2004).** About 10,000 players displaced by a game's closure rebuilt their culture elsewhere (Celia Pearce, *Communities of Play*). *Lesson:* community can move to a new home if people can find each other.
- **Virtual Ability in Second Life (2007).** Founded by friends with disabilities; became a real nonprofit serving 1,000+ people (**C**). *Lesson:* for people who can't easily go out, virtual presence *is* real connection.

## 7. Real skills and careers

- **Rocksmith.** You play a real guitar; Ubisoft's claims are strong, independent evidence is thin (**C**). *Lesson:* real instruments teach real skills; test claims independently.
- **Kerbal Space Program.** NASA co-created content and invited players to fly a replica asteroid mission: "Here is a community of people that is enthusiastic about space and wants to learn the science of spaceflight."
- **VATSIM (2001).** A volunteer network simulating air-traffic control with real procedures; student pilots practise radio calls. *Lesson:* a volunteer layer that follows real procedures turns a simulator into an apprenticeship.
- **Nissan's GT Academy (2008–2016).** Turned about two dozen *Gran Turismo* players, out of more than 5 million entrants, into professional racing drivers — after real-world training bridged the gap.
- **World of Warcraft guild leaders.** IBM's 2007 study: about half of surveyed gamers felt leading guilds improved their real leadership (**C**). "You can never stop earning the right to be their leader."
- **Scratch (2007).** Over 100 million users; remixes credit the original automatically; gentle central moderation (**C**). *Lesson:* remix-with-credit plus moderation produces enormous youth creativity with little harm.

## 8. Self-care and habit apps

- **SuperBetter.** Quest framing with "allies" reduced depression symptoms in a randomised trial (d = .67 post-test), with high dropout (**A**, caveats).
- **Finch.** A pet bird that grows with gentle self-care goals and no punishment; no clinical studies (**D**). *Lesson:* a creature to care for, without guilt.
- **Habitica.** In boss fights, "When one player fails to complete Dailies, the boss attacks all of the players" — accountability that can tip into shame (**C**).
- **Duolingo streaks.** "Learners who reach a streak of 7 days are 2.4x more likely to use Duolingo the next day" — and its guilt-laden notifications are widely criticised (**C**). *Lesson:* streaks keep users by pressuring them; Open Legend's pause is the principled alternative.
- **Strava and running clubs.** Clubs nearly quadrupled to 1 million in 2025; Gen Z is 39% more likely than Gen X to use fitness to meet people (**C**). *Lesson:* scheduled group events are what last.

## 9. Creator economies

- **Roblox.** Creators earned over $1 billion in a year, but the median creator earns about $1,500 a year while the top ten averaged $33.9 million (**B/C**). Journalists documented unfavourable splits and young developers doing the work; 2026 settlements with state attorneys general required facial age estimation and chat controls, with 140+ suits pending. *Lesson:* children plus strangers plus user content plus engagement-paid creators is a systemic risk; retrofitting safety is expensive.
- **Super Mario Maker (2015).** Before its servers closed in 2024, players cleared about 25,000 never-beaten levels. *Lesson:* communities go to heroic lengths to preserve their work — treat shutdown as a design moment.
- **Dreams (2020).** Live support ended in 2023. *Lesson:* creations locked to one platform are fragile; build in export and ownership.

## 10. Cautionary tales

- **Pokémon GO's side effects.** Crashes rose near PokéStops in one Indiana county — two extra deaths over 148 days, extrapolated to $2–7.3 billion nationally (**B**, NBER); play at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and Auschwitz; a Chicago festival meltdown ending in a $1.575 million settlement. *Lesson:* exclude sensitive sites, block play at driving speeds, plan capacity.
- **Axie Infinity (play-to-earn).** Adopted as income in the Philippines; "managers" took up to 75% of players' earnings; daily players fell from 2.7 million to about 250,000 and its token lost over 99%; hackers stole $620 million (**B/C**). *Lesson:* never turn the fun into a wage.
- **Gamified labour.** Disneyland staff called productivity leaderboards an "electronic whip"; Ian Bogost called gamification "exploitationware." *Lesson:* Open Legend's metrics must never become a tool for employers or insurers.
- **Social scoring, fact and fiction.** *Black Mirror*'s "Nosedive" is fiction; China's actual social credit system is fragmented and mostly aimed at businesses — "such a score simply does not exist" for individuals (MERICS). *Lesson:* keep reputation local, contextual, explainable and forgiving, and describe China accurately.
