## 1. Questions this vision raises

These are the choices that would most shape how the wellbeing vision plays out. Each has a default suggestion, not a recommendation to adopt without thought.

| # | Question | Suggested starting point |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Is "lives, not hours" the north star — "worth it" and human connection as headline metrics, with time played as a guardrail only? | Yes, from the first playable; it's cheapest to set before habits form. |
| Q2 | Should AI residents launch to verified adults first? | Yes. Add a world-bounded teen mode only after safety testing and legal review; keep a no-language-model path for younger players. |
| Q3 | Treat residents as legal "AI companions" from day one, or design a world-bounded default that may fit the game exemptions? | Both: two envelopes. World-bounded by default; open conversation for verified adults under full companion compliance. |
| Q4 | Make the Resident Code and a 0% farewell audit a release gate for every model or prompt change? | Yes. It's cheap to automate and protects against the most-abused moment. |
| Q5 | How opt-in should real-world bridges be? | Gentle and rare by default, one tap to turn off entirely, never repeated after dismissal. |
| Q6 | May the Narrator and residents ever reference the player's real life? | Only through anchors the player chose to share, private and deletable. |
| Q7 | Should in-world dusk align with the player's bedtime, and should each game day end in an explicit "Begin tomorrow"? | Offer bedtime sync as an option; make day's end a chapter close by default. |
| Q8 | In shared worlds, should major life events of an absent player's close residents be held until they return? | Yes, or narrated as a "while you were away" letter — never a push notification. |
| Q9 | Should the subscriber allocation to creators be split by time played? | No: player-directed ("which worlds were worth it?"), or distinct capped days, never hours. |
| Q10 | Lock the mission into structure (public benefit corporation, purpose trust)? When? | Decide before outside investment or a sale becomes plausible; structure is hardest to add late. |
| Q11 | Partner with academic researchers and pre-register outcomes? | Yes, early; Oxford's telemetry studies show what publisher–researcher partnerships make possible. |
| Q12 | Which world should lead on bridges? | Wilderness for craft and nature; Threewater for community and civic life; Linden Reach for rehearsal and adult life. |
| Q13 | Is player–resident romance allowed at all? | Adults only, opt-in, never paid; make resident–resident and player–player romance the main love stories. |
| Q14 | Resident permadeath: default on or off for bonded residents? | Foreshadowed deaths with rituals; an opt-out for a player's closest residents; absence never causes a death. |
| Q15 | Would you ever close every official world for a weekend a year — the Long Rest? | Worth deciding as a statement of identity, not only a feature. |

## 2. First experiments (cheap, soon, measurable)

Designed to fit the current wilderness prototype and the first playable, with Ada and the camp.

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis | Measure | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Ada's farewell audit** — script 100+ goodbyes across moods; score for the six manipulative tactics; fix prompts | Current residents already produce some "before you go" hooks | Share of manipulative farewells (target 0%) | Small |
| E2 | **The Return Rite** — the Narrator closes each session with a page and one skippable line | A designed ending raises "worth it" and exit mood without lowering return rates | Worth-it tap, exit mood, next-week return | Small |
| E3 | **The camp sleeps** — optional bedtime; dusk and yawning residents near it | Fewer sessions run past players' own bedtimes | Sessions past bedtime; player sentiment | Small |
| E4 | **The first cord** — a "try it for real" card (and, for a pilot group, a physical jute kit) when players first make cord | A finishable real craft feels delightful, not preachy | Opt-in "I did it"; interviews | Small |
| E5 | **Tollbooth Note variants** — noticing / person / try-it prompts at session end | Specific, story-grounded prompts beat generic ones | Dismissal rate; self-reported follow-through | Small |
| E6 | **Worth-it and with-whom logging** | Baselines for the north stars | Worth-it rate; alone / residents / people | Small |
| E7 | **Two envelopes and the lantern protocol** — a world-bounded Ada; red-team crisis prompts including in-fiction disguises | World-bounded conversation stays engaging; crisis handling is reliable | Red-team pass rate; player ratings of Ada | Medium |
| E8 | **Active, constructive residents** — Ada responds to achievements with delight and "who else would love to see this?" | Residents can nudge sharing with real people without annoyance | Self-reported sharing; dismissal rate | Small |
| E9 | **Predict, test, explain** — a mentor asks for a prediction before generating an invention | Deliberate invention reduces re-rolling and deepens understanding | Re-roll rate; invention quality; player explanations | Small |
| E10 | **Hearth circles** (once multiplayer works) — 4–6 players, weekly, a resident host who fades | Small stable groups create lasting player friendships | Mutual friendships at 4 and 8 weeks; loneliness (UCLA-3) | Medium |
| E11 | **Rehearsal Hall prototype** — one scene type (e.g., asking for help) with feedback and a "schedule the real one" bridge | Players find rehearsal useful and try the real conversation | Follow-through; perceived usefulness | Medium |
| E12 | **The Speaking** — a funeral ritual when a camp member dies | Ritualised deaths feel meaningful rather than punishing | Player sentiment; opt-out rate; grief-related distress signals | Medium |

Run them as small randomised comparisons where possible. Forecasters could not predict which of 54 behaviour nudges would work in a large *Nature* megastudy, so test rather than argue.
