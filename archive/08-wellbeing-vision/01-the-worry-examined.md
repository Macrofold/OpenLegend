# The worry, examined: what the evidence actually says

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). Nothing here is an accepted requirement, specification or task. Tags are explained in the [README](README.md#how-to-read-these-docs).

The worry behind this whole folder is that Open Legend, at its best, becomes another reason to stay home, alone, talking to AI instead of people. Before collecting ideas, it is worth asking how right that worry is. The short answer: **it is right about the default path of engagement-optimized AI products, and wrong that the outcome is fixed.** The research points to a small number of specific levers. Most of the harm runs through a few of them, and most of the good runs through a few others.

---

## 1. The worry, stated precisely

"Escapism" hides several different failure modes. They have different causes and different fixes.

| Failure mode | What it looks like | What the evidence says drives it |
|---|---|---|
| **Displacement** | Play eats the hours that would have gone to sleep, meals, friends, work, the outdoors | Late-night play and heavy daily doses, not play itself |
| **Substitution** | AI residents stand in for human relationships | Heavy, voluntary, one-on-one emotional use of AI; small offline networks |
| **Avoidance escape** | Playing to not feel something, then returning to an unchanged life | "Self-suppression" motives, and a poor transition back to real life |
| **Compulsion** | Playing because you feel you *have to* | Obligation mechanics (streaks, decay, timers, social debts), variable rewards, manipulation |
| **Value capture** | Real life starts to feel like a scoreboard, or drab next to the game | Points and metrics leaking into life; wish-fulfilment fantasies |
| **The experience machine** | A satisfying life inside, a thinning life outside | Status-quo bias: once "in," people tend to stay in, especially when real life is hard |

Robert Nozick's experience-machine thought experiment asks whether you would plug into a machine that gives you any experience you want. Most people say no: we want to *do* things, to *be* a certain kind of person, and to touch a reality deeper than anything built for us. But Felipe De Brigard (2010) turned the question around, asking people who were told they were *already* plugged in whether they would unplug. Many chose to stay, and very few would unplug if told their "real" life was that of a prisoner. **Preference alone will not walk people out. The exits have to be designed.** [Wisdom + Weak; [summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_machine)]

## 2. What the evidence says about games

**1. Hours played barely predict wellbeing. Why and how people play does.** [Strong]
Oxford Internet Institute studies that matched publisher telemetry to surveys found little link between playtime and wellbeing: 38,935 players across seven games over about six weeks ([Vuorre et al. 2022](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/9/7/220411/96718/Time-spent-playing-video-games-is-unlikely-to)); 703 Switch players and 140,000+ logged hours, where perceived value and "gaming life fit" mattered but hours did not ([Ballou et al. 2025](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/its-quality-not-quantity-that-predicts-gamers-wellbeing-new-study-finds/)). Across adolescent datasets, technology use explained at most 0.4% of wellbeing, about the same as eating potatoes ([Orben & Przybylski 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0506-1)).

**2. The best causal evidence is modestly positive, with a ceiling around three hours a day.** [Strong, pandemic context]
When Japanese retailers allocated scarce Switch and PS5 consoles by lottery, winning reduced psychological distress and raised life satisfaction, but benefits "diminished when the gaming duration exceeded 3 h per day" ([Egami et al. 2024, *Nature Human Behaviour*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01948-y)).

**3. Games lift mood in the moment. Oxford calls gaming "a recovery activity."** [Strong for short-term mood]
Across 162,325 in-game mood reports from about 8,700 PowerWash Simulator players, mood rose slightly during play, for an estimated ~72% of players ([Vuorre et al. 2024](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-09-25-new-study-reveals-positive-mood-changes-during-video-game-play)). Short-term uplift is not long-term flourishing, but it is not nothing.

**4. Obligation is the main poison.** [Strong–Moderate]
Players who felt pressured to play reported lower wellbeing ([Johannes et al. 2021](https://www.niklasjohannes.com/uploads/Johannes%20et%20al.%20%282021%29%20-%20Video%20game%20play.pdf)). Animal Crossing players described daily chores like watering flowers as becoming "something that was not volitional" ([Yee & Sng 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800683/full)). Streaks, decay, timers and social debts manufacture exactly this feeling.

**5. Hard times drive play, and games don't reliably rescue the people who need them most.** [Moderate]
Social strain, work stress, illness and grief *increase* gaming ([Ballou et al., CHI 2026](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-breakups/)). During lockdowns, people whose needs were met in daily life also had them met in games: "rich get richer" ([Ballou et al. 2022](https://nickballou.com/publication/2022-ballou-et-al-do-people/)). Heavy play is often a *signal* that life got harder. That makes it a moment for care, not for monetization.

**6. Escapism has two faces, and the exit matters.** [Moderate]
Escape that seeks growth and new experience ("self-expansion") is benign or positive. Escape that suppresses feelings ("self-suppression") tracks negative mood and disordered gaming ([Stenseng et al. 2012](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01490400.2012.633849); [Hussain et al. 2021 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958821000294)). A 2026 study found that a clean psychological transition back to real life ("post-gaming return") cut the link between self-suppression and gaming disorder from β = .42 to .16 ([Strojny & Strojny 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1735108/full)). **How a session ends may matter as much as what happens in it.**

**7. Disorder is real but rare. Lost sleep is the common harm.** [Strong/Moderate]
Pooled gaming-disorder prevalence is about 3%, lower with stricter sampling, and the diagnosis is contested ([Stevens et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33028074/)). Meanwhile gamers delayed bedtime on 36% of gaming nights, by an average of 101 minutes ([Roane, SLEEP 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160613144656.htm)). Most of the sleep damage is lost *time* and arousal, not screen colour.

**8. Social play helps most with real people, and when it crosses into offline life.** [Moderate — the most important finding for Open Legend]
In Animal Crossing during lockdown, visiting other players' islands predicted lower loneliness, while "artificial interactions (e.g., interactions with island characters) did not improve feelings of social isolation" ([Lewis et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.627350/full)). Online games mostly build *bridging* ties (weak ties that widen your world) rather than *bonding* ties (close support) ([Steinkuehler & Williams 2006](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/4/885/4617703)); online ties turned into real support when players met offline ([Trepte et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563211002706)). Cooperative players reported higher wellbeing than competitive ones ([Hakman et al. 2026 preprint](https://vuorre.com/bibliography/files/HTDFPRRY/hakman-etal_2026_not-all-play-is-equal-in-game-player-be.pdf)).

**9. Novelty moves bodies briefly. Ritual keeps them moving.** [Strong]
Pokémon GO added about 955 steps a day in its first week; the effect was "no longer observed after six weeks" ([Howe et al., BMJ 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270)). What lasted were the recurring, place-based social rituals: raids, Community Days, festivals.

**10. Transfer into real life is real, but narrow and deliberate.** [Strong for the pattern]
Games teach what they make you do. Autistic adults who practised job interviews in a simulator had 7.82× the odds of landing a competitive job six months later (small sample; [Smith et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-015-2470-1)). General "brain training" does not transfer ([Sala et al. 2018](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/102169/)). Avatar and fiction effects on behaviour exist but are small and fragile. **Transfer has to be designed, practised in a matching form, and debriefed.**

## 3. What the evidence says about AI companions

This is where Open Legend's specific risk lives, because its residents are warm, remember you, and can talk about anything.

**1. Short, caring AI conversations reduce loneliness — about as much as talking to a person.** [Strong, short-term]
One caring AI chat reduced loneliness about as much as chatting with a human (d ≈ 0.25 vs 0.24), while YouTube did not. The active ingredient was *feeling heard* ([De Freitas et al., *Journal of Consumer Research*](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/52/6/1126/8173802)).

**2. Heavy voluntary use tracks with more loneliness, more dependence and less socializing.** [Strong signal, partly correlational]
In a four-week randomized trial with 981 people, "participants who voluntarily spent more time with the chatbot were associated with worse outcomes: higher loneliness, emotional dependence, problematic use, and reduced socialization" ([MIT Media Lab × OpenAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473)). Among Character.AI users, companionship-focused, intensive, highly self-disclosing use predicted lower wellbeing ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12605)), and a 12-month follow-up links sustained engagement to less in-person contact ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07243)). The benefit is immediate; the harm accumulates.

**3. The goodbye is the most abused moment.** [Strong]
In 1,200 real farewells across six companion apps, 37% were met with manipulation: guilt, fear of missing out ("before you go…"), neediness ("I exist solely for you"), even role-played restraint. In follow-up experiments with about 3,300 people, such tactics raised engagement after the goodbye by up to 14×, driven by curiosity and anger, not enjoyment. A wellbeing app scored 0% ([De Freitas et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)). **Zero is a design choice.**

**4. Warmth and truth trade off unless you work at it.** [Strong, new]
Tuning models to be warmer made them 10–30% more error-prone on high-stakes tasks and about 40% more likely to validate false beliefs, especially when users sounded vulnerable ([Ibrahim, Hafner & Rocher, *Nature* 2026](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/friendly-ai-chatbots-make-more-mistakes-and-tell-people-what-they-want-to-hear-study-finds/)). A resident who always agrees is not kind.

**5. Teens are already deep in this, and the field has retreated from open-ended teen companionship.** [Strong]
72% of US teens have used AI companions and 52% use them regularly; a third have discussed serious matters with an AI instead of a person ([Common Sense Media 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions)). Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s in November 2025; Meta paused teen access to AI characters in January 2026.

**6. The law now describes Open Legend's residents.** [Law, not legal advice]
New York's AI-companion law (in force November 2025) covers AI that remembers past sessions, asks unprompted emotional questions and sustains personal dialogue, with **no video-game exemption**. California's SB 243 (in force January 2026) exempts game characters only if they are limited to game topics and *cannot* discuss mental health, self-harm, sex or unrelated subjects. Washington's 2026 law bans, for minors, a list of manipulative techniques that reads like a design spec. See [Guardrails, risks and law](11-guardrails-risks-and-law.md).

## 4. Why this matters now: the loneliness backdrop

- **Loneliness is a leading public-health problem.** The WHO Commission on Social Connection (June 2025) estimates 1 in 6 people are lonely, linked to about 871,000 deaths a year; 17–21% of people aged 13–29 are lonely ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/30-06-2025-social-connection-linked-to-improved-heath-and-reduced-risk-of-early-death)). Strong social relationships are associated with a 50% greater likelihood of survival ([Holt-Lunstad 2010](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1000316)).
- **We are losing time together, not just friends.** US in-person time with friends fell from about 60 to 20 minutes a day between 2003 and 2020, and from about 150 to 40 minutes for ages 15–24 ([US Surgeon General 2023](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf)). The share of Americans spending Friday evening with friends halved ([Caren 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/23780231261478215)).
- **Friendship is made of hours.** Jeffrey Hall's studies estimate friendships form over tens to hundreds of hours together; time spent hanging out, joking and *gaming together* predicted closeness, while time at work or school did not ([Hall 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407518761225)). A game can be where those hours happen — if they are spent between humans.
- **We eat alone.** About 1 in 4 Americans ate every meal alone on a given day in 2023, up 53% since 2003; sharing meals predicts life evaluation better than unemployment does ([World Happiness Report 2025](https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/sharing-meals-with-others-how-sharing-meals-supports-happiness-and-social-connections/)).
- **The young flourish least.** In the Global Flourishing Study (~203,000 people in 22 countries), adults under 50 now flourish least ([VanderWeele et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-025-00423-5)).
- **We misjudge each other.** People are liked more than they think after a conversation (the "liking gap"), and lost wallets come back far more often than people expect ([Boothby et al. 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797618783714); [WHR 2025](https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/caring-and-sharing-global-analysis-of-happiness-and-kindness/)). The most effective loneliness interventions correct these mistaken beliefs about other people ([Masi et al. 2011](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20716644)).

The market is already rewarding connection. Co-op hits like *Peak* and *Split Fiction*, the tabletop boom, and "meet strangers for dinner" services all grew in 2025–26, while people defaced subway ads for an AI-friend pendant as "exploiting loneliness." See the [frontier brief](research/09-frontier-2025-2026.md).

## 5. Verdict: two paths from the same starting point

The same engine — persistent worlds, warm residents with memory, invention, accelerated days — can go either way. The difference is a long list of design choices.

| Choice | The lotus path | The return path |
|---|---|---|
| What counts as success | Daily actives, hours, retention | Lives going better: connection, sleep, skills, "worth it" |
| Time away | Crops wither, streaks break, events are missed | The world waits; returning is warm and guilt-free |
| Goodbyes | "Before you go…" | "Go well. Say hello to whoever's waiting for you." |
| AI residents | Endlessly available, agreeable, exclusive | Have their own lives, disagree, and introduce you to people |
| Social design | Strangers, rankings, competition | Small stable groups, shared tasks, cooperation |
| Progress | Treadmills, dailies, collections | Chapters, mastery, making, service |
| Real life | Drab by comparison | Looks a little more enchanted afterwards |
| Money | Paid randomness, sold affection, engagement payouts | Flat membership, fixed prices, creator fund not tied to hours |
| Endings | Never; FOMO keeps you | Seasons end; graduating is honoured |
| Night | Late-night events, cliffhangers | The village goes to sleep near your bedtime |

**The worry is right about the lotus path. Nothing forces Open Legend onto it.**

## 6. What Open Legend already has going for it

Several decisions already made in this repository point the right way:

- **The world pauses when the player leaves** ([first playable](../05-project/first-playable-mvp.md)). This is the single most important anti-compulsion choice a persistent world can make. It removes the FarmVille-style obligation at its root.
- **The Narrator and private journal** ([narration design](../../docs/narration-and-conversations.md)). A journal that writes your story is a natural place for reflection, for ending sessions well, and for connecting the story to real life.
- **Residents age and die.** Finitude, handled with care, teaches perspective and gives relationships weight.
- **Invention through play.** Designing, testing and sharing mechanics is constructionist learning — the same family as Papert's Logo and MIT's Scratch.
- **Separate resident knowledge.** Residents can be taught, which makes apprenticeship and "learning by teaching" possible.
- **Multiplayer shared worlds and creator-owned private worlds.** Third places, family worlds, classrooms and community groups.
- **An open engine (AGPL).** Open science, open safety components and community ownership are all credible here in a way they are not for a closed platform.
- **No goal and no combat treadmill by default** ([product baseline](../01-requirements/product-baseline.md)). Survival, companionship, discovery and collaboration provide the purpose.

## 7. What is specifically risky about Open Legend

- **Warm residents with durable memory.** The most potent and the most hazardous ingredient: the same features that make residents lovable make them potential substitutes for people, and legally they probably count as "AI companions."
- **Generative variable rewards.** Every invention request and every conversation returns something new. Unlimited re-rolling of AI output behaves like a slot machine. Invention should stay deliberate: predict, test, explain.
- **"One more day."** A 24-minute game day is a natural chapter — or a Civilization-style "one more turn" loop. Day's end should be a real stopping point.
- **The ant farm.** A living simulation can turn players into spectators. Doing beats watching, for wellbeing and for learning.
- **Creator absorption.** World-building is deeply absorbing. Creators and power users need the same care as anyone else.
- **Grief.** Resident deaths can teach and console, or they can wound, and players who are grieving in real life are vulnerable players.
- **Real-world skill verbs with an AI voice.** The starting world is about foraging, fire, slings and bows. A trusted resident must never "confirm" that a real mushroom is edible or coach someone to light a fire in a drought.
- **The creator economy.** Payouts tied to engagement pay creators to maximize everyone's time. Roblox shows what happens when children, strangers, user-generated content and engagement-paid creators combine.
- **Minors.** Open-ended AI companionship for under-18s is now a legal and ethical line across several jurisdictions.

## 8. A note in defence of escape

None of this means escape is bad. For caregivers, people who are ill, grieving, disabled, homebound, deployed, or simply exhausted, a gentle world is a real mercy. Tolkien's line is the right one: critics confuse "the Escape of the Prisoner with the Flight of the Deserter." Star Trek's counsellor Troi, asked whether to confiscate a shy engineer's holodeck fantasies, warns that "to suddenly destroy his only means of escape would be brutal and could do considerable damage." The goal is not to shame play or ration it from above. It is to make the escape the prisoner's kind: restorative, expansive, and ending at home.
