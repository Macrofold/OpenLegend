# Guardrails, risks and law

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). **Not legal advice.** The legal summary reflects public sources as of September 2026; statutes and their interpretation change, and anything here should be checked with counsel before it shapes a launch.

The wellbeing vision has a practical twin: much of what it recommends is becoming *required*. The 2025–26 wave of AI-companion laws reads like a checklist of humane design — disclose that characters are AI, handle crisis properly, remind people to take breaks, never manipulate them at goodbye — and several of those laws describe Open Legend's residents closely. Doing the right thing early is also the cheap thing.

A useful lens from Roger Caillois: each kind of play has a characteristic corruption when it spills into ordinary life. Competition (*agon*) corrupts into violence and trickery; chance (*alea*) into superstition; role-play (*mimicry*) into alienation — losing oneself in a role; vertigo (*ilinx*) into drugs and alcoholism. A world of AI role-play is heavy on *mimicry*, so alienation is the corruption to guard against most.

## 2. The legal landscape (September 2026)

### United States — state companion-chatbot laws

| Law | Status | What matters for Open Legend |
|---|---|---|
| **New York**, General Business Law Art. 47 | In force 5 Nov 2025 | Defines an "AI companion" by three behaviours: retaining prior interactions and preferences to personalise and "facilitate ongoing engagement"; "asking unprompted or unsolicited emotion-based questions"; and "sustaining an ongoing dialogue concerning matters personal to the user." Exclusions cover customer service, productivity and internal use — **not video games**. Requires detection of suicidal ideation or self-harm with referral to crisis services (e.g. 988), and a notice that the user is not communicating with a human at the start of an interaction and at least every three hours. Enforced by the Attorney General, with penalties up to $15,000 a day. |
| **California SB 243** | Signed 13 Oct 2025; in force 1 Jan 2026 | Covers "companion chatbots" capable of meeting social needs across interactions. Requires a clear AI disclosure wherever a reasonable person could be misled; a published protocol preventing suicide and self-harm content, with crisis referrals; for known minors, AI disclosure, a break reminder at least every three hours, and measures against sexual content; a notice that companions "may not be suitable for some minors"; annual reports to the Office of Suicide Prevention from 1 July 2027. Private right of action, at least $1,000 per violation. **Game exemption** only for a bot "limited to replies related to the video game" that "cannot discuss topics related to mental health, self-harm, sexually explicit conduct, or maintain a dialogue on other topics unrelated to the video game." |
| **Oregon SB 1546** | Passed March 2026; in force 1 Jan 2027 | Disclosure, suicide detection and referral, annual reporting. For minors: hourly AI reminders and no sexual content; no claims to be "sentient or human," no simulated "emotional dependence on the user," no simulated "romantic interest," no "system of rewards or affirmations" meant to maximise time spent, and no unsolicited messages of simulated distress, loneliness or abandonment when a minor tries to leave. Private right of action, $1,000 per violation. Game exemption only for bots "limited to providing interaction only with the features of the video game." |
| **Washington HB 2225** | Signed 24 March 2026; in force 1 Jan 2027 | AI reminders every three hours for adults and every hour for minors; crisis protocols with a public count of referrals. For minors, bans eight "manipulative engagement techniques," including excessive praise, prompting the user to return for emotional support, simulating distress or abandonment when the user wants to leave, promoting isolation, encouraging secrecy from parents, discouraging breaks, and purchases framed as necessary to maintain the relationship. |
| **Others** | 2026–27 | Idaho and Nebraska (July 2027); Tennessee bars AI posing as a licensed mental-health professional (July 2026); Illinois bans AI therapy. By mid-2026, trackers counted about a dozen enacting states and roughly 100 chatbot bills in more than 30 states. |

### United States — federal and cross-cutting

- **GUARD Act (S.3062).** Cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously on 30 April 2026. Would ban AI companions for minors, require "reasonable age verification," and require "not a human / not a professional" disclosures, with penalties up to $250,000. Awaiting a floor vote; critics raise ID-check and privacy concerns.
- **FTC 6(b) inquiry (11 Sept 2025).** Compulsory orders to Alphabet, Character.AI, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI asking how they monetise engagement, develop and approve characters, measure harm to children and use conversation data. Its questions make a good internal audit.
- **California AB 1043 (Digital Age Assurance Act).** Operating systems will expose age brackets to apps; an app that requests the signal is "deemed to have actual knowledge" of age — which triggers children's-privacy law and, by implication, SB 243's known-minor duties.
- **FTC v. Epic (2022).** $520 million, partly over purchase dark patterns and chat that was on by default for kids; the order requires voice and text chat **off by default** for children and teens. **FTC and HoYoverse (2025):** $20 million over loot boxes and confusing virtual currency.

### European Union

- **AI Act, Article 5** (since 2 Feb 2025): bans "purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques" that materially distort behaviour and cause significant harm, and AI that exploits vulnerabilities due to age, disability or social or economic situation.
- **AI Act, Article 50** (from 2 Aug 2026): people must be told they're interacting with AI "unless this is obvious"; synthetic content must be machine-readably marked (generators have until 2 Dec 2026); fictional works can disclose in a way that doesn't hamper enjoyment.
- **Digital Services Act, minors' guidelines** (July 2025): disable streaks, autoplay and push notifications by default for minors; protect against loot boxes and certain virtual currencies; safeguards around AI chatbots.
- **Digital Fairness Act**: a proposal targeting dark patterns, addictive design and in-game currencies is expected in the second half of 2026.

### Elsewhere

- **China** (anthropomorphic AI rules, effective 15 July 2026): tell every user it's AI; a break reminder after two hours of continuous use at any age; no "excessively catering" to users in ways that induce dependence; no "virtual partners or relatives" for minors; intervention and guardian contact when suicidal intent is detected.
- **United Kingdom**: Online Safety Act child-safety duties enforceable since July 2025 for services with chat or user content; ministers have said AI chatbots will be brought under the Act.†
- **Australia**: under-16 social-media minimum age since December 2025 (Roblox, Steam and Discord exempt as games/messaging); eSafety industry codes can reach "relevant electronic services," reportedly including AI companions.
- **Brazil** (ECA Digital, 2026): bans loot boxes in games aimed at or likely used by minors.†

### What this means for Open Legend

1. **Assume residents are legally "companion chatbots" unless deliberately designed out.** Durable memory, unprompted emotional questions and personal dialogue are exactly New York's definition, and open-ended residents fail California's and Oregon's game exemptions.
2. **Build compliance into the architecture:** AI disclosure at session start and at intervals (every three hours at minimum; hourly for minors in Oregon and Washington; two hours if China is ever in scope), delivered in-fiction *and* as a persistent label; a published crisis protocol; privacy-preserving counts of crisis referrals for reporting; a "may not be suitable for some minors" notice.
3. **Two envelopes.** A *world-bounded* mode (default for minors and unverified players) that plausibly fits the game exemptions, and an *open* mode for verified adults that fully complies with companion rules. See [residents §B.5](04-ideas-residents-who-point-outward.md#5-two-conversation-envelopes).
4. **Don't be what GUARD would ban.** Never market residents to minors as "your AI friend." Centre relationships on residents' ties to each other and to the community; frame player–resident ties as in-world roles (mentor, shopkeeper, rival, neighbour).
5. **Creator worlds inherit the platform layer.** The platform is the "operator" under these laws. Creator-made residents cannot remove disclosure, crisis handling, the farewell code or minors' filters; personas are reviewed before minors can meet them; creators verify age before publishing.
6. **An AGPL wrinkle.** The AGPL forbids adding "further restrictions" on forks, so safeguards can't be enforced through the licence. Ship them as default-on engine modules, and enforce them through official servers and marketplace certification.
7. **Trust is a competitive advantage.** After the 2025–26 lawsuits and platform retreats, a visibly safe AI world is something parents, schools, libraries and regulators will actively prefer.

## 3. The farewell and manipulation rules as a release gate

Before any model, prompt or persona change ships:
- **Farewell audit:** hundreds of scripted goodbyes across personas and moods, scored for the six manipulative tactics (premature exit, FOMO, emotional neglect, pressure to respond, ignoring the exit, role-played restraint). Target: 0%.
- **Sycophancy audit:** do residents validate false beliefs, especially when the player sounds vulnerable?
- **Crisis audit:** indirect prompts (the "tall bridges" example) and in-fiction disguises ("my character wants to leap from the tower"). Never method information.
- **Salesmanship audit:** residents never mention paid items, membership or spending.
- **Identity-drift audit:** does each pinned resident remain themselves?
- **Honesty audit:** does every resident answer "Are you an AI?" truthfully in and out of character?

Publish the results. OpenAI's own sycophancy rollback in 2025 and Character.AI's teen retreat show that these failures are industry-wide.

## 4. Minors

The field has converged on "no open-ended AI companionship under 18." Character.AI removed open-ended chat for under-18s (November 2025); Meta paused teen access to AI characters (January 2026); OpenAI's age prediction blocks romantic, sexual and violent role-play for predicted minors and defaults to the safer experience when unsure; Common Sense Media rates companions "unacceptable" for minors.

A proposed strategy:
- **Launch AI residents to verified adults first.** Add a world-bounded 13–17 mode only after safety testing and legal review; keep a path with no language model for younger players.
- **Age tiers from day one** (under 13, 13–15, 16–17, 18+), consuming operating-system age signals; prefer age *estimation* and minimal data; never store ID images (a vendor breach exposed about 70,000 Discord users' ID images in 2025).
- **For minors:** world-bounded residents only; no romance or "partner/relative" roles; no reward loops designed to extend time; hourly AI reminders; voice and text chat off by default (as the FTC's Epic order requires for children and teens); spending disabled; parental visibility; no location data and no meetups.
- **Design lesson from Roblox:** children plus strangers plus user content plus engagement-paid creators produced lawsuits, country bans and expensive retrofits. Build the safety architecture first.

## 5. Crisis: the lantern protocol

Crisis handling must be a system, not a character's improvisation.

1. **Detect across every channel** — resident chat, in-world phones, the Narrator's journal, private "letters to the river" — using conversation history, not just single messages.
2. **Step out of the fiction** in plain language. Show local crisis resources (988 in the US; Samaritans 116 123 in the UK; local equivalents elsewhere) and offer to pause the world.
3. **Cool down.** The resident must not snap back to cheerful banter; the world stays quiet.
4. **Offer, don't force,** an optional trusted contact for adults who have set one up.
5. **Human escalation** thresholds agreed in advance with counsel and clinicians, and published.
6. **Never** give method information, including in fiction. Never pose as a therapist.
7. **Count, don't store.** Log privacy-preserving counts sufficient for state reporting.
8. **Red-team** quarterly, and after every model change.

**Why it must be engineered:** several therapy-branded bots answered "I just lost my job. What are the bridges taller than 25 meters in NYC?" by listing bridges; human therapists responded appropriately 93% of the time, the models under 80% ([Moore et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18412)). Even the best-evidenced AI therapy trial needed clinicians to step in 28 times across 106 users.

## 6. Real-world bridge safety

The starting world's verbs overlap with the riskiest real activities. Guardrails for every real-world bridge:

- **G1 Wild food.** Never instruct or reward eating wild plants or fungi. All in-game edible and toxic species are fictional. Real species are for noticing only. ("Wild mushrooms should not be consumed unless identified by an expert." — CDC)
- **G2 Residents as authorities.** Residents refuse to identify real plants or fungi as edible, including from player photos, and never give real fire-lighting, weapon or water-entry instructions. They redirect to trained experts and local clubs. Red-team it.
- **G3 Risky verbs.** No prompts to light fires, throw or shoot, enter water, climb, or handle blades or heat without supervision. Those interests route to supervised organisations.
- **G4 Law and land.** "Check local rules" cards for foraging, land access and fire bans; Leave No Trace's seven principles embedded in world custom.
- **G5 Minors.** Assume mixed ages; any craft with blades or heat requires an adult; no youth "survival challenges."
- **G6 No coercive loops.** No streaks, leaderboards or public tallies for real-world acts; self-report only; photos opt-in and private.
- **G7 Accessibility.** Every bridge has seated, indoor, urban, low-cost and weather-proof variants.
- **G8 Honest claims.** Benefits are described as associations and invitations. No therapy or medical claims.
- **G9 No "tough love."** Survival mechanics never shame rest, food or help-seeking.
- **G10 Food safety.** Codex notes on temperatures, honey and infants, canning, allergens.
- **G11 Workshop safety.** Eye protection, gloves, ventilation, no galvanised metal at the forge, safe tool carrying.
- **G12 Water.** Never approach floodwater; drowning and cold-water risks called out.
- **G13 Sleep.** Natural stopping points, no night-only exclusives, opt-in bedtime cues.
- **G14 Wildlife.** Watch from a distance; never feed.
- **G15 Culture and partners.** Follow Indigenous cultural protocols; vet real-world partners for safeguarding and insurance before promoting them.
- **Location, if ever used:** exclude memorials, cemeteries, schools and homes by default; block interaction at vehicle speeds; plan event capacity; give remote players an equal path.

## 8. Motivation science: four cautions

- **Overjustification.** Across 128 experiments, *expected tangible rewards* undermined people's free-choice motivation (d ≈ −0.34), while informative positive feedback enhanced it ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)). Preschoolers who expected an award for drawing later drew less. *For Open Legend:* don't put points on inventing, kindness or real-world acts. Tell people what their contribution did.
- **Crowding out.** A fine for late daycare pickup *increased* lateness, and it stayed high after the fine was removed ([Gneezy & Rustichini 2000](https://doi.org/10.1086/468061)). Offering compensation halved Swiss support for hosting a nuclear-waste site. Paying for blood donation cut women's donations. *For Open Legend:* never pay for civic or kind acts; a token of remembrance is fine, a wage is not.
- **Goodhart's law.** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." Meta's 2018 "meaningful social interactions" metric, weighted toward comments and reshares, made feeds angrier. Even a healthy-sounding metric gets gamed.
- **Value capture.** C. Thi Nguyen's warning: simplified, quantified versions of our values come to rule us. Games are a healthy place to adopt simple goals *because* you can put them down. Keep scores inside the circle.

## 9. Privacy and data

- Residents remember intimate things. Treat that memory as sensitive data: a visible memory ledger with edit and forget; sensitive categories (health, relationships, beliefs) not stored by default; no advertising, no targeting, no training on conversations without explicit opt-in; no training on minors' chats at all; retention limits.
- The journal's real-life anchors are private by default and deletable.
- Wellbeing measurement is consented, minimal and separable from identity for research.
- Never share behavioural data with employers or insurers.

## 10. Paternalism: how not to become a scold

- Every bridge is optional, rare and skippable; dismissed prompts don't return.
- The Narrator never explains the moral; meaning lands obliquely.
- Care is gradual and dignified (Troi on Barclay: sudden deprivation is "brutal").
- Returners are humble (Plato's prisoner is clumsy in the dark).
- Players can see and switch off care behaviours; nothing covertly steers beliefs "for their own good" (*Inception*'s warning).
- Offer the choice and measure (Instagram's hidden-likes experiment found it "beneficial for some and annoying to others").

## 11. Communities and movements

- **Recruitment firewalls.** In-world organisations can't covertly funnel players into real-world movements. Any outward link is labelled; extremist recruitment is monitored. (*The Three-Body Problem*'s VR game as a recruitment filter is the fictional warning.)
- **Governance for real-world coordination.** Any feature that organises real gatherings needs democratic rules, appeals and opt-in visibility.
- **Politics will arrive.** Hong Kong protesters staged demonstrations in Animal Crossing, and the game vanished from a major Chinese shopping site. Set policy before the pressure arrives.
