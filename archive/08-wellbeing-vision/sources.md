# Sources and verification notes

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). The key sources behind this folder, grouped by theme. The [research briefs](research/README.md) hold the full citations, many more sources, and each researcher's verification notes.

---

## How the research was done

Ten research briefs were written on 2026-09-28 with AI-assisted web research, one per theme, and then synthesised into the docs in this folder. Researchers tagged claims by strength of evidence and noted what they could and could not confirm. Where a later doc restates a finding, it links the most direct source the brief found.

## Verification notes

- **Research limits.** The web research budget ran out before every item could be re-checked, and a few pages could not be fetched because of rate limits (among them pages on the friendship-hours study and two quotation pages). The docs therefore describe the friendship-hours finding in general terms and mark unconfirmed quotation wording with †.
- **Figures are quoted as reported.** Check the primary source before using any number outside this folder. Several items are 2026 preprints or conference papers (for example Hakman et al. 2026; Zhang et al. 2026; Ballou et al. at CHI 2026) and may change on publication.
- **Press releases and summaries.** Some links go to a university press release, a publisher's summary, a law firm's client note or Wikipedia rather than the paper or statute itself. They are convenient entry points, not substitutes for the primary text.
- **Law changes fast.** The legal landscape in [guardrails, risks and law](11-guardrails-risks-and-law.md#2-the-legal-landscape-september-2026) is as of September 2026 and is not legal advice.
- **Quotations.** [Stories that already told us](09-stories-that-already-told-us.md#k-quotes-worth-keeping-checked-against-sources-in-this-research) keeps quotes checked against sources in this research and lists [common misattributions to avoid](09-stories-that-already-told-us.md#m-quotes-to-avoid-commonly-misattributed).

## Games and wellbeing

- [Vuorre et al. 2022](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/9/7/220411/96718/Time-spent-playing-video-games-is-unlikely-to) — 38,935 players across seven games: playtime barely related to wellbeing.
- [Ballou et al. 2025, via the Oxford Internet Institute](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/its-quality-not-quantity-that-predicts-gamers-wellbeing-new-study-finds/) — Switch players: perceived value and "gaming life fit" mattered, hours did not.
- [Orben & Przybylski 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0506-1) — technology use explained at most 0.4% of adolescent wellbeing.
- [Egami et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01948-y) — console lotteries in Japan: benefits diminished beyond about three hours a day.
- [Vuorre et al. 2024](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-09-25-new-study-reveals-positive-mood-changes-during-video-game-play) — in-game mood sampling in *PowerWash Simulator*.
- [Johannes et al. 2021](https://www.niklasjohannes.com/uploads/Johannes%20et%20al.%20%282021%29%20-%20Video%20game%20play.pdf) — feeling pressured to play predicts lower wellbeing.
- [Yee & Sng 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800683/full) — *Animal Crossing* chores that stopped feeling volitional.
- [Ballou et al., CHI 2026](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-breakups/) — life stressors increase gaming.
- [Ballou et al. 2022](https://nickballou.com/publication/2022-ballou-et-al-do-people/) — "rich get richer" need satisfaction during lockdowns.
- [Ballou et al. 2026 (preprint)](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-all-bang/) — need satisfaction tracks how play feels, not whether people return.
- [Ballou et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581924000739) — the BANGS scale for need satisfaction and frustration in games.
- [Stenseng et al. 2012](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01490400.2012.633849) and [Hussain et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958821000294) — self-expansion versus self-suppression escapism.
- [Strojny & Strojny 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1735108/full) — a clean "post-gaming return" appeared to buffer escapism's harms.
- [Stevens et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33028074/) — pooled gaming-disorder prevalence.
- [Roane, *SLEEP* 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160613144656.htm) — bedtime delayed on 36% of gaming nights.
- [De Rosa et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1389945724004362) — arousing versus calm play before sleep.
- [JAMA 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2835481) — addictive-use patterns, not total screen time, predicted poor outcomes in young people.
- [Zendle et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01669-8) — China's playtime caps for minors showed no reduction in heavy play.
- [Steinkuehler & Williams 2006](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/4/885/4617703), [Trepte et al. 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563211002706) and [Hakman et al. 2026 (preprint)](https://vuorre.com/bibliography/files/HTDFPRRY/hakman-etal_2026_not-all-play-is-equal-in-game-player-be.pdf) — bridging versus bonding ties, meeting offline, cooperative play.
- [Lewis et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.627350/full) — *Animal Crossing* in lockdown: visiting other players was linked to lower loneliness, talking to characters was not.
- [Howe et al., *BMJ* 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270) — *Pokémon GO*'s step boost faded by week six.
- [Sala et al. 2018](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/102169/) — general "brain training" does not transfer.
