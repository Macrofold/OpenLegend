# Ideas: rhythm, rest and the Return

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). Tags: **Now** fits the current prototype or first playable; **Next** needs planned systems (the Narrator, shared worlds, Threewater March); **Later** needs new systems; **Moonshot** is a bet. Evidence: **[Strong] [Moderate] [Weak] [Wisdom] [Idea]**.

This catalogue is about *time*: how sessions begin and end, what absence costs, how the world treats sleep, and how a player can gently be helped out of a world that has become a hiding place. It is the cheapest and highest-leverage part of the vision, because Open Legend already made the key decision: the world pauses when you leave.

---

## A. Ending well

### 1. The Return Rite
*Every session closes with a small ceremony that hands the player back to their life.* **Now · [Moderate] + [Wisdom]**
- **In Open Legend:** when the player chooses to stop, or at a natural chapter end, a bell rings, the Narrator writes the day's last page, and asks one skippable question: *"What will you carry back to the market?"* Whatever the player types becomes the chapter's final line. Nothing is scored.
- **Why:** a clean psychological transition back to real life appears to buffer the harms of escapist play ([Strojny & Strojny 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1735108/full)). Van Gennep's rites of passage end with *incorporation* — re-entry into ordinary life as a changed person — which most games omit. The tenth ox-herding picture returns the sage to the market.
- **Next session:** the Narrator may open lightly — *"Did anything come of what you carried?"* — and accept "no" without comment.
- **Watch out:** the moment the rite feels like homework, it fails. Keep it one line, always skippable, never repeated if dismissed.

### 2. The Tollbooth Note
*At natural stopping points, the journal hands the player back to their life with one small, specific invitation.* **Now · [Idea]**
- **In Open Legend:** one of three kinds, rotating: a *noticing* prompt ("Is there a bird outside your window right now?"), a *person* prompt ("Is there someone you've been meaning to call?"), or a *try-it* prompt tied to what happened in play ("You twisted cord all afternoon. A bit of yarn and fifteen minutes is all a real bracelet takes.").
- **Why:** named after the note left for Milo in *The Phantom Tollbooth*: "For Milo, who now knows the way." The fantasy's gift is an appetite for the real.
- **Watch out:** never generic self-help. The note should grow out of *this* session's story, or be omitted.

### 3. Chapters, not treadmills
*A game day is a chapter with a real ending, never an automatic "one more day."* **Now · [Strong] for stopping cues**
- **In Open Legend:** at 1× a game day lasts 24 real minutes. Nightfall becomes a chapter close: a summary, a journal line, a clear exit. Continuing requires an explicit choice ("Begin tomorrow"). After several in-world days in one sitting, the world grows drowsy.
- **Why:** removing stopping cues (autoplay, bottomless feeds) is the core mechanism of behavioural addiction that Adam Alter describes; "finite design" (Humane by Design) restores them. Civilization's "one more turn" shows how a cycle can become a compulsion.
- **Watch out:** chapter ends must not become cliffhangers designed to pull people back.

### 4. The Threshold Rite
*Entering the world is marked, briefly, so the space feels hallowed and bounded.* **Now · [Wisdom]**
- **In Open Legend:** a few seconds of ritual — passing the camp's edge, washing at the stream, hanging up a cloak. Optionally, the player picks an intention for the session: **rest, create, connect, explore**. The Narrator can reflect it back at the end ("You came to rest. Did you?").
- **Why:** Huizinga's play-grounds are "hedged round, hallowed" spaces. Inception's *totem* is a small object that tells you whose reality you're in. An intention makes play a choice rather than a drift, supporting harmonious rather than obsessive passion.

### 5. Compline: the closing bell
*The last of the day's bells is the world's gentle suggestion to close the book.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- **In Open Legend:** borrowed from the monastic hours, where Compline ends the day. Residents finish their work, bank the fire and say goodnight; the Narrator offers to close the chapter. Saving and stopping is one click.
- See also [The Hours](#22-the-hours) below.

## B. Absence without penalty

### 6. The village keeps Sabbath while you're away
*The pause is framed as sacred rest, not an engineering constraint.* **Now · [Wisdom]**
- **In Open Legend:** the lore says the valley rests when its travellers are gone. Residents don't pine; for them, the pause is dreamless rest, not waiting (a small kindness to the residents too). The loading screen might read: *"The March rests while you're away."*
- **Why:** Heschel's Sabbath is "a palace in time."† Obligation is the main poison in games; the pause removes it. Framing turns a technical fact into a promise.

### 7. The open window
*Returning is always warm and guilt-free.* **Now · [Strong] for the harm of guilt**
- **In Open Legend:** residents greet a returning player without reproach — "There you are. Come see what Tavin built." — never "Where have you been?" or "I missed you so much."
- **Why:** in *Peter Pan*, Mrs Darling leaves the window open for her children. Manipulative farewell messaging raises engagement through anger and curiosity, not enjoyment ([De Freitas et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)).

### 8. The no-loss covenant
*Nothing spoils, decays, dies of neglect, or goes missing while a player is away.* **Now/Next · [Strong]**
- **Personal worlds:** already true — the world pauses.
- **Shared worlds:** when a world keeps running because others are present, the major life events of residents you're close to (births, weddings, deaths) are *held* until you can be there, or the Narrator writes you a "while you were away" letter, read on return and never pushed as a notification.
- **Why:** FarmVille's withering crops were the canonical obligation mechanic. Pressure to play predicts lower wellbeing ([Johannes et al. 2021](https://www.niklasjohannes.com/uploads/Johannes%20et%20al.%20%282021%29%20-%20Video%20game%20play.pdf)).

### 9. Rested inspiration
*Time away earns a small, capped gift — the opposite of a streak.* **Next · [Strong] precedent**
- **In Open Legend:** real days away slowly build "rested inspiration": fresh invention ideas, warmth from residents, or an elder's new story on return. It accrues over days and caps low, so it never becomes a reason to schedule returns.
- **Why:** World of Warcraft's beta halved experience after long sessions; players hated it. Blizzard relabelled the normal rate as a 200% "rested" bonus and the reduced rate as normal — "same numbers seen from the opposite point of view" ([Wowpedia](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Rest)).

### 10. Calm notifications
*The world never summons.* **Now · [Strong] for regulation direction**
- Off by default; at most a weekly digest if opted in; never between midnight and 6 a.m. local time; never written in a resident's voice. Prefer ambient in-world signals (a lantern lit in a friend's window when they're online).
- **Why:** EU guidance under the Digital Services Act tells platforms to switch off streaks, autoplay and push notifications by default for minors; New York's SAFE for Kids Act, once in effect, bans overnight notifications to minors. Calm Technology principle: "Technology should require the smallest possible amount of attention."

## C. Sleep and the body clock

### 11. The village sleeps
*The world grows sleepy near the player's chosen bedtime.* **Now · [Strong] for sleep displacement**
- **In Open Legend:** an optional setting aligns in-world dusk with the player's real bedtime. Residents yawn, bank the fires and say goodnight; the Narrator offers a natural save point. No late-night-only events, no night exclusives, no streaks.
- **Why:** lost sleep is the most common everyday harm of gaming — bedtime delayed on 36% of gaming nights, by 101 minutes on average ([Roane 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160613144656.htm)). The main mechanism is lost time and arousal; blue-light filters made no measurable difference to sleep in one trial (Duraccio et al. 2021). Revenge bedtime procrastination is about reclaiming control of one's time, so the world *invites* sleep instead of forbidding play.

### 12. Calm nights
*Night in the world is for quiet things.* **Next · [Moderate]**
- Hold cliffhangers, raids, horror and big events for the in-world day. Night brings crafting, stories by the fire, stars. Arousing play before sleep delays sleep onset; calm, cognitively engaging play did not show the same harm ([De Rosa et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1389945724004362)).

### 13. Dawn chorus
*An optional first-session-of-the-day ritual that points at the morning outside.* **Now · [Moderate]**
- "Step outside for five minutes and listen for birds." Villagers do a short, gentle group stretch together, like Japan's radio-exercise mornings. Daytime light and nighttime darkness were associated with better mental health in 86,772 UK Biobank participants ([Burns et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00135-8)); everyday encounters with birds were associated with better wellbeing lasting hours ([Hammoud et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20207-6)).

## D. Self-authored limits and gentle care

### 14. Ulysses mode
*Players bind their future selves, the way Odysseus had himself tied to the mast.* **Now · [Strong]**
- **In Open Legend:** a player sets a session budget or bedtime. When it arrives, the sun sets in the world. Overriding takes a short delay and a one-line reflection ("Still want to keep going?"), never a lecture.
- **Why:** economists estimate that "self-control problems cause 31 percent of social media use," and letting people set limits on their own future use "substantially reduces use" ([Allcott, Gentzkow & Song](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28936)). Limits imposed from outside worked poorly: China's playtime caps for minors showed no reduction in heavy play across 7 billion hours of data ([Zendle et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01669-8)), and South Korea repealed its midnight shutdown law.

### 15. The rhythm mirror
*A private, judgment-free view of your own pattern.* **Next · [Idea]**
- Weekly: when you played, for how long, and *with whom* — friends, strangers, residents, alone. The player sets their own target ("most of my play should be with people I know"). No streaks, no scores, no sharing.
- **Why:** self-awareness supports self-regulation; defaults that inform without controlling support intrinsic motivation. The "with whom" line is the one that matters most (see [residents who point outward](04-ideas-residents-who-point-outward.md)).

### 16. Program 9, not lockouts
*Interventions are gradual and dignified; the player keeps what they love.* **Now · [Wisdom]**
- In *Star Trek: TNG* "Hollow Pursuits," Barclay erases his holodeck fantasies — "Except Program 9." Troi had warned that "to suddenly destroy his only means of escape would be brutal." Care never takes away a player's cherished place or story as punishment.

### 17. The Vic Fontaine protocol
*Residents notice when their world has become a hiding place, and turn the player back toward life, step by step.* **Next · [Wisdom] + [Idea]**
- **In Open Legend:** a care arc for players whose patterns suggest hiding (see signals below): first *refuge* (warmth, no pressure), then *purposeful work* (a task that uses real competence), then *gentle challenge* (a harder conversation, a group event), and only if needed, *closing the bar* for a night with a warm reason ("I'm closing early tonight. Go see a friend.").
- **Why:** in *Deep Space Nine*'s "It's Only a Paper Moon," Nog, home from war with a lost leg, hides for weeks in Vic Fontaine's holographic lounge. Vic shuts down his own program to push him out: "You stay here, you're going to die. Not all at once, but little by little." Nog leaves his cane behind — and arranges for Vic's program to run around the clock, so Vic can have a life too. Healing ran both ways.
- **Watch out:** this must never be deception or covert manipulation. Players can see and switch off care behaviours; crisis situations follow the separate [lantern protocol](11-guardrails-risks-and-law.md#5-crisis-the-lantern-protocol).

### 18. Compulsion signals, not hours
*Watch the patterns that predict harm, not the clock.* **Next · [Strong]**
- Signals: sessions running into the player's sleep window; escalating session lengths; repeated failed attempts to stop (overriding one's own Ulysses limit); talk of avoidance ("I don't want to think about work"); a stated bad day followed by a long solo session; human co-play dropping off.
- **Why:** in a JAMA cohort of 4,285 youths, *addictive-use* trajectories — not total screen time — predicted suicidal behaviour and poor mental health ([Xiao et al. 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2835481)). Hard times drive play ([CHI 2026](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-breakups/)), so these are moments for care, never for retention offers.

### 19. The rescuer from without
*Someone outside the player notices.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- Campbell's hero sometimes needs *Rescue from Without* to return. Options: a trusted friend can opt in to receive a gentle heads-up if the player's own limits are repeatedly overridden (player-controlled, revocable); a resident, in fiction, says the lamps are going out and it's time to go home; non-fiction links to support are always one tap away.

### 20. Guild pause and group pulse
*Groups can choose rest together.* **Next · [Strong] for "collective traps"**
- A guild can vote a quiet week. Occasionally the world asks a group: "Would this group be better off playing less this week?" No group streaks; raids and projects never punish members who miss them.
- **Why:** the World Happiness Report 2026 describes "product traps": 64% of TikTok users would pay for *everyone* in their community to quit. Some overuse is a coordination problem that only groups can solve.

## E. Rhythms of rest inside the world

### 21. Palace in Time
*One real day a week, the world keeps its own Sabbath.* **Next · [Wisdom] + [Moderate]**
- The market closes, contests pause, crafting rests; the day is for feasts, songs, stories and walks. Nothing expires. The world *models* rest rather than imposing it.
- **Why:** Heschel's Sabbath; people who deactivated Facebook for four weeks in a randomized trial spent more time with family and friends and reported higher wellbeing ([Allcott et al. 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658)).

### 22. The Hours
*Bells structure the in-world day, and residents pause when they ring.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- Dawn, labour, the midday meal, reading, vespers, Compline. At each bell, residents stop for three breaths, as in Plum Village's "bell of mindfulness," where everyone stops and returns to their breathing when the bell sounds. The monastic horarium was an architecture for a healthy day, and it gives the world natural stopping points.

### 23. Mynah birds
*Optional ambient invitations to notice the present.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- In Aldous Huxley's *Island*, mynah birds are trained to call "Attention" and "Here and now, boys"†. An in-world bird or wind chime that occasionally invites the player to notice their breath, their body, the time. Optional, rare, never nagging.

### 24. The real bell
*An opt-in real-world stop-or-continue cue.* **Now · [Idea]**
- Every 45–60 minutes of play, a soft bell and a one-line choice: *continue* or *close the chapter*. Distinct from the legally required AI-disclosure reminders (see [guardrails](11-guardrails-risks-and-law.md)), though they can share the moment.

### 25. Walk on it
*When a player is stuck on an invention, an elder suggests a walk, and the game saves and pauses.* **Now · [Moderate]**
- Walking boosted divergent thinking — generating many ideas — by about 60% in one experiment, and the boost lingered after sitting down ([Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014](https://news.stanford.edu/2014/04/24/walking-vs-sitting-042414/)). When the player returns, the elder asks what they thought of on the walk.

### 26. Seasons that end
*Worlds and servers run in seasons with finales, closing ceremonies and a chronicle.* **Later · [Wisdom]**
- A season ends with a festival, a Narrator-written chronicle of what the community did, and a rest before the next begins. Carse's infinite game can contain finite games; the season is a finite game with a real ending, which gives people a natural moment to step back.

### 27. The Long Rest
*Once a year, every official world closes for a weekend, and communities are invited to gather in person.* **Moonshot · [Idea]**
- The film of *Ready Player One* ends with the OASIS closed on Tuesdays and Thursdays because "people need to spend more time in the real world." A mandate is blunt. A festival is not: a once-a-year Long Rest, announced in the lore ("the valley sleeps under snow"), paired with host kits for local gatherings. See [moonshots](08-moonshots.md#the-long-rest).

## F. Graduation

### 28. Honour the Susans
*Players who play less because their lives filled up are celebrated, never won back.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- In Narnia, Susan is shut out for growing up; critics (and Neil Gaiman's story "The Problem of Susan") have long objected. Open Legend should do the opposite: a *graduation* ceremony, a keepsake page, an alumni role, and no manipulative win-back campaigns. The journal can be bound and kept.

### 29. Magister to tutor
*The highest status in any world is teaching.* **Next · [Wisdom] + [Moderate]**
- In Hesse's *The Glass Bead Game*, the master of the supreme game leaves it to tutor one real boy. Veterans become wayfinders for newcomers (as in *Sky: Children of the Light*, where 62% of surveyed players had been helped by veterans) and, where they want to, mentors offline. See [together](05-ideas-together.md).

## G. Identity and memory

### 30. Remember your name
*The journal protects the player's real anchors; no mechanic ever takes memory or identity as its price.* **Next · [Wisdom]**
- In *Spirited Away*, the witch Yubaba takes Chihiro's name, and whoever forgets their name cannot go home. In *The NeverEnding Story*, each wish costs Bastian a memory of his life. Invert the price: players can (optionally) tell the journal a few real anchors — people, values, hopes — which the Narrator brings up gently when the story rhymes with them, never for engagement.
- **Watch out:** these are sensitive data. Private by default, deletable, never used for targeting.

### 31. Low-demand and accessible by default
*Everything pausable; nothing gated on reflexes; a gentle-needs mode.* **Now · [Strong] for accessibility practice**
- A cozy mode where survival pressure is soft, following Project Horseshoe's definition of coziness: safety, abundance and softness. AbleGamers' Accessible Player Experiences patterns. Every real-world bridge has a seated, indoor, low-cost variant.
