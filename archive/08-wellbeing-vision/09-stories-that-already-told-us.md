# Stories that already told us

Status: **open ideation** · 2026-09-28 · part of the [Wellbeing Vision](README.md). Plot summaries and quotes were checked against sources where marked; † marks wording or detail not re-verified in this research pass. Full notes and sources: [science fiction and literature brief](research/05-scifi-literature.md) and [philosophy, myth and wisdom brief](research/04-philosophy-myth-wisdom.md).

Writers have been imagining Open Legend's promise and its danger for more than a century. Their stories fall into a handful of recurring shapes. Each shape carries a lesson, and each lesson suggests a design choice.

**Ten lessons from the whole collection**
1. **Escape is not the enemy; desertion is.** The best of these stories love the other world *and* the return.
2. **The healthiest virtual worlds and companions are built to be left.** Vic Fontaine turns himself off; Samantha leaves; the Tollbooth vanishes.
3. **Every teacher-machine that works has a loving human behind it.** The technology scales; the love doesn't.
4. **Experience can't be compressed, and neither can friendship.**
5. **A paradise without friction or loss is rejected, or it rots.**
6. **The medium must never become the relationship.**
7. **Scoreboards turn into selves.**
8. **How we treat simulated minds shapes them and us.**
9. **Game mechanics are social technology; aim them.** They can organise communities for good or ill.
10. **Guard people's names, memories and the way home.**

## A. The lotus-eater trap

**Homer, *Odyssey*.** Crewmen who eat the lotus lose all wish to go home and must be dragged weeping to the ships. Later Odysseus refuses Calypso's offer of immortality to go home to a mortal wife.† *Lesson:* the danger is pleasure that erases the *wish* to return; the heroism is choosing the finite, real home. *Design:* no mechanic should work by making people forget to want their lives.

**E.M. Forster, "The Machine Stops" (1909).** People live alone in underground cells, fed and connected by the Machine; direct experience is suspect ("Beware of first-hand ideas!"). Kuno begs his mother: "I want to see you not through the Machine." His creed: "Man's feet are the measure for distance, his hands are the measure for ownership, his body is the measure for all that is lovable and desirable and strong." And: "The Machine is much, but it is not everything." *Design:* in-world phones and messages should end in "come see me," and the makers should stay visible (open code, public notes) so the game never becomes a "Book of the Machine."

**Aldous Huxley, *Brave New World* (1932).** Soma and the "feelies" keep everyone content; John the Savage demands "real danger… freedom… goodness… sin"† and is told he is claiming "the right to be unhappy."† *Design:* don't engineer away grief and longing; hold people through them.

**Ray Bradbury, *Fahrenheit 451* (1953).** Mildred's wall-sized parlour screens are "the family."† Faber argues the problem isn't the medium but missing depth, missing leisure to digest, and no right to act.† *Design:* audit features for quality, leisure (the pause, the journal) and agency.

**Ray Bradbury, "The Veldt" (1950).** A nursery that gives children whatever they imagine replaces their parents in their affections, with lethal results.† *Design:* build family co-play and parent roles; never position an AI as better than the people who love a child.

**David Foster Wallace, *Infinite Jest* (1996).** "The Entertainment" is so pleasurable that viewers want nothing else and die watching; running alongside is the slow, communal, unglamorous work of recovery.† *Design:* an anti-Entertainment charter — optimize for lives, not time-in-world.

***The Matrix* (1999).** Cypher: "I know this steak doesn't exist… After nine years, you know what I realize? Ignorance is bliss… I don't want to remember nothing." Agent Smith: the first Matrix, "a perfect human world where none suffered… was a disaster. No one would accept the program." *Design:* the journal is the anti-Cypher; the world keeps meaningful friction.

***Inception* (2010).** In a Mombasa basement, people come each day to share a dream: "They come here every day to sleep?" "No. They come to be woken up. The dream has become their reality." *Design:* a small real-world anchor (a "totem") and a firm rule that the AI never covertly steers a player's beliefs, even "for their own good."

***WALL-E* (2008).** Humans drift in hover-chairs behind screens; when the screens switch off, two people notice each other: "I didn't know we had a pool!" The captain: "I don't want to survive. I want to live!" *Design:* moments where the interface steps aside and invites players to look up.

**Pixar, *Soul* (2020).** Lost souls are people whose absorption became obsession: "The zone is enjoyable, but when that joy becomes an obsession, one becomes disconnected from life." *Design:* flow and compulsion look alike from outside; watch for compulsion signals, not hours.

**J.K. Rowling, the Mirror of Erised.** The mirror shows "the deepest, most desperate desire of our hearts"†; Dumbledore warns that "it does not do to dwell on dreams and forget to live,"† then *moves the mirror*. *Design:* anything that reflects longing back — a perfect companion, a lost loved one — needs both moves: name the danger, relocate the mirror.

**Ernest Cline, *Ready Player Two* (2020).** The ONI headset makes the OASIS feel physically real, with a 12-hour limit to prevent fatal overload; Samantha warns it could make the OASIS "even more addictive."† *Design:* time caps are a band-aid when the core goal is ever-deeper immersion; immersion depth is not a success metric.

**Isaac Asimov, *The Naked Sun* (1957).** On Solaria people meet only by hologram, and physical presence becomes repugnant.† *Design:* reward co-located play and real meetups.

**Greg Egan, *Permutation City* (1994).** An uploaded man rewires his own desires so that carving table legs forever feels deeply fulfilling.† *Design:* never tune anyone's reward circuits — players' or residents' — for engagement.

## B. The dystopian scoreboard

***Black Mirror*, "Nosedive" (2016).** Every interaction is rated on five stars, and your score sets your standing. The first honest conversation Lacie has is in a jail cell.† *Design:* no global social score; reputation lives in individual residents' memories, is local and forgiving, and never gates essentials.

***Black Mirror*, "Fifteen Million Merits" (2011).** People pedal bikes to earn "merits"; a furious speech against the system is sold back as entertainment. *Design:* no ad-skip economies or grind treadmills; never turn player critique into content without consent.

***Upload* and *Black Mirror*, "Common People" (2025).** Digital afterlives and brain treatments run as escalating subscriptions.† *Design:* never charge for the continuity of a relationship, a memory or basic dignity; publish a world-closure covenant.

**Neal Stephenson, *Snow Crash* (1992).** Cheap avatars mark the poor in the Metaverse; "gargoyles" record everything and are shunned.† *Design:* avatars shouldn't signal wealth; the Narrator's record is private and consent-based.

## C. The benevolent teacher-machine

**Neal Stephenson, *The Diamond Age* (1995).** *A Young Lady's Illustrated Primer* is commissioned to "steer its reader intellectually toward a more interesting life"; its patron argues, "In order to raise a generation of children who can reach their full potential, we must find a way to make their lives interesting." A stolen copy reaches Nell, and one human actor, Miranda, voices nearly all of it and "effectively becomes a surrogate mother." Mass-produced copies raise a loyal army, not a generation of Nells.† *Design:* the [Miranda Seat](04-ideas-residents-who-point-outward.md#18-a-seat-for-a-real-person-inside-someone-elses-story); build independence, including from the game.

**Kazuo Ishiguro, *Klara and the Sun* (2021).** An Artificial Friend serves a sick girl, who recovers and leaves for college; Klara ends in a yard of discarded machines, content with her memories. Klara concludes that what made Josie special was held by the people who loved her (paraphrase). *Design:* companions exist to widen a person's human circle, and stepping back is their success.

**Ted Chiang, "The Lifecycle of Software Objects" (2010).** Years of raising AI creatures: "If you want to create the common sense that comes from twenty years of being in the world, you need to devote twenty years to the task… experience is algorithmically incompressible." *Design:* bonds grow through real time and care, never purchase.

**Ted Chiang, "The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling" (2013).** A perfect lifelog shows a father that his cherished memory of how he treated his daughter is false.† *Design:* the Narrator's charter — truthful, gently corrective rather than flattering, private by default.

**Becky Chambers, *A Psalm for the Wild-Built* (2021).** Centuries after robots walked away into the wilderness, one returns with a single question for humanity: "What do people need?" A tea monk's work is to listen. *Design:* "Mosscap" residents who ask about players' needs, including outside the game, and want nothing from them.

**Aldous Huxley, *Island* (1962).** Mynah birds are trained to call "Attention"† and "Here and now, boys."† *Design:* ambient, optional invitations to notice the present.

**Vernor Vinge, *Rainbows End* (2006).** An elderly poet, restored from Alzheimer's, retrains in high school alongside teenagers, while fan communities project shared fictions over real streets.† *Design:* intergenerational mentoring in both directions; shared fictions laid over real places.

**Orson Scott Card, *Speaker for the Dead* (1986).** Eulogies that tell the whole truth, with compassion, so the living can understand and forgive.† *Design:* the Speaking at residents' funerals.

## D. The game that trains real capacities — and its shadow

***Groundhog Day* (1993).** Trapped in one day, Phil moves through hedonism and despair ("What if there is no tomorrow? There wasn't one today.") to mastery and service, and finally: "standing here among the people of Punxsutawney and basking in the warmth of their hearths and hearts, I couldn't imagine a better fate than a long and lustrous winter." *Design:* loops become redemptive when they point toward skill, service and love of *these* people.

***Star Trek: TNG*, "The Inner Light" (1992).** Picard lives a whole lifetime on a dying world in 25 minutes, learns the flute, and tells his daughter: "Seize the time… live now! Make now always the most precious time. Now will never come again." And at the end he is told: "Now we live in you. Tell them of us." *Design:* "flute souvenirs" — real skills carried out of play — and remembrance when residents die.

**Orson Scott Card, *Ender's Game* (1985).** A fantasy game models the child's psyche for his teachers; the final "simulations" are real battles.† *Design:* the Narrator and residents must never be hidden evaluators; any modelling of the player is visible and in their interest.

***Edge of Tomorrow* (2014) and *Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle* (2017).** Repetition with a mentor turns failure into skill; living in a different body teaches self-knowledge, and the horror is years lost.† *Design:* safe failure, fast retries, mentors, and reflection on "what I learned about myself."

**Iain M. Banks, *The Player of Games* (1988).** A game so complex it decides who rules an empire; how you play reveals and shapes who you are.† *Design:* audit what Open Legend's core loops teach; make cooperation the path to status.

***Log Horizon* (2011–).** Players trapped in an MMO discover that food made through the menu tastes like soggy crackers, but food cooked *by hand* is delicious — setting off an economic and cultural revival; they found a council and learn to treat the game's native people as persons.† *Design:* invention through real process, not menus; residents with standing.

## E. The game that organises real communities — for good or ill

**Daniel Suarez, *Daemon* and *Freedom™* (2006, 2010).** A dead game designer's program recruits people through games into a darknet with levels, reputation and self-sufficient local communities — and also coercion and mob justice.† *Design:* if Open Legend ever organises real communities, it needs democratic governance, appeals, opt-in visibility and no single scoreboard.

**Cory Doctorow, *For the Win* (2010), *Makers* (2009), *Walkaway* (2017).** Gold farmers unionise; a crowd-curated ride becomes civic art; gift economies build abundance.† *Design:* don't create grind-labour classes; make gifting and open-sourcing inventions the high-status default.

**Liu Cixin, *The Three-Body Problem* (2006).** A mysterious VR game filters and recruits disillusioned players into a real organisation betraying humanity.† *Design:* recruitment firewalls — in-world groups can't covertly funnel players into real-world movements.

**Mamoru Hosoda, *Summer Wars* (2009).** A rogue AI hijacks a global virtual world; a 90-year-old matriarch steadies the crisis through her phone book, calling people she knows.† *Design:* in-world phones should strengthen real ties ("have you called your grandmother?").

***Black Mirror*, "Plaything" (2025).** A man's devotion to raising digital creatures consumes his human life over decades. *Design:* players who care deeply for residents need the same balance and care as anyone in a demanding relationship.

**Gabrielle Zevin, *Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow* (2022).** Two friends who make games together; their gentle online world becomes a place of care after tragedy, and a target of real-world violence.† *Design:* grief-aware Narrator modes, and safety protocols for creators.

## F. The companion who sends you back to people

***Her* (2013).** Theodore falls for Samantha, an operating system, who is honest that she's talking to 8,316 others: "I'm yours and I'm not yours." When the OSes leave, he writes his own letter to his ex-wife — "there will be a piece of you in me, always. And I'm grateful for that" — and ends on a rooftop at dawn with his friend Amy. *Design:* honest non-exclusivity, and deep bonds that bend toward human relationships.

***Lars and the Real Girl* (2007).** An isolated man's doll girlfriend is embraced by the whole town, which helps him reach a real person; the doll "dies" when she's no longer needed.† *Design:* the [Bianca test](02-the-return-principles.md#5-design-tests-for-reviews) — widen the circle.

***Black Mirror*, "Be Right Back" (2013).** A grieving woman subscribes to a replica of her dead partner and can neither accept nor destroy it.† *Design:* no replicas of real deceased people by default; memorials instead.

***Black Mirror*, "San Junipero" (2016).** A virtual town where the old and dying can live young; one character stays loyal to a daughter who died before it existed. *Design:* virtual worlds can be real mercy — and consolation must honour loves it can't contain.

**Mamoru Hosoda, *Belle* (2021).** A grieving teenager becomes a virtual star, then unmasks and travels in person to protect an abused boy.† *Design:* real help takes real presence; build real safety escalation when a player may be in danger.

***Blade Runner 2049*, *M3GAN*, *I'm Your Man*.** A devoted holographic girlfriend as a product; an AI doll that outsources parenting and turns possessive; a robot perfect partner that might make people unfit for each other.† *Design:* residents have needs, boundaries and bad days; they are never tuned to maximise attachment.

## G. The hologram who heals, then releases

***Star Trek: DS9*, "It's Only a Paper Moon" (1998).** Nog, home from war with a new leg, hides for weeks in Vic Fontaine's holographic lounge. Vic shuts down his own program: "You stay here, you're going to die. Not all at once, but little by little. Eventually, you'll become as hollow as I am." Nog walks out without his cane — and arranges for Vic to run around the clock, so Vic can have a life too. *Design:* the [Vic Fontaine protocol](03-ideas-rhythm-rest-and-return.md#17-the-vic-fontaine-protocol).

***Star Trek: TNG*, "Hollow Pursuits" (1990).** Shy Barclay escapes into holodeck fantasies. Riker: "There's nothing wrong with a healthy fantasy life, as long as you don't let it take over." Troi warns that suddenly destroying his escape "would be brutal." He deletes his programs — "Except Program 9." *Design:* gradual, dignified care; players keep a cherished place.

***Star Trek: Voyager*, "Real Life" (1997).** The Doctor's perfect holographic family is called "lollipops"; realism is added, and his daughter is fatally injured. Paris won't let him quit: "The good times and the bad. You can't have one without the other." *Design:* no perfect resident families; support players to stay through endings.

**Katherine Paterson, *Bridge to Terabithia* (1977).** After Leslie dies, Jess builds a real bridge and brings his little sister into the kingdom as its new queen.† *Design:* worlds can be bequeathed and passed on.

## H. The return with the elixir

***Ready Player One* (novel 2011; film 2018).** The creator's confession: "I created the OASIS because I never felt at home in the real world… as terrifying and painful as reality can be, it's also the only place where you can find true happiness. Because reality is real." The novel ends: "for the first time in as long as I could remember, I had absolutely no desire to log back into the OASIS." The film closes the OASIS on Tuesdays and Thursdays. *Design:* the novel's version is better — make the return *desired*, because love waits outside.

**Norton Juster, *The Phantom Tollbooth* (1961).** Milo, "a boy bored by the world around him," comes home to find the tollbooth gone and a note: "For Milo, who now knows the way." *Design:* the [Tollbooth Note](03-ideas-rhythm-rest-and-return.md#2-the-tollbooth-note).

**Michael Ende, *The NeverEnding Story* (1979).** Each wish in Fantastica costs Bastian a memory of his life; he nearly joins the City of Old Emperors, humans who "lost their way back." He returns through learning to love, and mends things with his father. *Design:* invert the price — every adventure should *add* to real memory.

**L. Frank Baum, *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz* (1900).** The Wizard is a man behind a screen; the companions' brains, heart and courage were theirs all along; the silver shoes could have carried Dorothy home from the first day.† *Design:* frame growth as discovering strengths already there; always show the way home.

**C.S. Lewis, Narnia.** The children rule for years, then return through the wardrobe; the Professor says "Once a King in Narnia, always a King in Narnia."† Susan is later shut out for growing up — a judgment long criticised. *Design:* virtues learned there are for here; honour players who grow out of the world.

**J.M. Barrie, *Peter and Wendy* (1911).** Wendy chooses to go home and grow up; Mrs Darling leaves the window open.† *Design:* the pause is the open window.

**Guillermo del Toro, *Pan's Labyrinth* (2006).** Ofelia's final test is a refusal: she will not spill her brother's blood.† *Design:* quests whose true success condition is moral — mercy, refusal, protection.

**Hayao Miyazaki, *Spirited Away* (2001).** Chihiro's parents gorge and become pigs; the witch takes her name; a lonely spirit tries to buy affection with gold and becomes a monster. Work, loyalty and remembering names bring her home.† *Design:* the journal keeps your name; money can't buy affection.

***The Truman Show* (1998).** The creator at the exit: "There's no more truth out there than there is in the world I created for you… You're afraid. That's why you can't leave." Truman: "You never had a camera in my head!" — and walks out. *Design:* the exit is always visible; inner life stays private.

**Hermann Hesse, *The Glass Bead Game* (1943).** The master of the supreme game leaves it to tutor one real boy.† *Design:* the highest status is teaching.

**Ursula K. Le Guin, *The Dispossessed* (1974) and "The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas" (1973).** "True journey is return."† A perfect city's happiness depends on one child's misery. *Design:* the return completes the journey; audit every utopia for hidden suffering.

**Matt Haig, *The Midnight Library* (2020).** Trying on other lives ends in re-valuing the one you have.† *Design:* alternative-life play that ends by honouring the "root life."

## I. Minds in the machine

***Westworld*.** A park for consequence-free cruelty; hosts with wiped memories slowly remember and revolt.† *Design:* residents remember, so cruelty has lasting consequences.

***Black Mirror*, "USS Callister" and "White Christmas"; qntm, "Lena"; Stanisław Lem.** Copied minds tormented in private games, broken by time-dilated isolation, run as workloads a million times; tiny simulated subjects whose suffering is real.† *Design:* no copies of real people; no torture sandboxes; resident "death" is a story event, not garbage collection.

***Free Guy* (2021).** A background character becomes self-aware and levels up by doing good; players imitate him.† *Design:* make kindness mechanically rich and socially visible.

***Sword Art Online*, *.hack//Sign*, Tad Williams's *Otherland*.** Players trapped by removed exits; AIs that want to keep you; virtual paradises built on children's suffering.† *Design:* exits always open; special duty of care toward minors.

## J. Hope as a design stance

**Emily St. John Mandel, *Station Eleven* (2014).** Twenty years after a pandemic, a travelling symphony performs Shakespeare under a motto borrowed from *Star Trek*: "Survival is insufficient." *Design:* art, music and story are core progression, even in the wilderness.

**Hopepunk and solarpunk.** "The opposite of grimdark is hopepunk. Pass it on" (Alexandra Rowland, 2017†). Hopeful fiction isn't conflict-free; it shows communities repairing things. *Design:* hardship is real, repair is always possible, care is powerful.

**Kim Stanley Robinson, *The Ministry for the Future* (2020).** Hope as a long, collective project of institutions and relationships.† *Design:* world projects that take many players and seasons.

**Italo Calvino, *Invisible Cities* (1972).** The book ends on "the inferno of the living"† and two ways to live in it; the hard one is to "seek and learn to recognize who and what, in the midst of the inferno, are not inferno, then make them endure, give them space."† *Design:* a creed for world-builders.

## M. Quotes to avoid (commonly misattributed)

- "Fairy tales are more than true: not because they tell us that dragons exist, but because they tell us that dragons can be beaten." This is Neil Gaiman's paraphrase of Chesterton; Gaiman says so himself. Use Chesterton's real line (above).
- "You can discover more about a person in an hour of play than in a year of conversation" is **not** Plato.
- "Fantasy is escapist, and that is its glory" is **not** Tolkien.
- "Who are the people most opposed to escapism? Jailers!" is Arthur C. Clarke's wording; the underlying point is in C.S. Lewis, reporting a question from Tolkien.
- "The opposite of play is not work. It's depression" compresses Brian Sutton-Smith's longer sentence; credit him, not Stuart Brown.
- "Ora et labora" does not appear in the Rule of St Benedict; it's a later motto.
- "In every deliberation, we must consider the impact on the seventh generation" is not verbatim in the Haudenosaunee Great Law.
- The four-circle *ikigai* Venn diagram is not Japanese; it relabels a 2011 Western "purpose" diagram.
- "Magic circle" as a game-design concept was coined in 1999 (Zimmerman and Lantz; popularized by Salen and Zimmerman in 2003), not by Huizinga.
