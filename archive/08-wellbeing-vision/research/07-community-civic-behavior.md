## 1. Ten headline takeaways

1. **The loneliness gap is a gap in *time*, and a game competes for exactly those minutes.** American Time Use Survey data cover 219,368 respondents. Between 2003 and 2019, time with friends fell from 60 to 34 min/day and time alone rose from 285 to 309 min/day. Isolation reached 333 min/day in 2020, and young people's time with friends "plummeted" (Kannan & Veazie 2023). Open Legend's wellbeing case therefore depends on *turning* play time into relationship time, not adding to screen time.

2. **"Shoulder to shoulder" beats "face to face."** The institutions that reliably create belonging give people a shared task and let talk happen alongside it. Examples: Men's Sheds ("Men don't talk face to face, they talk shoulder to shoulder"), parkrun, choirs, Repair Cafés, communal meals and barn raisings. Group singing bonds strangers faster than craft or creative-writing classes do (Pearce, Launay & Dunbar 2015). Open Legend's forge, oven, harvest and building loops already have this shape.

3. **Contribution roles are an underrated way in.** In 60,680 parkrun survey returns, people who *only* volunteered mostly felt part of a community (83.5%) and met new people (85.2%). 54.5% reported better mental health, although only 4.7% had volunteered for that reason (Haake et al. 2022). Observational meta-analyses link late-life volunteering to about 24% lower adjusted mortality risk, but RCTs haven't confirmed health effects (Okun et al. 2013; Jenkinson et al. 2013). Design helper roles, not only player roles.

4. **Social prescribing has scaled faster than its evidence.** England went from about 235,500 patients referred in 2019 to about 1.75 million in 2023, and 2,000 GP surgeries are registered "parkrun practices." Reviews still find little evidence that link workers themselves improve outcomes ("There is an absence of evidence for social prescribing link workers," Kiely et al. 2022). The national rollout came with only small population-level gains (Wilding et al. 2025). A well-evaluated layer for rehearsal and referral is something the field genuinely lacks.

5. **Contact reduces prejudice reliably but modestly, and its conditions can be designed.** Across 515 studies (713 samples, 250,089 people), the mean effect is r ≈ −.21. It rises to −.29 when Allport's conditions are present: equal status, common goals, cooperation and support from authorities (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006). Knowing that an in-group friend has an out-group friend also helps (r = .25), and even *imagined* contact helps (d = .35). Cross-group friendship is the active ingredient. Caveats: bad contact hurts more than good contact helps, and rigorous long-term evidence on adult racial prejudice is thin.

6. **Don't copy Robbers Cave: it was staged.** Gina Perry's archival work shows Sherif's staff engineered the rivalry. In the 1953 Middle Grove pilot, "the boys worked out that the adults were engineering their conflict" and turned on the researchers. Superordinate goals are real, but participants notice manipulation. Build honest shared problems, not orchestrated ones.

7. **Commons governance already has a spec, and it reads like a design doc.** Ostrom's eight principles are boundaries, local fit, collective choice, accountable monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, a recognized right to organize, and nesting. They map one-to-one onto the borderland's river crossings, forge, oven and gardens, and they are a transferable civic skill.

8. **Structured deliberation works at scale when the format does the heavy lifting.** Ireland's 99 randomly chosen citizens voted 79–12 on the Eighth Amendment question, which set up the 2018 referendum. Taiwan's online deliberation platform, vTaiwan, uses the Pol.is voting tool (no reply button; opinion clusters are shown). By 2018 it had handled 26 cases, about 80% of which led to government action. Deliberative polls shift opinion measurably, and in America in One Room the shift lasted about a year. These are ready-made templates for institutions in player-governed worlds.

9. **The best-supported behavior-change tools are small, cheap and planning-based, and most effects fade.** Examples: implementation intentions (d ≈ .65†); WOOP/MCII (g = .34, with guided versions stronger than worksheets); fresh-start timing; temptation bundling (+51% gym visits at first, then decaying); realistic habit timelines (median about 2 months, one missed day doesn't matter). In a megastudy of 61,293 people, 45% of 54 interventions raised gym visits by 9–27%. Only 8% produced measurable change after the four weeks, and forecasters couldn't predict the winners. Design for maintenance, and test everything.

10. **Positive-psychology effects are real but small, so test before you build them into rituals.** After correcting for small-study bias, the effects of positive psychology interventions (PPIs) on wellbeing shrink to r ≈ .10, and effects on depression are mostly non-significant (White et al. 2019). Other estimates: acts of kindness δ = .28; best possible self d ≈ .33; expressive writing r = .075 across 146 RCTs. The replication crisis claimed ego depletion (36-lab d = .06), power posing (no hormonal effect) and Blue Zones longevity claims. They are a warning against building mechanics on single striking studies.

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 The problem: disappearing third places and social capital

**Oldenburg's third places.** *The Great Good Place* (1989) named the informal gathering places that sit apart from home (the "first place") and work (the "second place") ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_place)). His eight characteristics†:
1. Neutral ground.
2. A leveler.
3. Conversation is the main activity.
4. Accessibility and accommodation.
5. The regulars.
6. A low profile.
7. The mood is playful.
8. A home away from home.

Steinkuehler & Williams (2006) argue that MMOs work as third places. They say MMOs are "particularly well suited to formation of bridging social relationships that, while not usually providing deep emotional support, typically function to expose the individual to a diversity of worldviews" (*J. Computer-Mediated Communication* 11(4); [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00300.x)). **C.** The implication: a game can be a real third place, but by default it produces *bridging* ties rather than *bonding* ones. Deep support has to be designed in.

**Putnam's social capital.** Putnam's essay (*Journal of Democracy*, 1995) and book (2000) documented a fall in league bowling while the total number of bowlers rose. He also found declines in PTA, union, church and fraternal membership, public-meeting attendance and trust. His rough breakdown of causes since 1965 was about 50% generational change, 25% TV and electronic entertainment, 10% time and money pressure, and 10% sprawl. He distinguished **bonding** capital (within groups) from **bridging** capital (across groups). Critics such as Nicholas Lemann argue civic life changed form rather than declined ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Alone)). **C.**

**Time-use data.** Kannan & Veazie (*SSM–Population Health* 2023) analyzed American Time Use Survey data for 2003–2020. Time alone rose from 285 to 309 min/day, time with friends fell from 60 to 34 min/day, and companionship fell from 202 to 182 min/day. Isolation reached 333 min/day in 2020. For 15–24-year-olds, engagement with friends "plummeted" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S235282732200310X)). **B** (descriptive).

### 2.2 Community institutions: origin, scale and evidence

**Social prescribing (England).** The NHS definition: "an approach that connects people to activities, groups, and services in their community to meet the practical, social and emotional needs that affect their health and wellbeing." Link workers start from "what matters to me?" Targets were 1,000 link workers by 2020/21 and 900,000 referrals by 2023/24 ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/personalisedcare/social-prescribing/)). Actual referrals, from CPRD records for 1.2M patients: about 235,500 in 2019 and about 1.75M in 2023. The deprived-area share rose from 23% to 42%, and refusals fell from 22% to 12% (Bu … Fancourt, *Lancet Public Health* 2025, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468266725002178)). The outcome evidence is thin:
- Bickerdike et al. 2017 (15 evaluations): "current evidence fails to provide sufficient detail to judge either success or value for money" ([BMJ Open](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/4/e013384)).
- Kiely et al. 2022 (8 studies, including 5 RCTs): link workers "may have little or no impact on HRQoL, mental health or a range of patient-reported outcomes though they may improve self-rated health" ([BMJ Open](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/12/10/e062951)).
- Wilding et al. 2025 (4.13M survey responses): the rollout brought "small improvements in patient experience" (e.g., OR 1.006 per extra link worker per 50,000 patients) ([BJGP](https://bjgp.org/content/early/2025/11/03/BJGP.2024.0542)).

**C** for outcomes.

**parkrun.** Started 2 Oct 2004 in Bushy Park by Paul Sinton-Hewitt with "13 runners, three volunteers." It now has 2,000+ events in 29 countries and 10M+ registrants; junior parkrun started in 2013 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkrun)).
- **Participation and value:** about 200,000 people a week in the UK. The wellbeing value is ≥£667m a year, or ≥£16.70 per £1, and life satisfaction rose "after doing as few as two parkruns, with the greatest increase among those least active" ([SHU](https://www.shu.ac.uk/news/all-articles/latest-news/parkrun-economic-value)).
- **Volunteers:** in Haake, Quirk & Bullas 2022 (60,680 returns; 681 volunteers-only), 83.5% felt part of the community, 85.2% met new people and 54.5% reported better mental health ([SHURA](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/29821/)).
- **GP link:** 2,000 GP surgeries were registered "parkrun practices" by 28 Mar 2025 ([RCGP](https://www.rcgp.org.uk/News/2000-GP-surgeries-registered-parkrun-practices)).
- **Fragility:** Little Stoke's council voted in 2016 to charge runners, which killed the event.

**C.** The features are the design: free, weekly, timed but not raced, walkers welcome, a tail walker, rotating volunteer roles, coffee after.

**Men's Sheds.** Wikipedia credits Maxine Chaseling's shed at Goolwa, South Australia (1993). The Australian Men's Shed Association (AMSA) formed in 2007, with the slogan "Shoulder To Shoulder" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_shed)). AMSA puts the global total at "approaching 3,000" sheds in 12 countries ([AMSA](https://mensshed.org/about-mens-sheds/mens-sheds-around-the-world/)). A mixed-methods review (Foettinger et al. 2022; 52 studies, 35 qualitative) found mainly mental-health and belonging benefits (90.4% reported more belonging). But the questionnaires were unvalidated and there were no controls ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/15579883221084490)). **C.**

**Repair Cafés.** Martine Postma, Amsterdam, 18 Oct 2009. There were 3,818+ by Oct 2025, and the open RepairMonitor database logs 305,649+ repairs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repair_Caf%C3%A9)). The mission "strives to preserve repair skills in society and to promote more repairable products" ([repaircafe.org](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/about/)). I found no wellbeing research (**D**). The open-data standard and starter-kit franchise are the model to borrow.

**Choirs.** Pearce, Launay & Dunbar (2015) followed new singing, craft and creative-writing classes for 7 months. Singers bonded much faster at month 1; the others caught up by month 3. The authors call this "the first evidence for an 'ice-breaker effect' of singing" ([abstract](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1098/rsos.150221?fields=title,abstract,authors,year,venue)). **B−.**

**Shared meals.** Dunbar (2017) used a UK national survey, reportedly for The Big Lunch†. People who eat socially more often report more happiness, life satisfaction, trust, support networks and community engagement. Path analysis suggests eating together leads to bonding rather than the reverse ([ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:353f2f08-df43-46c4-b802-bfbdfe5bb4eb)). **C.**

**Community gardens.** In the CAPS RCT (Litt et al. 2023), 291 Denver adults were randomized to a garden plot or a waitlist. Gardeners gained 1.4 g/day of fiber and 42 min/week of activity, and their stress and anxiety fell. Litt: "It's also about being in a natural space outdoors together with others" ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/today/2023/01/05/scientific-reasons-you-should-resolve-start-gardening)). **B.**

**Run clubs (2024–25).**
- Strava 2024: running clubs rose 59%, 58% of those surveyed made friends at one, and 1 in 5 Gen Z users dated someone met through exercise ([Canadian Running](https://runningmagazine.ca/the-scene/stravas-year-end-trend-report-shows-run-clubs-are-in-nightclubs-are-out/)).
- Strava 2025: clubs "nearly quadrupled," reaching 1M, and "39% more Gen Z than Gen X use fitness to meet people who share their interests" ([Strava](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025)).

**D** as evidence, but a strong signal of demand for socializing built around an activity. **Meetup†** (Scott Heiferman, 2002, after 9/11, inspired by *Bowling Alone*) is the template for "find people online, then meet offline."

**Gift and time economies.**
- **Buy Nothing:** Rebecca Rockefeller and Liesl Clark, Bainbridge Island, 2013; 7.5M members. Its mission: "build resilient communities where our true wealth is the connections forged between neighbors." In 2020 it redrew its equity approach after finding its group boundaries echoed redlining. In 2025 its trademark enforcement shut down many independent groups ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project)).
- **Timebanking:** Edgar Cahn; "Time Dollars," 1992. One hour of help earns one credit whatever the skill. The five core values: everyone is an asset; some work is beyond a monetary price; reciprocity; community is necessary; respect for all. There are about 34 countries, ≥500 US timebanks (276+ active) and about 300 UK ones. Evidence is mostly self-report, and staffed banks struggle to sustain themselves (Seyfang) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timebanking)). **C/D.**

**Conversation institutions.**
- **Human Library:** founded at the Roskilde Festival in 2000 by Ronni and Dany Abergel, Asma Mouna and Christoffer Erichsen, from the youth NGO Stop Volden. It has run in 85+ countries and uses the tagline "Unjudge someone." Its Copenhagen Reading Garden closed in Oct 2025 after losing funding ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Human_Library)).
- **StoryCorps:** Dave Isay, Grand Central Terminal, 23 Oct 2003. Two people hold a 40-minute facilitated conversation archived at the Library of Congress; 645,000+ have taken part. One Small Step (2018) pairs political opposites for 50 minutes about their lives, not politics ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StoryCorps)).
- **Death Café:** Jon Underwood, 2011, after Bernard Crettaz's *cafés mortels*. The objective is "To increase awareness of death with a view to helping people make the most of their (finite) lives." There is no agenda, it is not grief support, and it spreads as a social franchise. It has reached 100 countries and about 246,930 participants ([deathcafe.com](https://deathcafe.com/what/)).
- **Little Free Library:** Todd Bol, 2009; 150,000+ boxes in 115 countries; tends to cluster in affluent areas ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Free_Library)).

None of these has rigorous outcome studies (**D**). Their mechanisms are contact, narrative and generativity.

### 2.3 Communal labor, small circles and festivals

- **Talkoot (Finland).** Related forms include Norwegian *dugnad*, Estonian *talgu* and Latvian/Lithuanian *talka*. It is "a gathering of friends and neighbors organized to accomplish a task." The person who benefits must "offer food and drink." Taking part is nominally voluntary, but "one's honour and reputation may be severely damaged by non-attendance or laziness." People who can't do physical work contribute food or childcare ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talkoot)).
- **Gotong royong (Indonesia).** "Joint bearing of burdens." Villages used it to build irrigation, streets and places of worship. Sukarno co-opted it ("A Gotong Royong state!"), and later state propaganda "fossilized" it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotong-royong)). It is a caution about top-down capture of mutual aid.
- **Minka / ayni / mit'a (Andes).** Minka comes from Quechua *minccacuni*, "asking for help by promising something." It is voluntary collective labor for community infrastructure. *Ayni* is the underlying principle of reciprocity, and *mit'a* was the Inca state's mandatory labor ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minka_(communal_work))). That gives three distinct logics: a gift to the community, balanced reciprocity, and a duty to the state.
- **Barn raising (Amish and Old Order Mennonite).** It takes one to two days and nobody is paid. "Men construct the barn, women provide water and food, the youngest children watch, and older boys are assigned to fetch parts and tools." It ends with a feast and dance, and staying away without good reason draws censure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barn_raising)).
- **Okinawan moai.** A moai began as village financial pooling. Traditionally about five children committed to each other for life, and some groups have lasted 90+ years. Blue Zones claims about half of Okinawans belong to one ([Blue Zones](https://www.bluezones.com/2018/08/moai-this-tradition-is-why-okinawan-people-live-longer-better/)). The longevity story is contested: Saul Newman points to record errors, and Okinawa now ranks highest in Japan for obesity ([The World 2024](https://theworld.org/stories/2024/12/10/fake-news-unpacking-the-blue-zone-myth-in-okinawa)). **D** for longevity. A small, stable, lifelong peer group is still a sound design building block.
- **Collective effervescence.** Durkheim's term (1912†) is now measured as "perceived emotional synchrony." A 2022 meta-analysis from Páez's group (*Frontiers in Psychology*; 50 studies, 182,738 participants) found it "strongly associates with Individual Emotions –in particular, Self-Transcendent Emotions– and Communal Sharing constructs." At demonstrations, it correlated r = .58 with self-transcendent emotion, .52 with group identity and .37 with collective efficacy ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.974683/full)). **C.**
- **Burning Man's 10 Principles.** Larry Harvey wrote them in 2004 for the regional network, "not as rules to govern how we should act, but as a projection of the community's ethos." The ten are: Radical Inclusion ("Anyone may be a part of Burning Man. We welcome and respect the stranger."), Gifting ("The value of a gift is unconditional. Gifting does not contemplate a return or an exchange."), Decommodification, Radical Self-reliance, Radical Self-expression, Communal Effort, Civic Responsibility, Leaving No Trace, Participation and Immediacy ([burningman.org](https://burningman.org/about/10-principles/)). Framing them as principles rather than rules is what let regional events copy themselves.

### 2.4 Civic practice and conflict transformation

**Ostrom's eight design principles** (*Governing the Commons*, 1990; Nobel 2009†), in commonly used wording ([P2P Foundation](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Eight_Design_Principles_for_Common_Pool_Resource_Systems)):
1. Clearly defined boundaries.
2. Rules for using and maintaining the resource that fit local conditions.
3. Collective-choice arrangements that let most users take part in making rules.
4. Effective monitoring by monitors who are users or are accountable to them.
5. Graduated sanctions.
6. Cheap, accessible conflict resolution.
7. Higher authorities recognize the community's right to organize.
8. For larger systems, nested enterprises with small local units at the base.

Wilson, Ostrom & Cox (2013†) generalized the principles to all kinds of groups; this became the "Prosocial" method†. **B** (large comparative case literature).

**Robbers Cave, and Perry's critique.** In 1954, 22 boys were split into Eagles and Rattlers. The study moved from forming in-groups, to hostility driven by competition, to cooperation on superordinate goals such as fixing the water supply and pooling money for a film ([Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/robbers-cave.html)).

Gina Perry's *The Lost Boys* (2018), as summarized by a reviewer, shows staff "prevented cross-group friendships, encouraged the boys' pranks, showed favoritism towards a group… and accompanied them on their pranks." In the 1953 Middle Grove pilot, friendships formed across groups within days blocked the manufactured hostility. "The boys worked out that the adults were engineering their conflict" and turned on the researchers, and Sherif rarely mentioned that pilot afterwards ([Warne review](https://russellwarne.com/2021/06/27/book-review-gina-perrys-the-lost-boys/); [Simply Psychology](https://www.simplypsychology.org/robbers-cave.html); [Perry](http://www.gina-perry.com/the-lost-boys/)). **D** as evidence; strong as a warning.

**Contact theory.**
- **Allport (1954):** prejudice "may be reduced by equal status contact between majority and minority groups in the pursuit of common goals," and more so with institutional support ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_hypothesis); the full quote is from memory†).
- **Pettigrew & Tropp (*JPSP* 2006):** 515 studies, 713 samples, 250,089 participants in 38 countries. The mean effect is r ≈ −.21: −.287 with Allport's conditions and −.204 without. The authors treat the conditions as helpful rather than essential ([PDF](https://ideas.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Pettigrew-Tropp.pdf)). **A−.**
- **Extended contact** (knowing an in-group friend has an out-group friend): Zhou et al. (2019; 115 studies, 248 effects) found r = .25, or .17 after controlling for direct friendship ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1088868318762647)).
- **Imagined contact:** Miles & Crisp (2014; 70+ studies) found d = .35, stronger when scenarios are elaborated and with children ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1368430213510573)).
- **Friendship induced in the lab:** Page-Gould, Mendoza-Denton & Tropp (2008) induced cross-group friendship. It lowered cortisol reactivity across three meetings in people who were highly sensitive to race-based rejection, and led to more intergroup interaction afterwards ([record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0022-3514.95.5.1080)).
- **Caveats:**
  - Paluck, Green & Green (2019) found few experiments measure beyond one day, and none test racial prejudice in adults over 25. Effects were weaker for ethnic and racial prejudice.
  - Barlow et al. (2012) found negative contact predicts increased prejudice more strongly than positive contact predicts reduced prejudice.
  - Contact over the internet ("e-contact") shows promise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_hypothesis)).

**Seeds of Peace.** John Wallach founded it in 1993. Its camp in Otisfield, Maine, takes teens aged 14–16 for three weeks, with follow-up programs through the year ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeds_of_Peace)). Schroeder & Risen (2016) found that campers who formed an out-group friendship grew more positive toward out-group campers, "which generalized to an increase in positivity toward all outgroup members." The effect lasted months, though it faded once campers returned home ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1368430214542257)). **B−.**

**Braver Angels.** Founded in 2016 by Blankenhorn, Doherty and Lapp. Its Red/Blue workshops bring equal numbers of conservatives and liberals together, with a stereotypes exercise and "fishbowl" listening. The ground rule comes from couples therapy: "Speak for yourself; don't interpret what's going on in the other person's mind." About 1,600 workshops had run by 2022, and participants are "mostly white, college-educated, and older." Baron et al. (2021) found depolarization is "especially effective when it includes both informational and emotional components." Peter Coleman doubts one-off workshops can change much ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braver_Angels)). **B−.**

**Participatory budgeting.** Porto Alegre began in 1989 (Workers' Party, Mayor Olívio Dutra) with a pyramid of neighborhood assemblies, district forums and a city council. Sewer and water connections rose from 75% (1988) to 98% (1997), and health and education grew from 13% of the budget to about 40%. Gonçalves (2014) linked participatory budgeting to lower infant mortality across Brazil. There were 11,500+ processes worldwide by 2024, but Porto Alegre's own process lapsed after 2017 when political support faded ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participatory_budgeting)). **B.**

**vTaiwan and Pol.is.** vTaiwan came out of the 2014 Sunflower protests and was built by the g0v civic-tech community in 2015, with Audrey Tang's support. On Pol.is, people post statements, others vote agree or disagree, and the opinion clusters are shown. There is no reply button. Tang: "If people can propose their ideas and comments but they cannot reply to each other, then it drastically reduces the motivation for trolls to troll." By August 2018 there had been 26 cases, 80% leading to government action (Uber, online alcohol sales, fintech). On alcohol sales, about 450 citizens broke a four-year deadlock. The process is advisory ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2018/08/21/240284/the-simple-but-ingenious-system-taiwan-uses-to-crowdsource-its-laws/)). Pol.is is open source (AGPL†). **C.**

**Ireland's citizens' assemblies.** The 2016–18 assembly had 99 randomly selected citizens and a chair, Mary Laffoy, stratified by gender, age, region and class. It voted 79–12 that the Eighth Amendment should not be kept in full, and a referendum followed on 25 May 2018 (66.4% yes†) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland))). The earlier Convention on the Constitution (2012–14, citizens plus politicians) led to the 2015 marriage-equality referendum (62.1% yes†). Later assemblies covered gender equality and biodiversity. **B.**

**Deliberative polling (Fishkin, 1988).** Poll a random sample, give it balanced briefings, run moderated small groups and expert panels, then poll again. In America in One Room (2019), 526 participants moved toward the center, and the effect lasted at least a year. Texas utility deliberations (1996–98) raised support for renewables, and Zeguo, China, used the method to set budgets ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deliberative_opinion_poll)). **B.**

**Jane Jacobs.** *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* (1961) says safe streets need a clear line between public and private space, "eyes upon the street," and fairly continuous use of the sidewalk. Diversity needs mixed primary uses, short blocks, buildings of different ages, and density ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Death_and_Life_of_Great_American_Cities)). A commonly cited line†: "There must be eyes upon the street, eyes belonging to those we might call the natural proprietors of the street." New evidence: Khalil & Sanfelice (2025) found that improving housing in Chicago raised occupancy, and higher occupancy tracked falls in crime ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_on_the_street)). **C.**

**Christopher Alexander, *A Pattern Language* (1977; 253 patterns).** The following were checked this session on [iwritewordsgood.com](https://www.iwritewordsgood.com/apl/patterns/apl040.htm):
- **#40 Old People Everywhere:** "Old people need old people, but they also need the young, and young people need contact with the old." Its solution places housing for about 50 old people in every neighborhood, arranged in rings "never more than 200 yards from the core."
- **#45 Necklace of Community Projects:** "Allow the growth of shop-size spaces around the local town hall… lease them for a minimum rent to ad hoc community groups for political work, trial services, research, and advocate groups."
- **#67 Common Land:** "Without common land no social system can survive." It gives "over 25 per cent of the land in house clusters to common land."
- **#88 Street Café:** "a place people can sit lazily, legitimately, be on view" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Pattern_Language)).
- **#147 Communal Eating:** "Give every institution and social group a place where people can eat together. Make the common meal a regular event."
- **#157 Home Workshop:** "Make a place in the home, where substantial work can be done; not just a hobby, but a job."

### 2.5 Rehearsal arts: theatre, psychodrama, RPGs and larp

**Augusto Boal's Theatre of the Oppressed.** The book dates from 1974, with the English translation in 1979†; it draws on Freire's *Pedagogy of the Oppressed*. In **forum theatre**, a scene of oppression is played and then replayed. Audience members become "spect-actors" who stop the action and take over the protagonist's role to try other solutions, and a **Joker** facilitates. Boal also developed **image**, **invisible** and **legislative theatre**. As a Rio city councillor he used legislative theatre to produce about 13 laws ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_the_Oppressed)). The line as usually quoted from the 1979 translation†: "Perhaps the theater is not revolutionary in itself; but have no doubts, it is a rehearsal of revolution!" Wikipedia's paraphrase: "Theatre is not revolutionary in itself but is rehearsal of revolution." ⚠ I couldn't check this against the book (the fetch was blocked).

**Psychodrama (J. L. Moreno).** It grew out of Moreno's improvisational Theatre of Spontaneity in Vienna in the early 1920s.
- **Roles:** protagonist, director, auxiliary egos and the group.
- **Structure:** warm-up, then action, then sharing.
- **Techniques:** role reversal, doubling, mirroring and soliloquy. Its sister methods are sociometry and sociodrama ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychodrama)).
- **Evidence:** Kipper & Ritchie (2003) meta-analyzed 25 studies and reported large effects (d ≈ .95), strongest for role reversal and doubling†. The studies were small and old. **C.**

**Drama therapy.** "Acting techniques used for actors and non-actors to facilitate personal growth and promote mental health." It works through role and story, keeps an "aesthetic" safe distance, and draws on Phil Jones's nine core processes. Professional bodies include NADTA (North America) and BADth (UK), and UK practitioners are regulated by the HCPC ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_therapy)). The evidence base is limited. **C/D.**

**Tabletop RPG therapy.** Game to Grow (co-founders Adam Johns, LMFT, and Adam Davis, M.A.Ed.) runs therapeutic and educational gaming groups and trains clinicians through Antioch University Seattle ([Game to Grow](https://gametogrow.org/about/)). Geek Therapeutics offers "Therapeutic Game Master" training†. A scoping review by Arenas, Viduani & Araujo (*Simulation & Gaming* 2022) found:
- 50 sources out of 4,069 screened.
- 70% of interventions were for adolescents.
- Only 16% of studies were experimental.

Its conclusion: "a potential use of RPGs as a complementary tool in psychotherapies," with "more empirical and well-designed studies" needed ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10468781211073720)). **C/D.**

**Larp, educational larp and bleed.** Østerskov Efterskole is a Danish boarding school founded in 2006† that teaches the national curriculum through role-play themes that change each week†. Sarah Lynne Bowman (Nordic Larp, 2015) defines **bleed** as "real life feelings, thoughts, relationships, and physical states spill over into their characters' and vice versa."
- **Directions:** bleed-in (player to character) and bleed-out (character to player).
- **Positive bleed:** "golden moments," catharsis.
- **Negative bleed:** lasting aggression, damaged relationships.
- **Safety practices:** in-game signals or safe words, rituals for stepping out of role, debriefing, and socializing out of game ([Nordic Larp](https://nordiclarp.org/2015/03/02/bleed-the-spillover-between-player-and-character/)).
- **Grade:** **D** (practitioner knowledge), but essential for a game that *wants* positive bleed-out.

**Notes on the table**

- **Planning and timing.** Implementation intentions turn "I intend to reach Z" into "When situation X arises, I will perform response Y." WOOP adds mental contrasting first (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan). **Guided delivery beat worksheets**, which makes it a natural job for an AI mentor. Fresh starts work through "notional boundaries" that put distance between people and their past failures (Dai et al.). Temptation bundling pairs a guilty pleasure with a "should"; people wanted it, but it decayed. The megastudy shows nudges often work during an intervention, rarely after it, and experts can't pick the winners.
- **Habits.** Lally et al.: 96 volunteers, 12 weeks, 82 with usable data. Reaching 95% of peak automaticity took 18–254 days. Fogg's recipe† is "After I [existing routine], I will [tiny behavior]," then an immediate celebration. His model says behavior happens "when Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt come together at the same time."
- **Behavioral activation.** It schedules valued activities to break avoidance. Adding cognitive techniques contributed little (Jacobson 1996), and junior workers delivered it as well as CBT (COBRA, [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0)). The loop suits a game: log an activity, rate its effect on mood, schedule the next one.
- **Gratitude and kindness.** In Seligman et al. 2005 ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1037/0003-066X.60.5.410)), the gratitude visit gave the biggest boost but it faded in about a month. Three good things and using signature strengths lasted about six months†. A 2012 replication found the exercises no better than the control condition†. Kumar & Epley (2018)† found letter writers underestimate how good recipients feel.
- **Writing.** Best possible self affects positive affect (d = .51) far more than depression (.12). Expressive writing (15 minutes a day for 4 days on "your very deepest thoughts and feelings") cut physician visits. Writing about imaginary traumas also helped, which suggests more than one mechanism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressive_writing)).
- **Narrative identity (McAdams).** An "internalized, evolving story of the self" that integrates "reconstructed past, perceived present, and imagined future." Its themes are redemption (bad to good), contamination (good to bad), agency and communion. Redemptive stories go with wellbeing and generativity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative_identity)).
- **Generativity (Erikson's stage 7).** The Loyola scale has 20 items, e.g., "I try to pass along the knowledge that I have gained through my experiences" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generativity)). Experience Corps† (older adults tutoring children) is the intergenerational program usually cited. Reviews of intergenerational programs† report better attitudes and engagement, but in small, mostly uncontrolled studies (**C**).
- **ACT values†.** Values are chosen life directions, not goals. Common exercises: the eulogy exercise, the Valued Living Questionnaire, the bull's-eye. Reviews of meta-analyses find ACT beats waitlist and usual care and is roughly comparable to CBT.
- **Designing Your Life (Burnett & Evans 2016)†.** The *Good Time Journal* logs engagement and energy (reflected on through AEIOU: Activities, Environments, Interactions, Objects, Users). *Odyssey Plans* sketch three different five-year lives. *Prototype conversations* talk to people already living a path. Evidence is mostly course evaluations (**C/D**).
- **Wise interventions (Walton).** Brief, precise changes to how people interpret adversity. Walton & Cohen's participants "remained unaware" of the effect ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1126/science.1198364)). Scale-ups show small effects that depend on context ("seed and soil"†). Helper's high (Luks 1988)† rests on observational evidence.

### 2.7 Wellbeing frameworks and measures

- **PERMA† (Seligman 2011):** Positive emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment. Goodman et al. (2018; N = 517) found a latent correlation of r = .98 between PERMA and subjective wellbeing, so PERMA is not a distinct construct. It is a useful menu but a weak measure ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/17439760.2017.1388434)).
- **Ryff's six dimensions† (1989):** self-acceptance, positive relations, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth.
- **VanderWeele's flourishing measure.** Six domains: happiness and life satisfaction; mental and physical health; meaning and purpose; character and virtue; close social relationships; and financial and material stability, which is added in the "Secure Flourish" version. It has 12 items scored 0–10, e.g., "Overall, how satisfied are you with life as a whole these days?" ([Harvard HFP](https://hfh.fas.harvard.edu/measuring-flourishing); [PNAS 2017](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1073/pnas.1702996114)).
- **Global Flourishing Study (*Nature Mental Health*, 30 Apr 2025).** 202,000+ people in 22 countries.
  - Highest: Indonesia (8.47), Mexico (8.19), Philippines (8.11).
  - Lowest: Japan (5.93), Turkey (6.59), UK (6.88).
  - Young adults flourish least; on average scores rise with age (about 7.36 at 80+), though the pattern varies by country.
  - Weekly religious attendance (7.67 vs 6.86 for never), marriage and employment all correlate with flourishing ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-025-00423-5)).
  - These give free national baselines for measuring Open Legend players.

### 2.8 Replication watchlist: don't build on these

- **Ego depletion:** a 23-lab registered replication found no effect. A 36-lab study (N = 3,531) found d = .06 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ego_depletion)).
- **Power posing:** Ranehill et al. (2015) found no hormonal or behavioral effect. Co-author Dana Carney later wrote: "I do not believe that 'power pose' effects are real." A 2022 meta-analysis of 128 studies found effects only on self-reported feelings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_posing)).
- **Robbers Cave:** staged, per Perry (see 2.4).
- **Blue Zones longevity:** record errors; contested.
- **Positive psychology intervention effect sizes:** shrink sharply after bias correction.
- **Values affirmation and mindset scale-ups:** smaller and dependent on context.
- **Link-worker outcomes:** weak so far.

## 4. Design implications for Open Legend

Each item gives the mechanic, then the evidence behind it, then a real-world bridge or risk.

### A. Rituals built around a shared task

1. **Raisings (talkoot, minka, barn raising).**
   - **Mechanic:** Any resident or player can "call a raising" for a build too big for one household: a barn, a bridge at the disputed river crossing, a wilderness shelter, a sci-fi habitat module.
   - **Rules:**
     - Tasks are sized so no one can finish alone.
     - The host must provide the feast.
     - Non-builders get real roles such as cooking, childcare, water or tools.
     - AI residents remember who helped whom (ayni-style reciprocity). Absence costs warmth, never currency.
     - The raising ends in a feast and dance.
   - **Bridge:** park clean-ups, volunteer builds.

2. **The oven as a third place.** Design the communal bread oven, and the city world's café or laundromat, against Oldenburg's eight traits:
   - neutral ground, with no status displays;
   - conversation as the main activity, helped by built-in waiting (dough must rise);
   - a host NPC who greets regulars by name;
   - a playful mood;
   - Alexander's #88 seating for watching the world go by.

   Without deliberate idle time, nobody lingers.

3. **Ice-breaker songs.** Put every newcomer into a synchronous group activity in their first session: harvest work songs, rowing chants, a round at the fair. Group success depends on keeping time together, and individual voices are never scored (Pearce 2015). At a milestone, surface local choirs.

4. **The common meal.** Every settlement gets a weekly common meal by default (#147; Dunbar). The Narrator seeds the conditions that make meals bond people: more diners, laughter, reminiscing (NPCs retell world history, and players' inventions get toasted). Bridge: a "host a real meal" quest with a printable invitation. Measure self-reported meals eaten with others.

5. **The shed or guild workshop.**
   - **Mechanic:** A Home Workshop (#157) where players craft side by side with elder NPC masters.
   - **Conversation:** prompts come out of the work, not the UI.
   - **Bridge:** real Men's Sheds (~3,000 worldwide).
   - **Framing:** the mechanism that matters is working side by side on something purposeful, so the space isn't gendered.

6. **Mending Day (a Repair Café analog).**
   - **Mechanic:** a monthly event where volunteer fixers teach while repairing broken tools and inventions.
   - **Repair ledger:** an open log modeled on RepairMonitor, so the community learns what breaks.
   - **Marketplace hook:** inventions that are easy to repair earn a "mender's mark."
   - **Bridge:** 3,800+ real Repair Cafés.

7. **Beating the Bounds (a parkrun analog).**
   - **Mechanic:** a free, weekly walk or run around the village boundary at a fixed time, timed but not raced.
   - **Roles:** a tail walker so no one finishes last; rotating volunteer roles (marshal, timekeeper, token-giver).
   - **Why:** it makes volunteering a first-class way to take part; in parkrun, 83.5% of volunteers-only felt part of the community.
   - **Bridge:** the parkrun event finder. **Partner candidate:** parkrun.

8. **Harvest fair as an effervescence engine.** Plan synchronized peaks such as processions, dances and a massed choir, since emotional synchrony correlates with self-transcendent emotion (r ≈ .58). Put commitment moments right after the peak: founding institutions, pledges to the commons. Effervescence also powers mobs, so pair it with the Ostrom tools in item 13.

### B. Contribution and bridges to real life

9. **Bridge Board.**
   - **Mechanic:** an in-world noticeboard and in-world phone app, opt-in and using only coarse location.
   - **What it lists:** real local parkruns, Repair Cafés, sheds, choirs, gardens, run clubs, Human Library and Death Café events, Buy Nothing groups, timebanks and Little Free Libraries, framed as "your village has cousins in your town."
   - **Privacy:** log only opt-in reports of "I went!", never location trails.
   - **Partner candidates:** parkrun, the Repair Café Foundation, the Human Library, StoryCorps, the Men's Shed associations (AMSA/UKMSA), Little Free Library and Death Café. Several already use social-franchise models.

10. **Link-worker residents.**
    - **Role:** some AI residents act as social-prescribing link workers. They ask "what matters to you?", notice the player's interests, and introduce them to other *players* first, then to Bridge Board options.
    - **Hard rule:** residents broker human ties instead of becoming the tie.
    - **Evaluation:** pilot with a social-prescribing body and evaluate by RCT. The field's gap in evidence is Open Legend's opening.

11. **Helper roles as the default way in.** Every public event has three to six newcomer roles: greeter, lantern-lighter, scorekeeper, water-bearer. Contribution comes before performance. Reward it with recognition (thanks from NPCs, a line in the Chronicle), not grindable points.

12. **Gift and time economies.**
    - **Time credits:** one hour of help earns one credit whatever the skill (Cahn). Credits can't be bought or traded for gold (Burning Man's decommodification).
    - **Gift shelves:** unconditional gifts, with no trade-back.
    - **Staffing:** AI residents do the coordination chores that make real timebanks fail.
    - **Boundaries:** draw gift-circle boundaries so they mix cohorts, the lesson of Buy Nothing's redlining problem.

### C. Civic rehearsal and governance

13. **An Ostrom toolkit for every commons.** For the river crossing, mill-race, forest, forge and oven, provide:
    - a boundary marker;
    - a local rule editor;
    - a village moot (collective choice);
    - elected monitors who answer to the users;
    - a graduated-sanction ladder: reminder, then fine, then temporary exclusion;
    - a cheap dispute ritual such as the elder's bench;
    - charter recognition by the world administration (principle 7);
    - nesting from village to valley council.

    The Narrator names each principle in play and points to real analogs (allotment committees, co-ops, HOAs). Metrics: resource depletion and time to resolve disputes.

14. **Sortition assemblies.**
    - **Trigger:** a dispute that escalates, such as a fight over river rights.
    - **Process:** a random draw of players and AI residents, briefing packets from each side, expert NPC testimony, facilitated small groups, then recommendations that go to a ballot.
    - **Transparency:** show pre/post opinion shifts, as deliberative polls do.
    - **Bridge:** explainers on Ireland 2016–18 and links to local assemblies and participatory budgeting.

15. **The Village Well (a Pol.is board).** Short statements that others vote agree, disagree or pass on, with **no reply button**. Opinion clusters are shown, and statements that win agreement *across* clusters rise to the top. Integrate the open-source Pol.is code rather than rebuilding it (check its license†). AI residents may seed statements but are labeled and excluded from human tallies.

16. **Harvest participatory budget.** Fair surplus is allocated through a pyramid: neighborhood assemblies, then district forum, then council, with concrete before/after results. Porto Alegre shows that visible delivery sustains participation, and that the process dies when leaders withdraw, so build in renewal rituals.

17. **Forum theatre at the crossroads.**
    - **Mechanic:** the Narrator stages short conflict scenes: a bully at the mill, a boss who won't listen in the city world. Players act as spect-actors who stop the scene and replace the protagonist, and a Joker NPC leads the debrief.
    - **Personal use:** players can import an anonymized real situation to rehearse, such as asking for help, setting a boundary or apologizing.
    - **Extensions:** a "legislative theatre" mode turns repeated scene outcomes into council proposals; a psychodrama-lite role reversal lets players voice the other party, whose AI memories make it rich.
    - **Limits:** strictly non-clinical, with no trauma re-enactment.

18. **Institutions that spread as social franchises.** When a player-founded institution succeeds, its founders can publish principles, a name and a registry that other worlds adopt. This is how Death Café, Repair Café, parkrun and Burning Man's regionals spread: "principles, not rules." Avoid Buy Nothing's failure mode, where heavy trademark enforcement split its groups.

19. **Jacobs and Alexander as level-design rules.**
    - **Jacobs:** mixed uses, short blocks, buildings of different ages, doors facing the street ("eyes upon the street").
    - **Alexander:** #40 Old People Everywhere; #67 Common Land (≥25% of cluster land); #68 Connected Play; #45 cheap storefronts around the town hall for ad hoc groups.
    - **Why:** these are the places where institutions can emerge physically.

### D. Intergroup contact, without puppet-mastering

20. **Allport-grade cooperative quests.**
    - **Design:** cross-faction quests with equal status (roles not gated by gear), a real common goal (flood repair), genuine interdependence and visible endorsement from authorities. These conditions raise the contact effect from r ≈ −.20 to −.29.
    - **Transparency:** crises are real world events, never staged rivalries. Middle Grove shows players detect manipulation.

21. **Cross-group friendship as the win condition.**
    - **Mechanic:** lightly celebrate friendships formed across opt-in real-world identity tags (political leaning, generation, country), because friendship carries the change.
    - **Supports:** show players when their friends have cross-group friends (extended contact, r = .25), and offer imagined-contact warm-ups for anxious players (d = .35).
    - **Re-entry:** add support such as reunions and in-world pen-pal post, since Seeds of Peace effects fade after camp.

22. **Protect against negative contact.** Bad encounters weigh more than good ones (Barlow 2012). Cross-group matchmaking should be structured (Braver Angels ground rules, fishbowl listening), moderated, easy to leave, and never leave a player with a single bad match as their only experience.

23. **Borrow-a-person and Lantern Talks.**
    - **Human Library:** volunteer players host 30-minute conversations as "books"; NPC "books" rehearse the format first.
    - **StoryCorps-style talks:** 40-minute structured conversations, archived with consent in the village library, including One-Small-Step pairings across partisan lines.
    - **Family version:** a player and a real grandparent.

### E. Personal-growth rituals

24. **WOOP at the Wishing Well.** Set real goals as Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan, ending in "When X, I will Y" plans. It is guided conversationally by an AI mentor, since guided MCII beat worksheets (g .47 vs .28). Check-ins happen in the journal.

25. **Fresh-start calendar.** Line up festivals and "new chapter" ceremonies with real landmarks: Mondays, month starts, the player's birthday, the anniversary of joining. Offer the ceremony, never force it.

26. **Habit Garden (Tiny Habits × Lally).**
    - **Mechanic:** plants grow as the player logs a tiny real habit anchored to an existing routine, followed by a celebration beat.
    - **Expectations:** about 2 months, with wide variation. A missed day wilts nothing.
    - **Returns:** a "welcome back" micro-reward after a miss, the megastudy's best lever.
    - **Persistence:** rotate novelty, since only 8% of nudges persisted.

27. **Walk-bundles.** A beloved NPC's serialized audio story unlocks only during real walks (phone step integration). Rotate stories seasonally, because bundling effects decay.

28. **Gratitude post and kindness errands.** Gratitude letters at the in-world post office can be exported or printed, and the Narrator encourages delivering them in person (the gratitude visit). Weekly kindness errands run both in-world and in real life. Framing stays honest: effects are small (δ ≈ .28), so these work best as habits.

29. **The Seer's tent.** Write a best-possible-self piece (wellbeing d ≈ .33; short sessions work best) or three Odyssey Plans, then complete a quest to hold a real prototype conversation with someone living one of those lives.

30. **The Chronicle.** The Narrator co-authors the player's life story in chapters (high points, low points, turning points) and gently offers redemptive and agentic framings without toxic positivity. A private page ("letters to the river") supports expressive writing about hard events: 15 minutes over 4 days, with crisis resources always one tap away.

31. **Elders, apprentices and death.**
    - **Apprenticeship:** aging NPCs seek apprentices (generativity), and players inherit their crafts and stories.
    - **Death:** each death is followed by a Death-Café-style hearth conversation: tea, cake and talk of death.
    - **Bridges:** real Death Cafés, intergenerational programs, and quests to record a Lantern Talk with a grandparent.

32. **Campfire Deck and active-constructive NPCs.**
    - **The deck:** Aron's three escalating rounds of questions for pairs (45 minutes, alternating turns). Opt-in, age-gated, with exits; usable for cross-group pairs (Page-Gould).
    - **NPC behavior:** AI residents respond to players' good news with active, constructive interest ("capitalization," Gable et al. 2004†) and prompt them to share it with a real person.

### F. Measurement, safety and honesty

33. **Measure flourishing, not engagement.**
    - **Measures:** opt-in quarterly VanderWeele Secure Flourish (12 items) plus a short loneliness scale, benchmarked against the Global Flourishing Study.
    - **Tracking:** the ratio of each player's human-to-human interaction to human-to-AI interaction. If AI companionship displaces human contact, residents push outward.
    - **Experiments:** run pre-registered, megastudy-style A/B tests on wellbeing outcomes, because designers can't predict which nudges work. Tag every mechanic A–D and never market a D as a benefit.

34. **Bleed protocols.** Adopt Nordic larp safety practice: calibration before intense scenes, in-game signals and safe words, rituals for stepping out of role, optional debriefs, and out-of-game socializing. Aim for positive bleed-out (courage, skills) and monitor negative bleed between players.
