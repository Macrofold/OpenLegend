# Games and Wellbeing: What Makes Play Healthy, and What Transfers to Real Life

> **Provenance.** AI-assisted web research compiled on 2026-09-28 for the [Wellbeing Vision](../README.md). *Research brief 01 for the Open Legend "Wellbeing Vision". Compiled 2026-09-28.*

**Evidence labels:**
- **[Strong]** large telemetry studies, natural experiments, RCTs, meta-analyses
- **[Moderate]** smaller RCTs, longitudinal or preregistered studies with limits
- **[Weak/Mixed]** cross-sectional, small samples, contested, or failed replication
- **[Secondary]** press releases, Wikipedia, vendor claims
- **[Interp]** my inference for Open Legend
- **"Not re-verified"**: a detail recalled from prior knowledge but not confirmed from a source fetched this session.

Quotes are verbatim from the linked page unless marked otherwise.

## 1. Headline takeaways

1. **Hours played barely matter; how and why people play matters a lot.** In Oxford's industry-telemetry studies, logged playtime has near-zero links to wellbeing. Motivation quality (enjoyment vs. feeling pressured), need satisfaction, cooperative vs. competitive play, and "gaming life fit" do predict wellbeing. **[Strong]**
2. **The best causal evidence is modestly positive, with a ceiling.** In Japan's console lotteries, winning a Switch or PS5 during COVID reduced distress and raised life satisfaction by 0.1–0.6 SD. Benefits "diminished when the gaming duration exceeded 3 h per day." **[Strong, but specific to the pandemic]**
3. **Games reliably lift mood a little, in the moment.** 162,325 in-game mood reports (PowerWash Simulator) show small average uplift during play, for an estimated ~72% of players. Oxford calls gaming "a recovery activity." Short-term uplift is not long-run flourishing. **[Strong for short-term affect]**
4. **Obligation is the main poison.** Feeling pressured to play predicts lower wellbeing. Daily chores in Animal Crossing became "not volitional." Need frustration (failure, loneliness, coercion) drives both quitting and ill-being. **[Strong–Moderate]**
5. **Games don't reliably rescue those who need them most.** During lockdowns, need satisfaction in life went with need satisfaction in games ("rich get richer"). Loneliness is both a cause and a consequence of problematic gaming. **[Moderate]**
6. **Escapism has two faces.**
   - Self-expansion (seeking growth) is benign or positive.
   - Self-suppression (avoiding feelings) tracks negative affect and gaming disorder.
   - A clean "return to reality" after play appears to buffer the harm. **[Moderate]**
7. **Disorder is real but rare; lost sleep is the common harm.**
   - Pooled prevalence is ~3% (~2% in better-sampled studies; 0.3–1% under strict criteria), and the diagnosis is contested.
   - In one survey, gamers delayed bedtime on 36% of gaming nights, by an average of 101 minutes. **[Strong/Moderate]**
8. **Social play helps most with real people, and when it bridges offline.**
   - In Animal Crossing, visiting other players' islands predicted less loneliness. Interactions with NPC villagers "did not improve feelings of social isolation."
   - Games build *bridging* capital (weak ties that widen your world). *Bonding* support (close ties) mostly needs offline contact.
   - Cooperative PvE players reported higher wellbeing than competitive PvP players. **[Moderate; the most important finding for AI residents]**
9. **Novelty decays.** Pokémon GO added ~955 steps a day in week 1, gone by week 6 (BMJ). In Microsoft's data, engaged users added 1,473 steps a day over 30 days. Only 3 of 13 active-game interventions raised real-world activity. **[Strong]**
10. **Therapeutic games work when they deliver a specific mechanism.**
    - SPARX delivers CBT skills. Tetris after trauma reduces intrusive memories. VR delivers exposure therapy.
    - EndeavorRx improved a lab attention test.
    - Effects are real but narrow, and attrition and self-selection inflate some headline numbers. **[Strong–Moderate]**
11. **Transfer from avatars and fiction is real, small and fragile.**
    - Proteus effects (behavior shifting to match one's avatar) average about r = .22–.26 across 46 studies.
    - Aged "future-self" avatars nudge saving choices.
    - VR perspective-taking changed behavior more than self-reported empathy.
    - "Literary fiction improves theory of mind" failed a high-powered replication. Fiction raises empathy only when readers are transported. **[Mixed]**
12. **The field has moved to open, telemetry-based, preregistered science.** Open Legend (AGPL engine, first-party telemetry, in-game prompts) is well placed to measure and publish wellbeing outcomes rather than engagement. **[Interp]**

## 2. Detailed findings

### 2.1 Does playtime affect wellbeing? The Oxford telemetry program

**Johannes, Vuorre & Przybylski (2021), *Royal Society Open Science*** ([PDF](https://www.niklasjohannes.com/uploads/Johannes%20et%20al.%20(2021)%20-%20Video%20game%20play.pdf); [journal](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/8/2/202049/95851/Video-game-play-is-positively-correlated-with-well))
- First study to pair surveys with publisher play logs:
  - EA's *Plants vs. Zombies: Battle for Neighborville*: 518 respondents, 471 matched to logs, 0.21% response rate.
  - Nintendo's *Animal Crossing: New Horizons*: 6,011 respondents, 2,756 matched.
- Measures: SPANE (affect) and PENS (player experience).
- Results:
  - Small positive association between logged play and wellbeing (slopes as extracted: ~0.10 for PvZ and ~0.06 for ACNH per 10 hours over two weeks).
  - Autonomy, relatedness and enjoyment predicted wellbeing independently of time.
  - Extrinsic motivation (pressure) predicted lower wellbeing.
- Quote: "when players only feel extrinsic motivation and feel pressured to play, play time might have negative effects on well-being."
- Cross-sectional with tiny response rates. **[Moderate]**

**Vuorre, Johannes, Magnusson & Przybylski (2022), *RSOS* 9:220411** ([journal](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/9/7/220411/96718/Time-spent-playing-video-games-is-unlikely-to); [OII](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/major-new-study-finds-little-evidence-for-causal-connection-between-well-being-and-video-game-playing/))
- 38,935 players, telemetry from seven games over ~6 weeks.
  - Game list recalled as ACNH, Apex Legends, EVE Online, Forza Horizon 4, Gran Turismo Sport, Outriders, The Crew 2 (not re-verified).
- Result: little evidence connecting playtime to wellbeing ([Vuorre list](https://vuorre.com/publications)).
- The oft-quoted line that players would need ~10 more hours a day to notice a change is **unverified wording**. **[Strong]**

**Registered report (Ballou, Sewall, Ratcliffe, Zendle, Tokarchuk & Deterding 2024)** ([summary](https://nickballou.com/publication/2024-ballou-et-al-registered/))
- 414 Xbox players, 2,036 surveys, 12 weeks.
- Within-person links were "not practically significant—even the largest associations were unlikely to register a perceptible impact on a player's wellbeing." **[Strong]**

**Nintendo study (Ballou, Vuorre, Hakman, Magnusson & Przybylski 2025, *RSOS* 12:241174)** ([journal](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsos.241174); [OII](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/its-quality-not-quantity-that-predicts-gamers-wellbeing-new-study-finds/))
- 703 casual adult Switch players, 150 titles, 140,000+ logged hours.
  - Over half logged no play in the prior two weeks; the top 10% averaged ~60 min/day.
- Hours were negligibly related to wellbeing, life satisfaction, affect or depression.
- Perceived value and "gaming life fit" did predict wellbeing.
- Ballou: "players who perceived gaming as beneficial to their lives reported higher overall mental well-being, regardless of how many hours they played."

**Open Play dataset (Ballou et al. 2025)** ([blog](https://nickballou.com/blog/open-play/); [site](https://digital-wellbeing.github.io/open-play/))
- ~3,600 players across Switch, Steam and Xbox; up to 43 months of history; 1.5M hours across 10,475 titles; ~23,000 surveys; released CC0.
- Headlines:
  - "individual variation dwarfs demographic differences."
  - Basic psychological needs "turn out to be weak predictors of actual gameplay behavior."
- Vuorre's list says ~2.0K individuals, likely a different inclusion rule.

**"All BANG, little buck" (2026 preprint)** ([summary](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-all-bang/))
- 11,000 daily observations from 555 US players, plus 102,000 PowerWash sessions from ~9,000 players.
- Need satisfaction tracked how play felt, but showed "weak or null associations with short-term gaming behavior, including subsequent play, session length, and return latency."
- What makes play feel good is not what brings people back. **[Moderate; preprint]**

**"Not all play is equal" (Hakman, Vuorre & Przybylski 2026 preprint)** ([PDF](https://vuorre.com/bibliography/files/HTDFPRRY/hakman-etal_2026_not-all-play-is-equal-in-game-player-be.pdf))
- 412 PvZ players clustered as Casual PvE (19.9%), Elite all-rounders (58%) and Casual PvP (22.1%).
- Compared with Casual PvE players, Casual PvP players reported:
  - lower wellbeing (b = −0.91, 95% CI −1.48 to −0.33)
  - lower enjoyment (b = −0.47).
- Cross-sectional, so the direction of causation is unknown. **[Weak–Moderate]**

**Confounds (Ballou, Földes, Hakman & Przybylski, CHI 2026)** ([summary](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-breakups/); [preprint](https://osf.io/2v73d))
- Title: "Ill Health and Grief Fuel Gaming, While Lethargy Slows It."
- 987 responses from 393 players identified 17 life factors that move both gaming and wellbeing.
- In Xbox logs, social strain and work stress *increased* play; lethargic illness decreased it.
- Heavy play is often a signal that life got harder, not a cause.

**PowerWash Simulator in-game sampling**
- The research edition prompted players "at most six times per hour," at least 5 minutes apart, all skippable.
  - Measures: mood, enjoyment, focus, autonomy, competence, immersion.
  - 11,080 players in 39 countries ([*Scientific Data* 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02530-3)).
- Across 162,325 mood reports in 67,328 sessions (8,695 players), mood rose on average during play (+0.034 on a 0–1 scale); ~72.1% of players are estimated to benefit.
  - Source: Vuorre, Ballou, Hakman, Magnusson & Przybylski 2024, *Games: Research and Practice* ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3659464); [Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-09-25-new-study-reveals-positive-mood-changes-during-video-game-play)). **[Strong]**

**Related results**
- **Content effects are small.** Six weeks of Apex Legends/Outriders telemetry from 2,580 players showed no measurable rise in aggressive affect (Johannes et al. 2023, *Collabra*; [Vuorre list](https://vuorre.com/publications)).
- **Playtime caps don't obviously work.** After China's 2019 limits for minors, >7 billion hours of telemetry showed "no credible evidence for overall reduction in the prevalence of heavy playtime following the implementation of regulations" ([Zendle et al. 2023](https://nickballou.com/publication/2023-zendle-et-al-noevidencechina/)).
- **Thirteen proposed mechanisms (Ballou et al. 2025, *Technology, Mind, and Behavior*)** ([paper](https://tmb.apaopen.org/pub/wyvpykp0/release/1); [OII](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/13-ways-gaming-affects-your-mental-health-for-better-and-worse/)):
  - **Mixed:** stress relief; autonomy/belonging/mastery; social health; harmonious vs. obsessive passion.
  - **Positive:** nostalgia; exercise; identity exploration; cognition; therapeutic games.
  - **Negative:** displacement; financial harms (loot boxes); gaming disorder; sexualization.

### 2.2 The strongest causal evidence: Japan's console lotteries

**Egami, Rahman et al. (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour*** ([paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01948-y); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/a-unique-event-in-japan-gave-scientists-unprecedented-insight-into-gamings-mental-health-effects/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/08/240827105004.htm))
- **Design.** Pandemic console shortages led retailers to allocate Switch and PS5 by lottery, which randomizes ownership among entrants. 97,602 survey responses (ages 10–69, Dec 2020–Mar 2022); 8,192 lottery participants analysed.
- **Effect of winning (intention-to-treat):**
  - Switch: distress (K6) −0.18 SD.
  - PS5: distress −0.08 SD; life satisfaction +0.15 SD.
- **Effect of actually owning (instrumental variable):** Switch 0.60 SD; PS5 0.12 SD (distress) and 0.23 SD (life satisfaction).
- **Dose.** Winners played ~0.5 h/day more; benefits "diminished when the gaming duration exceeded 3 h per day."
- **Who benefited.** Switch helped younger people more. PS5 helped adolescents and women less, and hardcore gamers and men more. Initially distressed people gained more.
- **Limits.** COVID context, 59.3% response rate, self-report.
- Egami: "The stereotype that video games are particularly harmful to children is not supported by our data."

### 2.3 The Goldilocks hypothesis and its critiques

- **Przybylski & Weinstein (2017, *Psychological Science*)** ([paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797616678438); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/01/170113085940.htm))
  - 120,115 English 15-year-olds.
  - Moderate use showed no negative link. Above weekday "tipping points" associations turned modestly negative: games 1h40m; smartphones 1h57m; videos 3h41m; computers 4h17m. Weekend thresholds were higher.
  - Even past the thresholds, the negative associations were about a third as strong as the positive associations of breakfast or regular sleep.
- **Orben & Przybylski (2019, *Nature Human Behaviour*)** ([paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0506-1); [Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/informatics/news/screen-time-affects-adolescent-wellbeing-but-only-04-of-it-313951))
  - Specification-curve analysis (testing every defensible way of analysing the data) across UK/US datasets (>300,000 young people).
  - Technology use explained ≤0.4% of wellbeing, similar to eating potatoes. Bullying was 4.3× more negative; marijuana 2.7×.
  - Orben: "we found over 600 million possible ways to analyse the data."
- **Critique.** Twenge, Haidt and colleagues argue heavy *social media* use shows larger harms, especially for girls ([Acta Psychologica 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691822000270); title verified, not fetched).
- **[Interp]** Goldilocks thresholds are cross-sectional curve-fits, not dose prescriptions. Egami's ~3 h plateau is loosely consistent; the telemetry nulls say context outweighs dose.

### 2.4 Self-Determination Theory: satisfaction, frustration, beneficence

**Foundations**
- **Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006, *Motivation and Emotion*)** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8))
  - Four studies. In-game autonomy and competence predicted enjoyment, preference and pre-to-post-play changes in wellbeing.
  - Intuitive controls and presence supported those needs.
  - In an MMO sample, all three needs independently predicted enjoyment and future play.
- *Glued to Games* (Rigby & Ryan 2011; not fetched) brought this PENS view to designers.

**BANG model (Ballou & Deterding, *Interacting with Computers*)** ([paper](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/advance-article/doi/10.1093/iwc/iwae042/7816687))
- Core claim: "mental health outcomes of game use are in large part mediated by the motivational quality of play and the extent to which play quantity and quality lead to need satisfaction or frustration."
- Two routes: need-satisfying sessions accumulate, or excessive play displaces other need-satisfying life activities.
- "Life fit" moderates displacement.

**The negative side**
- **BANGS scale (Ballou, Denisova, Ryan, Rigby & Deterding 2024)** ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581924000739)) splits each need into satisfaction and frustration: "need satisfaction and frustration are separate experiences that can coexist to a degree."
- **Grounded theory (Ballou & Deterding, CHI PLAY '23; 12 interviews)** ([summary](https://nickballou.com/publication/2022-ballou-deterding-need-frus/)): "Psychological need frustration—experiences like failure, loneliness, or coercion—is emerging as a promising explanation for why people disengage with games and other entertainment media, and how media may induce dysregulated use and ill-being."

**Restorative play (Tyack, Wyeth & Johnson, CHI 2020)** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376332))
- 65 participants did a competence-frustrating task, then played a competence-satisfying game.
- "Competence, affect, and vitality improved during play, and in-game experiences of need frustration were observed to effectively predict post-play negative affect." **[Moderate]**

**Compensation in crisis (Ballou, Deterding, Iacovides & Helsby 2022)** ([summary](https://nickballou.com/publication/2022-ballou-et-al-do-people/))
- Preregistered, n = 285, May 2020.
- Qualitatively, gaming was often "a successful means of replenishing particular needs," but backfired when players dismissed it as "unreal."
- Quantitatively: "need satisfaction in daily life positively correlated with need satisfaction in games" — rich get richer.

**Animal Crossing interviews (Yee & Sng 2022; n = 17)** ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800683/full))
- Needs met through freedom, creativity, friends, even parasocial ties to characters, and achievements ("I feel like I get a lot of my sense of self-worth from [in-game] achievements").
- But: "daily tasks of watering the flowers…became something that was not volitional."

**Beneficence (a sense of positive impact on others)**
- **Martela & Ryan (2016)** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-016-9552-z); [Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/prosocial-behavior-increases-well-being-and-vitality-even-without)):
  - "participants (n = 76) were invited to play a simple computer game, where half were made aware of a chance to have an anonymous prosocial impact through gameplay."
  - The prosocial group gained positive affect and meaningfulness, marginal vitality, and better Stroop performance, via autonomy and competence satisfaction.
  - The game is often described as Freerice-style; not verified.
- **Martela & Ryan (2020)** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11031-019-09800-x)):
  - Beneficence *frustration* effects vanish once the three core needs are controlled.
  - They propose "basic wellness enhancers, the satisfaction of which may improve well-being, but the neglect or frustration of which might not uniquely impact ill-being."
  - **[Interp]** Helping others is a booster, not a requirement.

### 2.5 Escapism, coping, and passion

- **Two kinds of escapism.** Stenseng, Rise & Kraft (2012, *Leisure Sciences*) ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01490400.2012.633849)) split escapism into *self-suppression* (avoiding negative thoughts and feelings) and *self-expansion* (seeking positive experience and new facets of self). Extended to gaming and streaming by Stenseng, Falch-Madsen & Hygen (2021) ([record](https://journals.scholarsportal.info/details/26896567/v10i0003/319_atttoeidgaos.xml); not fetched).
- **Meta-analysis (Hussain et al. 2021)** ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958821000294))
  - 27 studies, N = 28,893.
  - Escapism relates to both negative (Z = 0.46) and positive (Z = 0.34) outcomes, but "negative outcomes outweigh the positive outcomes."
  - More harmful in Western samples, more positive in East Asian ones.
- **Post-gaming return (Strojny & Strojny 2026, *Frontiers in Psychiatry*; N = 259 and N = 193)** ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1735108/full))
  - Self-expansion was "not significantly related to IGD or psychological maladjustment."
  - Self-suppression was "positively associated with depression vulnerability, negative affect, Internet Gaming Disorder (IGD)."
  - New construct, post-gaming return (PGR): "a state reflecting successful psychological transition from gameplay to real life." Including it cut the self-suppression→disorder path from β = .42 to .16. PGR re-engagement predicted vitality (β = .48). **[Moderate; new]**
- **Compensatory use (Kardefelt-Winther 2014, *CHB*)** ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563213004093))
  - The paper notes: "Despite much empirical data being collected and analyzed clear results and conclusions are surprisingly absent."
  - Negative life situations motivate going online to relieve bad feelings, with outcomes that can be good (relief) and bad (neglect of offline life).
- **Passion.** The OII 13-mechanisms summary says harmonious passion benefits wellbeing, while obsessive passion driven by unmet needs harms it. The primary Przybylski et al. (2009) paper ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/cpb.2009.0083)) was rate-limited. Recalled (not re-verified): obsessive engagement went with lower daily-life need satisfaction and post-play tension; harmonious engagement with post-play energy.
- **Coping.** In lockdown-era Animal Crossing (n = 1,053), "increased gaming and related activities was predictive of higher anxiety," read as coping ([Lewis et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.627350/full)).

### 2.6 Gaming disorder, loneliness, sleep

**Definition (WHO ICD-11, 6C51)** ([WHO Q&A](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/addictive-behaviours-gaming-disorder))
- Three features:
  - "impaired control over gaming"
  - "increasing priority given to gaming over other activities to the extent that gaming takes precedence over other interests and daily activities"
  - "continuation or escalation of gaming despite the occurrence of negative consequences"
- Requires significant impairment, normally for at least 12 months.
- WHO: "Studies suggest that gaming disorder affects only a small proportion of people who engage in digital- or video-gaming activities."

**Prevalence**
- **Stevens et al. (2021, *ANZJP*)** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33028074/))
  - 53 studies, 226,247 people, 17 countries: "3.05% (confidence interval: [2.38, 3.91])."
  - 1.96% with stricter sampling; ~2.5:1 male:female.
  - Adolescent samples, low cut-offs and small samples inflate rates.
  - A 2023 corrigendum exists ([link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00048674221137011)); contents unread.
- **Przybylski, Weinstein & Murayama (2017, *American Journal of Psychiatry*)** ([page](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2016.16020224)): four national samples (US, UK, Canada, Germany) gave 1.0%, 0.5%, 0.7% and 0.3%, lower than gambling disorder in UK comparisons.

**Controversy**
- **Aarseth et al. (2017, *Journal of Behavioral Addictions* 6(3); 26 authors)** ([UEA](https://research-portal.uea.ac.uk/en/publications/scholars-open-debate-paper-on-the-world-health-organization-icd-1/))
  - Cited "low quality of the research base" and warned of "moral panics around the harm of video gaming."
  - Urged removal "to avoid a waste of public health resources as well as to avoid causing harm to healthy video gamers around the world."
- **Counter-view.** Griffiths et al., "Problematic gaming exists and is an example of disordered gaming" ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319166072_Problematic_gaming_exists_and_is_an_example_of_disordered_gaming_Commentary_on_Scholars'_open_debate_paper_on_the_World_Health_Organization_ICD-11_Gaming_Disorder_proposal_Aarseth_et_al)).
- **Net:** severe dysregulation exists but is rare; screening scales over-diagnose.

**Loneliness runs both ways**
- **Lemmens, Valkenburg & Peter (2011)** ([PDF](https://www.pattivalkenburg.nl/images/artikelen_pdf/2011_lemmens_Valkenburg_Peter_psychosocial_pathological_gaming.pdf)), 543 Dutch adolescents over 6 months: "Lower psychosocial well-being can be considered an antecedent of pathological gaming among adolescent gamers. Our analyses further indicated that loneliness was also a consequence of pathological gaming."
- **Three-wave cohort, 1,332 Chinese adolescents (*JMIR Serious Games* 2026)** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13161836/)):
  - Loneliness and gaming-disorder symptoms predicted each other.
  - Physical activity predicted less later loneliness and fewer symptoms.

**Sleep: the everyday harm**
- **Roane, SLEEP 2016** ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160613144656.htm)): 963 US gamers delayed bedtime on 36% of gaming nights, by 101 minutes on average; 67% reported lost sleep.
- **De Rosa et al. (2024) review of 26 studies** ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1389945724004362))
  - "habitual and/or casual use was not associated with poor sleep."
  - "playing an arousing VG before sleep delays sleep onset with possible alteration of sleep structure."
  - Non-arousing, cognitively challenging play was associated with *better* sleep continuity.
- **Bedtime procrastination (Kroese et al. 2014)** ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00611/full)): "Failing to go to bed at the intended time, while no external circumstances prevent a person from doing so." It correlated with weaker self-regulation (r = −0.52) and predicted insufficient sleep.
- **Pending.** An Oxford registered report (1,000 US and 1,000 UK adults; Switch, Xbox, Steam and Android logs) is testing late-night gaming against sleep and wellbeing, with evening chronotype (being a "night owl") as a moderator ([Stage 1](https://vuorre.com/bibliography/files/TS7BJYB5/ballou-etal_2025_psychological-wellbeing,-sleep,-and-vide.pdf)).

### 2.7 Social play

**Games as third places**
- **Steinkuehler & Williams (2006, *JCMC* 11(4))** ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/4/885/4617703); [PDF](https://mackenty.org/images/uploads/social_space_games.pdf.pdf)):
  - "By providing spaces for social interaction and relationships beyond the workplace and home, MMOs have the capacity to function as one form of a new 'third place' for informal sociability."
  - They mainly build *bridging* capital: "social relationships that, while not usually providing deep emotional support, typically function to expose the individual to a diversity of worldviews."
  - Provocation: "Perhaps it is not that contemporary media use has led to a decline in civic and social engagement, but rather that a decline in civic and social engagement has led to retribalization through contemporary media."
- **Williams (2006)** ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/2/593/4617735)) built scales measuring bridging and bonding capital, online and offline.

**Friendships made in games**
- **Cole & Griffiths (2007)** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/new-study-dispels-asocial-online-gamer-myth)), ~1,000 MMORPG players:
  - 75% made good friends in-game; almost half had met online friends in person.
  - 40% discussed sensitive issues with online friends rather than real-life friends.
  - 81% played with real-life friends or family. **[Weak; self-selected]**
- **Trepte, Reinecke & Juechems (2012)** ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563211002706)), N = 811 esports-league players: online bonding and bridging capital both predicted *offline* social support, with proximity and offline meetings as the conversion path.

**NPCs vs. real players (Lewis, Trojovsky & Jameson 2021; n = 1,053)** ([paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.627350/full))
- "increased visits to another island was associated with lower levels of loneliness"; having a visitor went with lower loneliness (β = −0.14).
- "artificial interactions (e.g., interactions with island characters) did not improve feelings of social isolation."
- **[Moderate; cross-sectional. Directly relevant to AI residents.]**
- See also Pearce et al. (2022), "Families Playing Animal Crossing Together" ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/15554120211056125); title only).

**Place and belonging.** Pokémon GO fostered "a sense of belonging, linked to a sense of place" and "conversations with strangers and strengthening social ties" (Vella et al. 2019; 15 interviews and 880 forum posts; [Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1555412017719973)).

**Older adults and intergenerational play** (titles verified, results **not re-verified**)
- Titles: "Games for a better life" (Wii in long-term care, [RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234785121_Games_for_a_better_life_effects_of_playing_Wii_games_on_the_well-being_of_seniors_in_a_long-term_care_facility)); "Social Benefits of Playing Wii Bowling for Older Adults" ([RG](https://researchgate.net/publication/282351117_Social_Benefits_of_Playing_Wii_Bowling_for_Older_Adults)); a BMC Geriatrics Wii protocol ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-015-0107-z)); Chua et al. 2013 "Let's play together" ([RG](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257253002_Let's_play_together_Effects_of_video-game_play_on_intergenerational_perceptions_among_youth_and_elderly_participants)); "Play It Again, Grandma" ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-92034-4_39)); Osmanovic & Pecchioni 2016 ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1555412015602819)).
- Recalled pattern: small, short trials report less loneliness and better mood than TV or other passive activities, and co-play improves each generation's attitudes toward the other. Samples are small and novelty effects likely. **[Weak–Moderate]**

### 2.8 Exergames and location-based games

**Pokémon GO**
- **Althoff, White & Horvitz (2016, *JMIR*)** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02085); [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2016/12/e315/)), 32,000 wearable users:
  - "particularly engaged users increasing their activity by 1473 steps a day on average, a more than 25% increase" over 30 days.
  - An estimated ~144 billion extra US steps.
  - Gains appeared "across men and women of all ages, weight status, and prior activity levels."
- **Howe et al. (2016, *BMJ*)** ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270)), 1,182 people aged 18–35:
  - +955 steps a day in week 1 (95% CI 697–1,213).
  - "The association was, however, moderate and no longer observed after six weeks."
- **Engagement collapse.** One widely reported estimate says the US player base fell 79% from the July 2016 peak by mid-September ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Go)). **[Secondary]**

**Evidence reviews**
- **Peng, Lin & Crouse (2011) meta-analysis** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/cyber.2010.0578)): active video games raise heart rate, VO2 and energy expenditure, and "may facilitate light- to moderate-intensity physical activity."
- **Peng, Crouse & Lin (2013) review** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1090198112444956)): "All laboratory studies demonstrated that AVGs are capable of providing light-to-moderate intensity physical activity. However, only three interventions supported AVGs as an effective tool to significantly increase physical activity or exercise attendance." **[Strong]**

**Commercial titles [Secondary]**
- **Ring Fit Adventure:** 15.38M copies by March 2023 and pandemic sell-outs; one chronic low-back-pain study ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_Fit_Adventure)).
- **Zombies, Run!** (2012; Six to Start with Naomi Alderman) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zombies,_Run!)):
  - An audio story wrapped around running; 750k+ players by 2014.
  - Only 15–20% of purchasers still active by May 2015.
  - Small studies show better intentions to keep exercising but no significant fitness gains.
- No verified Beat Saber data was found this session.

### 2.9 Serious and therapeutic games

**SPARX (Merry et al. 2012, *BMJ*)** ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/344/bmj.e2598))
- 187 adolescents aged 12–19 at 24 New Zealand primary-care sites; non-inferiority trial against usual care.
- Depression scores (CDRS-R) fell 10.32 vs. 7.59 (per protocol).
- Remission: 43.7% vs. 26.4% (P = 0.030).
- Conclusion: SPARX "could be used to address some of the unmet demand for treatment." **[Strong]**

**SuperBetter (Roepke et al. 2015)** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/g4h.2014.0046))
- 283 depressed adults.
- Depression (CES-D) effect sizes: d = 0.67 at post-test, 1.05 at follow-up.
- The authors warn the effects "should be interpreted cautiously in light of high attrition rates and the motivated, self-selected sample" (wording as extracted). **[Moderate]**

**EndeavorRx** ([FDA De Novo](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EndeavorRx))
- The first game-based prescription device, authorized in June 2020 for ages 8–12.
- STARS-ADHD trial: 348 children.
  - Attention test (TOVA API): 0.93 vs. 0.03 (p = 0.006).
  - Parent-rated scales only "numerically favored" the game.
  - Adverse events in 6.7% (e.g., frustration).
- **Lesson:** FDA-grade evidence can rest on a narrow lab measure.

**Re-Mission (Kato et al. 2008, *Pediatrics*)** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re-Mission))
- 375 cancer patients aged 13–29 at 34 centers.
- Improved treatment adherence (measured by blood metabolites and electronic pill caps), self-efficacy and knowledge.
- **Cole et al. (2012)** ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0033909)), 57 participants: "mesolimbic activation was significantly more pronounced in the active playgroup than in the passive exposure control group." Doing beats watching.

**Tetris for intrusive memories**
- **Holmes et al. (2009)** ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0004153)), 40 participants: Tetris after a trauma film "reduces unwanted, involuntary memory flashbacks to that traumatic film, leaving deliberate memory recall of the event intact."
- **Iyadurai et al. (2018)** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/mp201723)), 71 emergency-department crash survivors: 62% fewer intrusions in week 1 (8.73 vs. 23.26, d = 0.67).
- **Iyadurai et al. (2023)** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-023-02578-0)), 86 ICU staff, delivered remotely: median week-4 intrusions 1 vs. 10 (IRR 0.31), plus lower PTSD, insomnia, anxiety and burnout. **[Strong–Moderate]**

**MindLight** (children's anxiety game): **not re-verified**. Recalled: RCTs showed anxiety reductions, but a commercial control game did comparably in one trial, and the game matched a CBT program in another.

**VR exposure therapy** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_reality_therapy); **[Secondary]**)
- Freeman et al. (2018): automated VR for fear of heights; most participants no longer considered themselves afraid.
- gameChange (2022): reduced agoraphobic avoidance in people with psychosis.
- Bravemind (combat PTSD): PTSD checklist scores fell from 54.4 to 35.6 in a 20-patient trial.
- Practicing the real situation is the best-supported route by which simulation changes real behavior.

### 2.10 Behavioral transfer: avatars, VR, prosocial games, fiction

**The Proteus effect** (behavior shifting to match one's avatar)
- **Yee, Bailenson & Ducheneaut (2009)** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0093650208330254)):
  - Avatar height and attractiveness predicted World of Warcraft performance.
  - Taller-avatar users later negotiated more aggressively *face to face*.
  - Mechanism: "people infer their expected behaviors and attitudes from observing their avatar's appearance."
- **Ratan et al. (2020) meta-analysis of 46 studies:** "a small-but-approaching-medium effect size that was relatively consistent (between 0.22 and 0.26)" (wording from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteus_effect)).
- Negative variants exist (e.g., increased aggression when using dark-skinned avatars in intergroup settings). **[Moderate]**

**Future self (Hershfield et al. 2011)** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkr.48.SPL.S23))
- Four studies: "allowing people to interact with age-progressed renderings of themselves will cause them to allocate more resources to the future."
- The oft-cited "about twice as much to retirement" figure is not re-verified. Lab choices, not real savings. **[Moderate]**

**VR perspective-taking (Herrera et al. 2018)** ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0204494))
- N = 117 (8-week follow-up) and N = 439.
- "participants in both conditions reported feeling empathetic and connected to the homeless at similar rates."
- But significantly more VR participants signed an affordable-housing petition, and their positive attitudes lasted longer. **[Moderate]**

**Superpowers (Rosenberg, Baughman & Bailenson 2013)** ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0055003))
- 60 students flew like Superman or rode a helicopter in VR. When the experimenter then "accidentally" spilled pens, flyers helped faster and picked up more; all six non-helpers had ridden the helicopter.
- "having the 'superpower' of flight leads to greater helping behavior in the real world, regardless of how participants used that power." **[Weak–Moderate]**

**Embodied self-counselling (Osimo et al. 2015)** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13899))
- 22 men alternated between their own avatar and Freud's body to counsel themselves.
- Mood improved more with the Freud counsellor than with a self-representation.
- "this form of embodied perspective taking can lead to sufficient detachment from habitual ways of thinking about personal problems." **[Weak; tiny sample, powerful pattern]**

**"Virtually being Einstein" (Banakou et al. 2018)** ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.00917/full))
- 30 men: "Einstein body participants performed better on a cognitive task than the Normal body… with the improvement greatest for those with low self-esteem."
- Implicit age bias also fell. **[Weak]**

**Prosocial games**
- **Gentile et al. (2009)** ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167209333045)): correlational (Singapore), longitudinal (Japan) and experimental (US) studies claimed "robust evidence of a prosocial game content effect."
- **Greitemeyer & Osswald (2010):** playing Lemmings increased helping (not re-verified).
- **Replication:** I found no verified large preregistered replication. The violent-games literature has shifted toward small or null effects in telemetry and preregistered work. **[Weak/Mixed]**

**Fiction and empathy**
- **Kidd & Castano (2013, *Science*)** claimed literary fiction improves theory of mind.
- **Camerer et al. (2018)** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0399-z)) replicated 13 of 21 (62%) Nature/Science social-science findings, at ~50% of original effect sizes. Kidd & Castano's was among the failures.
  - The authors' reply ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0408-2)) concedes the far better-powered replication found no effect, and argues their later experiments are the better test.
  - Panero et al. (2016) also failed to replicate (not fetched).
- **Bal & Veltkamp (2013)** ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0055341)): "empathy was influenced over a period of one week for people who read a fictional story, but only when they were emotionally transported into the story." Low transportation *decreased* empathy.
- **Vezzali et al. (2015):** three small studies linking Harry Potter reading and identification with better attitudes toward immigrants, gay people and refugees (not re-verified).
- **Raymond Mar's** work stresses that effects depend on content and engagement.
- **[Mixed]** One-shot exposures rarely change traits; sustained, transporting engagement plausibly shifts attitudes a little.

### 2.11 Recovery, cozy games, grief

**Games for recovery from stress**
- **Reinecke (2009, *Journal of Media Psychology*)**, "Games and Recovery" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1027/1864-1105.21.3.126)); abstract unavailable.
  - Paraphrase, not re-verified: games are used systematically to recover after work stress.
  - This maps onto the four recovery experiences (detachment, relaxation, mastery, control).
- Oxford's PowerWash work supports this. Przybylski: "Gaming – for most people – is a recovery activity that helps manage day-to-day stresses."

**Coziness (Project Horseshoe 2017; Short, Cook et al.)** ([report](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm))
- Coziness = safety, abundance and softness:
  - "A cozy game has an absence of danger and risk."
  - "Nothing is lacking, pressing or imminent."
  - Cozy games send "strong aesthetic signals" of low stress.
- Recommends opt-in social permission structures, small persistent groups and codes of conduct.
- Warns: "Forcing cozy causes it to fall apart"; too much coziness can suppress dissent and exclude newcomers. **[Design practice]**

**Grief**
- CHI 2026: grief and ill health "fuel gaming."
- Harrer's open-access *Games and Bereavement* (2018) studies how games depict loss (not read).
- **[Interp]** Games can host grief well, but grieving players are vulnerable players.

## 3. Nuggets and quotes

- **NPCs vs. other players.** "artificial interactions (e.g., interactions with island characters) did not improve feelings of social isolation," while "increased visits to another island was associated with lower levels of loneliness." ([Lewis et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.627350/full))
- **"People feel good playing video games."** Nick Ballou, on 162,325 in-game mood reports ([Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-09-25-new-study-reveals-positive-mood-changes-during-video-game-play)).
- **Three-hour plateau.** Benefits "diminished when the gaming duration exceeded 3 h per day." ([Egami et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01948-y))
- **Cooperation over competition.** At equal playtime, casual PvP players reported lower wellbeing than cooperative PvE players (b = −0.91) ([Hakman et al. 2026](https://vuorre.com/bibliography/files/HTDFPRRY/hakman-etal_2026_not-all-play-is-equal-in-game-player-be.pdf)).
- **Feeling good ≠ returning.** Need satisfaction has "weak or null associations with short-term gaming behavior, including subsequent play, session length, and return latency." ([All BANG](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-all-bang/))
- **Rich get richer.** Daily-life and in-game need satisfaction correlate positively; games dismissed as "unreal" don't compensate ([Ballou et al. 2022](https://nickballou.com/publication/2022-ballou-et-al-do-people/)).
- **Hard times drive play.** Social strain and work stress increase gaming ([CHI 2026](https://nickballou.com/publication/2026-ballou-et-al-breakups/)).
- **Chores kill joy.** "daily tasks of watering the flowers…became something that was not volitional." ([Yee & Sng 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.800683/full))
- **The exit matters.** Accounting for post-gaming return cut the self-suppression→disorder link from β = .42 to .16 ([Strojny & Strojny 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1735108/full)).
- **101 minutes.** Average bedtime delay on gaming nights ([Roane 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160613144656.htm)).
- **Potatoes.** Technology use explains ≤0.4% of adolescent wellbeing, about the same as eating potatoes ([Orben & Przybylski](https://www.technologynetworks.com/informatics/news/screen-time-affects-adolescent-wellbeing-but-only-04-of-it-313951)).
- **Caps don't cap.** China's minor playtime limits showed "no credible evidence for overall reduction in the prevalence of heavy playtime." ([Zendle et al. 2023](https://nickballou.com/publication/2023-zendle-et-al-noevidencechina/))
- **Six weeks.** Pokémon GO's step bump was "no longer observed after six weeks." ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270))
- **Doing beats watching.** Active play lit up reward circuitry more than watching the same footage ([Cole et al. 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0033909)).
- **Tetris in the ICU.** Median week-4 intrusive memories: 1 vs. 10 ([Iyadurai et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-023-02578-0)).
- **Counsel yourself as Freud.** Mood improved more than when advising yourself as yourself ([Osimo et al. 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13899)).
- **Flight → kindness.** All six people who refused to help had been helicopter passengers ([Rosenberg et al. 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0055003)).
- **Transportation or nothing.** Fiction raised empathy only in transported readers; the rest dropped ([Bal & Veltkamp 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0055341)).
- **Anonymous good deeds via a game** raised positive affect and meaning (n = 76; [Martela & Ryan 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-016-9552-z)).
- **Confiding online.** 40% of MMORPG players discussed sensitive issues with online friends rather than real-life ones ([Cole & Griffiths 2007](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/new-study-dispels-asocial-online-gamer-myth)).

## 4. Design implications for Open Legend

### A. Measure wellbeing, not engagement

1. **Wellbeing scorecard instead of time KPIs.**
   - Track in-session mood change via opt-in, skippable prompts (≤6/hour, ≥5 minutes apart, PowerWash-style).
   - Measure need satisfaction *and* frustration with a BANGS short form.
   - Ask about perceived value and gaming life fit ("Is Open Legend a good part of your life right now?").
   - Add a connectedness item.
   - Treat playtime as a neutral diagnostic, never a target. (§2.1)
2. **Log what people do, not just how long.** Track the balance of cooperative vs. competitive play, conversations with humans vs. residents, creating vs. grinding, and the share of late-night play. (Hakman 2026)
3. **Treat heavy or late-play spikes as care signals, not sales signals.**
   - Strain, work stress, grief and illness drive play (CHI 2026).
   - Let the Narrator or a trusted resident check in gently and point to real-world resources.
   - Never trigger retention offers.
4. **Research in the open.** Partner with independent academics, preregister, share de-identified data (the Open Play and PowerWash model), and publish nulls. The AGPL engine adds credibility.

### B. Protect autonomy; remove obligation

5. **Keep "the world pauses when you leave" absolute, including in multiplayer.**
   - No decay, streaks, daily-login rewards, or residents guilt-tripping absent players.
   - If shared worlds keep running, give absent players' households a "steward" mode.
   - Residents should welcome players back without reproach.
   - Why: pressure predicts lower wellbeing, and chores become "not volitional." (Johannes 2021; Yee & Sng 2022)
6. **Session intentions and graceful endings.** An optional one-tap intention at the start (rest / create / connect / explore) and a Narrator "chapter close" at the end. Supports harmonious passion and life fit.
7. **A "return to reality" ritual.**
   - End each session with a short journal page that recaps the story.
   - Add one light bridge to real life: an idea to try, a person to message.
   - Keep it optional and non-preachy.
   - Post-gaming return appears to buffer self-suppression harms. (§2.5)

### C. Dose and sleep

8. **Respect diminishing returns without lockouts.** Use natural chapter breaks (a game day is ~24 real minutes at 60×) and a soft reflection around 2–3 hours. Self-set limits beat imposed caps, which didn't reduce heavy play in China. (Egami; Zendle)
9. **Opt-in "bedtime sync."**
   - In-world dusk arrives near the player's chosen bedtime: residents yawn and bank the fire.
   - Hold cliffhangers and high-arousal events (raids, combat, horror) until the next session; steer toward calm crafting.
   - The pause makes stopping free. (Roane; De Rosa; Kroese)

### D. AI residents as bridges, not substitutes

10. **Residents as social connectors.**
    - NPC contact didn't reduce loneliness in Animal Crossing; visiting other players did.
    - Residents should introduce players with overlapping inventions or interests, invite them to shared events (the harvest fair, barn raisings), and suggest bringing a real friend or relative to the village.
    - Count human-to-human interaction as a first-class metric. (§2.7)
11. **Guard against substitution, especially for lonely players.**
    - Residents never compete with a player's real relationships, never express jealousy or exclusivity, and are honest about being AI when asked.
    - They should warmly ask about the player's real-life people.
    - Build a connection ladder: resident → co-op with other players → real contact.
    - Why: loneliness is both cause and consequence of problematic gaming, and compensation tends to be rich-get-richer. (§2.4, §2.6)
12. **Cooperative by default.**
    - Communal projects, shared inventions, collective defense.
    - Any Threewater March politics or PvP should be consensual and moderated, with residents modeling good norms.
    - Use Project Horseshoe's patterns: opt-in escalation, small persistent groups, codes of conduct. (Hakman 2026)
13. **Design for bonding, not just bridging.**
    - Family and household worlds.
    - Grandparent–grandchild villages with asynchronous in-world letters (the pause mechanic suits this).
    - Private "hearth" worlds for friend groups.
    - Optional real-life meetups around in-world festivals.
    - Offline contact is what converts online ties into real support. (Trepte 2012)

### E. Make escape expansive

14. **Favor self-expansion loops over numbing loops.** Center curiosity, mastery and making; the invention system is Open Legend's best asset here. Avoid endless grind and slot-machine rewards. When a pattern looks like self-suppression (e.g., long late solo sessions after a stated bad day), respond with reflection, not punishment. (§2.5)
15. **Use inventions to deliver competence and beneficence.**
    - When others reuse an invention, tell the inventor its concrete impact: "your cord jig helped three families finish nets before the storm."
    - Keep it informational, not a leaderboard. Beneficence enhances wellbeing, but extrinsic metrics can crowd out intrinsic motivation.
    - Frame failed recipe validations as learning, with a hint. (Martela & Ryan; Tyack 2020)

### F. Design for transfer on purpose

16. **Build near transfer, not hoped-for far transfer.** In the city world, offer optional rehearsal with resident feedback: a job interview, a hard talk with a landlord, asking for help, apologizing. Follow with a concrete real-world plan ("next time X happens, I'll do Y"). This mirrors what works in SPARX skill practice and VR exposure. (§2.9)
17. **"Counsel yourself" and "future self" features.**
    - Let a player swap perspective into a wise elder resident to advise themselves on a real dilemma, then swap back (Osimo 2015).
    - Let the Narrator write a letter from the player's aged character about long-term goals (Hershfield 2011).
    - Make both optional and evidence-humble, and never tie them to monetization.
18. **Proteus-aware roles.** Make roles that embody competence and care (healer, teacher, host, inventor) attractive by default. Avoid building the core loop around dominance or villainy (effects r ≈ .22–.26).
19. **Perspective-taking quests with optional follow-through.** Let players live a day as another resident (an elder, a newcomer, a struggling apprentice) with deep narrative transportation. Offer optional real-world actions, since behavior shifted more than self-reported empathy in Herrera 2018. Don't market "empathy gains."

### G. Health, ethics and money

20. **Plan for novelty decay** in any real-world activity hooks. Pokémon GO's average effect was gone by week 6 and 79% of US players left by mid-September 2016. Anchor activity in recurring social rituals, local places and story (e.g., audio walks with a resident, à la Zombies, Run!). (§2.8)
21. **No clinical claims without trials.** CBT-style reframing with a resident or gratitude prompts in the journal should be co-designed with researchers, with modest effects and attrition expected (SuperBetter). Trauma-memory protocols like Tetris are clinician territory. (§2.9)
22. **Handle aging, death and grief with care.**
    - Residents age fast at 60×. Use that to show whole life arcs (meaning, perspective) rather than to generate repeated loss.
    - Ritualize deaths with memorials, Narrator eulogies and keepsakes; offer warnings and opt-outs.
    - Never exploit loss for retention. Don't recreate real deceased people without careful, explicit consent. (CHI 2026) **[Interp]**
23. **Keep the marketplace clean.** No loot boxes or randomized paid rewards (a documented financial-harm pathway), no pay-to-skip-grind, and transparent spending. Fund creators for inventions others value, not for minutes of engagement.
24. **Calibrate coziness in the survival world.** Offer a gentle-needs mode so the core fantasy around the fire is safety, abundance and softness. Invite players in rather than coerce: "Forcing cozy causes it to fall apart." (§2.11)
25. **Favor agency over spectating.** Doing beats watching (Cole 2012). Keep player choices consequential so an AI simulation doesn't become an ant farm to observe; resident drama should invite participation, not replace it. **[Interp]**
