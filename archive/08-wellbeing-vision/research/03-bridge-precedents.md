**How to read this**

## 1. Ten headline takeaways

1. **Recurring rituals at a fixed time and place work best. One-off campaigns fade.**
   - The rituals that last:
     - parkrun had 2.3M participants, 309,220 volunteers and 126,593 events in 2024–25.
     - Geocaching's CITO cleanups have drawn 875,000+ attendees across 50,000+ events since 2002.
     - Friday Night Magic ran in about 6,000 stores by 2018.
     - Pokémon GO has held Community Day monthly since January 2018.
     - JR East's Pokémon stamp rally has run most summers since 1997.
   - They share four things: a fixed time, a known place, near-zero cost to join, and a token for showing up.
   - EVOKE was a 10-week alternate reality game (ARG) with 19,324 registrants. It produced ideas, but few of them lasted.

2. **Novelty gets people moving briefly. Social structure keeps them moving.**
   - Pokémon GO added 955 steps a day in week 1. By week 6 the gain was gone (BMJ, n=1,182).
   - Even highly engaged players gained only 1,473 steps a day over 30 days (Althoff et al., about 32,000 users).
   - Only 15–20% of Zombies, Run! buyers were still active by 2015.
   - The group formats lasted: raids, Community Days and clubs. Strava's clubs nearly quadrupled to 1M in 2025.

3. **Real science work scales under four conditions.** It fits the game's fiction, it is fun in itself, it earns in-game rewards, and a scientist is waiting for the results.
   - Borderlands Science had 4.5M players. Half a day of play produced 5× the data its predecessor, Phylo, gathered in 10 years.
   - EVE Online's Project Discovery collected 41.4M COVID-19 classifications within months.
   - Foldit players solved a protein structure in 10 days that had resisted solution for 15 years.
   - Each had a lab partner, a way to check quality by consensus, and a route to publication.

4. **Results become real when an institution receives them.**
   - Block by Block's Minecraft models became lit underpasses in Hanoi. In Kenya's Kalobeyei settlement they became solar lights, 500+ trees and a boda-boda (motorbike taxi) station.
   - The New York Public Library (NYPL) put a book players wrote overnight into its collection.
   - Boston Public Library published 12,247 abolitionist letters transcribed by volunteers.
   - The real bridge is a recipient with the authority and budget to act.

5. **Kindness mechanics work when kindness is cheap, mostly anonymous, needs no return, and is limited to narrow channels.**
   - Kind Words: 4M+ letters, with about 3% needing moderation.
   - Journey: players communicate only through musical chirps.
   - Sky: players befriend each other by gifting candles; 62% of 563 surveyed players were helped by veterans.
   - Death Stranding: strangers leave ladders for each other and exchange "likes".
   - Whether this carries over into offline behavior is barely measured. Most evidence is self-report.

6. **Roles and responsibility carry over to real life better than content does.**
   - parkrun volunteers report bigger happiness gains than runners (86% vs 79%).
   - About half of IBM's gamers believed leading guilds improved their real-world leadership.
   - Several games give players civic practice: EVE's elected player council, A Tale in the Desert's petition laws, and Eco's legislatures.
   - The evidence is self-report. It is promising, not proven.

7. **Communities outlive their game worlds, so letting them move and preserving their work supports wellbeing.**
   - About 10,000 players displaced when Uru closed in 2004 rebuilt their culture in There.com and Second Life.
   - "Team 0%" cleared about 25,000 unbeaten Super Mario Maker levels before the servers shut down in April 2024.
   - A Tale in the Desert is now run by a studio its players founded.
   - Naomi Alderman bought Zombies, Run! back from a crypto owner in November 2025.

8. **Money and obligation are the most corrosive bridges.**
   - Axie Infinity: daily players fell from 2.7M to about 250,000. Its SLP token lost more than 99%. Hackers stole $620M. Guild managers took up to 75% of players' earnings.
   - Roblox: the median creator earns about $1,500 a year, against an average of $33.9M for the top 10. It also faces child-safety settlements and 140+ lawsuits.
   - Gamified labor has been called an "electronic whip".
   - Streaks rely on guilt.
   - Once play becomes income or duty, both intrinsic motivation and safety erode.

9. **Location-based play pushes risk onto bodies, neighborhoods and sacred sites. Charging for remote play excludes people.**
   - Pokémon GO:
     - Crashes rose near PokéStops, with 2 deaths in one Indiana county and an extrapolated US cost of $2–7.3B.
     - People played at the US Holocaust Memorial Museum and at Auschwitz.
     - The 2017 Chicago GO Fest melted down and ended in a $1.575M settlement.
   - In 2023 Niantic raised the price of remote raids and framed it as encouraging in-person play. Players organized a boycott, and the change was partly reversed in 2025.

10. **The evidence base is thin, short-term and often commissioned by the companies themselves. That is an opening.**
    - The best trials show modest or fading effects. SuperBetter showed d=0.67, but with high dropout. Pokémon GO's extra steps were gone by week 6.
    - Headline economic figures are commissioned: Niantic's $200M (by Statista) and Roblox's (by Nordicity).
    - Open Legend (OL) can stand out by registering its wellbeing outcomes in advance and publishing null results.

## 2. Precedent catalog

Each entry covers: **What** it is · **Bridge** (the mechanism) · **Scale/outcomes** · **Ev** (evidence grade) · **Pitfalls** · **Lesson**.

### 2.1 Physical movement and place

**Ingress (Niantic; beta November 2012)**
- **What:** A two-faction augmented reality (AR) game played over real landmarks called "portals".
- **Bridge:**
  - Players nominate the landmarks themselves.
  - Factions work as teams.
  - Weekend "XM Anomaly" events and monthly meetups are scheduled in advance.
- **Scale:**
  - 15M portal nominations, 5M accepted (July 2016).
  - The largest anomaly, in Japan, drew 10,000+ players.
  - 20M+ downloads by November 2018.
  - Stayed with Niantic Spatial after the 2025 split and was renamed "Ingress" in November 2025.
- **Ev:** C/D. Hanke said he didn't expect players to start talking to each other and forming clubs *(paraphrase)*.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - Faction tribalism.
  - Escalating travel costs: players chartered planes and boats to reach remote portals.
  - The map players built later fed geospatial AI.
- **Lesson:** A player-made map of places that matter is a civic asset. Settle ownership and consent before you start.

**Pokémon GO (July 2016): raids, Community Day, GO Fest, lures**
- **What:** An AR creature-collecting game.
  - 500M downloads by September 2016.
  - 100M+ unique players and 20M weekly active players in 2024.
- **Bridge:**
  - Pokémon appear at public places.
  - Paid "lures" are visible to everyone nearby and draw crowds to businesses.
  - Raids (from 2017) require players to be physically together.
  - Community Day has run monthly since 20 January 2018.
  - Ticketed city festivals.
- **Outcomes:**
  - GO Fest 2019 drew 60,000 in Chicago, 85,000 in Dortmund and 150,000 in Yokohama.
  - GO Fest 2024 (New York City, Madrid, Sendai):
    - 1.2M+ attendees and $200M in economic impact.
    - New York City alone: $126M, with average visitor spend of $795.
    - Attendees walked 18.3 km on average.
  - The 2017 Tottori event in Japan drew 89,000 visitors and $16M in tourism.
  - Restaurants linked to the game got more customer engagement and more positive reviews, depending on the type of restaurant (Pamuru et al., *ISR* 2021).
  - Players report "a sense of belonging, linked to a sense of place" and new ties with strangers (Vella et al., *Games and Culture*; 15 interviews plus 880 forum posts).
  - Steps:
    - Engaged users gained 1,473 steps a day (more than 25%) for 30 days, including people who had been inactive (Althoff et al., *JMIR* 2016).
    - Another study found 955 extra steps a day in week 1, back to baseline by week 6 (Howe et al., *BMJ* 2016).
- **Ev:** A/B for steps. B for business effects and belonging. C for festival economics, which Statista produced for Niantic.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - See 2.10.
  - Paid lures favor the businesses that pay.
- **Lesson:** What lasts is being physically together at raids, festivals and Community Days. The step boost is novelty. Design the recurring gathering first.

**Remote Raid Pass change (6 April 2023)**
- **What:**
  - A single pass rose from 100 to 195 coins, and a 3-pack from 300 to 525.
  - Remote raids were capped at 5 a day.
  - In-person raids got more rewards.
- **Stated rationale:** "Remote Raid Passes have come to dominate the experience of playing Pokémon GO in a way we never intended." The goal was to reward play "in person with your friends, family, and community."
- **Outcome:**
  - Players organized a boycott (Forbes, 3 April 2023).
  - In May 2025 the change was partly reversed: the cap rose to 10 a day, and Shadow raids and Max Battles became playable remotely.
- **Ev:** C/D. No retention data was published.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - A goal about values, delivered as a price rise, reads as a toll.
  - It hit hardest players who are disabled, rural, caregivers or shift workers.
- **Lesson:** Give in-person play distinctive rewards, but don't make them exclusive. Never charge for the path that makes the game accessible.

**Pikmin Bloom (October–November 2021) and Campfire**
- **What:**
  - Your Pikmin plant flowers along the route you actually walk.
  - A daily photo "Lifelog" records your day.
  - Group battles take place at landmarks.
- **Scale:**
  - 2M downloads in two weeks, and $44M gross revenue by April 2024.
  - Campfire is Niantic's social and meet-up layer.
  - Both moved to Scopely in 2025.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** The Lifelog is a close twin of OL's Narrator journal. Tie journal entries to the player's real walk or day.

**Niantic's 2025 split**
- **What:**
  - Scopely bought Pokémon GO, Pikmin Bloom, Monster Hunter Now, Campfire and Wayfarer for $3.5B. Scopely belongs to Savvy Games Group. The deal was announced 12 March 2025 and closed 29 May 2025.
  - Niantic Spatial was spun out to work on geospatial AI. It kept Ingress and Peridot, and Peridot is set to close in May 2026.
- **Mission and data questions:**
  - Niantic long used the tagline "adventures on foot with others" ⚠.
  - John Hanke, Niantic's founder: "Niantic games have always been a bridge to connect people and inspire exploration…"
  - The press raised questions about players' location data.
- **Lesson:** A pro-social mission, and whatever is decided about location data, only survives a sale if it is written into charters, licenses or governance.

**Geocaching and CITO (caching since 2000; CITO since 2002)**
- **What:** A GPS treasure hunt.
  - 3.4M+ active caches in 191 countries.
  - 1.2B+ logs.
  - 59,000+ events a year.
- **Bridge:**
  - Players organize "Cache In Trash Out" (CITO) cleanups.
  - Attending earns a seasonal digital souvenir (March–May and September–November).
- **Scale:**
  - 875,000+ geocachers have attended 50,000+ CITO events in 160 countries (2026 figures).
  - An older fact sheet says 333,000 volunteers, 16,000 events and 8M+ liters of trash.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - It counts attendance, not ecological results.
  - Some players chase souvenirs.
- **Lesson:** A cheap digital token for real service, offered in fixed seasons, can last for decades.

**Zombies, Run! (February 2012)**
- **What:** An audio-drama running game.
  - Its Kickstarter raised $72,627 from 3,464 backers.
  - 750,000 players by 2014, and 10M+ users over its lifetime.
- **Bridge:**
  - The story only advances when you physically run.
  - Zombie "chases" force you into speed intervals.
- **Ev:**
  - B: In the 12-week ZOMBIE trial, activity fell less among app users than among controls as the academic term began.
  - B: A 2021 study of users by University College London (UCL).
  - C: Only 15–20% of buyers were still active by 2015.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - OliveX, a crypto "fitness metaverse" company, bought the studio in 2021 for $9.5M.
  - It launched a crypto spin-off, *Dustland Runner*, in 2022, then laid off most staff in 2025.
  - Co-creator Naomi Alderman used a buy-back clause in November 2025 so the game "doesn't end up being turned into AI slop."
- **Lesson:** Story is an excellent pacer for real exercise. Protect it from monetization detours with creator buy-back or escrow rights.

**Japanese stamp rallies and eki stamps**
- **What:** Themed, time-limited routes of rubber stamps at train stations.
  - They descend from the seals collected on religious pilgrimages (shuin).
  - Permanent station stamps (eki stamps) sit alongside the seasonal rallies.
- **Scale:**
  - The JR East Pokémon Stamp Rally started at 30 stations in 1997 and reached 83 in 2003.
  - By 2005 it had 100 stamps at 97 stations, and "long lines formed every day".
  - It paused in 2020–22 and has since resumed.
- **Ev:** C/D.
- **Lesson:** A physical passport of real places is a century-old, low-tech bridge that families do together.

**Mario Kart Live: Home Circuit (October 2020)**
- **What:** Toy karts with cameras race through gates you set up in your own home. 1.73M sold by December 2022.
- **Lesson:** Mixed reality can turn the home into the game board and invite families to play together in the same room. The hardware cost limits how many people it reaches.

### 2.2 Citizen science and real work

**Foldit (2008)**
- **What:** A University of Washington protein-folding puzzle.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Players solved the structure of a monkey-virus protease in 10 days. It had resisted solution for 15 years (2011).
  - They raised an enzyme's activity more than 18× (2012).
  - Proteins they designed were confirmed by crystallography (*Nature* 2019).
  - 57,000 players were credited in a 2010 *Nature* paper, and 240,000 had registered by 2012.
- **Ev:** A/B.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - The results come from a tiny elite of expert hobbyists.
  - The interface is hard to learn.
- **Lesson:** Give real problems and real credit, including co-authorship. A small core can do frontier work.

**EteRNA (2010)**
- **What:** Players design RNA molecules, and the top designs are built in a Stanford lab.
- **Outcomes:**
  - The best algorithm of the time solved only 54 of 100 puzzles that humans had solved.
  - Players' strategies were turned into an algorithm, EteRNABot (*PNAS* 2014).
  - The OpenVaccine project (2020) collected 6,000 designs. It produced methods for mRNA vaccines that could last up to 3× longer on the shelf.
  - 250,000 had registered by 2016, and players appear as co-authors on papers.
- **Ev:** A/B.
- **Lesson:** Seeing your design built and tested in reality motivates deeply.

**EVE Online Project Discovery (2016 onward)**
- **What:** A classification minigame set inside EVE's fiction.
  - Earlier phases covered protein images and exoplanets ⚠.
  - The 2020 COVID-19 phase analyzed blood-cell data.
- **Scale:**
  - 41.4M classifications from 157,000 active players, equal to 148.35 work-years.
  - About 500,000 verified submissions were usable.
  - The lead researcher: "The accuracy of their analyses is very high…"
- **Ev:** B/C.
- **Lesson:** Make the task part of the story, pay in in-game rewards, and check quality by consensus.

**Borderlands Science (inside Borderlands 3, 2020; *Nature Biotechnology* 2024)**
- **What:** A tile-alignment puzzle that works on microbial DNA.
- **Scale:**
  - 4.5M players.
  - In half a day they produced "five times more data about microbial DNA sequences than our earlier game, Phylo, had collected over a 10-year period."
  - They refined the evolutionary relationships of more than a million gut microbes.
- **Ev:** A/B (peer-reviewed).
- **Lesson:** Put the science task where players already are, and pay in the main game's currency.

**Sea Hero Quest (2016)**
- **What:** A navigation game from Deutsche Telekom, Alzheimer's Research UK, UCL and the University of East Anglia (UEA). Playing it also measures your sense of direction.
- **Scale:**
  - 4.3M players.
  - Players carrying the APOE4 gene, which raises Alzheimer's risk, navigated less efficiently even though they had no symptoms. The comparison was 31 carriers against 29 non-carriers (*PNAS* 2019).
  - 27,108 UK players aged 50–75 were analyzed.
  - The partners claim "every two minutes spent playing the game is equal to five hours of lab-based research."
- **Ev:** A/B for the findings. C for the equivalence claim.
- **Pitfalls:** Consent for health data.
- **Lesson:** Play can be an opt-in research instrument.

**Stall Catchers (October 2016)**
- **What:** Players flag blocked blood vessels in videos of mouse brains for a Cornell Alzheimer's lab.
- **Scale:**
  - 34,000+ players by 2021, aged 6 to 88.
  - The organization says it "doubled the speed of Cornell's data analysis."
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Show players that their hour equals X lab hours, and run live team events.

**Zooniverse: Anti-Slavery Manuscripts (Boston Public Library, 2018–20)**
- **What:** Volunteers transcribed the library's collection of abolitionist letters.
- **Scale:**
  - 26,059 participants transcribed 12,247 letters (1.8M lines). Each letter was transcribed three times so the versions could be checked against each other.
  - Volunteers posted 16,971 comments across 8,121 discussion threads.
  - The transcriptions are now searchable in Digital Commonwealth and are used to train machine-learning models.
- **Ev:** B/C.
- **Lesson:** Humanities "real work" suits scribe and archive roles. The discussion forums are where the community forms.

**Corrupted Blood (World of Warcraft, 13 September – 8 October 2005)**
- **What:** An accidental plague that spread through the game via pets and immune non-player characters (NPCs).
- **What happened:**
  - Healers acted as first responders, and others quarantined themselves.
  - Griefers spread the plague on purpose. One said: "It's just funny to watch people run away screaming."
  - Blizzard fixed it with a hard server reset.
- **Research:**
  - Lofgren and Fefferman (*Lancet Infectious Diseases*, 2007) wrote that players "seemed to really feel they were at risk and took the threat of infection seriously, even though it was only a game."
  - Researchers revisited it during COVID.
- **Ev:** B (commentary).
- **Lesson:** In-world crises can teach, and can even inform science. Get consent, and expect bad actors.

### 2.3 Civic participation, design and governance

**Block by Block (UN-Habitat, Mojang and Microsoft, 2012 onward)**
- **What:** Residents model public spaces in Minecraft, and city planners use the models.
- **Scale:**
  - "Dozens" of projects in 55+ countries.
  - More than $5M raised from the Minecraft community through licensed merchandise.
- **Built results:**
  - Kalobeyei refugee settlement, Kenya (2017): solar lights, 500+ trees, the settlement's first boda-boda station, and shade.
  - Kim Chung, Hanoi (2017): schoolgirls' designs led to three underpasses being cleaned and lit.
  - Also Solar Park in Khulna (Bangladesh) and Parque Manhattan in Lima.
  - An early target of 300 spaces by 2016 does not appear to have been met.
- **Ev:** C. No independent evaluation was found.
- **Pitfalls:** Without a budget attached, consultation becomes theater.
- **Lesson:** Pair the design tool with money and a decision-maker.

**The Uncensored Library (Reporters Without Borders (RSF), 12 March 2020)**
- **What:** A Minecraft library of censored journalism.
  - 12.5M blocks, with a wing for each country.
  - A US room was added in March 2026.
- **Scale:**
  - 1M+ visits and 10M article readings by 2026.
  - A 2022 Peabody Award called it "an innovative back door for access to censored content."
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Game worlds can carry civic information past censors. An OL contemporary city could carry real journalism.

**EVOKE (World Bank Institute, 3 March – 12 May 2010)**
- **What:** A 10-week alternate reality game on social innovation, with a $500,000 budget.
- **Scale:**
  - 19,324 registered players (about 8,000 active) in 150 countries.
  - They produced 23,500 blog posts, 4,700 photos and 1,500 videos.
  - 74 venture proposals were submitted. The top 25 raised $30,000 on GlobalGiving, and a summit in Washington, DC, followed.
- **Evaluation:**
  - Players reported strong gains in ideas and learning.
  - It had "less effect on building a network of colleagues and friends and gaining self confidence."
- **Ev:** C (self-report).
- **Lesson:** Idea contests don't create ventures unless mentorship and funding are real and sustained.

**World Without Oil (ITVS/CPB, 30 April – 1 June 2007)**
- **What:** "Play it – before you live it." Players narrated their lives through a fictional oil shock.
- **Scale:** About 1,700 contributions. A 2015 analysis found "dominance of a handful of players."
- **Ev:** B/C.
- **Lesson:** Rehearsing the future through play is compelling, but a few players usually dominate. Design for the quiet majority.

**Find the Future (NYPL, 20 May 2011)**
- **What:**
  - 500 players spent the night in the New York Public Library's stacks.
  - They found 100 treasures and wrote essays about them.
  - The resulting joint book entered the library's permanent collection.
- **Ev:** C/D.
- **Lesson:** A physical artifact kept by an institution makes one night last.

**EVE Online governance and economics**
- **What:** An elected player council.
  - Announced 19 March 2008.
  - 9 members and 5 alternates, with 6-month terms.
  - Elected by about 250,000 players.
- **Economist:** In June 2007 CCP hired an in-house economist with a PhD, who published quarterly reports.
- **Quotes:**
  - "A democratic election process of representatives is one of mankind's greatest inventions."
  - "Economic information is the lifeblood of the game…"
- **Ev:** C.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - The council's influence depends on the developer's goodwill.
  - Large player alliances vote as blocs.
- **Lesson:** Representation plus published data gives players real civic practice.

**A Tale in the Desert (February 2003)**
- **What:** An online game with no combat.
  - Laws pass by petition: players collect signatures up to a threshold, then vote.
  - There are 56 "Tests" in fields including Architecture, Art, Harmony, Leadership, Thought and Worship.
  - The world resets about every 18 months, and each cycle is called a "Telling".
- **Notable events:**
  - An elected Demi-Pharaoh can ban up to 7 players.
  - A "Clear Cutting" skill felled about 3,450 of 15,300 trees before a player law stopped it.
  - Since 2018 the game has been run by a studio its players founded.
- **Ev:** D.
- **Lesson:** Social conflict can be as intense as combat. Shared resources plus lawmaking produce real deliberation, and real abuses of power.

**Eco (Strange Loop Games; early access February 2018)**
- **What:** Players share one ecology and have 30 days to stop a meteor.
  - They pass laws, set taxes and hold elections.
  - Over-harvesting causes extinctions.
- **Scale:** More than $1M in US Department of Education funding.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Ecology and law work as game mechanics. Classroom use needs tools for teachers.

**Twitch Plays Pokémon (12 February 2014)**
- **What:** A crowd played one Pokémon game together by typing commands into chat.
- **Scale:**
  - 1,165,140 participants (a Guinness record) and 121,000 at the peak moment.
  - 55M views.
- **Emergent culture:** Players could switch between "Anarchy" and "Democracy" input modes. A folk religion grew up around an in-game item, the Helix Fossil.
- **Lesson:** Crowd governance is fun when the mechanism is visible and can be switched.

**Animal Crossing as a protest square**
- **What:** Hong Kong activists staged protests on their islands. The game disappeared from the Chinese shopping site Taobao on 10 April 2020.
- **Lesson:** Any world where players can express themselves will host politics. Set policy before the pressure arrives.

### 2.4 Charity and giving

**Games Done Quick (2010 onward)**
- **What:** Week-long speedrunning marathons that raise money for charity.
- **Scale:**
  - More than $59.8M from more than 1M donations (January 2026).
  - The record is AGDQ 2022, with $3,442,033.
  - In 2025 the two marathons raised $2.5M+ for the Prevent Cancer Foundation and $2.4M+ for Doctors Without Borders.
- **Bridge:**
  - Donors buy a say in the show, for example by bidding on character names and routes.
  - Donations are read aloud.
  - A public tracker shows the running total.
  - A zero-tolerance harassment policy was adopted in 2016–18.
- **Ev:** B/C (the tracker is public).
- **Lesson:** Let donors shape the show, and keep the total public.

**Extra Life (2008 onward)**
- **What:** A 24-hour gaming marathon for children's hospitals, started in memory of Victoria Enmon.
- **Scale:**
  - Grew from $120,000 in 2008 to $14M in 2019.
  - More than $145M in total, by the organization's count.
  - About 50,000 participants a year.
  - The money stays with local hospitals.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Local attachment ("my hospital") plus a marathon ritual.

**Sky: Children of the Light charity seasons**
- **What:** Limited-time events where cosmetic purchases fund donations.
- **Scale:**
  - Days of Healing 2020 raised $719,138 for Doctors Without Borders (MSF).
  - Days of Rainbow 2021 raised $794,420 for the Trevor Project and the Global Fund for Women.
  - Days of Nature 2020 planted 40,576 trees.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Frame giving as a festival and keep it transparent.

**Forest (May 2014)**
- **What:** A focus timer. Your virtual tree dies if you leave the app, and the coins you earn fund real trees.
- **Scale:** 2M+ real trees planted with Trees for the Future.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Tie in-game growth to real planting, but don't overclaim what each user achieves.

### 2.5 Kindness and asynchronous help

**Kind Words (Popcannibal, 2019)**
- **What:** Players write anonymous letters asking for support or offering it.
  - Replies are thanked with stickers.
  - You cannot reply to a reply.
- **Scale:**
  - 4M+ letters by July 2022.
  - About 3% of the first 500,000+ letters needed moderation.
  - Won the 2020 BAFTA for "Game Beyond Entertainment".
- **Designer on trolls:** They "get bored quickly" *(abridged)*.
- **Ev:** C/D.
- **Lesson:** One-shot exchanges plus a way to say thanks give kindness without dependency.

**Journey (2012)**
- **What:** You travel with an anonymous companion.
  - No names and no text, only musical chirps.
  - Names appear in the end credits.
  - 3 of 25 playtesters cried.
- **Lesson:** Limiting how players can communicate reduces harassment and raises meaning.

**Sky: Children of the Light (2019)**
- **What:**
  - You befriend other players by gifting candles.
  - A friendship tree unlocks chat and hugs step by step.
  - Veterans can hold hands with newcomers to guide them.
- **Scale:**
  - 50M downloads by October 2020.
  - 62% of 563 surveyed players had been helped by veterans.
  - A concert with the singer Aurora (September 2023) set two Guinness records.
- **Designer:** Jenova Chen: "Sky is a game about compassion and generosity…nudging them to do good" *(abridged)*.
- **Ev:** C/D.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - Candles are also sold for money.
  - Many players are minors.
- **Lesson:** Make players climb a ladder of trust before they can talk freely, and use veterans as guides.

**Death Stranding (2019)**
- **What:** Other players' ladders, bridges and generators appear in your world and slowly decay. Players send each other "likes" as thanks. The system is called the "Social Strand System".
- **Scale:** 5M copies sold by March 2021.
- **Designer:** Hideo Kojima wanted players to feel "the true importance of forging connections with others."
- **Lesson:** Helping each other indirectly, across time zones, feels like community without the risks of chat.

**Cruel 2 B Kind (McGonigal and Bogost, 2006)** ⚠
- **What:** A street "assassination" game where the weapons are compliments and welcomes. Players don't know who else is playing, so strangers end up receiving the kindness.
- **Ev:** D.
- **Lesson:** Kindness mechanics spill over onto bystanders.

### 2.6 Community rituals and third places

**parkrun (2004 onward)**
- **What:** A free, weekly, timed 5 km run.
- **Scale (2024–25):**
  - 2.3M participants, of whom 897,689 were first-timers.
  - 126,593 events at 2,600+ locations in 23 countries.
  - 309,220 volunteers doing 2.2M volunteer shifts.
  - Milestone shirts reward both running and volunteering.
- **Wellbeing:** A Sheffield Hallam University survey (n=76,589; 2023–24) found:
  - 81% report being happier.
  - 86% of volunteers report being happier, against 79% of people who only run or walk.
  - Over 80% of people with health conditions report improvement.
  - The university estimates parkrun is worth £667M a year to the UK.
- **Ev:** B/C (the surveys are commissioned, and respondents choose themselves).
- **Lesson:** "Not a race", plus volunteer roles, plus milestones, builds belonging. Volunteering is itself something people come for.

**Friday Night Magic (promotional cards since 2000)**
- **What:** Weekly casual tournaments in about 6,000 stores (2018).
- **During COVID:** "FNM at Home" ran on the digital game MTG Arena, with rewards handed out through local stores. In-store play resumed in May 2021.
- **Ev:** C/D.
- **Pitfalls:** There are documented barriers to inclusion.
- **Lesson:** Route digital rewards through local venues so the local "third place" survives.

**D&D Adventurers League (2014)**
- **What:** Players keep the same character across tables and stores, tracked on logsheets.
  - Anyone can drop in.
  - Dungeon masters earn rewards.
- **Lesson:** A portable identity across venues turns strangers into a recurring table.

**Warhammer stores**
- **What:** Small "hobby centres" where staff teach painting for free and host gaming nights. Games Workshop's revenue was £565M in FY2025.
- **Lesson:** Teach a physical craft in person. The craft becomes the bond.

**Board game cafés**
- **What:**
  - Seoul had about 130 by 2004.
  - Toronto's Snakes & Lattes (2010) deliberately offered no Wi-Fi.
  - Staff "gurus" teach the games.
  - Wikipedia claims more than 5,000 US cafés by 2016, which is likely overstated.
- **Lesson:** A human teacher in a welcoming space turns beginners into players.

**Wii bowling leagues**
- **What:** The National Senior League started in 2009. By 2012 it had 242 teams from 116 senior communities in 30 states, with players in their 60s and 70s.
- **Research:** A small UC San Diego (UCSD) pilot study (Rosenberg et al., 2010) reported fewer depressive symptoms among older adults who played Wii Sports ⚠.
- **Ev:** D, plus weak B.
- **Lesson:** Simple controls, team identity, rivalry between communities, and staff who facilitate.

**Animal Crossing: New Horizons (20 March 2020)**
- **What:** Sold 11.77M in 12 days and 50.29M by June 2026.
- **Real events held in the game:**
  - Weddings, including one cancelled by COVID.
  - Graduations, funerals and a talk show.
  - The Biden campaign released yard signs for players' islands.
  - The UK's National Videogame Museum opened "Animal Crossing Diaries" (2021).
- **Ev:** D.
- **Lesson:** Ceremonial features (clothing, venues, photos) let a game host real rites of passage.

**Blockeley (UC Berkeley, 2020–21)**
- **What:** After COVID cancelled graduation, 500+ students applied to rebuild the campus at full scale in Minecraft. It hosted commencement in 2020 and 2021, and a music festival with 60+ artists.
- **Lesson:** When physical places close, communities will rebuild them for real rites if given tools and permission.

**Fortnite "Astronomical" (April 2020)**
- **What:** A Travis Scott concert inside Fortnite. It drew 12.3M players at once and 27.7M in total.
- **Lesson:** Spectacle at scale is not connection. These events are memorable one-offs.

**In-game funerals**
- **What:** In 2006 a World of Warcraft guild held a memorial for a player who had died in real life.
  - A rival guild, Serenity Now, ambushed the gathering and posted the video.
  - It became an academic case study in the ethics of griefing.
- **Lesson:** Sacred gatherings need protection mechanics.

**The Uru diaspora**
- **What:** When the game Uru closed in 2004, about 10,000 displaced players re-formed in There.com and Second Life.
  - Uru relaunched in 2007 and closed again in April 2008.
  - Celia Pearce documented this in *Communities of Play* (MIT Press, 2009).
- **Lesson:** Community can move to a new home if people can find each other.

**Second Life and Virtual Ability (2003 / 2007)**
- **What:** Virtual Ability was founded by three friends with disabilities and serves 1,000+ people on six continents.
  - It became a US nonprofit (501(c)(3)) in 2008, the "first virtual world project that evolved into a physical world nonprofit."
  - It won the Linden Prize in 2009.
  - It partners with the US Army on a project for amputees, and with the University of Pennsylvania.
- **Wider uses:** Second Life hosts education (for example, a special-education program at West Virginia University) and health education sites.
- **Economy:** Users cashed out about $60M in 2015. In 2009, only 233 users earned more than $5,000.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** For people with disabilities or who can't leave home, virtual presence *is* real-life connection. Don't equate "real" with "physical".

### 2.7 Real-skill games and career bridges

**Rocksmith**
- **What:** You plug in a real guitar, and the difficulty adapts to you. The original game sold 1.5M+ copies.
- **Ubisoft's claims:**
  - It cites an "independent national study" calling Rocksmith the "fastest way to learn guitar."
  - It says "95 percent" of players learned something.
  - It ran a 60-day challenge.
- **Academic view:** "little empirical evidence" (*Journal of Music, Technology & Education*, 2018).
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** A real instrument teaches a real skill, but test the claims independently.

**Kerbal Space Program**
- **What:**
  - NASA co-created an asteroid-redirect mission pack (April 2014).
  - In June 2016, NASA's Jason Dworkin, a player since 2011, challenged players to fly a replica of the OSIRIS-REx asteroid probe. He said: "Here is a community of people that is enthusiastic about space and wants to learn the science of spaceflight."
- **Lesson:** An institutional partner makes learning through play legitimate.

**Flight sims and VATSIM (2001)**
- **What:** A volunteer network that simulates air traffic control.
  - 191,316 active members (early 2025), 92% of them pilots.
  - Tiered ratings for pilots and controllers.
  - Student pilots use it to practice radio calls.
  - Microsoft Flight Simulator partnered with it in 2020.
- **Lesson:** A volunteer layer that follows real procedures turns a simulator into an apprenticeship.

**World of Warcraft guild leadership and careers**
- **Wired (April 2006):** "The day may not be far off when companies receive résumés that include a line reading 'level 60 tauren shaman in World of Warcraft.'"
- **Stephen Gillett**, then at Yahoo (later Starbucks' chief information officer ⚠): "I used to worry about not having what I needed to get a job done… Now I think of it like a quest."
- **IBM and Seriosity (2007):**
  - 173 surveys, 10 interviews and 200+ IBM gamers.
  - About 50% felt leading in games improved their real leadership; 40% already used techniques from online games at work.
  - The report highlighted leadership that is temporary and task-based, with skills and incentives visible to everyone.
  - One leader: "You can never stop earning the right to be their leader."
- **Ev:** C (self-report; the report was written by the companies).
- **Lesson:** Coordinating strangers is a transferable skill. Make it visible and portable without overclaiming.

**Scratch (2007)**
- **What:** A block-based coding community.
  - 103M+ users and 123M+ shared projects (February 2023).
  - 1.3B projects created (April 2024).
  - When you remix a project, the original creator is credited automatically.
  - New accounts start with restrictions, and a central team moderates.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Remixing with credit, plus gentle moderation, produces enormous youth creativity with relatively little harm.

**Minecraft Education (November 2016)**
- **What:** The education version of Minecraft. Adoption figures were not verified this session. It serves as the classroom wrapper for projects like Block by Block and the Uncensored Library.
- **Lesson:** Tools for teachers matter as much as the game.

### 2.8 Self-care and habit apps

**SuperBetter**
- **What:** An app that frames life challenges as quests you tackle with allies.
- **Ev:** A, with caveats.
  - In a randomized trial, 283 adults with depression used it 10 minutes a day for a month, compared with a waitlist.
  - Effect sizes were d=0.67 right after and 1.05 at follow-up.
  - The general version worked as well as the version based on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT).
  - Many participants dropped out.
- **Lesson:** Quest framing with allies can help, at least for motivated users.

**Finch**
- **What:** A pet bird that grows as you complete gentle self-care goals, with no punishment.
- **Evidence:** There are no clinical studies. A clinician-review site called it a "supplementary tool" (2024).
- **Ev:** D.
- **Lesson:** Give players a creature to care for, without guilt.

**Habitica (2013; open source)**
- **What:** A to-do list run as a role-playing game.
- **Group mechanic:** In boss quests, "When one player fails to complete Dailies, the boss attacks all of the players."
- **Research:** Results are mixed, including a 2019 study of counterproductive effects.
- **Lesson:** Accountability to a group is powerful, but it tips into shame. Let players share the upside, not the damage.

**Duolingo streaks**
- **What:**
  - Duolingo reports that "learners who reach a streak of 7 days are 2.4x more likely to use Duolingo the next day" (2021).
  - Reminder prompts framed around habit raised notification opt-in by 5%.
- **Pitfalls:** Its guilt-laden notifications are widely criticized.
- **Ev:** C.
- **Lesson:** Streaks keep users by pressuring them. OL's world pausing when you leave is the principled alternative.

**Strava, Zwift, Peloton**
- **Strava (2025):**
  - Clubs nearly quadrupled to 1M. Running clubs grew 3.5× and hiking clubs 5.8×.
  - Gen Z is 39% more likely than Gen X to use fitness to meet people.
  - Ev: C.
- **Zwift:**
  - Players have run races since 2015, with 1,000+ a week by 2022.
  - It hosted a virtual Tour de France and the first world championship for cycling esports (2020).
- **Peloton:**
  - Features leaderboards and "high-fives".
  - Its value fell from $50B (January 2021) to $1.3B (June 2024).
  - It laid off 2,800 staff (2022).
  - It recalled its Tread+ treadmill after a child's death (2021).
- **Lesson:** Scheduled group events are what lasts. Communities built in a boom can collapse along with the business.

### 2.9 Creator economies

**Roblox**
- **Payouts:**
  - Creators earned more than $1B between March 2024 and March 2025, and about $1.5B in 2025.
  - The median creator earned about $1,500 a year. The top 10 averaged $33.9M in 2024.
- **Company-commissioned survey:**
  - 33% of creators had never taken a computer science class.
  - 44% would not have made interactive experiences without Roblox.
- **Critiques:** The journalists at People Make Games (August and December 2021) reported:
  - Unfavorable revenue splits.
  - An in-game currency that works like company scrip, with high thresholds for cashing out.
  - Young developers doing this work.
  - Trading of "collectibles" likened to gambling.
- **Safety:**
  - Settlements with Nevada ($12M) and Alabama ($12.2M) in April 2026 require facial age estimation and chat controls.
  - Suits are pending in Texas, Florida, Louisiana, Iowa, Nebraska and Kentucky, plus a consolidated federal case. There are 140+ suits in total.
  - From June 2026 accounts are split into age tiers, with chat off by default for ages 5–8.
- **Business:**
  - 36% of its 151.5M daily users are under 13.
  - In Q3 2026 it forecast its first-ever fall in bookings. It blamed changes to how games are recommended and friction from age checks.
- **Ev:** B/C.
- **Lesson:** Creator economies turn play into labor, and children plus money plus open chat equals risk. Adding safety afterwards is expensive.

**Dreams (2020)**
- **What:** Sony's game-creation suite.
  - Live support ended in September 2023.
  - A 2020 beta let creators sell their work off the platform, but few documented careers came of it.
- **Lesson:** Creations locked to one platform are fragile. Build in export and ownership.

**Super Mario Maker (2015)**
- **What:** A Nintendo game where players build and share levels. It had 7.2M levels and 600M plays by May 2016.
- **Before shutdown:** Before the servers closed on 8 April 2024, "Team 0%" cleared about 25,000 levels no one had beaten.
  - One level, "Trimming the Herbs", turned out to have been "cleared" by its creator using tool assistance.
  - Players then beat it legitimately three days before shutdown.
- **Lesson:** Communities will go to heroic lengths to preserve their work. Treat a shutdown as a design moment.

### 2.10 Cautionary tales

**Pokémon GO's side effects**
- **Crashes:** In Tippecanoe County, Indiana, crashes rose disproportionately near PokéStops. The researchers attribute 2 extra deaths and $5.2–25.5M in costs over 148 days, and extrapolate $2–7.3B nationally (NBER working paper w24308).
- **Sensitive sites:** Play took place at Auschwitz-Birkenau and the US Holocaust Memorial Museum. A gym at the Korean border zone was removed.
- **Chicago GO Fest 2017:**
  - Phone networks overloaded and the CEO was booed.
  - Tickets were refunded, and a $1.575M class-action settlement followed.
- **Ev:** B/D.
- **Lesson:** Block play at driving speeds, keep lists of excluded sites, and plan for crowd capacity.

**Axie Infinity (play-to-earn)**
- **What:** From August 2020, Filipinos adopted the game as a source of income. About 40% of players were in the Philippines.
  - "Managers" lent game assets to "scholars" and took commissions of up to 75%.
  - At the peak, getting started cost $400+.
- **Collapse:**
  - The SLP token fell more than 99% (February 2022).
  - Daily players fell from 2.7M to about 250,000 (April 2023).
  - On 23 March 2022, hackers linked to North Korea stole $620M from the Ronin bridge, the service that moved the game's crypto funds.
  - Academics described the model as "a pyramid scheme that relies on cheap labor."
- **Related:** "Move-to-earn" apps such as STEPN (2022) followed a similar arc ⚠. Zombies, Run!'s crypto owner nearly killed it.
- **Ev:** B/C.
- **Lesson:** Never turn the fun into a wage.

**Gamified labor**
- Disneyland's 2011 staff leaderboards were called an "electronic whip" (LA Times).
- The game designer Ian Bogost called gamification "exploitationware".
- Uber's 2017 nudges toward earnings goals and automatically queued next rides (reported by the NYT) ⚠.
- Amazon's 2019 warehouse games tied to scanner productivity data, such as MissionRacer ⚠.
- **Lesson:** OL's metrics must never become a tool for employers or insurers.

**Social scoring: fiction vs reality**
- *Black Mirror*'s "Nosedive" (2016) is fiction.
- China's actual Social Credit System is fragmented and aimed mostly at businesses, which are the target of 73% of enforcement actions.
- Its sharpest tool against individuals is a court blacklist of people who defy court judgments. By June 2019 it had blocked 26.82M flight purchases and 5.96M rail ticket purchases.
- One city's points scheme (Rongcheng) became voluntary and rewards-only in 2021.
- Alibaba's Sesame Credit is a private company scheme, not part of the state system.
- The research institute MERICS (2022): "such a score simply does not exist."
- **Lesson:** Keep reputation local, contextual, explainable and forgiving. Describe China accurately.

**Also cautionary (covered above)**
- Duolingo's guilt (2.8).
- Roblox's safety and labor problems (2.9).
- The remote-raid toll (2.1).
- EVOKE's weak effect on networks (2.3).
- The Serenity Now funeral raid (2.6).
- Peloton's boom and bust (2.8).
- Animal Crossing's removal in China (2.3).

## 4. Design implications for Open Legend (26 ideas)

### Rhythm and ritual

1. **Seasons, not streaks.**
   - Make "the world rests when you do" a public promise.
   - No decay, no guilt notifications and no countdown timers.
   - The Narrator's closing line points outward, for example: "Who could you show this sling to?"
   - *Precedents: Duolingo and Habitica, compared with parkrun.*

2. **A monthly Moot and Market Day.**
   - Each world holds one festival a month at a fixed real-world time, such as a harvest fair or a river assembly.
   - Libraries, cafés and game stores get a "host kit" to run watch parties.
   - Attending remotely is worth the same as attending in person. In-person attendees get cosmetic keepsakes only.
   - *Precedents: Community Day, Friday Night Magic, and the remote-raid backlash.*

3. **Volunteer roles count as real progress.**
   - Roles include greeter, festival marshal, scribe, lore-keeper and newcomer guide.
   - Helping others earns milestones just as crafting does.
   - *Precedent: parkrun, where volunteers report the biggest happiness gains.*

4. **Milestones, not leaderboards.**
   - Count things like meals cooked for others, festivals attended and newcomers welcomed.
   - Residents celebrate these.
   - They can be printed as a physical "Threewater Passport" that partner venues stamp.
   - *Precedents: parkrun's milestone shirts and Japanese stamp rallies.*

### From in-game invention to real skill

5. **"Try it for real" cards.**
   - When the game validates a player's invention, such as cordage, a bread oven or a natural dye, the Narrator can offer a vetted real-world practice card and a prompt to "show a friend".
   - Weapon-like inventions, such as slings and bows, point to supervised ranges and clubs. They never come with do-it-yourself instructions.
   - *Precedents: Rocksmith and Kerbal Space Program.*

6. **An invention commons that shows lineage.**
   - Every recipe shows its family tree, and remixes credit their ancestors automatically.
   - A world's inventions can be exported as zines: shareable, real-world how-to guides.
   - *Precedent: Scratch's automatic remix credit.*

7. **A skill ledger endorsed by humans.**
   - Players keep a portable record of what they organized and taught. They own it and control who sees it.
   - Other *humans*, not AI residents, endorse the entries.
   - *Precedents: World of Warcraft guild leaders and the IBM/Seriosity report. The aim is to avoid credentials inflated by AI.*

### Real work inside the fiction

8. **The Scribe's Desk.**
   - Threewater's archive holds real historical letters or specimen photos from partner projects, in the style of Zooniverse.
   - Three players transcribe each item so the versions can be checked against each other.
   - Players earn in-world standing and can see the results published.
   - *Precedents: Borderlands Science, Boston Public Library and EVE.*

9. **Crisis simulations with consent.**
   - Floods, blights or fevers run as opt-in scenarios designed with an academic partner to research standards.
   - Findings are shared back with players, and loopholes for griefers are closed first.
   - *Precedent: Corrupted Blood.*

### Civic practice

10. **River Moot governance.**
    - Players petition, gather signatures up to a threshold, then vote on laws that carry real trade-offs.
    - An elected Player Council meets the development team every quarter.
    - Publish a quarterly Threewater economic report.
    - *Precedents: A Tale in the Desert, EVE's player council and economist, and Eco.*

11. **A Commons Studio.**
    - In the contemporary-city world, players model a real local park or street.
    - Run it only with a partner who commits a decision-maker and a budget.
    - *Precedent: Block by Block. The aim is to avoid consultation theater.*

12. **Visible crowd governance.**
    - Offer a switchable "village decides" mode in which many players steer a shared event together.
    - Show the voting rules on screen.
    - *Precedent: Twitch Plays Pokémon.*

### Kindness and connection

13. **Strands.**
    - Players leave bridges, caches, tools and notes that others find later.
    - Thanks flows one way only, with nothing owed in return.
    - Everything slowly decays.
    - *Precedent: Death Stranding.*

14. **Letters at the Well.**
    - Players post anonymous requests for encouragement and receive one-shot replies they can thank with stickers.
    - Crisis keywords route to real helplines, and human moderators back the system up.
    - *Precedent: Kind Words.*

15. **Trust ladders and wayfinders.**
    - Communication unlocks through mutual gestures.
    - Newcomers are paired with veteran guides.
    - Minors get restricted channels by default.
    - *Precedents: Sky and Journey, and the lessons from Roblox.*

16. **Residents who point outward.**
    - AI residents ask about the player's real people and celebrate real-world social news.
    - They suggest teaching a recipe to a friend, and they never claim to be the player's only relationship.
    - Their daily rhythm of sleeping and working naturally ends sessions.
    - A resident is a bridge, not a destination.

### Rites of passage and grief

17. **A Ceremony Hall with sanctuary mode.**
    - Host weddings, graduations and memorials for real people.
    - Browser spectator links let family who don't play attend.
    - Invite-only protection prevents a Serenity Now-style ambush.
    - *Precedents: Blockeley, Animal Crossing and World of Warcraft funerals.*

18. **Mortality as a time for reflection.**
    - When residents age and die, the community holds a funeral and the Narrator offers reflection prompts.
    - Real grief resources are available if the player wants them.
    - Treat this as a hypothesis to test, not a proven benefit.

### Giving and stewardship

19. **The Long Night.**
    - Hold an annual charity festival in which donation incentives shape world events, for example naming a new bridge.
    - Keep a public tally, and let the Player Council choose the beneficiaries.
    - *Precedents: Games Done Quick, Extra Life and Sky's Days of Healing.*

20. **Mirror stewardship.**
    - Pair in-world restoration seasons, such as cleaning the river or planting orchards, with real cleanups modeled on CITO.
    - The souvenir requires real attendance, confirmed by the organizer.
    - Pledges of real trees stay modest and are audited.
    - *Precedents: CITO and Forest.*

### Creator economy and preservation

21. **A fair creator economy.**
    - Transparent revenue sharing and low thresholds for cashing out.
    - No in-game currency that works like company scrip.
    - Only adults receive payouts.
    - Weight the creator fund toward experiences with measured real-world outcomes, such as meetups hosted and skills practiced, rather than minutes of engagement.
    - *Precedent: the critique of Roblox.*

22. **A sunset protocol.**
    - The AGPL engine plus full export of worlds and data.
    - Communities keep the right to host their worlds themselves.
    - Give long notice before any closure.
    - Put creator buy-back or escrow clauses into any sale.
    - *Precedents: Uru, Team 0%, Dreams, A Tale in the Desert and Zombies, Run!*

### Safety and ethics

23. **No play-to-earn.**
    - No tradeable tokens, no loot boxes, and no gambling-like markets for collectibles, especially anywhere near wellbeing features.
    - *Precedents: Axie Infinity, move-to-earn apps and Roblox's collectibles.*

24. **No global score.**
    - Reputation stays within each community, can be explained, and fades toward forgiveness over time.
    - Behavioral data is never shared with employers or insurers.
    - Use "social credit" comparisons accurately.
    - *Precedents: "Nosedive" compared with China's real system, and gamified gig work.*

25. **Safety on real maps.**
    - By default, exclude memorials, cemeteries, schools and private homes.
    - Block interaction at vehicle speeds.
    - Plan capacity for events.
    - Give disabled and rural players an equal remote path.
    - *Precedents: the Pokémon GO crash study, the Holocaust Museum incident, Chicago 2017 and Virtual Ability.*

### Measurement

26. **A pre-registered wellbeing trial.**
    - With an academic partner, register the outcomes before launch. Candidates:
      - Loneliness (the UCLA-3 scale).
      - Wellbeing (the WHO-5 index).
      - Life satisfaction.
      - Weekly hours of in-person contact.
      - Time spent in the game compared with time spent with people.
    - Measure at 6 weeks and at 3, 6 and 12 months.
    - Roll features out to randomly chosen groups, and publish null results.
    - Don't rely on commissioned impact reports.
    - *Precedents: the fading Pokémon GO step boost in the BMJ study, SuperBetter's dropout, and vendor statistics.*
