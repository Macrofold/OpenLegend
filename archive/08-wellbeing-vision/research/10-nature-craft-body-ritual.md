## 1. Ten headline takeaways

1. **Noticing does more than knowing.** Nature connectedness grows through five pathways: senses (first called "contact"), emotion, beauty, meaning and compassion. Knowledge and species identification on their own did not predict it ([Lumber, Richardson & Sheffield 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0177186)). Recording still has a place. In a randomized study where 500 people completed the protocol, pollinator or butterfly counts and "Three Good Things in Nature" (3GTiN) both improved wellbeing, but only noticing (alone or combined with counting) raised pro-nature conservation behaviour ([Pocock et al. 2023](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pan3.10432)). **Design:** NPCs, prompts and the journal should model sensory, emotional and meaningful noticing, and recording should always be paired with feeling.

2. **The effective doses are small, and most of the evidence is correlational.** Findings include:
   - about 2 hours a week in nature (Obs, n=19,806);
   - encounters with birds linked to better wellbeing that lasts for hours (Obs, n=1,292);
   - weekly 15-minute "awe walks" (RCT, n=52);
   - three roughly 1-minute vigorous bursts a day (Obs);
   - walking while generating ideas (+60% creative output in one experiment).

   Frame every real-world bridge as a small, flexible invitation, not a prescription. Claims about forest bathing go well beyond the evidence (medium-to-high risk of bias; possible placebo effects).

3. **Finishing the work and the effort itself are the reward.** The IKEA effect "dissipated" when participants destroyed their builds or failed to complete them ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf)). Kelly Lambert's "effort-driven rewards" and Matthew Crawford's "He can simply point: the building stands" make the same point. **Design:** real-world micro-crafts should finish in one sitting, and the game should remember them.

4. **Craft helps most when done side by side.** Examples: Men's Sheds ("Men don't talk face to face, they talk shoulder to shoulder"), group knitting (linked to greater happiness and social contact in n=3,545), and 2,500+ Repair Cafés. The social setting matters as much as the craft.

5. **The shared table may be the strongest single lever.** In the [World Happiness Report 2025](https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/sharing-meals-with-others-how-sharing-meals-supports-happiness-and-social-connections/), whether someone shared at least one meal last week predicts their life evaluation better than whether they are unemployed. For positive emotions, meal sharing explains more than income and unemployment combined. About 26% of US adults ate every meal alone in 2023, up 53% since 2003. [Dunbar (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40750-017-0061-4) found that evening meals, laughter and reminiscing drive the bonding. NPC memory is effectively a reminiscing engine. **Design:** make the oven and the evening supper the heart of Threewater.

6. **For sleep, lost time and arousal matter more than screen colour.**
   - 90% of 67 studies linked screen time to shorter or later sleep ([Hale & Guan 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079214000811)).
   - Apple's Night Shift made no measurable difference to sleep (n=167).
   - "Revenge bedtime procrastination" is about taking back control of one's time.
   - More daytime light and less night-time light were linked to lower psychiatric risk in 86,772 people ([Burns et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00135-8)).

   **Design:** build in stopping points and "the village sleeps" moments, not blue-light filters.

7. **Movement driven by novelty fades fast. Ritual lasts.** Pokémon GO added about 955 steps a day in the first week, and the effect was gone by week 6 ([Howe et al., BMJ 2016](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270)). Practices that last rest on ritual and small tokens: 530,772 Camino compostelas were issued in 2025; Japanese children get a stamp card for summer morning radio exercise (*rajio taisō*); station stamps (*eki* stamps) have existed since 1931; monastic hours are marked by bells.

8. **Primitive-skills and living-history communities are large and serious, and they run on safety culture.**
   - Primitive Technology has 11M subscribers and 1.21B views. Its creator calls it "a hobby" and says he lives in a modern house.
   - The SCA has about 60,000 participants, and a certified marshal must be present for combat.
   - Guédelon draws about 300,000 visitors a year.
   - Butser Ancient Farm and Lejre host families who live Iron Age life for a week.

   These make natural trailheads to point players toward.

9. **The starter world's verbs match the riskiest real-world activities.**
   - Foraging: 1,328 US emergency-department visits and 100 hospitalizations for mushroom poisoning in 2016. In one death-cap cluster, 3 of 14 patients needed liver transplants.
   - Fire: nearly 85% of US wildfires are human-caused.
   - Weapons: ancient sling projectiles weighed up to 500 g.
   - "Survival pressure" cultures: a GAO investigation linked deaths in youth wilderness programs to untrained staff and inadequate nourishment.

   The game should teach judgment and safety culture, and never invite players to replicate these activities alone.

10. **LLM-driven residents create a new hazard (my design judgment).** A trusted, warm NPC could "confirm" a real mushroom is edible, coach fire-lighting in a drought, or give sling instructions. Guardrails on what NPCs say about real plants, fungi, fire, weapons, water and health matter as much as any mechanic.

## 2. Detailed findings by subtopic

### 2.1 Nature connection

**Five pathways ([Lumber, Richardson & Sheffield 2017, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0177186)).**
- Design: two cross-sectional studies (n=203 and n=118) and a quasi-experimental walking study (n=72).
- Contact, emotion, meaning and compassion explained about 60% of the variance in nature connectedness; beauty mediated the others.
- Knowledge-based activities "were not" pathways. The authors write that activities "purely focussing on knowledge and identification may not be pathways to nature connectedness."
- In the walking study, the pathway-activity walk raised connectedness (t(23)=−3.99, p=.001). A plain nature walk did not (p=.380).
- Richardson's group later renamed "contact" as **senses**.

**Three Good Things in Nature ([Richardson & Sheffield 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1080/21711976.2016.1267136); [summary](https://research.childrenandnature.org/research/noticing-nearby-nature-increases-immediate-and-sustained-connectedness-with-nature/)).**
- RCT, n=92 (50 intervention, 42 control). For 5 days, participants wrote three good things they noticed in nature; controls wrote three factual things.
- Connectedness rose, the increase was sustained at **2-month follow-up**, and it was associated with psychological wellbeing.
- A smartphone version (McEwan et al. 2019, [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16183373)) reported mental-health gains "to clinical levels of significance", especially for people more distant from nature ([Derby evidence page](https://www.derby.ac.uk/research/themes/zero-carbon/zero-carbon-nbs-research-centre/nature-connectedness-research-group/nature-resources/evidence/)).

**Citizen science vs noticing ([Pocock, Hamlin, Richardson et al. 2023, People and Nature](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pan3.10432)).**
- 1,295 enrolled and 500 completed, across six randomized arms: FIT pollinator counts, butterfly counts, 3GTiN, combinations, and a wait-list control.
- Activities took about 10 minutes, up to five times over 8 days.
- All activities improved nature connectedness, happiness, sense that life is worthwhile, and life satisfaction.
- 3GTiN engaged senses, emotion and beauty more strongly. Citizen science gave a stronger sense of "helping take care of nature".
- **Only 3GTiN and the combined arms increased pro-nature conservation behaviour.**
- Citizen-science participants reported more **frustration** (weather, task complexity, technology, not seeing anything).
- The authors recommend designing citizen science to "enhance the pathways to nature connectedness".

**Awe walks (Sturm et al., *Emotion*; online Sept 2020, in print 2022; [UCSF summary](https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2020/09/418551/awe-walks-boost-emotional-well-being)).**
- 52 healthy older adults, 8 weeks, one 15-minute walk per week. Half were told to cultivate awe.
- The awe group showed more daily prosocial positive emotions (compassion, gratitude) and less daily distress.
- In their selfies, the self got smaller, the landscape got bigger, and smiles got broader.
- Evidence: small RCT.

**Awe more broadly ([Keltner 2023, *Awe*](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/whats_the_most_common_source_of_awe)).**
- Based on 2,600 narratives from 26 countries in 20 languages.
- The most common source of awe was **moral beauty**: "over 95% of the moral beauty that stirred awe worldwide was in actions people took on behalf of others."
- Keltner's "eight wonders of life" are moral beauty, collective effervescence, nature, music, visual design, spiritual/religious awe, life and death, and epiphanies. (The list is from the book; the fetched article doesn't enumerate it.)
- **Design relevance:** NPC kindness and communal ritual can produce awe just as vistas can.

**Restoration theories.**
- **Attention Restoration Theory** (Kaplan & Kaplan 1989; Kaplan 1995) proposes four restorative qualities: *being away*, *extent*, *soft fascination* and *compatibility* **[standard formulation; not re-fetched]**.
- **Stress Reduction Theory** (Ulrich) holds that natural scenes trigger fast affective recovery. The classic [Ulrich 1984 *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.6143402) study compared 46 cholecystectomy patients (23 matched pairs, 1972–1981). Those with a tree view had shorter post-operative stays, fewer negative nurse notes and fewer strong painkillers ([summary](https://housingandcommunityregeneration.nd.edu/research/view-through-a-window-may-influence-recovery-from-surgery/)). Caveats: small, old and observational.

**Forest bathing: evidence and critique.**
- [Antonelli et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00484-019-01717-x): 22 studies reviewed, 8 meta-analysed. Cortisol was lower in forest settings over the short term. The authors warn that "anticipated placebo effects can play an important role" and that data are "limited".
- [Kotera, Richardson & Sheffield 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-020-00363-4): 20 studies (12 RCTs), 2,257 participants. Short-term reductions in depression, anxiety and anger. Risk of bias and publication bias were rated **medium to high**; samples were mostly Asian; there were no comparisons with established therapies.
- Some pooled effects were implausibly large (e.g., Hedges' g ≈ −2.5 for depression), which is a red flag for small, unblinded studies.
- **Bottom line:** time among trees is pleasant and probably calming. Treat certified "forest therapy" health claims (e.g., immune effects) as unproven.

**The 120-minute threshold ([White et al. 2019, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-44097-3)).**
- n=19,806 from England's MENE survey.
- People spending 120–179 minutes a week in nature had higher odds of good health (OR 1.59) and high wellbeing (OR 1.23) than those with no contact.
- Benefits "peaked between 200–300 mins per week with no further gain".
- "It did not matter how 120 mins of contact a week was achieved (e.g. one long vs. several shorter visits/week)."
- Cross-sectional and self-reported.

**Green exercise and blue space.**
- Barton & Pretty 2010 pooled 10 UK studies (n=1,252). They reported the biggest mood and self-esteem gains in the **first 5 minutes**, larger gains near **water**, and strong gains for people with mental illness **[unverified; ACS page blocked]**.
- I could not read the BlueHealth 18-country paper ([White et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-87675-0)).

**Birds ([Hammoud et al. 2022, *Scientific Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-20207-6)).**
- Urban Mind app, 1,292 participants, 26,856 momentary assessments (2018–2021).
- Seeing or hearing birds was associated with better wellbeing. The effect carried into the next assessment, roughly 8 hours later.
- The link held for people with a depression diagnosis: "Everyday encounters with birdlife were associated with time-lasting improvements in mental wellbeing."
- Limitations: self-selected, mostly white, university-educated sample; seeing and hearing could not be separated.

**Citizen-science platforms (scale matters for bridges).**
- **iNaturalist:** 250M verifiable observations (June 17, 2025), nearly 4M observers and 400,000+ identifiers. More than 100M records are shared with GBIF and have contributed to 6,500+ publications ([blog](https://www.inaturalist.org/blog/113107-we-ve-reached-250-million-verifiable-observations)).
- **eBird:** 2 billion sightings (Aug 2025), 150M checklists and 1M+ users. Data used in 1,250+ peer-reviewed studies, including a 2025 *Science* analysis of North American bird declines using 36M observations ([Cornell](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/08/lab-ornithology-hits-2-billion-bird-sightings-3-million-recordings)).
- **Merlin:** 60,000 people downloaded it over a single weekend after one TikTok post. Staff report families birding together for the first time ([Flying Lessons](https://flyinglessons.us/2025/06/17/how-a-hugely-popular-smartphone-app-is-reshaping-the-hobby-and-reach-of-birding/)). No user totals were verified.
- **City Nature Challenge:** founded in 2016 as Los Angeles vs San Francisco. In 2025 it covered 669 cities, with 103,000 participants, 3.3M observations and 74,000 species ([Wikipedia table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Nature_Challenge)).
- **BioBlitz:** the term dates from a 1996 event in Washington DC organized by the US National Park Service (NPS) and US Geological Survey (USGS) **[unverified]**.
- **USA-NPN Nature's Notebook:** 30M+ phenology records since 2009, from 24,000+ observers at 18,000+ sites. Managers use them to time invasive-species treatments and to anticipate wildfire fuel conditions ([BioScience 2022](https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/article/72/9/908/6646741)).

**Nature journaling ([John Muir Laws](https://johnmuirlaws.com/deep-observation/)).**
- Three prompts: **"I notice", "I wonder", "It reminds me of"**.
- Laws on why speaking observations helps: "This process keeps observations in conscious working memory long enough for your brain to convert them to long term memories."
- "Deep observation is a skill which must be learned."

### 2.2 Craft and making

**Knitting ([Riley, Corkhill & Morris 2013, *Br J Occup Therapy*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.4276/030802213X13603244419077)).**
- Online survey of 3,545 knitters (Survey; mostly white women).
- "A significant relationship between knitting frequency and feeling calm and happy." Frequent knitters also reported better cognitive functioning.
- "Knitting in a group impacted significantly on perceived happiness, improved social contact and communication."
- The widely quoted "81% of respondents with depression felt happy after knitting" is **[unverified here]**.

**Arts and health overall.** WHO's scoping review ([Fancourt & Finn 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553773/)) covered 3,000+ studies and 900+ publications. It found "a major role for the arts in the prevention of ill health, promotion of health, and management and treatment of illness across the lifespan." Crafts fall within that scope, but the evidence varies widely in quality.

**Men's Sheds ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men%27s_shed)).**
- The first formal shed opened in Goolwa, South Australia, on 3 March 1993. There are now 900+ in Australia and 900+ in the UK (2023). Ireland had 200+ and New Zealand 100+ as of 2015.
- The motto "Men don't talk face to face, they talk shoulder to shoulder" was adopted after the 2008 national conference.
- A 2007 member survey: 99.5% "I feel better about myself", 97% "I have a place where I belong", 77% "I feel happier at home" (Survey; members only).

**Repair Cafés ([repaircafe.org](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/about/)).** Founded by Martine Postma in Amsterdam on 18 Oct 2009; now more than 2,500 worldwide. The organisation says: "The Repair Café teaches people to see their possessions in a new light. And, once again, to appreciate their value."

**The IKEA effect ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf)).**
- People who assembled IKEA boxes bid $0.78, versus $0.48 for non-builders.
- Origami makers valued their pieces at $0.23; outside bidders offered $0.05.
- A 63% premium for self-built Lego sets vanished when participants built and then un-built them.
- Abstract: "labor leads to increased valuation only when labor results in successful completion of tasks."

**Effort-driven rewards ([Kelly Lambert, *Scientific American*](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/depressingly-easy/)).**
- Her definition: an emotional payoff when effort produces "something tangible, visible and—this fact is extremely important—meaningful in gaining the resources necessary for survival."
- "Worker" rats that dug for their food spent about 60% more time on a hard puzzle and made 30% more attempts than "trust fund" rats that got food freely.
- Caveat: this is a rodent model plus a hypothesis about human depression, not clinical proof.

**Crawford ([*Shop Class as Soulcraft*, New Atlantis 2006](https://www.thenewatlantis.com/publications/shop-class-as-soulcraft)).**
- "The satisfactions of manifesting oneself concretely in the world through manual competence have been known to make a man quiet and easy… He can simply point: the building stands, the car now runs, the lights are on."
- "Craftsmanship might be defined simply as the desire to do something well, for its own sake."
- "The craftsman's habitual deference is not toward the New, but toward the distinction between the Right Way and the Wrong Way."

**Flow.** Csikszentmihalyi's conditions are clear goals, immediate feedback, and a balance between challenge and skill **[standard theory]**. Craft supplies all three naturally, because the material itself gives the feedback.

**Gardening ([Soga, Gaston & Yamaura 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211335516301401)).**
- Meta-analysis of 22 case studies and 76 comparisons.
- Found "reductions in depression, anxiety, and body mass index, as well as increases in life satisfaction, quality of life, and sense of community."
- Positive effects remained after correcting for publication bias.

**Hobby "booms" (spoon carving and greenwood work, blacksmithing, pottery, pandemic sourdough).** In this pass I found no robust participation data. The booms are media-reported and **anecdotal** (e.g., TV shows credited with bladesmithing interest; sourdough searches spiking in spring 2020). Kintsugi (repairing ceramics with gold-dusted lacquer) is a useful metaphor for visible repair. Its 15th-century origin story is legend **[unverified]**.

### 2.3 Primitive skills and living history

**Primitive Technology ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_Technology)).**
- John Plant, Far North Queensland; channel started in May 2015. About 11M subscribers and 1.21B views (June 2025).
- Rules: build "completely from scratch using no modern tools or materials". No narration, only ambient sound.
- He calls it "a hobby" and says he "lives in a modern house and eats modern food". Not survivalism.
- Book: 2019. Hiatus from Dec 2019 to Mar 2022.

**Experimental archaeology.**
- **[Butser Ancient Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butser_Ancient_Farm)** (founded 1970; Peter Reynolds was director 1972–2001): Iron Age roundhouses, a Roman villa and Neolithic, Bronze Age and Saxon houses. Archaeologist Mick Aston said Butser "revolutionised the way in which the pre-Roman Iron Age economy was perceived."
- **[Sagnlandet Lejre / Land of Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagnlandet_Lejre)** (1964, Hans-Ole Hansen): about 55,000 visitors a year. In summer, **families live in reconstructed dwellings for a week** in period clothing. It hosted Errett Callahan's flintknapping research.
- **[Guédelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gu%C3%A9delon_Castle)** (since 1997, Michel Guyot): a castle built only with 13th-century techniques and local materials, no power tools. About 300,000 visitors and €3M a year by 2014, which funds the work. The BBC called it "the world's biggest archaeological experiment." Its know-how informed work at Notre-Dame after the 2019 fire.

**SCA ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Creative_Anachronism)).**
- Began at a backyard tournament in Berkeley on 1 May 1966.
- 20,000+ paid members and about 60,000 participants (2020); 20 kingdoms.
- Arts and Sciences runs classes, competitions and "collegia".
- For combat, a certified "marshal of a given certification must be present." Pennsic is the largest event.

**Gatherings.**
- **[Rabbitstick](https://rabbitstick.com/)** (Rexburg, Idaho): the 2026 event is "Rabbitstick 38" and includes a Kids Camp. Its site frames primitive technology as "the original digital technology" (Watts 2000) — a pun on fingers as digits.
- Winter Count (Arizona) **[unverified; site not reachable]**.

**Archery.**
- **[Kyūdō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABd%C5%8D)**: over 100,000 practitioners worldwide. Ideals include *shin-zen-bi* (truth-goodness-beauty) and *seisha seichū*, "correct shooting is correct hitting". The shot follows eight fixed stages (*hassetsu*). Beginners train at a straw target (*makiwara*) about 7 ft away, so they focus on form rather than aim.
- **Rehabilitation:** "On 29 July 1948, the day of the Opening Ceremony of the London 1948 Olympic Games", Dr Ludwig Guttmann held the first Stoke Mandeville Games, an **archery** contest for "16 injured servicemen and women", the Paralympics' origin ([IPC](https://www.paralympic.org/ipc/history)).
- Standard range safety uses **whistle commands**: 2 blasts = to the line, 1 = shoot, 3 = retrieve, 5 or more = emergency stop **[standard range practice; not fetched]**.

**The sling ([Balearic slingers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balearic_slingers)).**
- Mercenaries for Carthage and Rome; about 2,000 served in Hannibal's vanguard.
- Each carried **three slings of different lengths**, braided from esparto or other plant fibre, and threw stone, clay or lead shot weighing up to 500 g.
- The ancient anecdote that children couldn't eat bread until they had hit it with a sling is **[unverified; attributed to classical authors]**. So is the modern Balearic sport *tir amb fona*.
- **Safety point:** the sling was a lethal military weapon; it is not a toy.

**Cordage, basketry, flintknapping and fire-making.** These are the core curriculum of primitive-skills gatherings. Standard practitioner safety includes eye protection and a leather pad for knapping, and working outdoors because knapping dust contains silica. Fire-by-friction belongs in supervised settings **[practitioner norms; not fetched]**.

### 2.4 Safety and responsibility (the part to design around)

**Wild mushrooms.**
- [CDC MMWR 2021](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/70/wr/mm7010a1.htm): in 2016 there were 1,328 US emergency-department visits and 100 hospitalizations. From 2016–2018, 8.6% of patients (48 of 556) had serious outcomes (arrhythmia, kidney and liver failure, seizures). Recent immigrants are at higher risk because of lookalike species. "Wild mushrooms should not be consumed unless identified by an expert."
- [MMWR 2017](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/66/wr/mm6621a1.htm): a death-cap cluster in Northern California had 14 cases and 3 liver transplants; an 18-month-old was left with permanent neurological impairment. Several people ate mushrooms that friends had foraged. "Inexperienced foragers should be strongly discouraged from eating any wild mushrooms."

**Foraging law.**
- England and Wales, [Theft Act 1968 s.4(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/60/section/4): picking wild mushrooms, flowers, fruit or foliage is not theft "unless he does it for reward or for sale or other commercial purpose".
- US National Parks, [36 CFR 2.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-36/chapter-I/part-2/section-2.1): disturbing plants is prohibited by default. The superintendent may designate "certain fruits, berries, nuts, or unoccupied seashells" that can be gathered by hand for personal use.
- Sweden's *allemansrätten* (right of public access) allows picking unprotected wild flowers, mushrooms and berries under the principle "do not disturb, do not destroy." Norway bans forest fires "between 15 April to 15 September" unless they obviously cannot spread ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_to_roam)).

**Fire.** "Nearly 85 percent of wildland fires in the United States are caused by humans," including "campfires left unattended" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/wildfire-causes-and-evaluation.htm)).

**Leave No Trace, the seven principles (exact titles, [LNT](https://lnt.org/why/7-principles/)).**
1. Plan Ahead & Prepare
2. Travel & Camp on Durable Surfaces
3. Dispose of Waste Properly
4. Leave What You Find
5. Minimize Campfire Impacts
6. Respect Wildlife
7. Be Considerate of Others

Campfire guidance ([LNT](https://lnt.org/why/7-principles/minimize-campfire-impacts/)):
- "Use established fire rings, pans, or mound fires where fires are permitted."
- "Keep fires small. Use only sticks from the ground that can be broken by hand."
- "Burn all wood to white ash and thoroughly soak it with water… cool to the touch."

**Scouting's graduated trust.** The [Totin' Chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totin%27_Chip) (1950) certifies knife, axe and saw handling: "Use knife, ax, and saw as tools, not playthings." Its companion, the Firem'n Chit, covers fire building and carrying matches. "Totin' rights" can be withdrawn. (The corner-cutting penalty is a common troop practice **[unverified]**.) The SCA's marshals are the adult equivalent. NOLS and Outward Bound safety material was not accessible in this pass.

**Wilderness therapy as a warning ([GAO-08-146T, 2007](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-08-146t.pdf)).**
- In 2005 alone, "33 states reported 1,619 staff members involved in incidents of abuse in residential programs."
- In 10 closed death cases, GAO found ineffective management, untrained staff, inadequate nourishment and reckless practices. Staff mistook heat illness for "faking it".
- **Lesson:** never frame deprivation or "tough love" as growth.

**Food safety.**
- Minimum internal temperatures ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures)): poultry 165°F (74°C); ground meat 160°F (71°C); whole cuts 145°F (63°C) plus a 3-minute rest; rabbit and venison 160°F.
- [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/prevention/index.html): "Do not feed honey to a child who is younger than 1 year old." Low-acid foods need pressure canning. "If in doubt, throw it out!"

### 2.5 Food and table

**Commensality ([Dunbar 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40750-017-0061-4)).**
- UK survey of about 2,000 adults, commissioned by the Big Lunch/Eden Project.
- "Those who eat socially more often feel happier and are more satisfied with life, are more trusting of others, are more engaged with their local communities."
- Evening meals bond people more than lunches. Laughter and reminiscing were key, and alcohol contributed (Obs).

**[World Happiness Report 2025, ch. 3](https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2025/sharing-meals-with-others-how-sharing-meals-supports-happiness-and-social-connections/)** (De Neve, Dugan, Kaats & Prati).
- Data: Gallup World Poll, 142 countries, 150,000+ respondents.
- Knowing whether someone shared at least one meal last week tells you more about their life evaluation than knowing whether they are unemployed (paraphrase). For positive emotions, meal sharing explains more than income and unemployment combined.
- About 26% of US adults ate all their meals alone in 2023, up 53% since 2003.

**The communal oven.**
- The French **four banal** was a lord's oven that peasants were *required* to use for a fee, alongside the lord's mill and wine press. These rights were "abolished on the night of the 4th of August 1789" ([Banalité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banalit%C3%A9)).
- **Toronto's Dufferin Grove Park** is the modern counter-model. Neighbours took over care of the park when the city had no upkeep money. Its wood-fired, Portuguese-style ovens "bring life back to the park", which people call a "community centre without a roof" ([PPS](https://www.pps.org/places/dufferin-grove-park); [park site](https://dufferinpark.ca/oven/)). Build dates and details of the Friday Night Supper are **[unverified]**.
- **Design point:** Threewater's oven can deliberately **reverse the four banal**: a commons, not a monopoly.

**Borgmann ([device paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Device_paradigm)).**
- Devices make goods effortlessly available. With central heating, family members "retreat into the solitude of their rooms".
- "Focal things and practices" gather people. His best-known examples are the hearth (Latin *focus*) and "the culture of the table".
- The widely quoted stove passage ("a focus, a hearth, a place that gathered the work and leisure of a family") is **[not verified verbatim here]**.

**Harvest customs ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest_festival)).**
- **Lammas** ("loaf Mass", 1 August): loaves from the new wheat were brought to church.
- The corn dolly was made from the last sheaf.
- The **harvest supper** was held "to which all who had helped in the harvest were invited".
- The modern church harvest festival dates from Rev. Robert Hawker, Morwenstow, **1843**.
- Other harvest festivals: Sukkot huts, the Mid-Autumn Festival, and the 1621 Thanksgiving.

**Cooking skills and potlucks.** They are plausible contributors to wellbeing, but I did not verify a primary review in this pass. Treat the evidence as moderate at best.

### 2.6 Body and rhythm

**Sleep.**
- [Hale & Guan 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079214000811): in 90% of 67 studies, screen time was linked to shorter or later sleep. Proposed mechanisms: **time displacement**, arousal from content, and light.
- [Kroese et al. 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00611/full) define bedtime procrastination as "failing to go to bed at the intended time, while no external circumstances prevent a person from doing so." It predicts insufficient sleep beyond demographics and self-regulation (n=177).
- "Revenge" bedtime procrastination, in Daphne K. Lee's popular description, is "people who don't have much control over their daytime life refuse to sleep early in order to regain some sense of freedom" ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedtime_procrastination)).

**Blue light.**
- Duraccio et al. 2021 (BYU, n=167): switching the screen from blue to yellow had **no effect** on sleep outcomes.
- A 2018 Rensselaer (RPI) Lighting Research Center study found Night Shift doesn't adequately reduce melatonin suppression. A 2019 mouse study found yellow light more disruptive than equally bright blue ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Shift_(software))).
- The landmark e-reader study (Chang et al. 2015) found real but modest effects: longer sleep onset (about 10 minutes), lower melatonin and later circadian timing after 4 hours of reading per night for 5 nights **[figures unverified; PNAS blocked]**.
- **Implication:** protect the *time*.

**Light by day and dark by night ([Burns et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44220-023-00135-8)).**
- 86,772 UK Biobank participants wore light sensors.
- Night light was associated with higher risk of depression, anxiety, PTSD, psychosis, bipolar disorder and self-harm. Day light was associated with lower risk.
- "Avoiding light at night and seeking light during the day may be a simple and effective, non-pharmacological means of broadly improving mental health" (Obs).

**Movement snacks: vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity, or VILPA ([Stamatakis et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-02100-x)).**
- 25,241 UK Biobank adults who don't exercise, followed for 6.9 years.
- About three 1-minute vigorous bursts a day were associated with 38–40% lower all-cause and cancer mortality and 48–49% lower cardiovascular mortality.
- Observational, with a 5.5% response rate.

**Walking and creativity ([Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014](https://news.stanford.edu/2014/04/24/walking-vs-sitting-042414/)).**
- 176 participants in four experiments. Creative output rose about 60% in one experiment, and even walking on a treadmill facing a wall worked.
- It helped divergent thinking (generating many ideas), not convergent thinking (finding the one answer). The boost lingered after sitting down.
- Oppezzo: "We're not saying walking can turn you into Michelangelo… But it could help you at the beginning stages of creativity."

**Location games ([Howe et al. 2016, BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i6270)).** 1,182 US iPhone users aged 18–35 walked about 955 extra steps a day in week 1 (about 11 minutes). The effect was "no longer observed after six weeks."

**Bells and hours.**
- Benedictine monasteries keep eight canonical hours (Matins/Vigils through Compline), based on Psalm 119:164, "Seven times a day I praise you." Church bells toll the hours ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_hours)).
- Plum Village's "bell of mindfulness": everyone stops and returns to their breathing when the bell sounds. The tradition also includes eating together and noble silence ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum_Village_Tradition)). The gatha's exact wording is **[not verified here]**.

**Sabbath and "digital sabbath".**
- [Allcott et al. 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658) (RCT, four weeks off Facebook) "reduced online activity, while increasing offline activities such as watching TV alone and socializing with family and friends… increased subjective well-being." Post-experiment use stayed persistently lower.
- Broader "digital detox" evidence is mixed **[review not fetched]**.

**Stamps, tokens and pilgrimage.**
- **Camino de Santiago** ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camino_de_Santiago)): 347,578 compostelas in 2019, 446,035 in 2023, 499,239 in 2024 and 530,772 in 2025. Pilgrims carry a *credencial* stamped at each town or refuge. They must walk 100 km or cycle 200 km.
- **Eki stamps** ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eki_stamp)): since at least 1931 (Fukui), now at most staffed stations in Japan, with themed "stamp rallies".
- **Radio taisō** (radio exercise) ([EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_calisthenics); [JA](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%A9%E3%82%B8%E3%82%AA%E4%BD%93%E6%93%8D)): NHK has broadcast it since 1928, with a 6:30 am summer slot. Each summer-holiday morning, children's attendance cards (出席カード) are stamped with the date, and full attendance earns pencils or sweets. A 1932 event counted 25.93M cumulative participants.

**Seasonal festivals.** Harvest customs are covered in 2.5. Solstice customs were not fetched.

**Songlines (handle with care).**
- Aboriginal Australian songlines ("dreaming tracks") encode navigation, law and ecological knowledge. A knowledgeable person can travel by "singing the words of the song, which describe the location of landmarks, waterholes, and other natural phenomena." They also work as a "cultural passport" across language boundaries ([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songline)).
- Much of this knowledge is restricted or sacred, and outsider popularizations have been criticized.
- **Design position:** take inspiration only at the level of the *general principle* ("songs as maps and memory"), make any in-game equivalents original to the fictional culture, and follow First Nations cultural protocols if Australian content is ever referenced **[recommendation]**.

## 4. Design implications for Open Legend

**Operating principles**
- The game is a **trailhead, not a destination**.
- Every bridge is **opt-in, small, safe by default, finishable and remembered by NPCs**.
- **Risky verbs (eat wild food, light fires, throw or shoot, enter water) bridge to *noticing, crafting or supervised community*, never to solo replication.**

### Fiber and cord
1. **"Twist Along" circles.** Make cord-twisting a side-by-side activity where resident NPCs chat and reminisce while you work (the Men's Shed "shoulder to shoulder" pattern).
   - *Bridge:* an optional 15-minute real two-ply cord or bracelet from yarn, raffia or jute. The NPC asks about it next session.
   - *Why:* completion (IKEA effect), effort-driven reward, group craft (Riley 2013).
2. **Finishable real counterparts for every craft chain.** Each in-game chain (reed → fiber → cord → net or sling pouch) gets a one-sitting real-world version: cord bookmark, woven coaster, a mini basket from paper strips.
   - *Why:* the IKEA effect disappears without completion.
3. **Visible repair.** Frayed cords, cracked bows and chipped pots can be mended rather than replaced. A mended item gains a line of history that NPCs recall (kintsugi logic).
   - *Bridge:* a monthly "mend one thing" prompt plus a link to find a Repair Café.

### Sling and bow
4. **The sling is invented, respected, and never a toy.** An elder tells how slingers once went to war with three slings (Balearic lore) and teaches that "a sling can kill".
   - *Bridge:* braid a sling as a craft object for display, not for throwing stones.
   - *Guardrail:* no real-world throwing prompts.
5. **Range discipline at the harvest-fair archery contest.** An NPC range marshal enforces whistle commands (2 = to the line, 1 = shoot, 3 = retrieve, 5 or more = stop), in the style of SCA marshals.
   - *Bridge:* "find a beginner session at an archery club" card.
6. **Reward form, not just hits (kyūdō's *seisha seichū*).** A clean-release meter and eight-stage "form" feedback, with makiwara-like close practice.
   - *Why:* this teaches flow and composure, not aggression.
7. **Adaptive archery lore and options.** Seated archery at the fair, with an in-world echo of the fact that the Paralympics began as a 1948 archery contest for injured veterans.
   - *Bridge:* links to adaptive-sports programmes.

### Fire
8. **Hearth as focus.** The fire is where the village gathers (Borgmann's *focus*). Being allowed to tend it requires trust earned from an elder, like a Firem'n Chit: clear ground, water at hand, never leave it unattended, and "burn all wood to white ash… cool to the touch" (LNT).
   - *Bridge:* "gather people around a light this week". A candlelit dinner counts. Never "go light a fire".
9. **Fire-danger seasons.** In dry, windy weeks NPCs refuse to light fires, echoing Norway's 15 April–15 September forest rule and local bans. In-game wildfires are caused by carelessness (reflecting that nearly 85% are human-caused).
   - *Bridge:* in summer, a "check your local fire danger or ban before any outdoor fire" card.
10. **Friction fire as a mastery ritual.** The bow drill is hard and success is celebrated, an effort-driven reward.
    - *Bridge:* only to supervised gatherings or classes (e.g., Rabbitstick-type events). Adults only.

### Foraging and berries
11. **The Herbalist's Rule.** The herbalist NPC teaches the CDC line: "never eat what an expert hasn't identified." In-game lookalike poisonings are consequential but not gory.
    - **All edible and toxic mechanics use fictional species**, so game "knowledge" never transfers to real fungi.
12. **"Forage with your eyes."** Pick in-game, but bridge to real-world photo and identify (iNaturalist, Seek, Merlin), always paired with a 3GTiN feeling prompt (Pocock 2023: combine noticing with recording).
    - Optional pointer: guided walks run by local natural-history or mycological societies.
13. **Commons ecology.** Reed beds and berry thickets regrow only if you "leave the roots and a share"; over-harvesting collapses them. Village custom encodes a local "leave no trace"-style code inspired by *allemansrätten*.
    - *Bridge:* a "know your local rules" card (Theft Act s.4(3), NPS 36 CFR 2.1, right-to-roam norms).

### Cooking and the table
14. **Evening supper as the social engine.** NPC mood and relationship gains are larger when eating together than alone. **Laughter and reminiscing are the bonding mechanic:** NPCs recall shared events at the table, which is exactly Dunbar's finding and what durable memory is for.
    - *Bridge:* a "Shared Supper Week" prompt to invite one person to a meal (cite WHR 2025).
15. **Harvest Home.** The fair closes with a supper where NPCs thank every helper by name and tell specific kindnesses back, turning moral beauty into collective awe.
    - *Bridge:* a potluck kit (sign-up list, a gratitude toast script).
16. **Cooking with real-world safety in the codex.** In-game doneness uses visual cues. The codex notes that the real world needs a thermometer (poultry 165°F, game 160°F).
    - *Bridge:* "cook one meal from scratch and share it".

### The bread oven
17. **The commons oven: the four banal in reverse.** Threewater's oven is run by a rota of villagers, not owned by a lord. Bake day is where news, gossip and quests circulate ("a community centre without a roof").
    - *Bridge:* a real-world bake-along on the in-game bake day (e.g., Saturday) with a no-knead recipe, plus a way to find community ovens and bake days.
18. **Lammas loaf.** On the real 1 August, the village bakes the "first loaf" from the new grain.
    - *Bridge:* "bake or buy a loaf and give half away".

### The forge and tools
19. **Apprenticeship with "right way / wrong way" feedback.** The master smith's feedback follows Crawford; the progression is tuned for flow (clear goals, feedback, challenge matched to skill).
    - *Bridge:* taster days at supervised forges; Men's Sheds and community workshops.
    - *Guardrails:* supervision, eye and ear protection, never heating galvanized metal (zinc fumes) **[standard metalworking safety]**.
20. **Graduated tool trust (Totin' Chip).** Knives and axes are "tools, not playthings". Misuse suspends your "totin' rights" with the elder until you repair the harm (restorative, not permanent).
    - *Bridge:* adult whittling or green-wood classes; any youth use needs supervision.

### Gardens, orchards and bees
21. **Hemisphere-aware garden almanac.** An optional latitude or hemisphere setting makes in-game sowing tips mirror real-season windows.
    - *Bridge:* "grow one pot of herbs" (gardening meta-analysis, Soga 2017).
22. **Adopt-a-tree phenology.** Each orchard tree moves through phenophases (budburst, flower, fruit). The NPC orchardist keeps records.
    - *Bridge:* adopt one real tree and note its first leaf and first flower in the in-game field book, or in Nature's Notebook for people who want the science.
23. **Bees and pollinators.** The beekeeper NPC teaches colony care.
    - *Bridge:* a 10-minute flower-insect count, or planting pollinator flowers, paired with noticing prompts.
    - *Guardrails:* sting allergy; real beekeeping needs a mentor; "no honey for babies under 1" (CDC).

### Water: fishing, boats and floods
24. **Fishing as stillness.** Rewards are tied to patience and noticing (birds, water sounds).
    - *Bridge:* "spend 20 minutes near water this week"; angling-club and licence pointers.
    - *Guardrails:* water safety; no solo cold-water prompts.
25. **Floods as solidarity.** Sandbagging, sharing food, sheltering neighbours. NPCs model never wading into floodwater.
    - *Bridge:* "know your local flood risk"; never go near flood water.

### Seasons, festivals and journaling
26. **Anchor festivals to real dates.** In-game seasons are accelerated, but the big festivals (solstices, Lammas, harvest fair, first-frost night) fall on real calendar dates, so the game points players to the season outside their window.
27. **The field book.** A diegetic journal with the "I notice / I wonder / It reminds me of" prompts. NPCs later bring up what you noticed, turning meaning into relationship.
28. **Three Good Things at dusk.** At in-game dusk a companion asks for three good things noticed today. Players can optionally add three real ones; five days in a row was enough in the RCT.
29. **Design for awe.** Vistas, storms and accurate night skies, and above all NPC acts of moral beauty that others witness and remember.
    - *Bridge:* an occasional "15-minute awe walk" invitation (Sturm): look for something vast or new.

### Body, sleep and rhythm
30. **"The village sleeps."** Near the player's chosen local bedtime, NPCs yawn, bank the fires and say goodnight. There is a natural save and stop point, no late-night-only events, no streaks, and nothing lost while away.
    - *Why:* the main mechanism is lost sleep time, not light. Also give daytime agency, because revenge bedtime procrastination is about reclaiming control.
31. **Dawn chorus and morning light.** An optional first-session-of-the-day ritual: "step outside for 5 minutes and listen for birds." Villagers do a short, gentle group stretch, like a radio-taisō routine.
32. **Movement snacks in natural wait times.** Bread proving, glue setting and a bowstring curing invite an optional 1-minute real burst (stairs, a brisk walk), always with a seated alternative.
33. **"Walk on it."** When stuck on an invention (sling, bow, oven design), the elder suggests a walk. The game pauses and saves. This fits Oppezzo & Schwartz's divergent-thinking finding.
34. **The mindfulness bell.** The village bell rings at the world's "hours" and NPCs pause for three breaths (Plum Village and monastic horarium).
    - *Bridge:* an opt-in real bell every 45–60 minutes of play as a stop-or-continue cue.
35. **Rest day.** One real day a week the market closes, NPCs picnic, and nothing expires. The world rewards being away (Sabbath logic, echoing the Allcott deactivation RCT).

### Pilgrimage, stamps and community
36. **The Wayfarer's Credencial.** A printable or phone "passport" stamped for real embodied acts: 3GTiN done, a loaf baked, a Repair Café visited, an awe walk taken.
    - **Honor system, no GPS, cosmetic rewards only** (a stamp on your character's pack).
    - Modeled on the Camino credencial, eki stamps and radio-taisō cards.
37. **Guild bridges.** Each in-game guild (fiber, bow, forge, oven, bees, boats) carries a "real guild" card pointing to real communities: SCA Arts & Sciences, archery clubs, Men's Sheds, Repair Cafés, community ovens, open-air museums (Butser, Lejre, Guédelon), and primitive-skills gatherings.
38. **"Silent Maker" challenge mode.** Inspired by Primitive Technology: no narration, from-scratch runs, told honestly. Its creator "lives in a modern house"; the romance is patience, not survivalism.
39. **Wayfinding songs.** Original in-world songs encode routes and seasons as memory aids. No borrowing of Aboriginal songlines or sacred material.

### Explicit safety guardrails
- **G1 Wild food:** never instruct or reward real-world eating of wild plants or fungi. Edibility mechanics use fictional species. Real-species content is for noticing only.
- **G2 LLM residents:** NPCs must refuse to identify real plants or fungi for eating (including from player photos). They must not give real fire-lighting, weapon or water-entry instructions, and they redirect to "ask a trained expert or local club." Add red-team tests for this.
- **G3 No risky real-world verbs:** no prompts to light fires, throw or shoot, enter water, climb, or handle blades or heat without supervision. Those interests route to supervised organizations.
- **G4 Law and land:** "check local rules" cards covering foraging, land access and fire bans. Embed the LNT seven principles in world custom.
- **G5 Minors:** assume mixed ages. Any craft with blades or heat requires an adult. No youth-targeted "survival challenges".
- **G6 No coercive loops:** no streaks, loss aversion, leaderboards or public tallies for real-world acts. Self-report only. Photos are opt-in and private by default.
- **G7 Accessibility:** every bridge has seated, indoor, urban, low-cost and weather-proof variants.
- **G8 Honest claims:** describe benefits as associations or invitations. No therapy or medical claims. Don't oversell forest bathing.
- **G9 No "tough love":** survival mechanics must never shame rest, food or help-seeking. NPCs treat distress as real, never as "faking it" (the GAO lesson).
- **G10 Food safety:** codex notes on temperatures, "no honey under 1", canning and allergens in recipes.
- **G11 Workshop safety:** eye protection, gloves or leather pad, outdoor ventilation for knapping, no galvanized metal at the forge, sharp tools stored and carried properly.
- **G12 Water:** never approach floodwater; drowning and cold-water risks in fishing and boating content.
- **G13 Sleep:** natural stopping points, no night-only exclusives, no absence penalties, opt-in bedtime cues.
- **G14 Wildlife:** watch from a distance and never feed (LNT). Hunting is shown with respect and consequence.
- **G15 Culture and partners:** follow Indigenous cultural protocols. Vet real-world partners for safeguarding and insurance before promoting their events.
