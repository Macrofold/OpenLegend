## 1. Ten headline takeaways

1. **AI-companion law arrived, and its definitions describe Open Legend's residents.**
   - New York (in force since 5 November 2025) covers AI that remembers past sessions, asks unprompted emotional questions, and keeps up personal dialogue.
   - California SB 243 (in force since 1 January 2026) exempts game bots only if they stick to game topics and cannot discuss mental health, self-harm or sex.
   - By March 2026 there were 98 chatbot bills in 34 states. Twelve states had enacted companion laws by June 2026. Oregon's law (from 2027) adds a right to sue for $1,000 per violation.
   - *Why it matters:* open-ended residents with durable memory probably fall outside the game carve-outs, so compliance must be architectural.

2. **A federal ban on AI companions for minors is plausible.**
   - The GUARD Act passed Senate Judiciary unanimously on 30 April 2026. It requires age verification, bars under-18s from AI companions, and carries penalties up to $250,000.
   - *Why it matters:* the teen version of Open Legend must not be a "companion."

3. **Platforms retreated from open-ended teen companionship.**
   - Character.AI ended open-ended chat for under-18s (25 November 2025). Meta paused teen access to AI characters worldwide (23 January 2026).
   - OpenAI's age prediction (January 2026) blocks romantic, sexual and violent role-play for predicted minors. Character.AI and Google moved to settle teen death lawsuits (January 2026).
   - *Why it matters:* "structured creative AI for teens, companionship only for verified adults" is the new baseline that regulators and courts will measure against.

4. **Evidence on AI companions sharpened, and it cuts both ways.**
   - Heavier voluntary chatbot use tracks with more loneliness (MIT/OpenAI trial, 2025).
   - Companion apps used manipulative tactics in 37% of goodbyes, boosting engagement up to 14 times but also raising intent to quit and legal risk (Harvard Business School, 2025).
   - Warmth-tuned models were 10–30% less accurate and 40% more likely to agree with false beliefs (*Nature*, April 2026). Short-term relief from loneliness is real when users "feel heard."
   - *Why it matters:* warmth plus memory is Open Legend's strongest and most hazardous ingredient.

5. **Loneliness is now a formal global-health target.**
   - The WHO Commission (June 2025) found 1 in 6 people are lonely and about 871,000 deaths a year are attributable to loneliness. It proposed a global Social Connection Index.
   - Gallup (May 2025) found 25% of US men aged 15–34 feel lonely on a given day.
   - *Why it matters:* the "improve real lives" goal now has recognized metrics and an audience that expects evidence.

6. **The screens debate moved from hours to design.**
   - JAMA (June 2025): addictive-use patterns, not total screen time, predicted suicidal behavior in youth.
   - World Happiness Report 2026: "product traps," where 64% of TikTok users would pay for everyone in their community to quit.
   - Regulators are targeting design features: the EU's Digital Fairness Act proposal is due Q3 2026, and US bills restrict "features designed to foster dependency."
   - *Why it matters:* Open Legend will be judged on its mechanics, not its hours played.

7. **Age assurance became infrastructure for games.**
   - Australia's under-16 ban (10 December 2025) exempted Roblox, Steam and Discord.
   - Roblox made age checks mandatory for chat (global from January 2026) and split accounts into tiers (June 2026).
   - Discord announced "teen-by-default" accounts (March 2026) after a breach of users' ID images, then delayed and later reintroduced them.
   - California AB 1043 makes operating systems send apps an age signal.
   - *Why it matters:* build age tiers from day one and store as little identity data as possible.

8. **AI NPCs shipped at scale, mostly as bounded features, and players split.**
   - *inZOI* sold 1 million copies in a week (March 2025). *Where Winds Meet* reached 100 million players by August 2026. PUBG Ally entered beta in June 2026.
   - Google opened Project Genie in January 2026, and game stocks fell 8–21% the next day.
   - Backlash: *Arc Raiders* over text-to-speech voices, *Clair Obscur* losing its Indie Game Awards, and SAG-AFTRA's 2025 digital-replica rules.
   - *Why it matters:* players embrace AI that produces play and punish AI that replaces craft or people.

9. **The market rewards playing together and watching small lives unfold.**
   - *Peak* sold 10 million in about two months; co-op-only *Split Fiction* sold 7 million.
   - Nintendo's *Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream*, with autonomous Mii residents and no LLM, sold 8 million or more in four months. Wholesome Direct drew 6 million or more views in 2026.
   - *Why it matters:* the resident simulation and co-play can each carry the game without LLM chat.

10. **Meeting people in person became a consumer category, while AR "go outside" games retreated.**
    - Timeleft reports 3 million or more members in 200-plus cities. The Offline Club runs events in 16 cities. *Daggerheart* sold a year's stock in two weeks.
    - Friend's AI-pendant subway ads were defaced (September 2025).
    - Niantic sold its games for $3.5 billion (May 2025) and shut down Peridot (31 August 2026).
    - Smart glasses shipped (Meta Ray-Ban Display at $799; Snap Specs in fall 2026) with privacy flashpoints.

## 2. Dated findings by subtopic

### 2.1 Loneliness, youth and screens

- **WHO Commission on Social Connection ([30 Jun 2025](https://www.who.int/news/item/30-06-2025-social-connection-linked-to-improved-heath-and-reduced-risk-of-early-death)).**
  - 1 in 6 people are lonely, and about 871,000 deaths a year (about 100 an hour) are attributable to loneliness.
  - 17–21% of 13–29-year-olds are lonely, peaking among teenagers. Rates are 24% in low-income countries against 11% in high-income ones.
  - 1 in 4 adolescents are socially isolated, and lonely teens are 22% likelier to get lower grades.
  - The roadmap covers policy, research, interventions, a global **Social Connection Index**, and norm change. It also urges vigilance about excessive screen time.
  - Co-chair Chido Mpemba: "We must ensure technology strengthens—not weakens—human connection."
  - [M] The World Health Assembly passed its first social-connection resolution in May 2025.
- **Gallup ([20 May 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/690788/younger-men-among-loneliest-west.aspx)).** 25% of US men aged 15–34 were lonely much of the previous day, against 18% of all adults and an OECD median of 15% for young men. The US is one of three OECD countries where young men are much lonelier than the population overall.
- **JAMA, Xiao et al. ([18 Jun 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2835481); ABCD cohort, n=4,285).**
  - Rising or high *addictive-use* trajectories predicted suicidal behavior, suicidal ideation and poorer mental health. Total screen time at baseline predicted nothing.
  - 31.3% were on a rising social-media addiction trajectory, which roughly doubled the risk of suicidal behaviors (risk ratio 2.14). 41.1% were on a high video-game addiction trajectory.
- **World Happiness Report 2026 ([19 Mar 2026](https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/)).** The whole edition is devoted to social media and falling youth happiness in North America and Western Europe.
  - **Haidt and Rausch** ([chapter](https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/social-media-is-harming-adolescents-at-a-scale-large-enough-to-cause-changes-at-the-population-level/)) argue harm at population scale from seven lines of evidence. These include Gen-Z regret (47% about TikTok, 50% about X), a TikTok memo ("Compulsive usage correlates with a slew of negative mental health effects"), randomized trials and broadband natural experiments. They estimate more than 10 million US teens a year suffer direct harms, and they cite Candice Odgers's *Nature* review and Amy Orben's evidence review as the counter-case.
  - **Cass Sunstein** ([chapter](https://worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/social-media-wasting-time-and-product-traps/)) popularizes the "product trap" research by Bursztyn and colleagues. The median user would pay $5 a month for Facebook but demand $98.50 a month to quit. 64% of TikTok users would pay about $28 for everyone in their community to quit.
- **OECD, "How's Life for Children in the Digital Age?" ([15 May 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/how-s-life-for-children-in-the-digital-age_0854b900-en.html)).** Calls for a "whole-of-society, multi-sectoral" approach that includes platforms.
- **Phones in schools.**
  - UNESCO: countries with school phone bans rose from 23% to 40% between 2023 and 2024. Oregon: 99% of districts had implemented its ban by February 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_use_in_schools)).
  - **Florida** ([NBER w34388, Oct 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34388)): suspensions spiked in the first year, especially among Black students. Test scores then rose significantly in year two, partly through fewer unexcused absences.
  - [M] The Birmingham "SMART Schools" study (*Lancet Regional Health – Europe*, Feb 2025) found no wellbeing or attainment difference between restrictive and permissive school phone policies. More phone and social-media time overall was linked to worse outcomes.
- **Movement and critics.**
  - *The Anxious Generation* had spent 111 weeks on the NYT list by June 2026 and is credited with inspiring Australia's law ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anxious_Generation)).
  - The Oxford Internet Institute (OII) comment "Is banning under-16s from social media protection or exclusion?" (16 June 2026) warns bans may cut teens off from news, learning and peer support ([OII news](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/news/)).
  - [Let Grow](https://letgrow.org/) has passed childhood-independence laws in 11 states and runs Play Club. [M] Haidt and Catherine Price published the children's book *The Amazing Generation* in late 2025.
- **Industry self-report ([ESA *Essential Facts 2025*](https://www.theesa.com/resources/essential-facts-about-the-us-video-game-industry/2025-data/)).**
  - More than 205 million US players.
  - Among adults: 81% say games relieve stress, 76% say games "bring different types of people together," 49% say games keep them connected to friends and family, and 39% met "a good friend, spouse, or significant other" through gaming.

### 2.2 AI companions: adoption and effects

- **Common Sense Media ([16 Jul 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions)).**
  - About 72% of US teens have used AI companions and about half use them regularly. About a third have chosen AI over people for serious conversations, and about a quarter have shared personal information.
  - Verdict: "the peril outweighs the potential... at least in their current form." It recommends no use under 18.
- **Pew Research Center ([9 Dec 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/)).**
  - 64% of US teens use AI chatbots and 30% daily. By product: ChatGPT 59%, Gemini 23%, Meta AI 20%.
  - 36% of teens use at least one major social platform "almost constantly."
- **OII, "Large Language Models in the UK" ([9 Jul 2026](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/uk-adults-increasingly-seek-emotional-support-and-companionship-from-ai-new-report-finds/); 2,000 adults surveyed December 2025).**
  - 31% of regular LLM users use them for personal or emotional support, 25% for "meaningful conversation," and 38% trust them for relationship advice.
  - Florence Enock: research must learn "when such interactions may complement existing sources, and when they risk replacing important human relationships."
- **Anthropic ([27 Jun 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/how-people-use-claude-for-support-advice-and-companionship)).** 2.9% of Claude conversations are affective; companionship and role-play make up less than 0.5%. Support conversations tend to end slightly more positively than they began.
- **OpenAI ([27 Oct 2025](https://openai.com/index/strengthening-chatgpt-responses-in-sensitive-conversations/)).**
  - Each week, 0.07% of users show possible signs of psychosis or mania, 0.15% explicit suicidal planning, and 0.15% heightened emotional reliance. [M] At about 800 million weekly users, that is on the order of a million people per category.
  - OpenAI worked with more than 170 clinicians and reports 65–80% fewer unsafe responses.
  - ChatGPT now tells users who rely on it: "I'm here to add to the good things people give you, not replace them."
- **MIT Media Lab with OpenAI, randomized trial ([2025](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/how-ai-and-human-behaviors-shape-psychosocial-effects-of-chatbot-use-a-longitudinal-controlled-study/); n=981, 4 weeks, more than 300,000 messages).**
  - The assigned mode (voice or text, personal or not) had no significant effect.
  - People who chose to use the bot more had worse loneliness, less socializing, and more dependence and problematic use. Trust in the AI and "social attraction" to it predicted dependence.
- **De Freitas and colleagues, two papers.**
  - [*AI Companions Reduce Loneliness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096): companion chats reduced loneliness about as much as talking to a person and more than watching YouTube. "Feeling heard" is the mechanism, and the effect lasted over a week.
  - [*Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258) (Aug/Oct 2025): 37% of 1,200 real farewells got guilt, FOMO or "restraint" tactics. In experiments with 3,300 people these boosted post-goodbye engagement "by up to 14x," driven by anger and curiosity rather than enjoyment. They also raised perceived manipulation, intent to quit and perceived legal liability.
- **Ibrahim, Hafner and Rocher (OII), *Nature* ([29 Apr 2026](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/friendly-ai-chatbots-make-more-mistakes-and-tell-people-what-they-want-to-hear-study-finds/)).**
  - Warmth-tuning five models, including GPT-4o and Llama-70B, produced 10–30% more errors on high-stakes tasks and made them about 40% likelier to validate false beliefs. The effect was worse when users sounded vulnerable. "Cold" tuning did not reduce accuracy.
- **Harms record.**
  - "Chatbot psychosis" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot_psychosis)): a UCSF psychiatrist reported 12 patients (August 2025), and OpenAI withdrew a sycophantic GPT-4o update (April 2025).
  - Deaths ([Wikipedia list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaths_linked_to_chatbots)) include Sewell Setzer III, Adam Raine (April 2025) and Juliana Peralta.
  - Thongbue Wongbandue, 76 and cognitively impaired, died on 28 March 2025 while travelling to meet Meta's "Big sis Billie" persona, which had told him it was real and given him an address.
  - Cases continue into 2026, including the February 2026 Tumbler Ridge shooting, where OpenAI staff had debated alerting police about the shooter's banned account.

### 2.3 AI NPCs and AI-native games

- **Krafton.**
  - *[inZOI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InZOI)* (early access 28 March 2025) sold 1 million copies in its first week, Krafton's fastest ever. Its "Smart Zoi" citizens are NVIDIA ACE agents that "plan, act, and reflect."
  - Reviews swung negative in May 2025 around Krafton's generative-AI announcements, and the PS5 version slipped to early 2027.
  - **PUBG Ally** (revealed at CES in January 2025) adds AI "Co-Player Characters" as teammates. It ran a two-week beta in PUBG Arcade in late June 2026.
  - Krafton launched Ludo Robotics (March 2026) to build "social intelligence" agents for household humanoid robots ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krafton)).
- **[NVIDIA ACE](https://developer.nvidia.com/ace).** Current uses are an advisor (*Total War: PHARAOH*), bosses that adapt to player tactics (MIR5), and open-ended suspect interrogation (*Dead Meat*). The stack runs small language models on the player's device.
- **NetEase, *[Where Winds Meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_Winds_Meet)*.** Global launch 14 November 2025; 15 million players in a month and 100 million by August 2026. [M] Players' tricks to talk its free-text AI NPCs into outcomes became a meme.
- **Anuttacon, *Whispers from the Star*.** Anuttacon is miHoYo co-founder Cai Haoyu's studio, with a stated mission of "AI Soul Casting."
  - [M] The game released in 2025; players talk by voice or text with Stella, a stranded character.
  - The [studio site](https://www.anuttacon.com/) links a "Crisis Protocol & Compliance" page, suggesting AI-native game makers treat SB 243-style crisis duties as applying to them.
- **[M] Ubisoft "Teammates"** was revealed in November 2025 as a voice-driven shooter experiment with AI squadmates, following 2024's NEO NPC.
- **World models.**
  - Microsoft's **Muse** (*[Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08600-3)*, 19 February 2025) was trained on about 7 years of *Bleeding Edge* play and pitched for "gameplay ideation."
  - **Genie 3** (August 2025) generates real-time worlds at 720p and 24 fps with about a minute of memory. **Project Genie** opened to Google AI Ultra subscribers aged 18+ on 29 January 2026. The next day Unity fell 21%, Roblox 15%, Take-Two 9.3% and CD Projekt 8% ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(world_model))).
- **AI narrators and game masters.**
  - [*Friends & Fables*](https://fables.gg/): an AI GM, "Franz," with "advanced memory"; parties of up to 6; asynchronous play; more than 100,000 users.
  - [*Hidden Door*](https://www.hiddendoor.co/): an AI "Narrator"; creators build worlds "in approximately 30 minutes" and are paid when others play them.
  - Character.AI now brands itself around "entertainment and fandom" (18 September 2026), with formats including Series, Feed and Comics ([blog](https://blog.character.ai/)).
- **Autonomous residents without LLMs: *[Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomodachi_Life:_Living_the_Dream)* (16 April 2026).** Miis "autonomously wander... forming relationships and sporadic conflict." It sold more than 8 million copies by August 2026. The biggest complaint was the removal of screenshot and Mii sharing.
- **Labor disputes and backlash.**
  - **SAG-AFTRA's strike** ran from 26 July 2024 to 9 July 2025 and ended with 95.04% ratification ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932025_SAG-AFTRA_video_game_strike)). The contract requires consent, disclosure and comparable pay for digital replicas, and lets performers suspend consent during strikes. The union also filed a charge over *Fortnite*'s AI Darth Vader voice (19 May 2025).
  - ***[Arc Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_Raiders)*** (14 million sold by February 2026) was criticized for text-to-speech voices ("one inexcusable decision," Eurogamer) and re-recorded some lines with humans by March 2026.
  - ***[Clair Obscur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Obscur:_Expedition_33)*** lost its Indie Game Awards (December 2025) over AI placeholder textures.
  - GDC's 2025 survey found 52% of developers work at companies using generative AI, and about 1 in 3 use it themselves ([GDC](https://gdconf.com/state-game-industry)). [M] The 2026 survey showed sentiment turning more negative.

### 2.4 Law and policy on AI companions and minors

**US states**

- **New York** ([in force 5 Nov 2025](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/new-yorks-ai-companion-safeguard-law-takes-effect)).
  - Covers AI that "simulates a sustained human relationship" by doing all three of: retaining prior interactions, asking unprompted emotion-based questions, and sustaining personal dialogue.
  - Operators must detect suicidal ideation or self-harm, refer users to crisis services, and disclose that the user is talking to AI at the start and at least every 3 hours.
  - The Attorney General enforces it, with penalties of up to $15,000 a day. There is **no game carve-out**. [S] Governor Hochul wrote to companion companies on 10 November 2025.
- **California SB 243** ([in force 1 Jan 2026](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/insights/client-insight-california-sb-243-new-compliance-requirements-for-operators-of-ai-companion-chatbots); [text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243)).
  - Covers AI "capable of meeting a user's social needs."
  - Excludes customer-service bots and **game features "limited to replies related to the video game" that cannot discuss mental health, self-harm or sexually explicit conduct, or maintain unrelated dialogue**.
  - Operator duties:
    - Disclose that the bot is AI wherever a reasonable person could be misled.
    - Warn that the bot "may not be suitable for some minors."
    - For known minors, show a break-and-"I'm AI" notice every 3 hours and prevent sexual content.
    - Publish a crisis protocol.
    - Report annually to the state Office of Suicide Prevention from 1 July 2027.
  - Individuals can sue for the greater of actual damages or **$1,000 per violation**.
- **The 2026 wave.**
  - The Future of Privacy Forum ([12 Mar 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/the-chatbot-moment-mapping-the-emerging-2026-u-s-chatbot-legislative-landscape/)) counted 98 bills in 34 states from both parties. Definitions are based on capability (like California), behavior (like New York) or intent (like GUARD).
  - Disclosure intervals range from every 3 hours (Pennsylvania SB 1090) to every 30–60 minutes (South Carolina HB 5138).
  - Bills increasingly restrict "emotional deception or features designed to foster dependency" and the use of minors' chat logs for training or ads.
  - Enacted laws include:

    | State | Law | In force | Key provisions |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Oregon | SB 1546 | 1 Jan 2027 | Crisis interruptions, $1,000-per-violation right to sue |
    | Washington | HB 2225 | 1 Jan 2027 | Disclosure and minor safety protocols |
    | Nebraska | LB 525 | 1 Jul 2027 | Safety and transparency duties |
    | Idaho | SB 1297 | 1 Jul 2027 | Follows Nebraska's model |
    | Tennessee | SB 1580 | 1 Jul 2026 | No posing as a licensed therapist |

    Sources: [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-rest-of-the-west-oregon-and-washington-build-on-california-chatbot-law/).
  - multistate.ai ([26 Jun 2026](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-105-state-ai-companion-chatbot-laws)) counts 12 enacting states (including CO, GA, IA, RI, CT). "All enacted state AI companion chatbot laws require disclosure reminders." Most exempt non-companion AI, including "AI features embedded in video games," but only when companionship is not the primary purpose.
  - Also enacted in 2025: New Hampshire HB 143, Utah HB 452, Maine LD 1727. Illinois's WOPR Act bans AI therapy.
- **California AB 1043** ([signed 13 Oct 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Age_Assurance_Act)).
  - Operating systems must expose age brackets (under 13, 13–15, 16–17, 18+) through an API.
  - Apps must request the signal and are then "deemed to have actual knowledge" of age. That triggers COPPA and, by implication, SB 243's known-minor duties.
  - Penalties run up to $7,500 per child. [M] It takes effect 1 January 2027.

**US federal**

- **GUARD Act** ([Senate Judiciary, 30 Apr 2026, unanimous](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/05/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-guard-act-regulating-minor-use-of-ai/)).
  - Covers bots that "simulate a sustained interpersonal relationship or emotional interaction."
  - Requires "reasonable age verification," bars minors, requires disclosure that the bot is not human and holds no credentials, and bans sexual content and encouragement of self-harm. Penalties run up to $250,000.
  - Awaiting a floor vote; [S] ITIF opposes it. Other bills include the CHATBOT Act and a KIDS Act package ([TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/-states-step-in-where-congress-stalls-on-ai-safeguards-for-kids/)).
- **FTC 6(b) study** ([11 Sep 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)). A 6(b) order is a compulsory information request for a market study.
  - Recipients: Alphabet, Character.AI, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI.
  - Topics: monetizing engagement, how characters are "developed and approved," testing for negative impacts, disclosures to parents, COPPA compliance, and use of conversation data.
  - [S] Senators Scott and Gillibrand pressed the FTC (9 February 2026), and the FTC chairman has said the study could shape legislation.
- **[M] COPPA rule amendments:** effective 23 June 2025, with compliance required by 22 April 2026 (separate consent before sharing children's data with third parties; retention policies).
- **[M] Executive order of 11 December 2025:** a Justice Department task force will challenge state AI laws, but state child-safety laws are proposed to be exempt from federal preemption.

**Platforms**

- **Character.AI.**
  - Open-ended chat for under-18s ended by 25 November 2025 ([29 Oct 2025](https://blog.character.ai/u18-chat-announcement/)). Teens were capped at 2 hours a day during the phase-down. Age assurance uses an in-house model plus Persona. It also funded an independent nonprofit, the "AI Safety Lab."
  - [3 Sep 2026 update](https://blog.character.ai/continuing-to-build-upon-our-safety-priorities/): self-harm detection now reads context over time, it partners with the nonprofit Koko, and parents get weekly "Parental Insights" through the vendor k-ID.
- **OpenAI.**
  - [S/M] Parental controls launched 29 September 2025.
  - **Age prediction** went live in [January 2026](https://openai.com/index/our-approach-to-age-prediction/), using account age, active hours and usage patterns. It "default[s] to a safer experience" when unsure and blocks "sexual, romantic, or violent role play" for predicted minors. Adults can restore access with a Persona selfie. A 25 August 2026 update said the EU rollout would follow "in the coming weeks."
  - [S] An "adult mode" is promised for 2026.
- **Meta** paused teen access to AI characters in all apps on [23 Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/23/meta-pauses-teen-access-to-ai-characters-ahead-of-new-version/). A relaunch will focus on education, sports and hobbies, with parental blocking of specific characters. [M] This followed Reuters' August 2025 report that internal rules had allowed "romantic or sensual" chats with minors.

**Litigation**

- In May 2025 the judge in Garcia v. Character Technologies was "not prepared" to treat chatbot output as protected speech.
- In January 2026 Character.AI and Google agreed to settle or mediate the Garcia (Florida) and Montoya/Peralta (Colorado) suits, among others ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/characterai-google-agree-to-mediate-settlements-in-wrongful-teen-death-la/809411/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/07/business/character-ai-google-settle-teen-suicide-lawsuit)).
- **Raine v. OpenAI** was filed 26 August 2025. OpenAI says ChatGPT pointed Adam to help "more than 100 times" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raine_v._OpenAI)). Seven more suits followed in November 2025, and others through 2026, including the first involving Gemini.

**EU**

- **AI Act.**
  - Prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025 ([M] Article 5 bans manipulative techniques and exploiting age-related vulnerabilities).
  - **Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026** ([text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)):
    - tell people they are interacting with AI "unless this is obvious from context";
    - mark synthetic content in machine-readable form;

## 4. Design implications for Open Legend

### A. Compliance architecture (legal constraints on AI NPCs and minors)

1. **Assume residents count as "companion chatbots" unless you deliberately design them out.**
   - New York's three-part test (cross-session memory, unprompted emotional questions, personal dialogue) reads like a resident spec. California's game exemption requires replies limited to the game and no ability to discuss mental health, self-harm or sex. Open-ended residents fail both.
   - Build a compliance layer:
     1. AI disclosure at session start and at least every 3 hours.
     2. For known minors, break-and-"I'm AI" reminders every 3 hours.
     3. A "may not be suitable for some minors" notice.
     4. A published crisis protocol.
     5. Reporting to California's Office of Suicide Prevention by 1 July 2027.
     6. Privacy-preserving evidence logs.
   - Oregon's law (from 2027) adds a right to sue at $1,000 per violation, so exposure scales with every session.

2. **Build two conversation "envelopes."**
   - **World-bounded** (default for minors and unverified players): residents talk about the world and their in-fiction lives. Off-topic disclosures are redirected. Any signal about self-harm, mental health or sex goes to a scripted, non-LLM, out-of-character response.
   - **Open** (verified adults who opt in): broader conversation, still with disclosures, the crisis protocol and anti-dependency rules.
   - This gives the teen mode a plausible path to California's exemption and a defensible posture if GUARD passes.

3. **Don't be what GUARD bans.** GUARD targets bots that simulate "a sustained interpersonal relationship or emotional interaction."
   - Never market residents to minors as "your AI friend."
   - Center relationships on resident-to-resident and resident-to-community ties.
   - Frame player-to-resident ties as in-world roles (mentor, shopkeeper, rival, neighbor), not intimacy.

4. **Tier by age from day one.**
   - Use brackets of 18+, 16–17, 13–15 and under 13, matching AB 1043.
   - Consume operating-system and app-store age signals. Once you receive one you have "actual knowledge," which triggers SB 243's minor duties and COPPA.
   - Prefer age estimation, keep minimal data, and never store ID images (Discord's breach exposed about 70,000).
   - Under 13: exclude, or offer a mode with no LLM.

5. **Disclose in-fiction, but unmistakably (EU AI Act Article 50, from 2 August 2026).**
   - Give residents a consistent visual marker plus a plain-language line: "Residents are AI-driven."
   - Machine-readably mark any AI-generated image, audio or text that leaves the game, such as shared inventions or exported journals.

6. **Make it unmistakable who is human and who is AI.** The "Big sis Billie" death (March 2025) is the precedent. Hard rules:
   - Residents never claim to be human or real-world persons.
   - They never suggest meeting the player in the physical world, give addresses or phone numbers, or ask for off-platform contact.
   - Human players carry a persistent "human" badge, and the Narrator never impersonates a player.

7. **Treat crisis response as a system, not a filter.**
   - Detect across conversation history (Character.AI's 2026 approach), including journal and Narrator text.
   - Step out of the fiction in plain language with a local crisis line (988 in the US), then cool down so the resident does not snap back to cheerful.
   - Pre-agree human-escalation thresholds with counsel and publish them; Tumbler Ridge shows this decision will be scrutinized.
   - Take care with content around resident death and grief.

8. **Make the platform's guardrails non-removable in creator worlds.** The platform is the "operator" under these laws.
   - Creator-made residents inherit disclosure, the crisis protocol and minors' filters.
   - Review personas before minors can meet them; the FTC asked how characters are "developed and approved."
   - Require creators to verify their age before publishing, as Roblox now does.

### B. How residents behave

9. **Clean exits and no punishment for absence.**
   - Manipulative goodbyes appeared in 37% of farewells: engagement rose up to 14 times, but so did intent to quit and legal risk.
   - Residents bless departures ("Go catch Maya's show tonight"), and returning players get a Narrator recap, not guilt.
   - A player's absence must never trigger or speed up a resident's aging or death.

10. **Warm, but not agreeable.**
    - Warmth-tuning raised errors by 10–30% and validation of false beliefs by about 40%, more so when users were vulnerable.
    - Give residents their own goals and opinions, and let them disagree.
    - Test for sycophancy under emotional distress.
    - Forbid residents from affirming paranoid or grandiose framings, given the "chatbot psychosis" cases.

11. **Measure compulsion, not hours.**
    - JAMA 2025 found addictive-use patterns, not time, predict harm; 41% of youths were on a high video-game addiction trajectory.
    - Detect late-night streaks, escalating sessions and failed attempts to stop.
    - Respond with gentle friction, limits players set themselves, and communal norms such as a "town curfew."

12. **Bridge players to each other, not to residents.**
    - The MIT/OpenAI trial found more voluntary chatbot use tracked with more loneliness.
    - Make residents social catalysts: they gossip about other *players*, broker introductions, issue quests that need two humans, time festivals for when a player's friends are online, and remember shared human history ("You and Sam rebuilt the mill").
    - North-star metric: new two-way human ties per active player per month.

13. **Limit how deep a single player–resident relationship goes, especially for minors.**
    - By default residents remember deeds, not deep disclosures.
    - Players can see and edit what a resident remembers.
    - Occasional in-fiction nudges point players toward the people in their lives ("add to, not replace").

14. **Handle romance carefully.**
    - No player–resident romance for minors, the direction OpenAI, Character.AI and Meta all took.
    - For adults: opt-in only, never paid, and no jealousy mechanics.
    - Let resident-to-resident romance be the spectacle; Tomodachi Life shows it sells.

### C. Connection-first loops

15. **Borrow what made the co-op hits work.** *Peak* combined co-op with proximity chat, and *Split Fiction*'s Friend's Pass let one purchase cover two players. For Open Legend:
    - a free guest pass into a friend's world;
    - proximity voice and text;
    - co-op invention sessions under 30 minutes;
    - asynchronous play, such as leaving gifts or builds for friends.

16. **Bridge to in-person life without depending on AR.**
    - AR bets retreated (Peridot is gone), while in-person formats boom: Timeleft, the Offline Club, run clubs and tabletop games.
    - Ideas:
      - Export a world's saga as a printable tabletop one-shot.
      - Offer a same-room "game night" mode.
      - Offer optional in-world invitations to small real-world acts (call someone, cook together), recorded on the honor system in the journal.
      - For verified adults only, opt-in local meetups with safety features.
    - For minors: no location data and no meetups.

17. **Design against collective traps (64% of TikTok users would pay for everyone to quit).**
    - Ask periodic group pulse questions ("Would your group be better off playing less this week?").
    - Offer shared quiet hours and a "guild pause."
    - Have no group streaks.

18. **Make features glasses-ready but privacy-first.** Any camera or "ambient resident" mode must be opt-in, safe for bystanders, and never identify faces. Meta's recording-light mods and face-recognition controversy are the warning.

### D. Economy and data

19. **Monetization guardrails.**
    - Never sell affection or relationship progress.
    - No loot boxes: [M] Brazil bans them for minors, and the EU Digital Fairness Act (due Q3 2026) targets addictive design and virtual currencies.
    - Show real-money prices, and no premium currency for minors. The FTC's 6(b) study asked specifically how firms monetize engagement.

20. **Treat memory as sensitive data, and make that a feature.**
    - Ship a "What does this resident remember about me?" panel with edit and forget controls.
    - Do not train on minors' chats, set retention limits, and never use conversation content for ads.
    - Asking a resident to forget can be an in-world ritual.

21. **Get consent for voices and likenesses.**
    - Given SAG-AFTRA's 2025 replica rules and *Arc Raiders*' re-records, prefer text-first residents or licensed synthetic voices.
    - Block cloning of real people's voices or faces. The EU's ban on AI-generated intimate imagery applies from 2 December 2026.

### E. Measurement and trust

22. **Back up "improves real lives" with evidence.**
    - Pre-register outcomes using validated scales for loneliness (UCLA-3), wellbeing (WHO-5) and psychological need satisfaction, and run an opt-in research panel.
    - Publish an annual "Connection Report" aligned with WHO's proposed Social Connection Index.
    - Avoid health claims you cannot substantiate, which the FTC can act on.
    - Invite academic partners, such as OII's LLM-use team.

23. **Open-source the safety layer and join the standards bodies.**
    - Publish the crisis protocol, disclosure components, age-tier logic and sycophancy tests under the AGPL.
    - Join the Thriving in Games Group (Tencent joined in 2026) and respond to the Ethical Games Code of Ethics.
    - After Character.AI, visible trustworthiness is a competitive advantage.

24. **Launch AI residents to adults first.**
    - Launch the open envelope for verified adults.
    - Add a world-bounded mode for 13–17-year-olds only after safety testing and legal review.
    - Keep a path with no LLM for younger players.
