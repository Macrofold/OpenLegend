# Positioning and copy — create a world by playing it

Status: **draft concepts**, September 19, 2026. These describe the intended experience. None should be published as present product capability until demonstrated. The suggested wording is not trademark-cleared or tested with an audience.

## Positioning hypothesis

Open Legend is a world-building simulation in which playing, experimenting and revising mechanics are part of the creative process. Creators choose an initial world and its rules, introduce new possibilities, then watch people and environments respond over time.

The strongest initial message to test is **“Create a world by playing it.”** Support it with three observable behaviors: an interaction becomes a saved mechanic; its rules can be edited; the revised mechanic works consistently when reused.

The user endorsed the expanded line **“Create a world by playing it. Share what you discover.”** A later demonstration can follow a refined mechanic into a reusable pack and another creator's world. A separate patronage message to test is **“Help bring this world into existence, and become part of its history.”** Recognition is explained in the [patron proposal](patrons-contributors-and-world-history.md).

“AI-powered,” “generative” and “agent platform” are secondary explanations. For developers, describe the proposed architecture separately: an open simulation and interaction interface, with Macrofold providing reusable AI execution and optional managed infrastructure.

## Audiences and entry points

| Audience hypothesis | What they might value | Demonstration to test |
|---|---|---|
| Sandbox players who invent their own activities | Trying an idea and seeing it become repeatable | Create the tree spell, then cast it again |
| World builders and game masters | Setting rules, inviting people, guiding a distinctive setting | Add magic to one world while another retains ordinary survival |
| Simulation enthusiasts | Consequences emerging from interacting systems | Compare two worlds after introducing a new source of food or healing |
| Modders and developers | Inspectable mechanics, versioned packages, extensibility | Inspect a generated definition and edit one constraint |

Start with the creator/curious-player overlap. A broad research or enterprise story can follow evidence of demand rather than crowding the first message.

## Draft homepage copy

**Headline:** Create a world by playing it.

**Supporting copy:** Enter a living world, try an idea, and shape what becomes possible. Create a spell, refine its rules, and watch your world's inhabitants discover the consequences.

**Creator-focused alternative:** Your next game starts inside the world.

**Supporting copy:** Choose the foundations. Add mechanics through play. Refine them with AI or edit their definitions yourself, then invite others into the world you've made.

**Short description:** A world-building simulation where your experiments can become lasting rules.

**Developer-focused description:** An open simulation platform for evolving worlds, with reusable mechanics and AI workflows powered by Macrofold. This wording depends on the eventual open-source release and integration actually existing.

Candidate calls to action: **Watch a world take shape**, **Try the creator demo**, **Start your world**. Select a call to action that has a real destination at that stage of development.

## The magic-tree creator journey

1. **Enter an owned world in creator mode.** Walk up to a tree and select it. This authoring authority is distinct from the ordinary character's abilities.
2. **Express the idea.** “Use my magic tree spell on this tree.” If the effect is unspecified, the authoring interaction establishes what it means. A demonstration might choose “make its leaves glow at night.” That particular effect is a draft example.
3. **Inspect a concrete definition.** Show the proposed effect, eligible targets, duration and any cost. Unsupported effects require additional authoring or engine work; the UI must not pretend they exist merely because text was generated.
4. **Try and save it.** Apply a validated preview, then save a reusable mechanic in this world's library. Show it working a second time to establish that it persists beyond one response.
5. **Refine it through play.** Open the mechanics list and say “Only mages can cast this.” Show the changed prerequisite and a new version. If the world has no mage class yet, establish a supported class/role or equivalent skill requirement rather than silently inventing an entire class system.
6. **Verify the consequence.** A qualifying character can cast it; an ordinary non-mage cannot. Creator override remains explicitly separate. Decide how existing enchanted trees are affected rather than silently rewriting their history.
7. **Choose its distribution.** Keep the definition private to the world, share a package under chosen terms, or publish an eligible version to an open library. Those are separate actions from saving it.

Characters need their own knowledge and permissions to use a new mechanic. Adding a spell to the world's capabilities need not teach every resident that spell immediately.

## Hero demo concept

A short recording should establish: tree before → creator request → actual effect → saved mechanic → edit to mage-only → visible enforcement → later reuse. Keep the mechanics list visible long enough to show that the behavior is inspectable and editable.

Use actual working gameplay when available. Label concept footage and show or disclose generation waits rather than implying measured instant execution. A later follow-up can explore inhabitants reacting to the spell; do not imply unimplemented autonomous discovery in the first demo.

## Messages to keep precise

| Candidate claim | Intended meaning / qualification |
|---|---|
| Create a world by playing it | Authorized creators can author supported mechanics through in-world interaction |
| Your world, your rules | Creator control within the platform's actual capabilities and the world's access policy |
| Open platform, private worlds | Platform code and starter content can be open while separate world data/packs have distinct access and license terms |
| Shape a world that remembers | Persistence of approved state and character memory, not perfect recall or simulated consciousness |

Avoid “anything is possible,” “instant game creation,” “unlimited AI,” “guaranteed creator income,” “every creation is legally yours,” or “nobody can copy your mechanics.” See the [ownership boundaries](open-platform-and-private-worlds.md).
