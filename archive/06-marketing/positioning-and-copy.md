## Positioning hypothesis

Open Legend is a survival-adventure where a player can explore, face meaningful obstacles, invent practical alternatives and encounter consequences shaped by people with their own aims. Useful rewards, progression, crafting and building make those ideas part of an enjoyable game. A small instance of real invention and independent character consequence belongs in the opening experience, not only in the distant platform vision.

The endorsed line is **“Create a world by playing it. Share what you discover.”** Explain it through an actual activity and payoff. An ordinary player does not acquire creator authority over all world rules; an invented capability still needs supported realization, knowledge and permission. Hosting is a delivery choice, not the player's genre or the sole reason to care.

The core positioning question is why someone would choose their next gaming session here. Test an understandable attraction, rewarding actual play and a reason to try another approach or encounter a later consequence. Art, motion and sound should express that attraction as well as provide clarity. Do not rely on “AI-powered,” “generative” or “agent platform” as the primary player benefit.

A separate authoring proposition is a world-building environment where authorized creators choose foundations, refine mechanics and see people and environments respond. A developer proposition concerns an open engine and reusable AI execution. These may interest related audiences but are not automatically the same buyer or the first commercial package.

## Audiences and entry points

| Audience hypothesis                    | What they might value                                                           | Demonstration to test                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Survival-adventure and sandbox players | Worthwhile goals, choices, reward and a place worth returning to                | A connected expedition, obstacle, useful improvement and another activity          |
| Players interested in experimentation  | Inventing a practical alternative instead of only selecting a predefined answer | A supported non-canned invention realized and useful beside a valid known approach |
| Consequence-focused simulation players | Characters with independent aims and understandable later responses             | Someone reacts to what they actually perceived, affecting a later opportunity      |
| Worldbuilders and game masters         | Authoring a distinctive setting and inviting others                             | A separately qualified creator session with explicit authority                     |
| Modders and developers                 | Inspectable definitions and an extensible open project                          | Inspect a real mechanic and make a bounded useful contribution                     |

Prioritize the intended adventure audience for the first game test. Test creator and hosting preferences separately rather than counting all technology or authoring interest as game demand. Do not force invention, persuasion or social participation when a reliable known route is a valid choice.

## Draft homepage copy

**Headline:** Create a world by playing it.

**Companion line:** Share what you discover.

**Player-facing supporting copy:** Explore a living world. Invent a practical way through an obstacle, meet people with plans of their own, and discover what your choices change.

**Short description:** A survival-adventure built around useful invention, independent characters and consequences worth returning to.

Publish these claims only to the extent a representative build delivers them. A creature's personality description is not proof of independent behavior, and saved text is not proof of useful invention or repeat value.

**Creator-focused alternative:** Your next world starts inside the game.

**Creator supporting copy:** Choose the foundations. Author supported mechanics through play. Refine their definitions with AI or direct editing, then decide who can use the world you've made. Sharing packs and broader authoring remain separately qualified capabilities.

**Developer-focused description:** An open engine for authored realities, with a playable bundled world and reusable AI workflows through Macrofold. Technical material should distinguish current implementation, supported capabilities and planned integration using their existing owners.

Candidate calls to action: **Watch the adventure**, **Request a playtest**, **Play the demo**. Use only a destination that genuinely exists. **Try the creator demo** and **Start your world** belong to separately available creator/hosting offers; do not imply they are already implemented or selected.

A separate patronage message is **“Help bring this world into existence, and become part of its history.”** Explain recognition under the [patron proposal](patrons-contributors-and-world-history.md), and do not treat supporter payments as proof of demand for the game itself.

## Hero demo concept

Show a player's goal → readable obstacle → meaningful choice → a practical realized invention or known method → an independent character response → useful payoff → another occasion when the consequence matters. This is an illustrative storyboard, not a mandatory quest or predetermined outcome. The player can choose the known method; do not secretly script a character's cooperation to make a clip work.

Use the integrated game and a qualified small scenario. Preserve normal waits or disclose material time edits, show limitations honestly and keep the actual game configuration separate from recording-only adjustments. Another session should demonstrate continued value rather than merely repeat the same prepared spectacle.

The creator sequence below is a separate demonstration for an authoring audience. It can explain inspectability and editability without replacing the adventure's player-facing introduction.

## The magic-tree creator journey

1. **Enter an owned world in creator mode.** Walk up to a tree and select it. This authoring authority is distinct from the ordinary character's abilities.
2. **Express the idea.** “Use my magic tree spell on this tree.” If the effect is unspecified, the authoring interaction establishes what it means. A demonstration might choose “make its leaves glow at night.” That effect is a draft example requiring support.
3. **Inspect a concrete definition.** Show proposed effect, eligible targets, duration and cost. Unsupported effects require authoring or engine work; generated text does not make them exist.
4. **Try and save it.** Apply a validated preview, then save a reusable mechanic in this world's library. Show it working again to establish persistence beyond a single response.
5. **Refine it through play.** Open the mechanics list and say “Only mages can cast this.” Show the changed prerequisite and actual authored revision. If the world has no mage class, establish a supported role or equivalent requirement rather than silently creating an entire class system.
6. **Verify the consequence.** A qualifying character can cast it; a nonqualifying character cannot. Creator override stays explicit. Decide how existing enchanted trees are affected rather than rewriting their history silently.
7. **Choose its distribution.** Keeping a definition private, sharing a package under selected terms and publishing to an eligible library are separate actions from saving. Each requires its own supported capability and permission.

Characters need their own knowledge and permissions; adding a spell does not automatically teach it to every resident. A creator recording can show tree before → request → actual effect → saved definition → refinement → enforcement → reuse. It must not imply unimplemented autonomous discovery or a measured instant response.

## Messages to keep precise

| Candidate claim                | Intended meaning / qualification                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Create a world by playing it   | Practical invention and consequence within the game's supported capabilities; broader authoring belongs to authorized creator controls |
| Your world, your rules         | Creator authority over the permitted world, not universal player authority                                                             |
| Open platform, private worlds  | Repository code and separate world data/packs have their own documented access and licensing boundaries                                |
| Shape a world that remembers   | Persisted outcomes and supported character memory, not perfect recall or simulated consciousness                                       |
| People with plans of their own | Observable supported behavior with independent aims, not a personality paragraph or guaranteed cooperation                             |

Avoid “anything is possible,” “instant game creation,” “unlimited AI,” “guaranteed creator income,” “every creation is legally yours” and “nobody can copy your mechanics.” See [ownership boundaries](open-platform-and-private-worlds.md). A managed subscription, a paid game and authoring rights are separate purchase propositions; use only the approved terms of the actual offer.
