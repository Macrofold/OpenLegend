## Let the player's experience carry the explanation

Show someone wanting something, choosing an approach and living with the result. The audience can understand a capability through what it lets a player do and why the outcome matters. Name the feature when that helps clarify what they have seen; every moment need not become a feature announcement.

For Open Legend, connect an attractive activity with a distinctive consequence: a useful invention changes an approach, a person acts on their own aims, or something the player did matters later. The [hero demonstration](positioning-and-copy.md#hero-demo-concept) owns the fuller example. Establish what kind of game people will play as well as making them curious about one story.

### Reference: Outcast Tales

The [Outcast Tales demo trailer](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5111950/Outcast_Tales_Demo/) demonstrates the technique through a reckless charge that ends in defeat, companions moving between affection and hostility, dangerous encounters and a narrator questioning a route choice. Its [narration transcript](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/xb6twam) supplies context for the short visual sequence.

Battles, relationships and consequential decisions become understandable through situations. The narration creates anticipation, reversal and humor while the footage shows what happens. This is a presentation reference; Open Legend can use its own tone, including wonder, warmth, tension and satisfaction.

## Build a small story around a choice

- **Make the intention readable.** Establish what the player wants and what makes reaching it interesting.
- **Let the decision create anticipation.** Show the tempting opportunity, dangerous route, disagreement or improvised approach before the result.
- **Make the consequence legible.** Preserve the useful reward, changed situation or later opportunity that gives the choice meaning.
- **Give narration a point of view.** A confident plan followed by an unexpected result can communicate freedom and stakes naturally. Do not describe every visible action aloud.
- **Show why success feels good.** Beauty, discovery, mastery, relief and useful improvements belong alongside danger and failure.
- **Keep a coherent thread.** Related moments help viewers follow the player. A montage can establish breadth without implying that unrelated clips form one causal sequence.

### Illustrative treatments

These are hypothetical presentation ideas, not claims about the current build or ready-to-publish scripts. Select a treatment when representative footage supports the whole event.

| Treatment                         | Possible narration                                                           | What the footage must show                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A useful solution matters later   | “We needed a way across. So we made one. Someone else found it useful, too.” | The obstacle, the supported solution and another person's actual use; distinguish a known method from a genuinely invented one |
| A relationship complicates a goal | “We lent her our best tool. Then we needed it back.”                         | Why the loan made sense, the other person's situation and the choice that follows                                              |
| A tempting destination            | “We could head home with what we'd found. Or see what was beyond the ridge.” | The actual choice, understandable stakes and real outcome                                                                      |

Choose the story from the recorded experience. Preserve what caused the result.

## Adapt to the format

| Format                                   | Role of narrative                                                                                            | Clarity the audience still needs                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Short trailer or social clip             | One desire, decision and memorable result; action, sound and concise narration carry much of the explanation | The activity, its appeal and the available next step                                                                                                |
| Longer trailer or gameplay video         | A connected activity with payoff and later consequences                                                      | The ordinary play loop, player control, unfamiliar decisions and representative pacing                                                              |
| Article or devlog                        | Open with a concrete moment, explain how and why it works, then return to the example                        | Clear headings, named capabilities, evidence and current work versus plans                                                                          |
| Storefront or project landing page       | A strong scene invites interest; readable gameplay descriptions explain the offer                            | What kind of game it is, what players do, what makes it distinctive and what the available build contains                                           |
| Kickstarter or another crowdfunding page | A story can open the page and illustrate each major promise                                                  | Premise and play loop, demonstrated versus planned scope, platforms, development status, delivery plan, funding purpose, rewards and material risks |

A crowdfunding page can benefit from narrative. Someone considering a pledge also needs a structured explanation of the concrete project and offer. Pair an evocative opening with headings and direct answers; do not make readers reconstruct the project from a trailer. Actual offer choices belong in the [commercial decision process](../../docs/projects/initial-commercial-offer-decision-brief.md).

## Keep the promise connected to the game

Follow the [public-claim guidance](positioning-and-copy.md#messages-to-keep-precise): distinguish ordinary play from creator authority, demonstrated behavior from concepts, and representative pacing from material edits. A compelling story must preserve actual causes and permitted information.

Before publishing, ask whether a newcomer can explain what they would do, why the moment appeals to them and how their choices matter. Use the existing [message experiments and measures](ideas-channels-and-experiments.md#message-experiments) to test that understanding. Enjoying a trailer and enjoying the game are separate evidence.
