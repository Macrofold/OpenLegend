# Character experience and inner life

This proposal describes the kind of characters OpenLegend should make possible and the ideas informing that ambition. The [compelling characters feature spec](../../docs/projects/compelling-characters-feature-spec.md) owns the proposed behavior; the [character experience tracker](../../docs/maintainers/character-experience.md) owns follow-through. This companion does not establish another work queue or claim that the proposed experiences are implemented.

## A particular person living a life

The ambition is a character whose life makes sense from inside their situation. They have things to protect, enjoy, discover, avoid, and become. Food matters, but so can an unfinished invention, an awkward friendship, an attractive view, wounded pride, or the pleasure of getting better at something. A character can want company and fear embarrassment, enjoy a demanding task, or prefer a familiar companion while needing time alone. These are possibilities for authored people and worlds, not a universal checklist.

The game benefits when these concerns create recognizable choices, surprises that make sense, and relationships the player cares about. NPC enjoyment and player enjoyment are different questions: a frustrated inventor can be compelling to help, challenge, or watch. The target is a life with continuity, freedom, consequence, and variation. Survival, social drama, discovery, mastery, beauty, and quiet pleasure can each matter without becoming everyone's compulsory loop.

## Context is a window onto the world and the person

A decision context should give the character a grounded view of what is happening and the relevant inner experience through which they encounter it. The same rain might suggest danger to an exhausted traveler, relief to a gardener, or a welcome excuse to leave an uncomfortable gathering. Their surroundings have not changed to match their interpretations. An interpretation can be mistaken, and another person's intentions remain uncertain until evidence supports them.

This perspective includes bodily experience, feelings, remembered associations, relationships, commitments, expectations, and possibilities that currently matter. It must distinguish observation, memory, inference, and imagination. A remembered rejection can influence an approach without proving that the other person still feels the same way.

Selection makes this moment intelligible; it does not authorize removing required identity or evidence. The complete accepted **About me** remains included in every decision under the existing memory contract. Relevant inner experience provides a readable foreground alongside that continuity. Past events and private concerns need not appear equally urgent. The character cannot acquire another person's private knowledge because it would improve a scene.

## A need, an experience, and a choice are different things

Consider an inventor who has eaten little, has food available, and wants to finish testing a sling. The underlying bodily condition, experienced hunger, interpretation of that hunger, and eventual choice are related but distinct. The world's actual food state remains authoritative. In the current native representation that includes **fullness**, with lower values meaning less full; meaningful values, units, and scales must remain available where required. A description of hunger supplements those facts rather than replacing them with an invented measurement.

The character might understand the discomfort as manageable, decide a short break would improve the test, or worry that eating the remaining food leaves tomorrow uncertain. The choice depends on evidence, personality, competing concerns, and available actions. Psychological concerns such as belonging are also part of the proposed ambition; that does not claim they already have implemented bodily-style mechanics.

Suppose the inventor eats, rests, and returns to the sling. Successful eating changes the real food state and can relieve hunger. It need not erase curiosity or the desire to master the invention. A failed test supplies new evidence; disappointment might prompt another attempt, a request for advice, or a pause. Later recollection should reflect what actually happened and how it mattered. Hunger returning on another occasion does not require another complete invention–hunt–cook cycle, especially when food is already accessible.

## Contact, belonging, enjoyment, and solitude

Social contact is having an interaction. Belonging concerns being accepted, valued, understood, or meaningfully included by particular people or groups. A crowded conversation can leave someone alienated; a brief familiar exchange can reassure them. Company can also be irritating, threatening, amusing, useful, or welcome without creating intimacy.

These differences should affect choices. After an embarrassing exchange, a character might seek a trusted friend, repair the misunderstanding, practice privately, or enjoy solitude before trying again. Their response should follow their interpretation and history, without a mandatory loneliness timer or a single correct social remedy. Solitude can be chosen and restorative. Enjoyment can come from companionship, play, craft, exploration, beauty, or a personally satisfying challenge.

Characters also need room to refuse, negotiate, change their minds, and maintain interests that do not serve the player. Agreeableness is one possible disposition. Another person's warmth is evidence to interpret, not an automatic friendship award. A player's chosen speech and actions remain theirs; an authored personality or psychological concern must not silently take control of them.

## Thought earns its place through consequences

An inner life need not arrive as a running monologue. Sometimes a brief concern explains the choice; sometimes behavior, timing, or a later remark communicates it better. Private thinking may help a character reconsider an assumption or hold a difficult intention, but obligatory verbose thoughts would consume attention and resources without necessarily improving the game.

Variation should come from changed circumstances, memories, priorities, opportunities, and interpretations. Random novelty can disguise the absence of a life that develops. Repetition can be credible too: someone may cherish a routine or persevere after failure. The question is whether continuation or change makes sense and gives the player something worth experiencing. Reflection should improve later conduct without making ordinary play wait for a philosophical account of every action.

## Judge the complete causal loop

Evaluate a small lived sequence: what happened, what the character could know, what became salient, how they interpreted it, what they chose, what really resulted, and what subsequently changed. Fluent explanations alone cannot show this. A character who describes loneliness beautifully but approaches everyone identically has not demonstrated the intended social experience.

The inventor's sequence should expose whether relief releases attention for another valued pursuit. A social sequence should expose whether a mistaken interpretation can change after a specific person's response, while leaving room for embarrassment, skepticism, forgiveness, or withdrawal. Compare understandable conduct with actual consequences and the player's experience: curiosity, affection, tension, amusement, or a satisfying sense of influence. Richer inner description earns its cost when it improves those qualities, rather than merely making the transcript longer.

## Research as inspiration, with limits

These sources suggest useful distinctions and questions. They do not establish a universal psychology for fictional people or prove that this design will be fun.

| Primary source | Relevant finding or argument, and its limit |
| --- | --- |
| Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski, [*The Motivational Pull of Video Games*](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf), 2006 | Their studies associate perceived autonomy and competence with enjoyment; multiplayer findings also concern relatedness. Autonomy means volition, not isolation. These are findings about human players, not evidence for NPC need meters. They motivate asking whether choices, mastery, and connection matter to the experience, without prescribing a character taxonomy. |
| Bates, [*The Role of Emotion in Believable Agents*](https://acordo.net/acordo/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Role-of-Emotion-in-Believable-AgentsBATES.pdf), 1994 | Bates argues that goals, emotional responses, personality, and expression contribute to the artistic impression of life. An internal state can fail if observers cannot perceive its significance. This is a design argument informed by prototypes, not a validated universal model of emotion; it supports judging recognizable character through behavior. |
| Park and colleagues, [*Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior*](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2), 2023 | Observation, retrieval, reflection, and planning improved rated believability in a bounded prototype. Reported failures included fabricated memories and excessive cooperation that displaced established interests. The work motivates continuity and credible refusal; its limited evaluation does not establish long-term play quality, economical scale, or consciousness. |
| Seth and Friston, [*Active Interoceptive Inference and the Emotional Brain*](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1532767/1/20160007.full.pdf), 2016 | This theoretical review connects bodily regulation with interpretation, expectation, and action. The authors identify empirical gaps in the proposed mechanisms. It inspires separating a bodily condition from its experienced significance and response; it supplies neither a hunger formula nor a reason to implement biological machinery. |
| Dehaene and Changeux, [*Experimental and Theoretical Approaches to Conscious Processing*](https://www.unicog.org/publications/DehaeneChangeux_ReviewConsciousness_Neuron2011.pdf), 2011 | Their review describes a theory in which selected information becomes broadly available to processes including evaluation, memory, and action. For this design, the useful analogy is a concern influencing several kinds of behavior. A language-model context is not thereby a demonstrated biological workspace or evidence of consciousness. |
| Butlin and colleagues, [*Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence*](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08708v3), 2023 | The report distinguishes functional access from phenomenal experience and develops provisional indicators from several theories. It cautions that satisfying indicators would not establish consciousness. This supports careful language: the proposal describes a functional character perspective while leaving subjective experience an open question, not something narration can certify. |

“Inner life” therefore names an ambitious product and philosophical direction, expressed through grounded experience and consequential behavior. Neither asserting consciousness nor ruling it out is necessary to pursue that direction. The game can seek richer characters without importing a brain architecture or treating eloquent introspection as the achievement itself.
