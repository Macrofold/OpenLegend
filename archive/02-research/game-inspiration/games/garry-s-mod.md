### 4.12 Garry’s Mod — reusable tools plus reasons to gather

**Evidence.** Garry’s Mod combines a physics sandbox with a community of game modes and addons; its published description points to modes such as Trouble in Terrorist Town and Prop Hunt. [GM1](../references/sources-01.md#source-gm1) [GM2](../references/sources-01.md#source-gm2)

**Interpretation.** A toolset can generate value beyond its original use, but finished activities help non-creators participate. Reusable components and playable conventions make creation legible. Not everyone must start from an empty editor.

**Transfer.** OpenLegend’s packs should support several adoption levels: play an example world, install a compatible mechanic, specialize a template, or author a new construct. A short playable demonstration may communicate a pack’s value better than a technical feature list.

**Pitfall.** Mods and community modes extend a product; they do not excuse a weak default experience. Do not assume that because a mature toolbox eventually supports many genres, a new engine should ship with no opinionated game at all.

#### Gameplay field guide — Garry’s Mod

**What you actually do.** The base sandbox lets you spawn and manipulate objects and construct physics contraptions. Community modes then place different rules around those tools and assets. It is essential to distinguish playing Sandbox from joining a server running Prop Hunt, Trouble in Terrorist Town, role-play, or another addon-defined experience. [FG-GM-O](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-gm-o) [FG-GM-S](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-s)

**What the reviewed accounts value.** Player accounts value creative experimentation, friendships, and the discovery of different activities inside one tool-rich environment. Even strongly negative long-term reviews describe memorable years of play and creation. A more positive retrospective emphasizes the ability to have an enjoyable social experience rather than complete a conventional progression checklist. [FG-GM-P](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-p) [FG-GM-P2](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-p2)

**What they dislike or find limiting.** The significant counterpoint concerns the experience around the content: finding a suitable server, large addon downloads, variable moderation/rules, and a community’s changing expectations. These are selected historical accounts, not proof that the present community is uniformly hostile. The lesson is that a good creation API does not itself guarantee a good place to play. [FG-GM-P](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-p)

**The design tension.** Tool freedom and dependable shared play are separate products. Community rules can create meaningful scenarios or turn participation into administrative friction.

**Watch to understand the game**

1. **[Garry’s Mod Tutorial for Beginners! — How GMod Works](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N9N63OhRPjU)** — GrobeMan Guides & Gameplay; 2017 introductory guide. Orient yourself to Sandbox tools, additional content, and community game modes. This is a better first reference than a heavily edited meme compilation. **Caution:** Older UI/content ecosystem. Not every addon shown is part of the base purchase.

2. **[How To Play Prop Hunt on Gmod With Friends](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GKDwOrx-K5k)** — 2020 community-mode guide. See how a clear hide-and-seek rule gives props and player movement a different meaning. Compare reusable platform tools with the finished activity layered on them. **Caution:** Prop Hunt is a community mode, not the default Sandbox rules.

**Three concrete in-game situations**

1. **Build and test a moving contraption.** *Documented base-tool interaction.* A player can connect props and movement-producing parts into a vehicle, try it, notice instability, and alter the construction. The interesting artifact is executable and revisable. It is not simply an image of a vehicle or a declaration that the vehicle succeeded. [FG-GM-O](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-gm-o)

2. **An ordinary prop becomes a hiding place.** *Documented community mode.* In Prop Hunt, players disguise themselves as environmental props while opponents search. Recontextualizing familiar objects creates a social perception problem without adding a vast new physics simulation. The uncertainty belongs to the opposing players, not an omniscient narration system. [FG-GM-S](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-s)

3. **A shared map becomes a trust problem.** *Documented community mode.* Trouble in Terrorist Town places hidden-role deception around a common environment. Information, accusation, cooperation, and betrayal generate variation under a recognizable rule set. Exact server rules vary, so one dramatic clip does not establish a universal TTT behavior. [FG-GM-S](../references/sources-04.md#source-fg-gm-s)

**What to borrow for OpenLegend.** For OpenLegend, distribute a reusable mechanic with a small understandable activity that demonstrates it. Separate “can author,” “can install,” and “can find a reliable group.” Do not confuse human-performed role-play videos—including fake “self-aware AI” shows—with evidence of implemented AI autonomy.

*Scope/evidence note:* Base-game systems unless noted; examples describe documented mechanics, not a play session conducted for this research.
