### 5.2 EverQuest Next — integration is where the promise must become a game

**Evidence.** Daybreak canceled EverQuest Next in March 2016. Its president’s statement acknowledged substantial technical achievement but said the assembled game was not fun enough to meet expectations. The original statement is reproduced in contemporary reporting. [EQ1](../references/sources-01.md#source-eq1)

**Interpretation.** The warning is not “ambitious worlds are impossible.” It is that technical demonstrations can each be impressive while the integrated activity remains weak. A project can validate terrain, simulation, characters, and creation separately without validating what a player wants to do after the novelty wears off.

**OpenLegend implication.** Require recurrent end-to-end play sessions while the engine grows. The important milestone is not “a new sense can register.” It is “a creature using that sense creates a distinct, understandable encounter worth playing.” The important invention milestone is not “the declaration activated.” It is “the player used the invention, experienced a tradeoff, and wanted to improve or reuse it.”

**Limit of inference.** A cancellation statement does not reveal every internal cause, financial constraint, or alternative that could have worked. Do not turn this case into an argument against a specific architecture without evidence. Use it to keep integrated experience testing ahead of cumulative feature ambition.
