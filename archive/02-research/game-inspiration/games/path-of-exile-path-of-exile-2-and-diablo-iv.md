### 4.16 Path of Exile, Path of Exile 2, and Diablo IV — mastery, resets, and the cost of breaking a build

**Why compare these together?** They offer overhead action, loot, character builds and repeated progression, but differ in onboarding, complexity, commercial structure and update expectations. Their popularity is evidence against a mandatory first-person camera, not proof that every audience wants intricate spreadsheets or grind. A fixed-ish camera is compatible with demanding real-time action.

**The predecessor’s explicit design philosophy.** Chris Wilson’s 2019 GDC abstract frames Path of Exile as a long-lived product built around repeated content and procedural variation. Reporting on the talk discusses a roughly thirteen-week league cadence; the 2016 interview distinguishes mastery-oriented players and challenge preferences. These are the creator’s stated intentions. This brief did not watch the full GDC recording. [POE1](../references/sources-02.md#source-poe1) [POE2](../references/sources-02.md#source-poe2) [POE3](../references/sources-02.md#source-poe3)

**Path of Exile 2’s trajectory.** Its paid early-access launch in December 2024 produced a recorded Steam peak of 578,569 on December 8. The September 2026 capture has 226,614 total reviews and a raw positive share near 75%. Those figures are not comparable with lifetime free-to-play registration counts for its predecessor. [MET-POE2](../references/sources-02.md#source-met-poe2) In reporting around the 2025 response to Dawn of the Hunt, the director discussed repairing a disappointing period through changes intended to restore enjoyable progression. That supports a narrow conclusion about balance and trust, not a claim that every proposed repair succeeded. [POE4](../references/sources-02.md#source-poe4)

**Diablo IV’s contrast.** The 2023 critic review praised combat and class/build appeal while identifying repetition and friction. [CRIT-D4](../references/sources-02.md#source-crit-d4) The inspected Steam negative reviews later add dissatisfaction about premium-game monetization and practical interruption costs. Some 2026 reviewers complain about changed progression or expansion-linked value, but their exact paywall allegations are not independently established here. [REV-D4-N](../references/sources-02.md#source-rev-d4-n) A September 2026 Steam snapshot contains about 78,000 reviews with a raw positive share around 65%; Steam is only one of its distribution channels. [MET-D4](../references/sources-02.md#source-met-d4)

**What appears enjoyable.** My interpretation is that these games support two interacting rewards: immediate feedback from combat and the longer satisfaction of constructing a character whose parts work together. Learning a combination can make an earlier obstacle trivial in an earned way. A good new item is therefore not just a larger number; it changes the set of viable choices or completes an understood plan.

**What can go wrong.** Complexity can become a tax when the game does not make the consequences of a choice intelligible. Grind can cease to feel like preparation and become a barrier between the player and the enjoyable experiment. A balance patch can also feel like confiscation of learned competence: the player invested in understanding a build and wakes up to a different contract. That does not mean never nerfing anything. It means considering explanation, alternative routes, transition cost and whether the old strategy was carrying a legitimate fantasy.

**Growth versus retention.** A league or season gives experienced players a shared return date, a fresh problem and a reason to compare builds. It also creates a release-production obligation. Peaks can recur without implying uninterrupted daily retention, and a decline between peaks does not alone establish failure. Paid early access, premium purchase/expansion models and free-to-play services monetize different stages of that return cycle. Public data here does not support a comparative profitability ranking.

**For OpenLegend’s reusable constructs.** A mechanic library can supply a similar combinatorial reward: specialize a target selector, substitute a compatible resource transform, or combine effects within explicit limits. The important property is comprehensible synergy. Arbitrary number inflation and opaque generated code are weaker. Give users enough feedback to predict a result, then allow interactions they can discover rather than requiring a guide for every binding.

**Do not copy the reset promise blindly.** Players knowingly choosing a fresh league have a different expectation from people inhabiting a persistent settlement with relationships and memories. OpenLegend could offer optional scenario worlds or explicit fresh starts without periodically erasing its principal continuity. A module update should also preserve or clearly transition the capabilities on which active plans and player investment depend.

**Evidence limit.** Direct individual Path of Exile 2 review bodies were not reliably accessible in this run. Its reception section therefore uses chart aggregates and reported developer response; it is not presented as an independent qualitative coding of its whole review population. Diablo’s directly inspected complaints are time- and sample-qualified separately.
