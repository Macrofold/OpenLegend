### 4.10 Caves of Qud — general capabilities inside a highly particular world

**Evidence.** Qud combines unusual biological/cybernetic capabilities, faction relations, material interactions, and both authored and procedural content. Its official store description explicitly allows differently embodied creatures and offers modes with different loss/exploration emphasis. [QU1](../references/sources-01.md#source-qu1) [QU2](../references/sources-01.md#source-qu2)

**Interpretation.** General simulation rules become more memorable when expressed through a distinctive culture and aesthetic. “Supports arbitrary species” is infrastructure; strange organisms with specific needs, language, artifacts, and relationships are a world.

**Transfer.** Use Qud as an ambition anchor for extensible embodiment and senses, but also as a warning against content-neutral presentation. A touch-only organism should change how one explores and interacts—not merely expose another component schema. Different challenge modes can preserve the same underlying world while serving different motivations.

**Pitfall.** A new player may need a strong starting character or bounded premise before appreciating expressive complexity. Do not make maximum character configurability a prerequisite for the first enjoyable encounter.

#### Gameplay field guide — Caves of Qud

**What you actually do.** You explore a strange science-fantasy world turn by turn. Character embodiment, equipment, mutations, factions, and materials can give very different ways to deal with a situation. Its dense text and relatively abstract visuals describe a highly specific culture rather than a neutral collection of interchangeable fantasy systems. [FG-QU-O](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-o)

**What the reviewed accounts value.** The strongest attraction in the review is the conjunction of mechanical breadth and an authored identity: unusual abilities matter in an unusual world. Exploration rewards curiosity, and creatures are interesting partly because they participate in shared systems. Different modes also let players choose how much repeated loss they want around that exploration. [FG-QU-C](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-c) [FG-QU-O](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-o)

**What they dislike or find limiting.** The barrier is learning an unfamiliar world, interface, and set of interactions before those possibilities become legible. Permanent-loss play can make that learning costly. These are taste and onboarding tensions, not evidence that abstract presentation or deep simulation is intrinsically bad. Use the supplementary discussion as an individual perspective, not a score for all players. [FG-QU-C](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-c) [FG-QU-P](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-p)

**The design tension.** General capabilities do not require a generic setting. A strong vocabulary, culture, and tone help the player care about the mechanical differences.

**Watch to understand the game**

1. **[Caves of Qud Gameplay Overview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CRolH9MWEFE)** — 2015 gameplay overview. Use it to understand the turn-based exploration and text/system relationship. Watch for capabilities changing available approaches rather than only changing an avatar. **Caution:** Historical build: interface, content, and balancing are not a current-release audit.

2. **[10 Things to Know Before You Play Caves of Qud](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B6auFYGid80)** — 2026 orientation guide. Use this after the overview to identify the assumptions a new player must learn; compare those onboarding demands with your proposed unusual-organism worlds. **Caution:** Mechanical spoilers; tips do not establish a universal optimal playstyle.

**Three concrete in-game situations**

1. **The money is also your drinking water.** *Documented mechanic.* Fresh water is both a practical resource and a medium of exchange. Spending it therefore has a survival implication as well as a purchasing implication. This simple overlap makes an inventory number participate in more than one decision system. [FG-QU-C](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-c)

2. **A different body changes a build.** *Documented character system.* Mutation and cybernetic options can change what a character can physically do and equip. Additional anatomy is not merely descriptive character art: it alters the set of usable approaches. The engine-like lesson is capability-based interaction; the game-design lesson is making the resulting organism recognizable and playable. [FG-QU-O](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-o)

3. **A powerful line attack has collateral consequences.** *Documented interaction.* The review describes an eigenrifle’s piercing effect as something that can harm more than the intended enemy. Positioning and what lies beyond the target matter. The same general attack property creates both power and responsibility; a larger damage number alone would not supply that decision. [FG-QU-C](../references/sources-03.md#source-fg-qu-c)

**What to borrow for OpenLegend.** For OpenLegend, pair extensible embodiment with authored meaning and clear feedback. A third sense or additional limb should change investigation or action—not just its schema. Preserve a choice of challenge policy rather than assuming permadeath is necessary to make experimentation meaningful.

*Scope/evidence note:* Base-game systems unless noted; examples describe documented mechanics, not a play session conducted for this research.
