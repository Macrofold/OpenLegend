### 3.3 Roblox — a distribution and operating system, not merely an editor

**Separate the platform from its experiences.** Roblox is not one genre, one camera, one age group or one retention loop. Its creation, hosting, social identity, discovery and transaction systems support games whose individual appeals differ. The 2025 results report 126.5 million average daily active users, with quarterly averages rising from about 98 million in Q1 to 152 million in Q3, then falling to 144 million in Q4. The annual report rounds the annual measure to 127 million. [RB3](../references/sources-02.md#source-rb3) [RB1](../references/sources-01.md#source-rb1)

**Economics.** The 2025 report records about $4.89 billion revenue and substantial developer-exchange, infrastructure and trust-and-safety costs. Bookings, revenue, cash generation and accounting profit measure different things. An ecosystem can distribute considerable money to creators without the typical creator earning a sustainable wage. The ledger retains exact categories and dates rather than turning total platform activity into title-level profit. [RB1](../references/sources-01.md#source-rb1)

**How the platform can grow.** Its official discovery documentation discusses early engagement, sustained/repeated use and social behavior. These are platform matching signals, not proof that more engagement is always more welfare or fun. [RB2](../references/sources-01.md#source-rb2) My interpretation is that growth requires at least four distinct loops: a player finds something appealing; friends can join easily; creators receive useful audience response; and new experiences can inherit distribution infrastructure. A low-friction editor solves only part of that system.

**Growth is not monotonic.** The Q2 2025 report linked strong growth with viral experiences and reported 111.8 million average DAU. A later Q1 2026 report described 132 million average DAU, below late-2025 peaks, with company explanations including access/safety changes. These are dated quarters, not a claim that Q1 2026 is September’s latest quarter. [RB5](../references/sources-02.md#source-rb5) [RB4](../references/sources-02.md#source-rb4) A year of extraordinary growth should therefore not be extrapolated as a permanent slope.

#### Grow a Garden — an accessible loop becomes a social appointment

**Creator account and history.** In the September 2025 GamesBeat interview, Janzen Madsen describes joining an initially small experience, using accumulated development tools, iterating rapidly and building a weekly update cadence. He reports growth from roughly 500 concurrent players at involvement to much larger milestones, ultimately including a 22.3-million concurrency peak and around 30 billion cumulative plays. Those are creator-reported, historical figures. In particular, cumulative plays are not quarterly unique people. [GAG1](../references/sources-03.md#source-gag1)

**Why the loop is approachable.** Planting, growth, harvesting and expanding a garden are familiar actions with visible accumulation. Offline progress can let returning feel rewarding rather than beginning from zero. Original reporting describes collection, rare outcomes, social participation and paid options around that accessible core. [GAG2](../references/sources-03.md#source-gag2)

**Why some people dislike it.** The inspected discussion contains disagreement over waiting, repetitive collection, monetization and whether scheduled events remain a positive community ritual. Neither a negative thread title nor a lower snapshot establishes that a game has died. These are signals about the quality of participation, not a demographic survey. [REV-GAG](../references/sources-03.md#source-rev-gag)

**Design inference.** The crucial package is not horticultural realism. It is a readily understood project, recognizable possessions, accessible re-entry and a shared reason to appear at a particular time. But the same structure can feel like an obligation if missing an event means losing status or opportunity. OpenLegend should borrow comprehensible accumulation and shared occasions, not uncritically optimize fear of missing out.

#### Steal a Brainrot — accumulation made vulnerable and theatrical

**The loop.** The official listing explicitly combines acquiring characters, generating money, stealing from others, rebirth and disruptive tools. This places risk and interpersonal conflict inside a simple collection/economy frame. [SAB1](../references/sources-03.md#source-sab1) Roblox’s results coverage names the experience alongside Grow a Garden as a source of major 2025 activity. [RB3](../references/sources-02.md#source-rb3)

**Reception and uncertainty.** The sampled Reddit discussion includes both enjoyment and criticism of waiting or losing valued items. Its subreddit name does not verify anyone’s age. Neither comments nor the retrieved experience page provide an audited title profit or reliable quarterly unique-user series. [REV-SAB](../references/sources-03.md#source-rev-sab)

**Design inference.** Theft creates an instantly comprehensible scene: somebody had a prized thing, somebody else took it, and a reaction followed. That is a powerful sharing unit. It is also potentially a poor fit for a game promising long-lived attachment unless consent, recovery and expectations are carefully designed. A funny short-round loss can be devastating when applied to a week of personal world-building.

#### What OpenLegend should actually borrow

**Make adoption layered.** A person should be able to play a world, try a working mechanic, specialize a construct, or author a new one. Do not require every consumer to become a technical creator. Give reusable mechanics an example scene and a plain-English account of their tradeoffs.

**Make community value distinct from extraction.** Public counts of artifacts, visits or playtime do not prove creators received meaningful feedback or players felt respected. Track successful reuse, time to an enjoyable first experience, understandable purchases and returning by choice. If minors are ever part of the audience, the product needs an explicit safety and commercial policy; this research is not a rationale for exploiting developmental vulnerabilities.

**Start smaller than Roblox.** A good local creator-consumer loop can exist with two people. One authors a useful mechanic; another understands and adapts it; ordinary play becomes better. Distribution, moderation, compatibility and support can grow around that demonstrated value. A giant empty marketplace would invert the order.

**Key versus incidental.** Easy participation, a clear activity and effective discovery are stronger lessons than copying a particular meme, avatar style, growth timer or virtual currency. A meme can start attention while the underlying economy/community sustains it. Conversely, a strong collection loop can still lose trust through confusing or aggressive monetization. These are distinct design choices, not one inseparable success recipe.
