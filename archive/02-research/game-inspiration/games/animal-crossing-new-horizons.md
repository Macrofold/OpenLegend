### 4.26 Animal Crossing: New Horizons — large-scale appeal without combat mastery

**A useful commercial control case.** Nintendo reports 50.29 million worldwide life-to-date Switch units as of June 30, 2026, under its disclosure definitions including digital and bundled units. This is not current concurrency, unique active players or title profit. [AC1](../references/sources-03.md#source-ac1)

**Why include it with limited deep-review evidence?** Its broad home/community-creation premise is a useful contrast to combat-heavy explanations of success. However, original creator/review pages sought in this pass were not reliably retrievable. It is therefore a lower-depth comparator: no detailed player-complaint ranking, demographic assertion or causal attribution to pandemic timing is claimed here.

**OpenLegend design hypothesis.** A place can become valuable because it embodies taste, routine and social memory rather than because it maximizes production or survives repeated attack. That suggests room for decorating, hosting, remembering and caring between moments of danger. The engine should not mistake the lack of combat optimization for a lack of agency.

**The useful question.** Could someone enjoy returning to OpenLegend when there is no crisis and no immediately superior item to craft? If not, either improve the home/social layer or explicitly choose a more expedition-focused promise. Do not assume the game serves both motivations simply because its engine supports furniture and relationships.

**Do not overread the example.** The sales figure establishes that non-FPS, non-combat-centered play can have enormous reach. It does not establish how much camera, brand, hardware, social timing, content quality or distribution contributed. Nor does it prove that the same audience wants an AI-native survival simulation.
