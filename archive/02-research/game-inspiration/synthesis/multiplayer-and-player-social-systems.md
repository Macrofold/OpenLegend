## Scope and questions

What makes doing something together better than parallel solo play?

- Compare complementary roles, rescue, shared projects, asymmetric information, communication and spontaneous social play.
- How do joining, regrouping, drop-in/out, progression gaps, reward sharing and time commitments support real groups?
- When do competition, trade, betrayal or loss create valued stakes, and when do they destroy cooperation or exclude friends?

## Desired experiences and contrasting approaches

A shared activity can provide a useful exchange, enjoyable company or a continuing acquaintance. These are distinct successes. A demanding expedition can depend on specialists; a roadside encounter can permit a small contribution and a comfortable departure. A game should choose which experience an activity promises before treating more invitations or longer sessions as improvement.

## Cross-game evidence and counterexamples

The current incorporation is limited to [Forever's beta social evidence](../dossiers/world-of-warcraft-forever.md#what-direct-players-value-and-where-the-same-systems-disappoint), including its written criticism and compatibility rules. Players report both sustained spontaneous cooperation and parties that end with one objective. The source explains each writer's context and contrary experiences. This is a comparison within one case, not a completed survey of the candidate games or proof of long-term community health.

## Consolidated lessons and tradeoffs

<a id="multi-l001"></a>

### MULTI-L001 — Evaluate what continues after the shared objective

Recognition, an understandable contribution and another worthwhile opportunity can make a person a preferred companion. Group creation is only one step. Rewarding proximity can make that step common without making the encounter memorable. Conversely, a brief, useful exchange can succeed without creating a friendship. Compare both outcomes with the player's own intention.

<a id="multi-l002"></a>

### MULTI-L002 — Give dependence a proportionate cost

Useful differences make help valuable, but a missing specialist can consume everyone's available time. Cooperation can widen the options or reduce danger without being mandatory for every small activity. More demanding group undertakings remain a legitimate alternative. Joining, reaching the activity, leaving and meeting again belong in the evaluation alongside the exciting middle of the encounter.

## Complete situations and cross-topic consequences

**Hypothetical Open Legend situation:** one player can join only the first part of a ruin expedition. They provide an appropriate tool, help overcome an encounter and leave with something worthwhile. A companion later recognizes the contribution and offers another outing. The continuation is possible rather than compulsory; another participant can enjoy the same encounter and never return. If the short-session player loses every reward by departing, or waits until their available time expires, the social design defeats that intended experience.

This intersects with PACING, GROWTH and TRUST. Human recognition here must remain distinct from an NPC remembering an event: the latter additionally depends on what that character actually observed and has reason to care about.

## Possible OpenLegend applications and limits

The [Forever synthesis](../essays/wow-forever-and-a-world-worth-returning-to.md) develops alternatives for shared places, enjoyable adventure and independent inhabitants. These are research possibilities under the existing [gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md), not newly accepted multiplayer behavior. A workable interface, compatible participation and an activity worth doing are prerequisites; elaborate social incentives cannot substitute for them.

## Open questions and missing evidence

The beta corpus does not establish how often strangers meet again, which social arrangements survive population changes, or whether the same activity remains enjoyable after optimization. Useful later evidence would distinguish voluntary reunion, satisfied brief contact, exclusion and imposed waiting. Quiet participants, organizers and competitive groups may value different outcomes; their preferences should not be collapsed into one ideal community.

## OpenLegend-specific research subtopics

### Being together beyond survival or combat

Examine shared leisure, minigames, sport, performance, conversation, hospitality and social rituals alongside coordinated danger and rescue. What makes mutual contribution rewarding without making solo participation inadequate?

### Different schedules, goals and tolerances for risk

How can asynchronous help, regrouping, complementary expertise and negotiated stakes support real groups? Connect LIVE, TRUST and NPC while distinguishing human cooperation from scripted companion behavior.

### Additional boundaries from the broader source review

Separate a shared activity's rules in LEISURE, communication in SPEECH, relationships in NPC and human coordination here. Include invitation, arrival, catch-up, asynchronous contribution, departure and return, not only the exciting middle of a cooperative encounter. Shared canonical victories and individual rewards may make different promises.
