## Scope and questions

What makes communicating an activity with consequences, rather than exchanging interchangeable lines of exposition?

### Conversation as action and relationship

Compare asking, listening, bargaining, teaching, joking, flirting, promising, threatening, refusing and apologizing. Examine initiative, turn-taking, interruptions, silence, misunderstanding and repair. Does what is said change a later choice or relationship? Neither persuasive wording nor affection guarantees agreement or obedience.

### Language, culture and interpretation

Distinguish speech, gesture, writing, naming, social register, literacy, translation and constructed language. How do professions, intimacy, status and cultural references shape meaning? A glossary is not automatically linguistic depth. Preserve differences between fictional language barriers and accessible presentation for the human player.

### Audience, distance and retained words

Compare private exchanges, overhearing, public performance, letters, recordings, messages and calls. Who actually receives what, when, and through which medium? Distinguish exact words, partial hearing, gist, later testimony and inference; a shared location or translation feature does not grant everyone the same knowledge.

## Ownership boundary

Owns the act and medium of communication and how it is understood. KNOW owns evidence, belief and investigation; NPC owns individual motivation and personal change; SOCIETY owns institutional recognition; LORE owns historical meaning; UX owns accessible controls. Platform messaging and fictional communication have different permission boundaries.
