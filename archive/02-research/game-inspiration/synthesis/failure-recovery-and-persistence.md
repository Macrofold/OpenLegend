# Failure, recovery and persistence

**Status: Partial synthesis. Topic: LOSS.** Rust's scoped evidence is incorporated; broader game/world comparison remains open.

## Scope and questions

What worthwhile decision, lesson or continuing story comes after a setback?

- Compare death, injury, lost equipment, rescue, checkpoints, permadeath, voluntary resets and campaign continuity.
- What survives as knowledge, relationships, possessions or unlocks, and how quickly does rebuilding become interesting?
- When does loss renew discovery versus repeat chores, separate friends or break trust? How are technical failures kept distinct?

## Desired experiences and contrasting approaches

A setback can invite rescue, adaptation, a comeback or a dignified ending. Checkpoints, partial recovery, permanent loss and voluntary fresh starts support different relationships with time and identity. The desired experience should determine the loss rule; “more consequential” is not automatically better.

## Cross-game evidence and counterexamples

[Rust's recovery analysis](../dossiers/rust.md#rust-loss) and [persistence table](../dossiers/rust.md#rust-servers) distinguish death, logout, map wipe and blueprint wipe. Stored reserves and spawn options can support recovery, while the preserved reviews describe both memorable comebacks and disproportionate rebuilding burdens. No current representative recovery-time or wellbeing study is claimed; other candidate games remain unassessed.

## Consolidated lessons and tradeoffs

<a id="loss-l001"></a>

### Publish the continuity contract at each boundary

State what happens to carried goods, stored property, learned capability, relationships and account entitlements on death, absence, reset and relocation. These need not share one persistence rule. Distinguish fictional loss from technical failure; a save defect or failed delivery is not a valid substitute for an authored setback. Different modes can differ, but the player should know the applicable promise before investing.

<a id="loss-l002"></a>

### Evaluate the first interesting decision after defeat

A recovery point or replacement kit matters because it restores agency, not merely because it shortens a timer. Compare regrouping, retrieving possessions, changing tactics, relocating and leaving satisfied. Repeating already-mastered labor without a new decision can turn loss into obligation. Strong attachment also increases the cost of involuntary erasure; protect the option of durable success where that is the world's promise.

## Complete situations and cross-topic consequences

**Constructed comparison:** an expedition fails while the home survives. The player can use reserves, seek help, attempt recovery or pursue another goal. A companion may have reasons not to return to danger; a lost object may matter emotionally rather than economically. Contrast this with a full campaign reset and with recovery after a technical failure. Record what persists, who can act and what choice becomes worthwhile next. LOSS owns the continuity promise; BODY, NPC and LORE own their distinct consequences.

## Possible OpenLegend applications and limits

Examine one supported defeat/return path in the selected world and preserve its accepted continuity rules. Do not infer a need for forced wipes, offline destruction or total inventory loss from Rust's popularity. Permanent history and a satisfying ending remain legitimate outcomes.

## Open questions and missing evidence

How long until a person can make a new valued choice, and which portion is repetition rather than learning? Compare voluntary return with feeling compelled to protect investment. Existing Rust sources do not establish a universal acceptable loss magnitude.

## OpenLegend-specific research subtopics

### Defeat beyond a reload screen

Compare capture, flight, rescue, grief, broken relationships, inheritance and ruined places as consequences of conflict or ordinary life. What meaningful choices remain for the player and independently acting residents?

### Rebuilding, absence and trustworthy continuity

What persists through a setback or time away, and what must be recoverable when the software rather than the fiction fails? Link LIVE, NPC, SOCIETY and LORE without assuming either total loss or permanent accumulation is always better.

### Additional boundaries from the broader source review

Distinguish mechanical defeat, bodily death, grief, a lost project, technical failure and a satisfied ending. BODY owns biological outcomes, NPC personal interpretation and LIVE continuing world history. Persistence does not require universal total loss, permanent accumulation or repeated invalidation of victories.
