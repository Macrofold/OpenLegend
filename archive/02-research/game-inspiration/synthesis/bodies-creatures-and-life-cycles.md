## Scope and questions

How do different ways of being embodied change possible lives, rather than merely change a sprite or a list of statistics?

### Capabilities, senses and accommodations

Compare anatomy, movement, perception, equipment compatibility, environmental requirements, fatigue, injury, illness, treatment and disability. Ask which differences create meaningful choices or opportunities for care. Do not confuse an accessible interface with an authored body's capabilities, or assume bodily variation requires repetitive medical administration.

### Creatures, care and relationships

Examine wildlife, predation, pets, mounts, livestock, companions and unfamiliar minds. Distinguish ecological behavior, learned capability, personhood, attachment, ownership and willingness. A creature's usefulness or ability to speak does not settle every relationship; compare handling, teaching and cooperation without assuming all beings think like humans.

### Life cycles and transformed identities

Where relevant, study birth, growth, reproduction, family care, aging, death, succession, transformation, synthetic bodies and shared or unusual minds. What changes in play and relationships? Keep biology, culture and individual personality distinct. Scope audience-sensitive subjects deliberately, and do not mandate a full generational simulation.

## Ownership boundary

Owns embodied capability and life-cycle experience. SURVIVE owns recurring survival pressure; SYSTEMS owns populations and habitats; NPC owns motives and bonds; LOSS owns recovery and continuity after setbacks; MAGIC and TECH own mechanisms of extraordinary change. Existing accepted identity, privacy and world contracts remain authoritative.
