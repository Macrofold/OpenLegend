## Scope and questions

What makes “could I try this?” a meaningful invitation rather than a long list of commands or an omnipotent text box?

### Recognizing an opportunity and choosing a method

Compare visible affordances, object inspection, direct manipulation, contextual actions and free-form requests. How do a person's body, knowledge, tools, access and situation change what is possible? Separate the player's intention, an attempted action and its actual result.

### Improvisation with ordinary rules

Examine multi-use objects, combining actions, environmental changes, social intervention, stealth, deception and force. How can a useful alternative be discovered without requiring a hidden recipe or a single author's intended solution? Not every imaginable method must work, and a clever explanation does not bypass a missing capability.

### Commitment, resistance and follow-through

What does selecting an action commit, when can it be interrupted, and what survives a partial result? Compare meaningful resistance and clear refusal with opaque failure. Ask what opponents and other inhabitants can do in response, and whether success really changes the next choice.

## Ownership boundary
