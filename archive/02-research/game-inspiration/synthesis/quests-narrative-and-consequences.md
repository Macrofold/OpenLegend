## Scope and questions

How do goals and stories make player choices matter without removing initiative?

- Compare authored quests, emergent situations, branching choices, goal discovery and multiple solutions.
- How do dialogue, actions, delayed consequences and recurring characters acknowledge what really happened?
- When does procedural variation create a different dilemma rather than a renamed errand, and when should a story end?

## Desired experiences and contrasting approaches

Players should recognize a concern, choose how to engage, and see what their contribution meant. A directed adventure can achieve this through authored scenes; a continuing world might use independent purposes and remembered actions. More branches are one possible means, not a sufficient definition of meaningful choice.

## Consolidated lessons and tradeoffs

<a id="story-l001"></a>

### STORY-L001 — Acknowledge actions at their actual scale

**Hypothesis:** accurate recognition can make a shared route feel personally consequential. Distinguish changed testimony, relationships, resources and destinations. **Counterpoint:** acknowledgment disappoints when presentation promises control over an outcome that cannot change. A simpler honest response can be preferable to an elaborate false branch. A consequential final choice also cannot, by itself, establish that earlier decisions changed the route. Describe the actual scope of agency at each stage.

<a id="story-l002"></a>

### STORY-L002 — Connect resolution to something the player uses

**Hypothesis:** an accomplishment can change practical capability and personal significance together. **Counterpoint:** requiring emotional exposition for every improvement burdens ordinary play. Let useful work, affection and historical discovery sometimes stand alone; reserve combined rewards for situations where their relationship is intelligible.

A person's capacities can also change the cost of a contribution: [Robo's restoration](../dossiers/chrono-trigger.md#care-consequence-and-uncertainty) makes elapsed time into service. For a continuing world, preserving a useful result and its remembered contributors can support meaning without continually undoing accomplishments to create more quests.

<a id="story-l003"></a>

### STORY-L003 — Let interpretation change without silently changing history

**Hypothesis:** discovering another person's perspective can renew a familiar place without generating an incompatible past. **Counterpoint:** consistency alone produces no worthwhile investigation. Evidence must support a decision or wanted understanding; some mysteries can remain unresolved. KNOW owns discovery methods, while LORE owns historical meaning. G77 motivates a further question: does a revelation preserve reasons to care about what the player already accomplished? A coherent explanation can still change the perceived value of earlier effort. Test that reaction separately from whether the reveal was predicted.

For a selected revelation, distinguish the clue being present, being encountered, being remembered, explaining an available action and earning an emotional response. Those questions can diverge. A later participant may find the aftermath in a different order and still deserve an intelligible opportunity. A newly understood adversary may become a collaborator while grievances remain; explanation does not itself confer trust. These are questions within the existing authored-history work, not additional approval or implementation gates.

<a id="story-l004"></a>

### STORY-L004 — Make failure and closure legible

**Hypothesis:** a failed attempt can remain a complete event with understandable consequences and a possible next choice. **Counterpoint:** a surprise failure imposed during reading or unclear controls can feel arbitrary. Explain relevant stakes; do not require every loss to spawn a replacement quest or every resolution to reopen itself. Ask whose interests the ending resolves and whose remain unsettled. Completion can be deliberate without making every character’s wishes subordinate to a single protagonist’s satisfaction.

## Complete situations and cross-topic consequences

**Constructed OpenLegend situation, not delivered behavior:** a traveler wants a flood-damaged crossing reopened. A resident remembers the repair materials; another fears reopening an old hazard. The traveler can investigate, negotiate a narrower crossing, or abandon the project. Repair consumes time and materials; finding the hazard can change the method. The result might be a usable crossing with restricted access, accurately attributed to its contributors. A resident can disagree without denying what occurred. The traveler may use it, return later, or stop satisfied. CRAFT owns repair, SOCIETY the access dispute, and STORY the recognizable change and acknowledgment.

After a successful repair, an old map and a resident’s account reveal that the crossing also led to a workshop known for an unusual climbing tool. The new route makes that place a practical destination. The traveler can plan an expedition, seek a willing companion whose expertise helps interpret what survives, or enjoy the crossing as a finished contribution. A recovered tool or a genuinely learned method could later enable another route, if the selected world supports it. Later visitors encounter the repaired crossing and remaining traces, with their own reasons to use them. The discovery connects an earned achievement to a fresh adventure without requiring everyone to pursue it.

## Possible OpenLegend applications and limits

Compare one short authored situation with several meaningful resolutions against a larger branching tree. Evaluate remembered contributions, understandable stakes and actual alternatives before expanding narrative volume. Neither reference establishes that generated dialogue, a shared world or independent characters will preserve these qualities automatically. Use their disagreements to form test questions, not to prescribe a universal tragic, hopeful or ambiguous ending.

## Open questions and missing evidence

Confidence is moderate in the distinctions above and low in their transfer to unscripted shared play. Test whether participants understand what changed, whether another participant can encounter the aftermath coherently, and whether refusal remains worthwhile. Conflicting memories and competing simultaneous projects remain unassessed. The G77 review sample demonstrates divergent interpretations, not their prevalence or the emotional response of OpenLegend’s audience.

## OpenLegend-specific research subtopics

### Situations arising from lives and conflicting purposes

How can independent actions, practical needs, social dilemmas and player initiative produce worthwhile stories without an assigned quest? Compare peaceful, adversarial and mixed solutions rather than treating every narrative as a combat errand.

### Acknowledgment without invented history

How do consequences persist through places, objects and relationships, and how does later narration accurately acknowledge them? Link NPC, KNOW and LORE; authored possibilities, actual outcomes and characters' accounts must remain distinguishable.

### Additional boundaries from the broader source review

Separate authored possibilities, generated proposals, committed events and later telling. Compare authored quests, consequences of independent lives, self-chosen projects and finite endings without a universal story director that erases player initiative. SPEECH owns conversations; LORE the evolving shared interpretation of the past.
