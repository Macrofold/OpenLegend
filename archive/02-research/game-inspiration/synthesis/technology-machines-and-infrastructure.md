## Scope and questions

What makes technology a useful, understandable change in what people can do—and in what they need from one another?

### Working devices and connected systems

Compare mechanisms, energy, signals, control, sensing, tools, communication, medicine, transport and public infrastructure. Distinguish a working device from an impressive description. What inputs, interfaces, conditions and limits make systems composable and their failures diagnosable?

### Invention, adoption and dependence

How does learning to build or use a device differ from a society adopting it? Examine expertise, access, maintenance, obsolete skills, lost knowledge, standards and unequal benefits. Does a new capability change prices, institutions, warfare or everyday care—or conveniently disappear when the plot needs an old obstacle?

### Abundance, restraint and world-specific possibilities

Compare scarce power with dependable abundance, manual methods with automation, and reliable established tools with experimental ones. Needs can change rather than require endless shortages. A medieval tool, modern service and fantastic machine can share a research question without obeying identical world laws.

## Ownership boundary

Owns functional device systems and infrastructure capabilities. CRAFT owns making; LOGISTICS owns coordinating work and flows; GROWTH owns learning and unlocks; CREATE owns rule/asset authoring; MAGIC owns supernatural mechanisms. A material or interface shared with several systems has one defining owner, not a separate copy per topic.
