# G73 — The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Full research pass, September 26, 2026.** Scope: the 2017 Wii U/Switch game, its Expansion Pass, and the separately identified Switch 2 Edition. This is not Tears of the Kingdom, Age of Calamity, an emulator/mod feature inventory, or an accepted OpenLegend design. Five independent written review bodies and contrasting direct player accounts were read. The earlier G73 checkpoint's observations, attributed example and evidence limits are retained below. The expansion roster does not designate an earlier dedicated chapter or mechanics owner for this subject; original packet/chapter files are unchanged. Packet-wide preservation remains a separate integration gate.

## 1. Identity, promise and session shape

Link is a fixed protagonist, not a created avatar, class selection or party leader. The opening Great Plateau introduces a representative mixture of fighting, finding food, temperature preparation, climbing and rune puzzles. Its eventual paraglider changes the surrounding landscape from a backdrop into destinations. Established play alternates between selecting a distant landmark, preparing a route, discovering interruptions, solving a local problem, and deciding whether to continue or resupply. Nintendo's March 3, 2017 release offered an authored Hyrule whose traversal and solutions are unusually flexible; this does not make its quests procedurally generated. [B1](#b1) [B7](#b7)

The fantasy is practical ingenuity: a person with limited resources can read a dangerous place and improvise. The final objective is visible early, while most preparation is optional. This creates a meaningful distinction between **permission to attempt** and **ability to survive**. Faulkner's review particularly connects enemy danger with stealth, meals and route choice. It also warns that freedom requires self-motivation: a player wanting a continuously assigned itinerary can feel lost. [B3](#b3)

The Switch 2 Edition released June 5, 2025. Nintendo identifies improved resolution/frame rates, HDR, faster loading, an additional save slot and the companion-app service Zelda Notes. Its upgrade route is distinct from buying the base game or the Expansion Pass; membership access described on the product page concerns the relevant upgrade entitlement, not universal ownership of all content. It remains a one-player game. Neither this edition nor app assistance makes the original game multiplayer. [B4](#b4)

## 2. Player actions and interlocking mechanics

### Movement and reading terrain

Walking, sprinting, jumping, swimming, climbing, gliding, riding and shield surfing offer different costs and lines through the same landscape. Climbing makes height an attainable resource; gliding spends that height to cross horizontal distance. Stamina means a direct line is not necessarily a feasible line. Rain, exposed slopes and water can change the route calculation. Horse travel trades some freedom of climbing for speed on suitable terrain. Rafts make water an interactive surface rather than merely a border. [B5](#b5) [B7](#b7)

The game's practical environmental vocabulary includes movable objects, cuttable trees, fire, wind, water, electricity and temperature. The visually simplest question—what material is this?—can matter more than an item's damage statistic. This is bounded interaction, not universal real-world simulation. A rock is not automatically a crafting ingredient in an unlimited construction system, and a tree is not automatically attachable to every other object. Nintendo's documented development goal was to connect object reactions to the world and Link, not to implement every conceivable action. [B9](#b9)

### Slate tools: reusable capabilities, selective targets

The Sheikah Slate supplies Remote Bombs, Magnesis, Stasis and Cryonis during the Plateau. Bombs are reusable with a cooldown; Magnesis manipulates eligible metal objects; Stasis temporarily arrests a target; Cryonis makes ice pillars on water. Later Camera/Compendium and Sensor upgrades connect observation with finding a desired subject. Stasis+ extends the target vocabulary to enemies. These are not the sequel's Ultrahand, Fuse or Ascend. [B10](#b10)

Maps, scope, pins, stamps, shrine/tower travel and an optional sensor serve different information needs. A pin is the player's chosen destination, whereas a compendium target is a repeated search interest. Their distinction is valuable: assistance can help execute a self-authored intention without deciding the intention. The Slate's status as recognizable ancient technology also supplies an authored reason for particular NPCs to help Link. [B10](#b10)

Carter's attributed play account captures knowledge progression particularly well: a later dungeon taught him another use of an existing tool, allowing him to return to an earlier shrine. This research did not reproduce his experiment. The useful observation is that the **same inventory can support new solutions after learning**, rather than every obstacle requiring a newly acquired key. [B2](#b2)

### Combat, stealth and equipment decisions

Melee weapons differ in reach, tempo and whether Link can readily use a shield alongside them. Bows add ammunition, aiming and elemental options. Guarding, timed evasion, counterattacks and parries reward execution; stealth and an advantageous opening reduce the need for a prolonged exchange. Enemy camps are therefore not only health-bar tests: approach angle, noise, surrounding objects and the possibility of obtaining another weapon matter. Taking an exposed route or entering under-equipped can turn a modest encounter into an expensive one. [B1](#b1) [B3](#b3)

Weapons, shields and bows are expendable resources. Finding something powerful does not permanently remove the need for lesser equipment. This supports experimentation for some players but can also encourage avoidance or hoarding. The Master Sword should not be described as an ordinary indestructible replacement for the equipment economy; players specifically criticize its depletion/recharge boundary. Nor should a loose review description of post-clear play be mistaken for a conventional new-game-plus campaign. [B2](#b2) [B6](#b6)

### Cooking, collection and environmental preparation

Cooking combines up to five held ingredients in a lit pot. Food families and critter-plus-monster-part elixirs are distinct recipes; indiscriminate mixing can produce poor food. Ingredients can restore health and impart effects, but different timed effects do not simply accumulate into an unrestricted buff stack. A meal can therefore be both recovery and advance preparation. This is not a compulsory hunger meter or a farming/manufacturing economy. Ingredient discovery, acquisition and consumption form the relevant production loop. [B11](#b11)

Armor adds a more persistent alternative to repeated consumption. Head/body/leg pieces can supply environmental protection, quieter movement or climbing-related benefits. Examples include Snowquill clothing, Flamebreaker gear, the Stealth set and the Zora Armor's waterfall capability. Acquisition varies among shops, quests and hidden chests; one uniform loot source does not explain the wardrobe. Material collection also feeds enhancement rather than only sale value. [B12](#b12)

Great Fairy fountains require rupee offerings to reopen, with escalating costs across fountains. Their enhancement service then makes the collection of particular materials useful. This separates **access to an upgrade service** from **resources consumed by an upgrade**. Finding a fountain is a discovery reward, paying its opening cost is an economic decision, and returning with materials is a collection goal. [B13](#b13)

## 3. Progression, economy, time and recovery

There is no player-facing class tree, conventional experience-level allocation or learned spell list replacing the early toolkit. Growth is distributed across hearts, stamina, inventory capacity, armor, equipment quality, powers and knowledge. Shrine Spirit Orbs can be exchanged in groups of four for a heart or stamina increase. Korok seeds buy equipment carrying capacity through Hestu. Regional Champion rewards add abilities: Mipha's recovery, Daruk's protection, Revali's updraft and Urbosa's lightning. Their recharge limits preserve a distinction between a powerful safety valve and an always-available action. [B14](#b14)

The early game makes a single usable weapon, meal or warm garment important. Middle play turns those immediate needs into routes among shrines, villages, upgrade services and regional stories. Late play may prioritize a build, remaining shrines, memories, armor improvements, challenge encounters or self-imposed restrictions. NWR's account identifies 120 base-game shrines, but completion is not a prerequisite for attempting the final objective. The reward structure offers multiple ambitions rather than one mandatory completion percentage. [B15](#b15)

Rupees buy goods and services; materials can be sold, consumed or retained for later needs. This creates an opportunity cost: selling something now can postpone an enhancement later. Shops and quest rewards are authored economic structures, not evidence of simulated supply chains or player-to-player markets. A house and Tarrey Town provide recognizable spending/collection destinations without becoming a free-form city builder. [B12](#b12) [B16](#b16)

Clock and weather affect encounters and travel, while the Blood Moon restores defeated ordinary enemies with exceptions for completed quest bosses. The reset is explicitly framed in the fiction. It means clearing a route is not equivalent to permanently pacifying the world. The source also discusses technical memory behavior; this dossier does **not** turn those community technical claims into an audited implementation explanation. [B17](#b17)

Failure generally returns the player to a save rather than creating a corpse-retrieval currency loop. Autosaving reduces the price of an experiment, although retrying a difficult encounter still costs attention and repeated execution. Finishing the final battle is not a license to infer an indefinitely simulated, politically rebuilt post-Ganon world. Ongoing completion play and the story's ending are different layers. The research preserves the reviews' observations of accessible saving and optional preparation without adopting their occasionally imprecise postgame terminology. [B2](#b2) [B15](#b15)

## 4. People, world activities and narrative

**Story spoilers in this section.** Link awakens after a century; the failed defense of Hyrule is already history. Zelda, Calamity Ganon and the lost Champions frame an investigation of that failure. Impa and the surviving settlements offer routes into the past, while recovered memories let geographical recognition unlock authored scenes. The ruined landscape is therefore not just a large combat map: exploration changes what the player understands about people who were present before the disaster. NWR emphasizes the sadness and loss beneath the brighter adventure surface. [B4](#b4) [B15](#b15)

The four regional communities and Divine Beasts divide major story preparation into revisitable places rather than a single uninterrupted cinematic sequence. Yet physical freedom exceeds social freedom. This is not a faction-alignment simulator, a party tactics game, a romance-choice system or a generative conversation world. NPC dialogue, services and quest conditions are authored. Their locations and availability can matter, but the player does not design their personalities or command a standing adventuring party. Native amiibo support can add an optional Wolf Link companion; it is not ordinary co-op. [B7](#b7) [B16](#b16)

Side activities range from collecting and photographing subjects to horse-related tasks, treasure searches, local errands and specialized traversal challenges. The quest catalogue includes cooking requests, missing-person searches, riddles, riding challenges and investigations such as the Zora monuments. These are distinct uses of existing movement, observation, equipment and collection mechanics rather than a separate minigame engine for every task. Optional activities can deepen place attachment, but repeated fetching can also make exploration feel like inventory maintenance. [B18](#b18)

**Tarrey Town is a useful boundary case.** After the house-purchase prerequisite, Hudson requests lumber and particular recruits, including Greyson, Rhondson, Fyson and Kapson. Their arrival produces services and culminates in Hudson and Rhondson's wedding. Names, professions, places and time-of-day availability help turn a materials request into a social itinerary. But the sequence and recruitment qualifications are fixed. It does not model arbitrary employment, autonomous migration or a marriage system available to all characters. [B16](#b16)

## 5. Worked interactions

These are **constructed rules-based illustrations**, not claims of firsthand play or reproduced footage. They deliberately include limits and a next decision.

**A. Reach a high destination without treating the map as a straight line.** Intention: approach a distant shrine. Conditions: limited stamina and a steep face. Actions: identify a lower ledge, climb in stages, regain footing and spend acquired height on a glide. Interaction: topology and endurance jointly determine feasibility. Result: a longer horizontal route can be safer than a direct ascent. Next decision: spend future shrine rewards on stamina or health. Limitation: visibility is not proof of a safe landing, and rain can invalidate the assumed climb. This illustrates why movement knowledge and character growth complement rather than replace one another. [B5](#b5) [B14](#b14)

**B. Use an environmental solution instead of exchanging hits.** Intention: reduce the cost of a camp encounter. Conditions: an eligible heavy metal object and enemies within reach of its useful path. Actions: survey from safety, position with Magnesis, and use the object rather than immediately committing a valuable weapon. Interaction: object manipulation becomes a combat option. Result: equipment may be conserved, but positioning and exposure still matter. Next decision: retrieve loot, use a conventional weapon on survivors, or leave. Limitation: Magnesis is not telekinesis over every enemy or prop. [B1](#b1) [B10](#b10)

**C. Prepare for climate without wasting ingredients.** Intention: cross a cold area. Conditions: cooking access, ingredients and limited protective equipment. Actions: choose a coherent warming recipe rather than mix incompatible desired effects; carry recovery separately. Interaction: a preparation decision changes the viable travel window. Result: a temporary route opens. Next decision: explore within that window or invest in persistent clothing. Limitation: food does not abolish terrain, stamina or enemy hazards, and its timer can expire at an inconvenient location. [B11](#b11) [B12](#b12)

**D. Turn a catalogue into an intentional collection route.** Intention: obtain a known subject rather than wander indiscriminately. Conditions: its compendium entry and the upgraded Sensor. Actions: select that subject, follow stronger directional feedback and search the relevant surroundings. Interaction: observation history becomes a reusable information aid. Result: a previous discovery helps with a later goal. Next decision: keep that target or switch to another need. Limitation: the sensor does not select the player's priorities, collect the resource, or guarantee that the intervening terrain is safe. [B10](#b10)

**E. Build attachment through a constrained town quest.** Intention: make the emerging settlement useful. Conditions: Hudson's current stage and sufficient lumber. Actions: find the requested profession/person, invite them and return. Interaction: collection and geographical social knowledge produce a visible place change. Result: a service opens and a character has a new role. Next decision: use the new service or continue the next request. Limitation: bringing any arbitrary skilled NPC is not a supported substitute. OpenLegend should study that authored clarity without falsely labeling it general social simulation. [B16](#b16)

**F. Revisit a once-solved landscape after its reset.** Intention: travel a familiar route with less preparation. Conditions: a Blood Moon has occurred. Actions: scout rather than assume the cleared camp remains empty. Interaction: remembered geography remains useful, but remembered enemy absence has expired. Result: familiarity helps planning without guaranteeing safety. Next decision: repeat the fight, bypass it or seek newly useful resources. Limitation: the reset does not imply that every completed story event has been undone. [B17](#b17)

## 6. Presentation, controls and feel

The art direction's stylized surfaces and distinctive landmarks support route recognition at a distance. Bright, legible objects coexist with weather and a world of ruins; realism is selective rather than photographic. Nintendo's preserved GDC statements describe a reconciliation of playability and reality and identify experimentation with visual direction. Strange early concepts shown in that presentation are discarded exploration, not shipped features or proof of a different game secretly implemented. [B9](#b9)

Sound director Hajime Wakai retrospectively describes a move toward piano and the sounds around the player in Breath of the Wild. This is a primary account of direction, not permission to import the sequel's later sound-system changes. Jason M. Yu's separate musical analysis explains how interrupted phrases and silence can disguise a loop's continuity, and how familiar motifs in places such as the Temple of Time acquire a different emotional effect. That is his analysis of the music, not newly watched/listened footage in this pass. [B19](#b19) [B20](#b20)

Interface and control choices also carry costs. The player can mark an intention rather than follow a constantly prescribed chain, but selecting equipment and ingredients can interrupt momentum. Gyro-assisted aiming and motion-oriented shrine interactions should be considered part of the original control context, not an assumption that all players experience identical comfort or accessibility. Original Switch reviews report frame-rate dips; the 2025 edition's stated improvements must not be used to erase that launch criticism. [B1](#b1) [B4](#b4) [B15](#b15)

## 7. Production, release and reach

Nintendo developed and published the game. The documented leadership includes director Hidemaro Fujibayashi, technical director Takuhiro Dohta and art director Satoru Takizawa. Nintendo's GDC communication describes a deliberately small prototype to communicate the active, reactive-world idea to the team: visually resembling a 2D Zelda, it used the new game's three-dimensional physics/chemistry underneath. That is a concrete example of testing systemic behavior without first finishing the final presentation. It is not a source-code audit or a recommendation to copy Nintendo's proprietary implementation. [B4](#b4) [B9](#b9)

The development/release history includes public discussion of changing series conventions, delays from the earlier announced window, and eventual simultaneous Wii U/Switch release. The 2016 E3 Plateau demonstration and the March 2017 launch situated the game both as a familiar franchise and as an invitation to try a different structure. The historical chronology in the community reference is secondary; the official product page establishes the released product, while the GDC record establishes the developer-facing presentation. No budget, precise staffing total or marketing-attribution percentage was verified. [B7](#b7) [B21](#b21) [B22](#b22)

The commercial model is a premium game with optional paid expansion content and an edition-upgrade path, not a recurring multiplayer-service subscription. Nintendo's worldwide consolidated Switch table reports **34.06 million units as of June 30, 2026**. Its definitions include downloadable software and bundled copies, and its Switch 2 Edition accounting footnote allocates packaged and downloadable versions differently. This is not unique active players, retention, revenue or profit; it also is not a fresh combined Wii U/Switch total. [B23](#b23)

**Interpretation, not measured attribution:** concise clips of surprising solutions are plausible word-of-mouth units because the viewer can recognize the problem, action and consequence. The game's visual landmarks also make travel stories easy to recount. These hypotheses complement documented trailers, demonstrations, reviews and developer presentations; they do not establish how many purchases any channel caused. A huge installed franchise and launch-platform context are not reproducible advantages for a new project.

## 8. Expansion and return-loop boundaries

The Expansion Pass supplies two materially different additions. **The Master Trials** includes Trial of the Sword's stripped-equipment combat progression and Master Sword reward, Master Mode's altered enemy pressure, Hero's Path, the Travel Medallion and additional equipment. **The Champions' Ballad** adds Champion-focused challenges and story, another dungeon, Master Cycle Zero and further equipment/horse-related rewards. Its main sequence follows completion of the four Divine Beasts; it is not an alternative opening campaign. [B7](#b7)

The design contrast is useful: one package stresses mastery and revisitation, while the other adds authored context and a late mobility reward. Neither introduces the sequel's construction toolset. Additional content can extend a return loop without changing what kind of game the base world is. Master Mode's greater resource pressure is also not a universal improvement: it changes who will welcome durability and repeated preparation.

## 10. Transferable inspiration and limits

**Consistent affordances with a legible target boundary.** OpenLegend could benefit when a heavy object, weather state or learned action remains meaningful outside one quest. The dependency is reliable feedback about eligible targets and consequences. The alternative is a clearly authored special interaction. The failure mode is suggesting universal possibility while accepting only invisible exceptions.

**Knowledge as a second progression track.** An old ability can become valuable again through a new context. This avoids equating depth with an ever-longer ability list. It depends on recognizable causal rules, opportunities to test them, and recoverable failures. It should not become a demand that players discover undocumented edge cases to perform ordinary actions.

**Player-authored navigation with optional execution aids.** A pin and a selected search target respect different intentions. A comparable system could remember an agent's or player's chosen purpose without inventing a destination for them. It needs readable geography and a route to recover from confusion; minimal instruction alone is not good onboarding.

**Do not copy durability independently of the whole economy.** Rapid replacement, varied weapon behavior and flexible combat make breakage tolerable for some audiences. A world that emphasizes heirlooms, crafting investment and personal history might instead need repair, scarring, salvage or a protected signature item. The object-history fantasy and the expendable-tool fantasy can conflict.

**Permanent relief after temporary preparation.** A temporary protective meal can make a region approachable; later clothing can remove repeated preparation overhead. The general pattern is to make mastery reduce friction without making the place meaningless. Avoid assuming every need should remain a permanent tax on attention.

**Constrained social projects can still feel consequential.** Tarrey Town makes recruitment visible through people, services and a celebration. For OpenLegend, the interesting question is which of those effects should generalize and which remain authored. A universal migration/economy framework is not justified merely because a fixed quest feels alive.

**Use a small systemic prototype to test combinations.** The documented prototype tested whether interactions produced interesting decisions before final visual polish. An OpenLegend experiment could compare the same situation under a few coherent rules and inspect clarity, surprise and failure recovery. It need not reproduce this game's art, copyrighted setting or proprietary engine.
