## Player promise and preliminary system map

The player controls Link rather than designing a class or party. The opening Great Plateau supplies a miniature exploration curriculum, including combat, gathering, weather preparation and rune puzzles. Once the paraglider opens the larger world, routes and much of the order of objectives become the player's responsibility. Climbs and glides make a visible destination actionable rather than merely scenic. This is authored terrain and authored quests supporting flexible solutions, not procedurally generated narrative. [B1][B2]

A useful distinction for the completed study is **more uses for a small vocabulary**, not simply more powers. Remote bombs, movable metal, stored motion and water-made platforms interact with terrain, enemies and existing equipment. Discovering another use for an early tool can reopen a shrine that seemed impossible. Destructoid's Chris Carter explicitly described returning to an earlier shrine after learning a new application in a dungeon. That is his attributed play account, not a reproduced experiment by this research pass. [B2]

The resource layer should be studied beside that freedom. Weapons, bows and shields wear out; food and elixirs make exploration safer; stamina limits continuous climbing and movement. Avoiding a fight can conserve equipment. The opportunity to go somewhere is not a guarantee that an underprepared player will survive there. Shacknews particularly tied stealth, cooking and route selection to increased enemy danger. [B3]

## Reception evidence captured so far

**Thomas Whitehead, Nintendo Life, March 2, 2017 — Switch review.** Praised the opening area's indirect teaching, traversal, world variety and the connection between gathering and preparation. The review also recorded performance dips, a few repetitive NPC interactions and occasional breaks in world logic. Its enthusiasm for the character presentation is a perspective to compare with complaints about the lighter authored-story structure, rather than a universal preference. [B1]

**Chris Carter, Destructoid, March 2, 2017 — Switch review.** Valued early access to multi-use tools, the absence of repetitive dungeon-item tutorials, player map annotations and experimental cooking. Durability encouraged trying equipment for this reviewer, rather than only causing frustration. Reservations included frame-rate drops, limited boss memorability, and brief stealth/escort sequences. His favorable judgment is not evidence that every player welcomes consumable equipment. [B2]

**Jason Faulkner, Shacknews, March 2, 2017 — Switch review.** Praised the large explorable world, challenging encounters and flexible session lengths. Equally important, he warned that minimal guidance demands self-motivation and can be disorienting. His technical complaints were expressly about the Switch build he played, not Wii U or later editions. The distinction between enjoyable wandering and loss of purpose belongs in the final transfer analysis. [B3]

A VideoGamer review route was opened but exposed only a short verdict and links to related features; it is **not counted** toward the five substantive review bodies. GameSpot and Eurogamer were inaccessible through the research browser. Additional independent reviews are being sought rather than treating a roundup's scores as read criticism.

## Edition boundary already verified

Nintendo has a separate **Nintendo Switch 2 Edition**, whose product page identifies improved presentation/loading and Zelda Notes support. It must not be collapsed into the 2017 Switch/Wii U launch account. The final pass will distinguish the edition upgrade, Expansion Pass content and accessory/app-dependent functions. No Steam release is represented by these Nintendo listings; Steam review sampling is not applicable to this title. [B4]

## Transfer hypotheses, not accepted requirements

The most useful OpenLegend question is whether an affordance stays understandable when moved between contexts. A heavy object should not become usable only because the current quest expects it. Conversely, consistent reactions do not require every conceivable interaction to exist. The reference study should identify where the game permits experimentation and where terrain, item families, resource caps or authored objectives intentionally stop it.

A second hypothesis concerns onboarding: a small area can introduce a representative mixture of systems instead of teaching isolated buttons through a long corridor. This still depends on good landmarks, forgiving recovery and opportunities to recognize a mistake. Copying minimal instruction without those supporting conditions could reproduce confusion rather than discovery.
