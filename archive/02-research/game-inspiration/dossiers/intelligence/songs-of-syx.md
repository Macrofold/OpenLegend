## The strongest verified contribution

In a 2020 interview, creator Jake explains the intended bridge between detailed small settlements and more abstract large city games. As the settlement grows, the player's attention can move from particular people to groups, trade and borders while retaining the city. The interview explicitly presents some regional systems as future work at that date; those statements must not substitute for the parent's later V71 evidence. [S1]

## What remains unknown

Public descriptions and a source-distribution route are not sufficient to establish the exact current priority functions, interruption tests, allocation costs, path-search cadence or data layout. This pass does not label the AI 'utility', 'GOAP' or 'behavior tree' merely because that would fit observed behavior. Nor does a named, needs-bearing individual imply open-ended planning, broad semantic memory or expensive inference.

A useful next source, should it become available through an authorized game installation, is the shipped source archive and its citizen/work/service controllers. The present conclusions do not depend on claiming it was inspected.

## Transfer: keep a person real while making routine life inexpensive

Our inference is that an individual can remain the owner of a body, possessions, relationships, a place in an economy and an ongoing activity without reconsidering all of them each simulation step. Native continuation and shared infrastructure can make ordinary participation cheap.

But demographic representation cannot silently replace personal history. A character with a particular promise is not interchangeable with another worker of the same occupation. A coarse calculation may estimate background production without knowing every footstep; it cannot thereby invent who witnessed a dispute or which unique object was consumed.

A hypothetical baker's shift illustrates the distinction. Known recipes and assigned supplies can support repeated accepted work. A stock shortage is a dependency change; a visitor revealing that the bread is needed by someone the baker cares about creates a new personal choice. No global 'workers always serve the ruler first' rule should settle that choice unless it is an explicit authored controller policy.

## Transfer: scale the observer's explanation as well as the computation

At ten people, individual stories can be followed directly. At thousands, aggregate views help find shortages and unusual changes. An aggregate view should still lead back to particular causes and people where those are represented. The interface must distinguish a measured population total, an estimated background outcome and an individual's belief.

A visually busy city can conceal shallow agency; a less crowded scene can support deeper relationships. Both can be valid products. OpenLegend should define its promise before selecting a simulation shortcut, rather than borrowing a population headline and discovering afterward that it depends on treating people as replaceable statistical units.

## Limits

The relevant transfer is architectural discipline and a continuity-of-scale question, not a proven reusable implementation. No exact maximum population, CPU budget or current decision algorithm is certified here. The parent full-game dossier is preserved unchanged; this supplement adds a more explicit distinction between researched behavior and unverified internals.
