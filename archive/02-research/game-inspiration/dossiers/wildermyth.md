# Wildermyth — people whose histories change their available actions

**G25 · Completed research pass · September 26, 2026.** Research inspiration, not an accepted OpenLegend specification. PC campaigns, DLC and historical console criticism are distinguished. No gameplay, current source-code audit, benchmark or full video viewing was performed. The unsuccessful negative-rated Steam retrieval is recorded in §9, not disguised as a completed negative sample.

## 1. Identity and the changing player agenda

Worldwalker Games' Wildermyth combines comic-panel encounters, an overland campaign and turn-based party battles. The PC game left Early Access on June 15, 2021, after entering it in November 2019. Its Steam distribution supports Windows, Mac and Linux, solo play, online/LAN cooperation and Workshop content. The product recommends up to five players, assigning heroes within a shared party; that is not a persistent massively multiplayer world. [W9]

The beginning offers ordinary people becoming adventurers. Later goals include assembling a particular tactical combination, seeing how a changed hero behaves, protecting a relationship, completing another campaign or recruiting a remembered hero. These are connected but not interchangeable motivations: a player can enjoy a character's story while disliking another required battle.

**Mode boundary:** the base campaign's travel, work and intermittent events differ from Omenroad's combat-focused node map. **Continuity boundary:** Legacy retells heroes in new campaigns rather than simulating one uninterrupted chronology. **Edition boundary:** a 2024 Xbox or Switch complaint is evidence about that port and date, not proof of a current PC defect. [W1][W7][W26]

## 2. What the player actually does

The player selects and develops heroes, chooses who travels together, allocates time to places and responds to scenes. A battle is a spatial problem involving attacks, movement, actions that end a turn, defensive positioning and objects; the campaign is a scheduling problem involving people whose bodies and relationships continue changing. [W11][W13]

| Action family | Operation and a consequential constraint |
| --- | --- |
| **Scout and travel** | Reveal a region, move a group and choose when to engage. Geography and elapsed time make the next destination matter. Scouting is work, not instant omniscient map revelation. [W11] |
| **Secure and develop** | Resolve a liberated site, search more thoroughly for a reward, repair or build a resource station. A longer task competes with other uses of the same heroes. [W11] |
| **Prepare a route or defense** | Build bridges, open mountain passages, establish defenses or patrol infestation. A strategically convenient route requires investment before it exists. [W11] |
| **Fight and protect** | Move, attack, flank, use cover or form a defensive position. Armor and ward oppose different damage types; pierce bypasses protection while shred reduces armor for later attacks. [W13] |
| **Use scenery** | Interfuse with a suitable object, perform its action, withdraw or change links. The object supplies an origin and a limited opportunity rather than simply decorating a generic spell. [W15] |
| **Develop a hero** | Choose from offered abilities, improve equipment and accept or resist story changes. This is not a fully unconstrained class editor. [W12][W14] |
| **Manage loss** | React to a mortal choice, recover an injured survivor, accommodate maiming or prepare for retirement. Continuing with a consequence differs from reloading it away. [W14] |
| **Remember and retell** | Promote a hero into Legacy and selectively recruit an incarnation in another campaign. Recognition survives without importing every prior advantage. [W1] |

The interface exposes movement, prospective attacks and the current tactical situation, but a visible probability is not a guaranteed result. Line of sight, cover angle and the origin of an attack matter. Adjacent defensive walling does not mean every nearby relationship bonus follows precisely the same adjacency rules. [W13]

**Interpretation:** a good unit of agency here is a plan with an intelligible opportunity cost. Choosing a different person, source object, route or time should change a meaningful outcome—not merely the accompanying prose.

## 3. Named mechanics and equipment inventory

### Three classes are starting grammars, not three fixed builds

| Class | Examples of changed decisions |
| --- | --- |
| **Warrior** | **Sentinel** intercepts; **Vigilance** expands reaction opportunities; **Stalwart** trades mobility for protection; **Shieldshear** reduces armor; **Broadswipes** adds nearby damage. |
| **Hunter** | **Ambush** prepares a ranged response; **Jumpjaw** places a pinning trap; **Rogue** links a kill to concealment; **Thornfang** rewards melee kills with action recovery; **Through Shot** changes attack geometry. |
| **Mystic** | **Arches** creates a tree and control opportunity; **Compulsion** moves an enemy; **Elementalist**, **Humanist** and **Naturalist** improve different material families; **Openmind** allows additional interfusions. |

These are functional examples from the ability documentation, not a current tier list. Its table identifies a particular revision, not a verified latest executable build. [W12]

A mystic's ordinary kit connects to fire, lamps, bone, liquid, plants, metal, shards, stone, textile, tools and wood. **Flare** blinds near a lamp; **Wild Grasp** pulls and pins through a plant; **Shackle** uses metal; **Splinterblast** destroys its wooden source. Limited active links require selection. Range and attack origin are often measured from the object, so the same character can face genuinely different options in another room. [W15]

### Gear has roles, slots and a deliberately restricted circulation model

Weapons, offhand tools, armor and augments are distinct. A hand lost or replaced can invalidate a former loadout. Heroes cannot freely pass equipment around after allocation. Armor branches trade mobility, physical protection, ward or other properties rather than forming one universal best tier. Base examples include the warrior's **Brawlgard/Wildergard** and **Linkmail/Knightmail**, and the mystic's **Dustydrab/Heatherdrab** versus **Embergown/Ravenwing**. Augments occupy particular slots and do not carry through Legacy recruitment. [W2]

**Armors and Skins**, released May 4, 2023, adds mechanical armor branches as well as cosmetic transformations. For example, the warrior's Glimcowling/Spellcowling emphasizes ward and potency, while other branches emphasize movement or protection. Calling the entire pack cosmetic would therefore be inaccurate. Its PC sharing rule allows a party to access the pack when one participant owns it; that should not be generalized to every DLC or console arrangement. [W27]

### Five materials, several competing purposes

**Ingots, Hide, Fabric, Heartwood and Spellthread** support equipment crafting. Resource stations and controlled sites contribute supplies used at appropriate crafting opportunities. This is an abstract campaign economy, not a physically hauled factory inventory or an open market where every party member sells to every other. [W10]

**Legacy Points** recruit heroes, establish stations, counter eligible calamities and substitute for missing crafting materials. Victories and events supply them. A point spent to solve the next battle's equipment shortage is no longer available for a recruit or a later threat. The guide's preferred spending strategy is not a universal optimal policy. [W20]

**Interpretation:** small inventories can still produce rich decisions when uses conflict. More currencies are not necessary if one understandable resource connects immediate survival, infrastructure and future people.

## 4. Time, pressure and progression

Overland jobs use hero time. Different classes suit different tasks, and a larger crew does not always improve work proportionally. Scouting, securing, defenses, construction and recruitment can all be useful; doing everything first is not free. Defenses provide a continuing buffer and battle advantages rather than merely adding decorative fortifications. [W11]

Calamity cards strengthen enemy forces or introduce new threats as the campaign proceeds. Some can be cancelled by spending Legacy Points; not all are optional. The player is balancing preparations against an opponent whose capabilities are also changing. This is systemic campaign pressure, not another human directing a rival faction. [W19]

**Early play:** learn a small party and secure reliable equipment. **Established play:** coordinate builds, relationships, infrastructure and split tasks. **Later chapters:** account for retirement, replacement and persistent changes while facing stronger threats. These are analytical phases, not fixed hour counts or one required sequence.

Injury persists after a battle and heals over time, with activity and age affecting recovery. A mortal choice can offer a maimed withdrawal or sacrifice rather than immediately ending all progress. Retirement changes the party at chapter boundaries and can benefit younger heroes. These mechanics prevent the same optimized starting trio from remaining unchanged indefinitely. [W14]

**Interpretation:** time is strongest when it changes who can do the next job, not only how soon the next alarm rings. But constant urgency can discourage the leisurely character attention the narrative wants to invite. Difficulty and pacing should be judged for the intended audience, not treated as a proof that one style is superior.

## 5. Characters, authored events and the remembered world

### Particular people supply the cast

The writing tools describe scenes with roles selected from available characters using traits, hooks, relationships and conditions. Mandatory roles can prevent an unsuitable scene from firing. Outcomes can write history, change aspects or produce a physical consequence. Recurring NPC identity can be maintained explicitly rather than generated afresh each appearance. [W22]

That is bounded authored variation, not a claim that the game uses an LLM to invent unlimited dialogue or autonomously infer every motivation. The writer's guide describes a rural, whimsical Yondering Lands. **Deepists**, **Drauven**, **Gorgons**, **Morthagi** and **Thrixl** supply different enemy identities: cultists, reptilian people, corrupted creatures, constructed undead and dreamlike insect-dragon beings. Its historical TODOs should not be read as current implementation guarantees. [W21]

Campaigns give this material different frames. **Age of Ulstryx** introduces a shorter beginning campaign; **The Enduring War**, **Monarchs Under the Mountain**, **Eluna and the Moth**, **All the Bones of Summer** and **The Sunswallower's Wake** have their own central structures. Generic campaigns use different combinations. Names and premise families are not interchangeable with a fully simulated political history. [W18]

### Relationship types do different work

Friends gain a defensive benefit from adjacency. A lover's response targets the enemy that attacked their partner. A rival's stunt can encourage the other's next stunt. Family is separate from those relationships and does not simply add the same combat bonus. Working and fighting together can develop bonds; romance and rivalry can be locked for Legacy continuity. [W17]

**Interpretation:** rivalry can be a useful relationship rather than merely failed friendship. Yet fixed bonuses can also invite treating people as optimal pairs. Presentation and decisions need to preserve why these individuals matter, not just which combination yields the most damage.

### Bodies preserve some consequences more clearly than a biography does

Themes such as **Flamesoul** can change body parts, appearance and capabilities. Compatibility rules constrain combinations; a changed limb can remove ordinary equipment use while adding a different action. Events, later opportunities and maiming interact with that progression. A transformation is neither an unrestricted character editor nor necessarily a uniform upgrade. [W16]

Legacy preserves selected identity, themes and abilities while rescaling equipment to the receiving chapter. Most maimings persist, with explicit exceptions. A returning hero must be promoted to save that new version. Family continuity and every prior possession do not automatically follow. The promise is a recognizable retold hero, not an untouched save imported into another campaign. [W1]

## 6. Worked situations and counterexamples

Unless explicitly attributed, these are constructed explanations of documented rules, not played sessions or guaranteed random outcomes.

### A. The useful lamp stops being the same resource

A mystic links to a lamp positioned near enemies, then uses **Steal Fire**. The attack originates from the source and leaves it extinguished. The next turn's options have changed: repeating the same initial setup is not automatically possible. Choosing a different source or preserving this one could have produced a different sequence. [W15]

**Transfer hypothesis:** an action's source can be both a tactical origin and a consumable world affordance. An invention system should explain what happens to the source, not only what damage appears at the destination.

### B. The best worker is also the person who needs to recover

The player wants a site secured, an injured hero rested and another region defended. Assigning everyone to the current project speeds some work but leaves other intentions unattended. Splitting the party improves coverage while changing the strength of an encounter group. The meaningful feedback is the task forecast, the current threat and each person's readiness—not an assurance that any delegation will succeed. [W11][W14]

**Transfer hypothesis:** a helper can propose a plan across actual people and deadlines. It should not silently assign someone to incompatible simultaneous obligations merely because both requests are reasonable in prose.

### C. Survival changes a build rather than merely lowering a score

A wounded hero survives a mortal choice with an altered body. A previous two-handed or offhand arrangement may no longer work; a transformation may offer an alternative capability. The player must reconsider equipment and party positioning. Calling the result a generic penalty would miss the new options; calling it a free upgrade would erase the loss. [W2][W16]

**Transfer hypothesis:** a small set of visible, persistent consequences can carry more identity than a large passive trait list. They must remain comprehensible and compatible with the actions the game still advertises.

### D. One resource cannot solve three future problems

A party lacks a material needed for better equipment, wants a new recruit and expects an eligible calamity. Legacy Points can help each problem, but spending now removes flexibility later. The best choice depends on who can already win the next battle and what additional work the recruit would enable. No universally best spending order follows from the existence of these options. [W20]

**Transfer hypothesis:** broad resources create meaningful tradeoffs when players can see the alternatives. Invisible future gates instead make sensible present decisions feel like traps.

### E. A player gives an offered skill a personal meaning

Sin Vega's review describes Shay choosing a first-aid ability after her brother Uvwane dies. The choice becomes meaningful through the critic's interpretation of a shared history. It is not evidence that the game diagnosed bereavement and automatically selected a healer role. The same review preserves complaints about costly tactical and interface frustrations. [W3]

**Transfer hypothesis:** an AI game can offer or acknowledge a plausible connection without claiming that it knows the only true motive. Leave space for a player's account of the person.

### F. A reusable scene should sometimes decline to run

Consider an authoring pattern that needs two eligible rivals and a particular prior fact. No suitable pair is present. The documented casting system permits conditions to withhold that scene rather than forcing the nearest two people into contradictory roles. A different scene or no scene may be more coherent than maximizing event frequency. This is an explanatory authoring example, not an observed shipped event. [W22]

**Transfer hypothesis:** generation needs eligibility and refusal, not only output fluency. A world can feel more consistent when it produces fewer appropriate incidents rather than constant personalized but contradictory drama.

### G. A returning face does not promise a frozen family tree

A hero is recruited into another story with a familiar transformation and selected abilities. Gear power is adjusted and prior relations are not universally reconstructed. A player expecting chronological continuation may find this disappointing even when the system follows its intended retelling rules. Reviews explicitly reveal that expectation mismatch. [W1][W4]

**Transfer hypothesis:** portability should state what is preserved: identity, behavior, history, relationships or present power. “Bring your character” is too vague when those dimensions diverge.

## 7. DLC, cooperation, creation and presentation

**Omenroad**, released May 16, 2024, offers a gauntlet whose node map replaces ordinary overland management. Rewards occur within fights, making their collection tactical. Challenge runs omit random story events; Peril increases difficulty and Odes strengthen the party between runs. Selected heroes can return to the base game. Its separate **A Walk in the Unlight** campaign uses that format for specifically authored scenes, a journey into Netherflare and the Neverbefore Ball. This is not simply an expanded pool of base-game random encounters. [W26]

PC cooperative play assigns members of a shared party to players. The comic scene gives the group something to interpret together; the ensuing battle gives them concrete responsibilities. A particular playthrough's banter is not evidence that every group enjoys the same story, and historical console limitations should not be inferred from the PC feature list. [W9][W18]

The **Content and Comics Editor**, mod data and Workshop routes allow authored events, items, aspects and other supported changes. Campaign-enabled content and additional overrides have different roles. These are structured creation tools, not proof that arbitrary natural-language intent becomes a working mechanic on every platform. Permission to share a mod does not establish permission to copy another creator's assets. [W23]

Papercraft bodies, recurring faces and comic panels make combinations recognizable. The writer's documentation also exposes production considerations: a body part or prop and a completely new environment do not necessarily cost the same to add. This is a particular authored pipeline, not evidence that procedural presentation eliminates art or writing labor. [W21]

The reviews describe a distinctive prose rhythm and visual style that some players love and others do not. Historical complaints about misclicks, camera limits, defense placement and port rendering matter because an accidental loss is harder to accept as meaningful tragedy. Audio and the soundtrack are part of the finished presentation, but this pass did not listen to the full score or claim spoken performance for every panel. [W3][W5][W7][W25]

## 8. Production, distribution and economics

In his December 2019 interview, Nate Austin describes a self-funded project already representing years of work, papercraft/comic presentation, tabletop influences and the burden of responsibilities beyond programming. Communicating a distinctive hook beneath the generic label “fantasy RPG” was itself difficult. His account supports the importance of deliberate authorship and public communication, not a claim that every team can repeat the same path or budget. [W24]

Worldwalker's May 2024 announcement explains its hibernation as the completion of planned active work, with critical support and existing commitments retained. The developer explicitly rejects an investor-imposed shutdown or an inference that Omenroad failed. A finished premium game is not a failed live service merely because its creators do not promise endless new content. The announcement's remaining soundtrack, port and translation intentions are historical commitments, not independently verified 2026 completion dates. [W25]

The business model is a purchased base game with separately sold content. **No independently verified unit-sales total, development cost, revenue or profit figure was established in this pass.** Steam review counts are not units sold, and a creator's statement that a release is doing well does not establish a margin. The research therefore retains a qualitative business account rather than inventing a success multiple. [W9][W25][W26][W27]

**Marketing interpretation:** a changed face, remembered sacrifice or returning hero provides a retellable demonstration of the central promise. The source accounts establish that such stories exist; they do not measure their effect on purchases. A spectacular anecdote should not substitute for showing what an ordinary battle, travel decision or repeated event feels like.

### Steam helpful evidence: positive bodies, not a fabricated negative sample

The accessible mixed listing rendered **Most Helpful (All Time), English**, inspected September 26, 2026. **Mundas**, June 17, 2021, recounts a stone-handed warrior becoming a valued defender. **Jaduggar's positive Early Access review** praises the experience while criticizing event repetition. These illustrate enthusiasm with reservations; a positive-rated review containing criticism is still not a negative-rated review. [W8]

Repeated negative-only requests, language variants and the attempted review-API route did not yield usable bodies. The inspected mixed page's visible reviews were recommended. **Negative-rated Steam sampling therefore remains an explicit access limitation.** The five written critics provide disagreement but are not mislabeled as the missing Steam population. No unavailable review text, author or quotation was invented.

Helpfulness selection, historical versions and very small samples limit inference. The durable tension is whether repeated authored structures keep creating new meaning for the player or reveal machinery they no longer enjoy. More possible names or body combinations alone cannot settle that question.

## 10. OpenLegend hypotheses and failure modes

**Persist identity through consequences.** A changed hand, learned action, remembered person or surviving object can connect past and future. A biography without any changed possibility is weaker evidence of continuity. Do not make every unusual change either an obligatory upgrade or a punishment for curiosity.

**Give places a learnable action vocabulary.** Interfusion suggests that origin, material, placement and source consumption can matter as much as a spell's label. The dependency is predictable affordances. A narrator that invents an incompatible effect after a request would undermine experimentation.

**Use authored situations without forcing the cast.** Conditions, roles and prior facts can create coherent opportunities while leaving outcomes open. A scene should be allowed not to run. “More events” is not an adequate objective when the result contradicts a person's established relationships.

**Separate persistence dimensions.** A reusable hero or invention can retain some identity without retaining all current power or every private relationship. Clearly distinguish a retelling from a continuing world. Otherwise technically correct imports can violate the player's understanding of what was promised.

**Preserve interpretation.** Shay's skill choice matters partly because a player connected it to loss. AI can help notice evidence without dictating the only motive. Believable history requires restraint as well as recall.

**Protect meaningful consequence from interface accidents.** Loss can be interesting when the player understands the choice; accidental commands or unreadable deployment make it feel arbitrary. Test ordinary control and pacing independently from the quality of the stories people tell afterward.

These are research hypotheses, not new engine requirements, a mandate to copy relationship counters or permission to reuse Wildermyth's art and dialogue.
