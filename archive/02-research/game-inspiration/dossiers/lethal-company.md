# Lethal Company — a shared job becomes an uncertain expedition

**G38 · Full research pass · September 26, 2026.** Windows Early Access, with Version 80 distinguished from launch-era criticism and future plans. Five independently authored written reviews and English helpful-all-time positive/negative Steam bodies were read. No gameplay, footage playback, proprietary-code inspection or accessibility testing performed. Community mechanics documentation was often available through indexed passages rather than direct page retrieval; exact tuning and disputed details are qualified below.

## 1. Identity, scope and the promise

Zeekerss develops and publishes this first-person cooperative scavenging game. Its Steam Early Access release was October 23, 2023. A crew lands on abandoned moons, retrieves scrap, sells it to the Company and tries to meet a recurring quota. Native play supports solo or up to four people; enlarged lobbies and voice-mimicking modifications must not be mistaken for that baseline. The shop's old six-month Early Access estimate is not a current completion schedule. [P1]

**Version 80, The Blooming Update, left beta on April 5, 2026.** The official Steam announcement feed still has V80 as its newest shipped release at this September 26 audit. Zeekerss' later August development note still treats Version 85 as future and says its timing is undecided; the hoped-for October completion of the whole game had become unrealistic, with a large spooky update and the ending update still ahead. V80 adds Backwater Gunkfish, Feiopars and Cadaver Bloom, returns the Kidnapper Fox, and revises forest exteriors and factory rooms. This is delivered scope, not an assertion that every newly named creature's complete behavior was independently inspected or that the roadmap has a guaranteed date. [P2][P5]

**Interpretation:** the invitation is compact: bring enough valuable things home without losing the crew. Much of the complexity comes from incompatible local priorities rather than a complicated initial objective. The observer can understand a rescue attempt before understanding every piece of equipment.

## 2. Contract, money, time and failure

### Three collecting days, then the deadline

The quota is the required **sold value**, not everything picked up and not a balance that must remain unspent. The documented cycle has three expedition days followed by the zero-days-remaining deadline, when the crew must transact at the Company on Gordion. The quota grows over successive cycles; failing it ends the run by dismissal into space. [G1]

The money earned by selling also remains available for purchases. Selling is therefore simultaneously progress toward the obligation and funding for future capability. The Company's buying rate rises toward the deadline, creating a reason to defer sales; excess delivery can produce an overtime payment. No exact quota-growth formula or optimal sale strategy is asserted here. Keeping surplus scrap rather than selling everything may help the next quota, but that stock remains exposed to a later crew wipe. [G2] [G3]

The Company counter is an interaction, not an abstract menu. The crew places goods, rings for collection and waits for payment. Excessive disturbance around the counter can be dangerous. This makes even the apparent safe end of an expedition part of the fiction; it does not mean a surprise transaction death will feel proportionate to every player. [G4]

### Three different kinds of loss

An employee's death leaves equipment and, where recoverable, a body. The employee returns for the subsequent work period; this is not a permanent named-character campaign. Recovering bodies reduces the monetary penalty, but some deaths leave nothing recoverable. One contract guide loosely says recovery avoids the fine; the more specific body reference describes a reduced residual fine. Accordingly, this dossier says **reduces**, not universally eliminates, and avoids treating either percentage table as personally verified. [G2] [G5]

A **whole-crew wipe** removes saved scrap while retaining purchased equipment, upgrades, decor and remaining credits. **Missing quota** is the larger run reset. These are not interchangeable. A survivor aboard can preserve a stockpile even when the expedition itself failed. The ship also departs automatically near midnight, or earlier if launched; being left behind counts as a loss rather than an automatic return with one's belongings. [G3]

**Interpretation:** the game has both physical transport risk and an explicit team-wipe penalty. The disappearance of already stored scrap is a designed stake, not evidence of simulated thieves or a hidden causal ecology. That distinction matters when borrowing the rule into a persistent world.

### What progression actually means

Cash unlocks tools, ship capabilities and access to costly destinations. Learned routes, enemy cues, coordination and a filled bestiary make the human player more competent. This is not a traditional class/skill-tree RPG, a manufacturing economy or a base-building settlement simulator. Furnishing a ship gives a run a recognizable shared space without making every furnishing a permanent account unlock. Longer play pursues harder quotas, cleaner execution, exploration, discovery and social episodes rather than a demonstrated complete branching campaign ending. [P1] [R1] [S2]

Weekly Challenge Moons provide a separate seeded, single-day comparison format: the objective is a profitable attempt under shared generated conditions, not the ordinary continuing contract. The documented design intention emphasizes a common experience as well as a leaderboard. This must not be confused with a procedurally evolving persistent civilization. [G6]

## 3. The employee and the action vocabulary

The basic verbs are walk, sprint, crouch, jump, inspect, scan, speak, carry, drop, activate, strike, purchase, navigate, signal and return. Health, critical injury, stamina and load constrain those actions. A character may have survived a blow but still be poorly placed to escape. The scanner reveals nearby relevant objects and can add encountered creatures to the terminal's records; it is not continuous omniscience. A body scan can give evidence about a death without replaying the entire event. [G7]

Scrap has more than price. It has weight, handling requirements and sometimes useful or hazardous behavior. Four ordinary cargo slots allow several objects, but holding a two-handed object prevents using the other slots until it is put down. The item does not literally occupy every slot. A stop sign or recovered weapon can be both saleable material and equipment; losing saleable gear in a wipe differs from losing a purchased tool. [G8]

**Important revision:** V80 adds a dedicated utility slot for most shop-purchased non-scrap tools, excluding shovels. Zeekerss explains that the old tool-versus-cargo competition led many players to skip tools. The earlier study's four-slot tradeoff remains a historical lesson, not the complete current rule. [P2]

**Interpretation:** scarcity can make equipment choices meaningful, but can also suppress the interesting interactions the equipment was supposed to enable. An interface cost should be evaluated by the behavior it produces, not by the elegance of its theoretical tradeoff.

### Equipment inventory: several kinds of leverage

| Equipment family | Function and consequential limitation |
| --- | --- |
| Flashlight / Pro-Flashlight | Visibility and battery duration, not a guarantee of safety. |
| Walkie-Talkie | Remote communication; receiving and transmitting are different handling states. |
| Shovel, Stun Grenade, Zap Gun | Damage or temporary control. The Zap Gun requires maintaining its beam; a stun is not universal elimination. |
| Spray Paint / Radar-Booster | Marking routes or providing a remotely activated signal/flash; useful information must still be interpreted. |
| TZP-Inhalant | A fictional performance aid with sensory side effects, not a real-world drug recommendation. |
| Extension Ladder / Jetpack | Change traversal, but introduce deployment, handling and fall/collision risks. |

The Store reference and historical equipment documentation establish these distinct purposes. Prices, durations and damage numbers vary between sources/builds and are deliberately not flattened into a current shopping tier list. [G9] [G10]

The **belt bag** is a separate solution to tool logistics: it holds non-saleable items, not a second unrestricted sack of profitable scrap. Its interface requires taking equipment out before using it. The indexed reference also warns that its contents can matter to conductivity. This is an example of container rules that do not simply erase the properties of everything inside. [G11]

### Ship upgrades and the vehicle

The terminal routes the ship, orders supplies, presents records and lets the operator interact with coded doors and supported hazards. A secure door depends on facility power; removing the apparatus or changing a circuit can undermine the operator's earlier plan. **Signal Translator**, **Loud Horn** and a **Radar-Booster** offer different ways to convey a warning or location, not interchangeable global chat. [G12] [G13]

The **Teleporter** extracts the selected monitored employee but leaves their carried goods behind. It can recover some bodies, not every destroyed or consumed one. The **Inverse Teleporter** sends people into randomly selected interior positions without their carried equipment. These are asymmetric tools: one exchanges property for a chance at safety, while the other exchanges controlled entry for speed and uncertainty. A risky placement is not necessarily an intended procedural puzzle with a guaranteed solution. [G14] [G15]

The **Company Cruiser** adds a driven loading bay rather than instantly converting all cargo into safe inventory. Driving, collision damage, terrain, loading, recovery and attachment to the ship's magnet all matter. It can move bulky goods, but a useful truck on an open route need not be useful beside a large staircase or fragile bridge. The wiki includes mod-only indoor-creature collision experiments and contradictory old pricing; those are not adopted as native encounters or current prices. [G16]

The V70 developer account independently confirms later improvements to Cruiser durability and self-righting. It also describes a clearer radar, a face camera, compass, exit-path display and terrain contours, plus more interactive mansion rooms. Historical complaints about the early monitor and interior repetition belong beside those delivered changes. [P3]

## 4. Places, hazards and distinct creature rules

### Choosing a destination is already a decision

Experimentation and Assurance offer a different expedition shape from forest valleys, snowy Rend/Dine, industrial Titan or the hidden destinations Artifice and Embrion. Costly routing is a commitment, but repeated days at the same destination do not require paying again until the crew changes destination. Interiors combine authored room types into generated layouts; factory, mansion and mineshaft are distinct families, not an unlimited catalog of unique architecture. [G17]

Place matters beyond a difficulty label. **Adamance's valley and bridges** organize the route; **Titan's vertical exterior** creates different transport problems; **Dine's current scrap mix** can make item count misleading as a proxy for value. Hidden-moon clues offer a knowledge route into exploration: a discovery can reveal somewhere new without awarding a character stat. Exact spawn percentages, ranking claims and catalog totals are omitted. [G18] [G19] [G20] [G21]

Weather alters the trip outside. Rain can introduce quicksand; fog restricts readable distance; floods change passable routes over time; storms interact with conductive objects; an eclipse changes the ordinary expectation of safer daylight. The weather reference says worse weather does not itself increase the scrap reward. Its algorithms include stale fixed-count assumptions, so only the supported qualitative interactions are used. [G22] [G23]

Turrets, mines and other facility hazards compete for attention with the creatures and the return route. A supported coded hazard can be temporarily disabled remotely; that does not establish that every environmental danger can be turned off from a terminal. A command, its duration and the field worker's position must line up. [G12] [G24]

### A small vocabulary acquires different meanings

| Encounter | Documented distinction | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bracken | Brief observation can interrupt stalking; prolonged staring can provoke it. | Checking behind oneself is useful without making continuous staring universally safe. |
| Coil-Head | Observation restrains its movement. | Looking becomes an active duty that may conflict with navigation and other threats. |
| Eyeless Dog | Detects sound rather than ordinary visual exposure. | Speech, movement and noisy equipment can become risks. |
| Jester | Changes from following to winding to a dangerous chase. | A cue changes the group's objective from collecting to coordinated evacuation. |
| Forest Keeper | Long-range sight and pursuit reward cover and caution. | Silence alone is not the solution to every outdoor danger. |
| Snare Flea | An overhead ambusher attaches to the employee's head. | Looking only ahead misses a relevant part of the environment. |
| Giant Sapsucker | Nest/egg defense and escalating aggression. | Territory and disturbance matter, not just proximity to a generic enemy. |

These are community-documented behavioral distinctions, not inspected AI implementation. Bestiary prose, tuning tables and hidden-rule claims are not all equally reliable. Particularly precise claims that an enemy knows every coordinate or always selects an optimal route are not used to infer a general cognition architecture. [G25] [G26] [G27] [G28] [G29] [G30] [G31]

Other roster members include the Hoarding Bug, Thumper, Hygrodere, Butler, Masked, Nutcracker, Maneater, Barber and Old Bird. Naming them is not a claim to have audited every interaction. The relevant breadth is that some encounters concern possessions, apparent identity, attention, timing or environmental obstruction rather than only health and attack range. Neither the presence of many species nor fan requests for inter-species behavior establish a complete simulated ecosystem. [G27] [Earlier interaction-study boundaries](../mechanics/lethal-company-tools-partial-information-and-cooperation.md)

## 5. People, narrative and presentation

### Useful cooperation is not duplicated labor

The crew can divide into scouts, carriers, an operator or a driver, or deliberately send everyone inside. These are emergent assignments, not mandatory character classes. The operator sees a representation of teammates and hazards; the explorer has immediate sensory context. Giving each a different kind of evidence makes a clear callout causally useful, but also permits mistakes. A larger team is not automatically better coordinated. [G12] [P3]

Proximity communication makes distance and interruption part of the event. Horning describes approaching panic, a voice cut off by death and speech muffled while sinking. The person aboard can contribute while avoiding some direct horror, though the ship is not invulnerable. These are attributed experiences, not a technical audit of the audio system. [R3]

Native multiplayer, a good private group and a welcoming public lobby are separate propositions. Public matchmaking can involve incompatible expectations, kicking and mod requirements. V80's browser indicates detected mod loaders; the developer explicitly does not equate that with official mod support. A browser label alone cannot solve social exclusion or compatibility. [S2] [P2]

### Authored frame, discovered records, personal episode

The Company establishes an absurdly indifferent employer and a dangerous work routine. Sigurd's logs and bestiary annotations reveal a previous crew through found records rather than a companion dialogue tree. They are read through the terminal; a sticky note supplies the access clue. The notes are fallible in-world testimony, not an omniscient encyclopedia. The player's own rescue or betrayal can remain narratively important without becoming a bespoke scripted quest. [G32] [G33]

There is no demonstrated native romance system, diplomatic faction simulation, player-written spell language or recruitable LLM party in this loop. A human friend's improvised story and a fan-made voice mimic are not evidence that the base creatures generate dialogue. This boundary is particularly important for OpenLegend comparisons.

### Art, sound, readability and access

Low-fidelity industrial spaces, obscured distances and recognizable silhouettes let the presentation alternate unease with physical comedy. The reviewer at Corrosion Hour values its atmosphere but finds some signs and monitor information unnecessarily difficult to read. Sparse authored narrative and repeated places are not automatically failures, yet a distinctive style does not excuse every visibility problem. [R1]

The audio can communicate danger while carrying a joke or a friend's location. Music from a boombox is also an object in the world, not only a soundtrack. V70's developer note credits a friend for boombox/disco-ball music rather than supporting the claim that every asset was made by one person. [P3] [G34]

Porter's March 2024 criticism specifically flags missing sound alternatives. Current V80 options include disabling head bob and toggling sprint, while Steam's language table still does not mark subtitle support. Those improvements do not demonstrate equivalent hearing accessibility. Remapping or simplified graphics also do not certify the terminal, monster cues or multiplayer communication for every player. No device, assistive technology or frame-rate test was performed. [R2] [P2] [P1]

## 6. Eight worked situations

These cases explain documented interactions. Except the expressly attributed incident, they are constructed examples, not personal gameplay reports.

### A. Rescue the person, surrender the load

**Intent:** get a trapped carrier home. **Conditions:** an operator, a purchased teleporter and valuable carried scrap. **Action/result:** extract the monitored employee, leaving their goods behind. **Next decision:** attempt a separate recovery or accept the lost earnings. **Limit:** rescue does not equal fulfillment of the original delivery goal; destroyed bodies cannot all be recovered. An AI helper would need to understand which outcome the player values most. [G14]

### B. Keep a door shut—or harvest the apparatus

**Intent:** isolate a threat while another worker collects valuable material. **Conditions:** a powered secure door and an operator who has closed it. **Interaction:** interfering with its circuit or removing the apparatus changes the door's controllability. **Next decision:** coordinate the order of collection and retreat instead of assuming the earlier command remains effective. **Limit:** this is a supported power dependency, not unrestricted hacking of every object. [G12] [G25]

### C. One person looks while another navigates

**Intent:** withdraw from a Coil-Head without abandoning the route. **Action:** assign observation while a teammate handles movement or an obstacle. **Result:** attention becomes a shared resource. **Next decision:** hand over the watch, retreat together or call for extraction. **Limit:** a Bracken or another hazard can make the simple plan inadequate; different creatures demand different observation policies. [G26] [G25]

### D. The profitable object changes the trip

**Intent:** carry a gold bar home during a storm. **Conditions:** high weight and conductivity, not merely high sale value. **Interaction:** movement is burdened and an electrical warning can require separating from the object. **Next decision:** delay retrieval, choose another route or leave it. **Limit:** nominal value is not realized profit if the trip destroys the crew's stored goods. This preserves the earlier awkward-cargo lesson with a specific item. [G35] [G22]

### E. Keep a survivor—or send another carrier

**Intent:** maximize earnings before the deadline. **Conditions:** saved scrap and a living ship operator. **Choice:** send the operator out to carry more or retain a person able to leave with the existing stock. **Result:** the marginal load is weighed against whole-crew loss. **Next decision:** decide a retreat threshold before communication fails. **Limit:** the ship can be threatened too; the role reduces a risk rather than creating invulnerability. [G3] [R3]

### F. A warning changes the entire assignment

**Intent:** continue a promising interior search. **Trigger:** a Jester starts winding. **Action/result:** pass the warning and converge on exits rather than treat the new creature as another loot obstacle. **Next decision:** account for separated people or accept the cost of leaving. **Limit:** the warning is useful only if recipients can recognize it and act before the chase; another paragraph of lore is not a substitute for actionable communication. [G28]

### G. A truck is useful only on a suitable route

**Intent:** move several bulky objects in one trip. **Conditions:** a Cruiser, cargo and a traversable exterior. **Actions:** load, drive, recover from obstacles if possible and secure it for departure. **Result:** transport becomes a skill and planning problem rather than repeated hand carrying. **Next decision:** use foot transport on an unsuitable route. **Limit:** collision, terrain and vehicle loss can make increased capacity less valuable than expected. [G16]

### H. A death may be memorable without being satisfying

**Attributed incident:** Porter entered a cluster of turrets and died immediately. His review still praises the overall game, but uses that event to distinguish strong suspense from random difficulty spikes. **Interpretation:** a funny retelling does not prove fair warning or reliable counterplay. **Next design question:** which failures should teach a usable precaution, and which are acceptable surprises under this game's short-run contract? A persistent settlement may require a different answer. [R2]

## 7. Production, discovery and commercial context

This is a paid Steam product, not a free-to-play economy supported by randomized purchases. The captured US storefront price was **$9.99**. On September 26, the English review population was **276,581 at 97% positive**, with **1,035 recent reviews at 92% positive**. Those are review populations, not owners, concurrency, retention or receipts. [P1]

Simon Carless's **November 15, 2023** analysis attributes the breakout to shared incidents, recognizable low-fidelity presentation, creator participation and iteration. He identifies Zeekerss's prior Roblox work and cites the developer's account of roughly eighteen months of testing. These are reported development context and analyst interpretation, not private attribution data. His estimate of **642,000 copies and $5.7 million Steam gross** at that early date is explicitly modeled, not audited sales or the developer's take-home profit. It is not a September 2026 total. [P4]

Carless points to a VanossGaming playthrough as a concrete amplification example; the video was not watched here. Horning independently documents one discovery path from YouTube clips to an invitation from friends. These two observations support a plausible loop—understand the incident, imagine participating, invite a crew—without establishing the relative contribution of any single creator or channel. [P4] [R3]

The primary store describes Discord, Patreon and watching playthroughs as feedback channels. Later announced changes, including the utility slot, demonstrate adjustment to observed behavior rather than merely more content. The full release-announcement body linked by Carless failed to render in this pass; it is not represented as an additional directly read interview. No private budget, marketing spend, revenue split or audited current sales total was obtained. [P1] [P4]

**Interpretation:** an accessible premise can coexist with hard moment-to-moment play. An engaging clip is an invitation, not proof that an ordinary solo session, onboarding experience or public lobby is equally compelling.

## 8. Five written reviews and contrasting player accounts

The five substantial review bodies below were read. Historical dates and product boundaries are retained; scores are not averaged into a research verdict.

**Voice in My Head — Corrosion Hour, January 2024.** Values creature-specific learning, spatial sound and a crew's self-created stories. Reservations concern repeated environments, visibility, limited lasting progression and poor solo appeal. Its historical eight-moon count is not imported into the current inventory. Visual comparisons to other horror works are the critic's interpretation, not verified developer influences. [R1]

**Tom Porter — GameCritics, March 11, 2024, 9/10.** Finds the quota gives fear consequences and enjoys highly variable cooperative expeditions. He also objects to spikes like the turret incident, bugs and hearing-accessibility gaps. His disclosed solo and group play gives the criticism context; it does not establish defect prevalence. Modded additions are explicitly separate from the ordinary four-person game. [R2]

**Shannon Horning — The Tartan, December 10, 2023.** A horror-averse player finds a useful role aboard the ship and values the interplay between sound, panic and friendship. Wants more environmental variation and lore. The detailed communication incidents and discovery path are important first-person evidence, not a general survey. [R3]

**Marc Saltzman — Common Sense Media, updated September 18, 2025.** Describes an inexpensive, entertaining group scavenger with a learning curve, join-session bugs and suitability concerns around violence and strangers. The page's release date and early content counts conflict with primary/current sources, so its critical experience is retained without using its metadata as the mechanics baseline. [R4]

**Alfonso Majarucon — Game8, November 21, 2023, updated August 14, 2024.** Values the uncomplicated objective, suspenseful audio and comedy with friends. Criticizes weak onboarding and a cumbersome terminal. His strong claim that success is wholly skill-driven is treated as opinion, not a universal rule; Porter's unpredictable turret encounter provides a useful counterpoint. Later utility-slot and interface changes qualify the early equipment discussion. [R5]

### Helpful-all-time Steam samples

The English **Most Helpful (All Time)** positive and negative surfaces were both read. **John Bloodborn, November 8, 2023**, emphasizes dynamic proximity audio and proposes a voice-recording ambush creature. The proposal is not a delivered feature. **Spooked, December 20, 2023**, characterizes a shifting mixture of fear, slapstick and pursuit. [S1]

On the negative surface, **Undy1ngWill, November 17, 2023**, enjoyed group progress and furnishing but wanted an end state other than an eventually impossible quota; the author explicitly used a negative recommendation to make criticism visible. **big floppa gaming, November 6, 2023**, wanted more interiors. **Fox, February 27, 2024**, found public-group admission frustrating. These freshly recovered bodies preserve the earlier chapter's three distinct complaints. [S2]

A later **Derp, December 6, 2025** account combines affection for mastery with objections to escalating randomness and newcomer difficulty. Claims about the developer's motives or every public group are not adopted. The current patch's changes are evidence of work, not proof that each complaint is resolved. Helpful sorting favors notable historical posts and jokes; it is not representative sampling. [S2]

**Synthesis:** social access, lasting progression, variety, execution fairness and sensory access are separate dimensions. Increasing content may help one without repairing another. It is also possible for a negative recommendation to contain a strongly positive account of the central experience.

## 9. OpenLegend patterns and limits — interpretation

**Make complementary knowledge actionable.** A remote inhabitant who knows a gate, route or recent danger can contribute without becoming another combatant. An omniscient narrator eliminates the cooperation being borrowed.

**Represent partial fulfillment.** Saving a person while losing the cargo is an intelligible mixed outcome. A helper should not silently treat it as identical to accomplishing the entire request.

**Let objects change plans through several properties.** Handling, weight, conductivity, sound and location make possession different from safe use. Avoid multiplying properties that never affect a meaningful choice.

**Different threats can reuse ordinary verbs.** Looking, listening, staying quiet and leaving acquire contextual meanings. Consistent cues matter more than making every rule surprising.

**Do not confuse friction with depth.** The utility-slot revision is evidence that a theoretically interesting constraint can discourage the desired tool use. A hard-to-read monitor is not automatically productive uncertainty.

**Match loss to investment.** Short-session catastrophe and long-lived world care have different emotional contracts. Borrow clear consequences and rescue decisions without automatically copying total stock loss or inevitable dismissal.

**Separate social design from social availability.** A wonderful private-group experience does not prove solo value, inclusive public matching or equivalent accessibility. AI partners would need to provide useful independent action, not merely imitate friends' screams.

These are research observations, not accepted OpenLegend architecture changes or implementation tasks.

## 10. Coverage, preservation and viewing

### September 26 requirements-and-corrections audit

A fresh audit against the assignment's full mechanics inventory confirms coverage and makes the meaningful absences explicit:

- **Identity / classes / attributes / skills / leveling / trees:** employees are role-neutral avatars; emergent operator/scout/carrier/driver duties are team choices rather than persistent classes. There is no character XP, stat allocation, perk tree or job-skill progression (§§1, 5).
- **Items / inventory / weapons / armor / equipment / crafting / upgrading:** scrap, ordinary cargo slots, V80's utility slot, belt bag, purchased tools, ship upgrades and Cruiser are covered in §§2–3. There is no native crafting/manufacturing or armor-upgrade tree.
- **Magic / spells / powers:** absent natively. Capability is technological/equipment-based and human-coordination-based.
- **Traversal / environmental and object interaction:** walking/sprinting/crouching/jumping, carrying, doors/hazards, terminal interactions, teleporters, ladder/jetpack/Cruiser use and weather-dependent routes are covered in §§3–4.
- **Activities / minigames:** ordinary quota expeditions and Weekly Challenge Moons are the principal activity structures; furniture/ship social play is ancillary. There is no separate persistent casino/sport-style minigame economy (§2).
- **Combat / stealth / loot / rewards:** shovel/control tools, enemy-specific observation/sound/avoidance, valuable scrap and creature-linked equipment are covered in §§3–4. “Stealth” is contextual threat avoidance rather than one universal stealth-stat system.
- **Death / failure / recovery / economy / trading:** individual death, body recovery, whole-crew scrap loss, quota dismissal, credits, Company selling and routing costs are distinguished in §2. There is no human merchant market beyond the Company/store contract loop.
- **Story / relationships / romance / reputation / party / companions:** Sigurd's logs, bestiary records and the Company's frame are authored narrative; the crew's relationships are human multiplayer relationships. Native romance, NPC reputation and recruitable autonomous party companions are absent (§5).
- **NPC/AI schedules / factions:** creatures have distinct encounter behaviors, but there is no civilian daily-schedule or political-faction simulation. The Company is an authored institutional frame rather than a diplomacy system (§§4–5).
- **World map / environment / quests / events:** selectable moons, generated interiors, authored exterior geography, weather and the recurring quota provide world/objective structure. There is no conventional branching quest journal (§§2, 4).
- **Building / settlements / management:** ship furnishings/upgrades and stored cargo create a shared base-like space, but there is no settlement construction, farming or population-management layer.
- **Multiplayer / social / endgame / return loops:** native Windows Early Access play is solo or up to four-player co-op; larger lobbies and voice-mimic features remain mods unless separately delivered (§§1, 5). Escalating quotas, challenge moons, new versions and social stories provide replay, while a finished authored ending is still future scope rather than current endgame.

The current-version audit therefore keeps **V80 as delivered scope**. Zeekerss' August 2026 development note says V85 “might” arrive before a planned spooky update or might itself be that update, and that the ending comes later; those are intentions, not shipped content or fixed dates. [P5]
