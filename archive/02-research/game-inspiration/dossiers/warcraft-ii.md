## A larger war needs more than larger armies

The principal change from Warcraft I is not simply roster size. Oil makes a separate maritime production economy possible; transports make an army's ability to arrive different from its ability to win a fight; scouts and recurring fog make the age of information matter. The official original strategy guide distinguishes gold, lumber and oil, while its fog guide separates unknown black terrain from previously explored gray terrain whose current enemy movement is not visible. [S01](#s01) [S02](#s02)

**Interpretation:** an old map can remain correct while an old assessment of safety becomes false. A player does not need to forget the coastline to be surprised by a fleet. Reconnaissance is consequently an ongoing action rather than a permanent checkbox. A second implication is that economic strength in one theater does not automatically buy access to another: a formidable ground army can remain irrelevant across a body of water without a landing plan.

## Buildings expose dependencies

The Human building chart places the Shipyard behind the Lumber Mill and distinguishes the Foundry, Oil Refinery and Oil Rig. Town Hall, Keep and Castle form successive settlement tiers; later institutions unlock the specialized military and magical branches. This is not an unrestricted skill tree attached to an individual commander. Workers, site availability, resource expenditure, construction and travel still connect the institutions to the actual battlefield. [S03](#s03)

The original combat guide distinguishes health, armor, basic damage and piercing damage. Armor reduces the basic component, while upgrades and magical attacks interact differently with protection. A researched weapon upgrade is therefore not merely the same thing as healing an existing unit or building an additional one. Exact historical formulas must remain qualified to the legacy rules rather than presumed to describe the remaster's rebalanced client. [S04](#s04)

## Preservation does not mean unchanged balance

Blizzard's official forum thread is unusually important because its title now says **1.0.2**, while its first post still preserves **1.0.1, build 2207, December 4, 2024**. That post reduces Bloodlust's duration, raises its mana cost from 50 to 60 and moves its damage multiplication after armor reduction. It also improves Healing efficiency, changes ranged-unit research and restores multiplayer alliance/vision controls. The later post, **March 4, 2025**, changes ranged target acquisition, flying-unit command responsiveness and lobby controls. Neither set of changes should be silently applied to the 1995 original or the 1999 Battle.net Edition. [S05](#s05)

This corrects two possible research errors before they enter the finished dossier. First, the original guide's advice about extreme Bloodlust advantage is not a current quantitative balance analysis. Second, a November 2024 review's missing-alliance-menu complaint describes launch software; a later documented repair does not invalidate the original experience, but it does prevent reporting that absence as an unchanged 2026 fact. The latest build number alone would not establish the state of every feature, and this checkpoint does not claim a hands-on audit of a September 2026 installation.

## Spell families: capabilities, costs and misleading names

Human **Paladins** combine combat with Holy Vision, Healing and Exorcism. Reconnaissance and restoring a living unit are different expenditures of magical opportunity. Exorcism specifically matters against undead opponents; it is not a generic answer to every expensive enemy. The legacy guide's original Healing cost differs from the later patch, illustrating why a complete inventory must identify its edition. [S06](#s06)

The **Mage** has Fireball, Slow, Polymorph, Invisibility, Flame Shield and Blizzard. These cover line damage, hindrance, removal of a living combatant, concealment, a moving hazard and area bombardment. Concealment assists approach, not an indefinitely invisible attack. Polymorph can remove a powerful living target without winning an ordinary damage exchange. Flame Shield can threaten nearby friendly units as well as enemies. [S07](#s07)

The **Death Knight** has Death Coil, Haste, Whirlwind, Raise the Dead, Unholy Armor and Death and Decay. The legacy strategy page explicitly warns that Haste does not simply accelerate every unit's attacks: its consequences differ by unit and action. Similarly, a short protective spell is not permanent invulnerability, and Polymorph can remain a counter to a protected living target. Skeletons arise from the suitable corpse state and are temporary, not persistent recovered soldiers. [S08](#s08)

**Interpretation:** names alone are an unsafe interface to a ruleset. A player who thinks “haste means all actions faster” can spend mana without getting the expected result. This is a useful inspiration question about legibility, not a recommendation to copy the original's exceptions automatically.

## Expansion and reissue boundaries already established

Cyberlore's surviving developer page confirms that Blizzard hired it for **Beyond the Dark Portal**, with two twelve-scenario campaigns, more consequential heroes, a new setting tileset and ending cinematics. The page advertises a custom-scenario total differently from a contemporary written review, so the exact bonus-map count is not collapsed into a confident universal number. The expansion's stronger named units are not Warcraft III's experience-and-inventory hero system. [S09](#s09)

Ron Dulin's full GameSpot expansion review praises difficult, varied missions but questions selective early technology restrictions. Greg Kasavin's Battle.net Edition review identifies a more deliberately conservative reissue: online access and selected control improvements, not the abandoned additional Platinum campaign. It specifically notes retained nine-unit selection and omitted production queues/waypoints. The finished dossier will preserve those two different judgments rather than treating every rerelease as a new expansion. [S10](#s10) [S11](#s11)
