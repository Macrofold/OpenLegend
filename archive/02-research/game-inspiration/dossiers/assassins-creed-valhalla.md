## Different progression owners create different experiments

The original skill tree's Wolf, Bear and Raven paths combine statistical improvements with actions such as Heavy Dual Wield and Chain Assassination. Ubisoft explicitly permits resetting skills without paying currency. These paths are not three exclusive character classes. **Interpretation:** respecialization reduces the cost of learning through experimentation, but a very large map of small bonuses can make the destination harder to understand. The number of selectable nodes does not itself establish the number of meaningfully different playstyles. [S01](#s01)

Forgotten Saga, released August 2, 2022, uses a separate loadout and repeat-run structure rather than importing England's arsenal unchanged. Paths can close behind the player, and taking a replacement weapon removes the previous one. **Interpretation:** the same combat vocabulary becomes a different decision problem when the player cannot reverse every route or carry every alternative. A reset here is part of authored progression, not proof that the normal campaign erases equipment on death. [S03](#s03)

## A content finale is not the end of every maintenance obligation

Ubisoft's November 18, 2022 announcement describes The Last Chapter as a final content update and rejects adding New Game+. Its permanently raised hood option is expressly cosmetic, not a stealth buff. A later January 2025 Steam announcement, preserved in SteamDB's linked transcription, documents a Windows 11 compatibility patch. The first-party Steam destination returned no substantive body in this pass, so that later evidence is explicitly a mirrored publisher statement, not a personally tested fix. [S02](#s02) [S09](#s09)

**Interpretation:** content completeness, platform compatibility, service access and replay value are different questions. A feature promised in an old review or a store still selling a game answers none of the others automatically. The completed dossier will keep the stopped seasonal events separate from their subsequently obtainable rewards.

## Reception disagreement worth preserving

Steven Messner's PC Gamer review values the settlement, interlocking regional stories and weapon combinations, while criticizing the limited consequences of raiding and uneven stealth. Brett Makedonski's Destructoid review appreciates the combat and reduced inventory clutter but finds those regional stories insufficiently cohesive. This is a useful disagreement about the same structure, not evidence that one writer failed to discover the feature. [S04](#s04) [S05](#s05)

Stacey Henley at TechRadar prefers the small local encounters to long journeys and major plot beats. Michael Leri at GameRevolution values aspects of the combat vocabulary while finding overly effective combinations and guided exploration limiting. Marcello Perricone at GameWatcher praises customization, animation and music but strongly prefers the older series' narrative/combat identity. The last review's misspelled developer name and sweeping historical or audience claims are not adopted as factual evidence. [S06](#s06) [S07](#s07) [S08](#s08)
