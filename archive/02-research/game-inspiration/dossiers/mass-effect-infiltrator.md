## The actual mobile-game boundary

IronMonkey Studios' Infiltrator is a third-person single-player action game about **Randall Ezno**, not a portable Shepard campaign or the Infiltrator class from Mass Effect 2. Cover, touch aiming, weapon changes, powers and encounter scores supply its repeated activity. It does not contain the trilogy's conversation wheel, recruitable field squad, romance routes, free planetary exploration or general crafting economy. Its original iOS release coincided with Mass Effect 3; later Android, Windows Phone and BlackBerry availability must remain edition-specific. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

The initial control scheme combines virtual movement/look areas, contextual cover and tapping a highlighted enemy to begin aimed fire. The player can refine the shot toward a weak point, change weapons or use a power. Several reviewers praise that adaptation; others find the same system unreliable when enemies close in or surround the player. This disagreement is more informative than labeling all touch aiming either inherently accessible or inherently broken. [S2](#s2) [S4](#s4) [S5](#s5)

## Progression and composition already established

Encounter performance evaluates **time, style and health preservation** and supplies credits for improvements. Replaying a segment can therefore serve two purposes: mastering its combat and financing upgrades. Those are not necessarily the same motivation. A fast but reckless clear can differ from a safer or more varied one. The full dossier will retain that distinction rather than treating victory as the only feedback. [S1](#s1) [S5](#s5)

The four main firearms are the **M-96 Mattock Auto, M-22 Eviscerator, M-29 Incisor and M-333 Particle Beam**. Shop purchases develop a compact arsenal instead of generating an unlimited stream of random loot. The named Mattock Auto must not silently inherit the exact firing behavior of the trilogy's similarly named gun. Powers such as Pull and Leash add target-control possibilities but also have immunity, cover and readiness limits. [S6](#s6) [S7](#s7)

**Intel Points** are a particularly useful cross-system resource: they can be converted into local credits or, through the original linked service, contribute to Mass Effect 3 readiness. Campaign completion's war-asset contribution is a separate effect. Some reviews use those terms loosely; the full pass will not copy that ambiguity into the rules. No present-day activation or server operation is claimed, and Legendary Edition's readiness rules are not the original system. [S8](#s8)

## Story choices are narrow but not entirely absent

Randall's escape from Cerberus connects to his earlier participation in the organization's violence. Encounters with noncombatants offer a limited spare-or-kill choice rather than a full persuasive dialogue system. The final treatment of **Inali** follows the accumulated moral history rather than presenting a new, unrestricted last-minute dialogue choice. The relationship between repeated small actions and a later automatic outcome is worth studying, but it should not be described as a simulation of every person's trust or daily life. [S9](#s9)

The later bonus campaign controls a turian prisoner with a different, restricted toolkit. It is neither a new selectable protagonist for the entire original campaign nor a multiplayer mode. Primary patch notes are still being checked before recording the complete launch-versus-update boundary.
