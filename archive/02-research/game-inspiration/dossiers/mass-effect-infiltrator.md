# G106 — Mass Effect: Infiltrator

[Research roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Mass Effect 3](mass-effect-3.md) · [Mass Effect Galaxy](mass-effect-galaxy.md) · [Library](../README.md)

**Research date:** September 26, 2026. **Pass:** R01–R14 complete with the access limitations below. Documentary research, not a playtest. **Spoilers:** the story, morality and bonus-mission sections disclose Inali's fate and the turian prisoner's ending. No gameplay, device benchmark or video-viewing claim is made.

**Central finding — interpretation:** Infiltrator makes improvement within a small encounter its principal repeatable pleasure. Time, damage avoidance and varied attacks can give the same room several different challenges. That approach supports a compact mobile experience, but only when controls reliably express the player's intention. A score that punishes accidental cover exits or unregistered attacks can turn a mastery loop into frustration. Its cross-game reward adds a second motivation without replacing that local obligation.

## 1. Identity, release and the first session

**IronMonkey Studios**, working with BioWare, developed Infiltrator; Electronic Arts published the original mobile release. It launched on iOS on **March 6, 2012**, alongside Mass Effect 3. BioWare announced the Android release on **May 22, 2012**, explicitly including the recent 1.0.3 content. Windows Phone and BlackBerry 10 versions followed in 2013. The official Lumia announcement establishes availability by May 28; it is not evidence of a Windows desktop port. [S10](#s10) [S11](#s11) [S12](#s12) [S13](#s13)

This is a **single-player, third-person touch-controlled action game** about Randall Ezno, not Shepard's campaign or the Infiltrator class selected in another title. There is no native cooperative campaign, manually commanded field squad, trading system or character-creation suite. Opening action on an icy world establishes Randall as a Cerberus operative involved in capturing an alien, not an innocent outsider who only later encounters the organization's violence. The subsequent escape story changes the meaning of that original employment. [S14](#s14)

The campaign alternates directed combat segments, short transitions and radio-delivered story. It does not reproduce the trilogy's free destination selection, party conversations, romantic routes or extensive side-mission hubs. The repeated decision is how to clear the next encounter and improve a previous performance. A recognizable setting supplies context, but the player is buying a distinct type of participation. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

EA retired the application in **September 2015**. Contemporary reporting quotes a September 1 removal notice but, on September 11, still distinguishes observed App Store removal from expected removal elsewhere. The exact platform rollout should not be collapsed into one independently verified minute. That reporting's old assurance that previous owners could redownload is not a present-day compatibility guarantee. No official store installation or account-link service was successfully tested here. [S13](#s13)

## 2. Controls, combat and failure feedback

The original control arrangement uses touch areas for movement and looking, contextual cover, and tapping a highlighted target to begin aimed fire. The player can refine the aim toward a vulnerable part rather than accept the first hit location. A brief slowdown helps chain actions. Developer Jarrad Trudgen describes this as assistance for players who might otherwise struggle with a console-like dual-stick arrangement. It is not automatic victory: range, target choice, cover and timing still matter. [S10](#s10)

| Intention | Supported operation | Constraint or failure case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross a firing lane | Move, sprint, slide, use cover or activate cloak. | The intended cover transition can be unreliable; mobility does not remove enemy aim or collision. |
| Defeat a distant threat | Select an in-range target, refine aim and use a suitable weapon. | A target indicator does not make a blocked shot unobstructed; a short-range gun may require a dangerous approach. |
| Deal with a nearby attacker | Use contextual punches, pushes or a running charge rather than ordinary gunfire at contact distance. | The contextual transition changes what a tap means. An unexpected melee action can defeat a planned shot. |
| Control an enemy | Activate an available biotic ability and choose an eligible target. | Power-specific immunities, cover, range and cooldowns apply; the trilogy's identical names do not guarantee identical behavior. |
| Recover from damage | Break contact long enough for health and shielding to regenerate. | Damage can reduce the encounter grade even when it is later healed. Death restarts the checkpoint. |
| Improve a loadout | Pause and use the store, then replay or continue with the purchased capability. | Credits constrain upgrades. The bonus protagonist does not share Randall's store access. |

These are supported verbs, not a claim that every scene accepts every tactic. The combat reference explicitly distinguishes touch colors and proximity-based melee, while the review evidence identifies cases in which the interface did not behave as desired. [S2](#s2) [S15](#s15) [S16](#s16)

Enemies expose health and protective states, but the protagonist's condition is communicated largely through effects rather than a permanent numerical health display. Shields re-form visibly; blood at the screen edge warns of low health. Both can recover after avoiding further damage. A nearby unstable canister may damage enemies but can also kill the player. Cover, therefore, is a temporary tactical resource rather than a universal safe state. [S15](#s15)

**Interpretation:** distinguish three questions when evaluating assisted controls: did the game understand the selected target, did the world permit the action, and did the chosen tactic make sense? The player cannot learn an encounter reliably when all three failures look like the same unresponsive input.

## 3. Equipment, powers and the local economy

Randall's arsenal contains the **M-96 Mattock Auto**, **M-22 Eviscerator**, **M-29 Incisor** and **M-333 Particle Beam**. The Mattock Auto is an automatic variant, not a guarantee of the trilogy weapon's firing behavior. The Incisor supports distant precision; the Eviscerator rewards appropriate range; the Particle Beam addresses protection differently and limits mobility while carried. Weapons have a compact upgrade ladder rather than endlessly generated quality tiers. [S6](#s6)

Named upgrades express different improvements. **Tungsten Jackets** improve the Mattock's penetration, **Target Optics VI** its accuracy, and **Antarctic Heatsinks** its heat handling. These are purchased progression steps, not arbitrary craftable attachments that can be combined in any slot order. Weapon heat and firing recovery matter, but the campaign should not be described as using every thermal-clip pickup rule from Mass Effect 2. [S6](#s6)

The four biotic families are **Pull, Leash, Storm and Salvo**. Pull can lift eligible opponents and remove a Riot Trooper's shield; Leash moves a target but cannot simply seize a protected enemy remaining behind cover. Certain heavy enemies are immune. Storm and Salvo expand the damage vocabulary rather than creating a general-purpose physics sandbox. Upgrades can change how many opponents a power handles or how often it can be used. [S7](#s7)

Sprint, Slide and Cloak support movement and evasion; Punch, Push and Charge supply close-range actions. Armor and helmets add another small set of role choices. **Aegis Defensive** gear favors protection; **Stealth** equipment supports cloak or headshot use; **Cerberus Assault** gear favors offense. Body armor and helmet can be mixed, though some exterior sections require a helmet. This is equipment selection, not visual character creation or a universal clothing simulation. [S16](#s16) [S17](#s17)

The store accepts credits earned through play and can be opened from the menu, during a pause and from the failure screen. Purchased upgrades remain available when replaying earlier checkpoints. Credits also had a real-money purchase route. A shop offering an upgrade after failure can provide another way forward, but its presence does not prove that spending money is necessary to finish. Randall's purchases do not equip the bonus turian. [S16](#s16)

There is no researched basis for a crafting profession, autonomous merchant economy, player market, container-storage network or settlement-production chain. The economy chiefly changes Randall's capacity to perform in authored encounters. **Interpretation:** meaningful progression can arise from a small set of tools if each affects a plan. Conversely, buying enough damage to bypass a poorly explained encounter is not the same as learning to use that plan well.

## 4. Scoring, replay, intel and the limits of persistence

**Combat Analysis** evaluates a checkpoint through **Style, Time and Health**, with separate star ratings. Varied weapons, powers and chained kills improve the intended style performance; quick completion rewards pace; damage avoidance rewards controlled execution. These goals can compete. Remaining in cover may preserve health while increasing time, and a risky weapon change may help variety without being the safest route. Exact thresholds depend on the encounter rather than a universal score formula presented here. [S18](#s18)

Completing a chapter or replaying checkpoints can earn credits and improve a rating. A New Game Plus continuation retains upgrades, making a fresh run different from deleting or resetting the application. The game's reset and uninstall behavior should not be equated with a safe narrative restart: the store reference warns that purchases can be lost. Modern restoration was not tested. There is no perpetual postgame society in which liberated prisoners independently rebuild Cerberus facilities. [S1](#s1) [S16](#s16)

**Intel Points** provide a different choice. A point can become **25 local credits** or, through the original online connection, contribute to Mass Effect 3's Galactic Readiness. Campaign completion and later mastery rewards can add a war asset; those are not the same as raising the readiness multiplier. The promotional description and guide distinguish them even when individual reviews use the terms loosely. Legendary Edition does not use the old active readiness link. [S8](#s8) [S19](#s19)

An encounter can show its analysis screen before a dropped intel item is collected. Continuing far enough to collect it before restarting matters; the score presentation should not be mistaken for confirmation that every reward has automatically entered inventory. **Interpretation:** the interface needs to separate “encounter finished,” “loot acquired” and “external account credited.” Treating those as one event can create confusing losses. [S8](#s8)

The readiness tie-in is historically specific. It is not multiplayer inside Infiltrator, and it does not import Randall as Shepard's selectable squadmate or transfer his guns. No currently operational upload, paid-credit purchase or entitlement-restoration service is promised by this dossier.

## 5. People, story and moral state

The orbital facility known as **the Barn** supplies a concrete institutional setting: the Director's experiments on human and alien subjects turn Cerberus's ambitions into visible harm. **Inali Renata**, Randall's handler, becomes personally central to the escape; a volus ally helps undermine the facility. The setting connects laboratories, security forces, prisoners and the protagonist's former duties, but it remains a linear authored facility rather than a simulation of every employee's workday. [S14](#s14) [S20](#s20)

The player encounters noncombatants and can spare or kill them. These are limited actions, not the trilogy's branching conversation wheel. The moral state also influences cosmetic presentation after the relevant update. This gives repeated behavior a recorded identity, but does not establish independent relationships, faction standing or a freely negotiated surrender system. [S9](#s9) [S19](#s19)

**Ending spoilers.** After the Director's experimentation, Inali becomes a final opponent on the desert world identified as LV426. Her ultimate treatment follows the accumulated moral history rather than offering a new universal final conversation choice. A previous pattern of actions therefore constrains the closure. Whether that feels like recognition or the removal of agency depends partly on how clearly the connection has been communicated. The latter sentence is our design interpretation, not a measured audience consensus. [S9](#s9) [S21](#s21)

The story has no native romance-progression system, family simulation, recruitable companion party or autonomous diplomacy. Inali's personal importance should not be converted into a fabricated courtship mechanic. Similarly, taking the role of a Cerberus defector does not mean the player can choose to remain an organization manager and build an alternative campaign.

## 6. Five worked interactions

These are **constructed, rules-based illustrations**, not observed sessions. They assume the stated abilities, enemy and edition. They demonstrate dependencies and counterexamples, not one optimal walkthrough.

### A. Turn a sniper's warning into a tactical opportunity

**Intention:** stop a distant sniper before taking a high-damage hit. **Conditions:** a visible aiming warning, suitable cover and a ready ability that can affect that opponent. **Actions:** interrupt the line of fire, use Pull when the target is eligible, then exploit the exposed target with an appropriate ranged attack. **Interaction:** enemy telegraph, geometry, control and weapon range support one plan. **Result:** a window to act without simply exchanging damage. **Next decision:** finish the sniper or reposition for another threat. **Limit:** this does not make the same power valid against an immune heavy mech, and exposing Randall to other attackers can still lose the health grade. [S22](#s22)

### B. Choose between a clear and a better clear

**Intention:** improve a checkpoint's rating. **Conditions:** a familiar room and more than one usable weapon or power. **Actions:** vary attacks while preserving a route between cover positions, rather than repeat the safest single weapon indefinitely. **Interaction:** scoring rewards variety, speed and health preservation separately. **Result:** a more accomplished clear may look different from merely surviving. **Next decision:** replay for another star or spend the earned credits and advance. **Limit:** the game does not certify stylish risk as strategically wise in every situation; maximizing one category can damage another. [S18](#s18)

### C. Decide which adventure benefits from a find

**Intention:** improve Randall immediately or contribute to the linked Shepard campaign. **Conditions:** collected Intel Points and, for the historical remote route, a working account connection. **Actions:** redeem locally or upload rather than assume both rewards occur automatically. **Interaction:** one resource has mutually exclusive local and external uses. **Result:** an immediate purchase opportunity or a different game's readiness contribution. **Next decision:** acquire an upgrade, continue collecting or leave the link unused. **Limit:** an unavailable external service cannot be treated as a functioning present option, and readiness is not identical to a war asset. [S8](#s8)

### D. Let earlier treatment determine a later automatic act

**Inali spoilers. Intention:** give Randall a consistent response to helpless people. **Conditions:** earlier spare-or-kill encounters and the campaign's recorded moral state. **Actions:** repeatedly make the selected kind of intervention, then observe how the later authored resolution uses that history. **Interaction:** immediate conduct influences a future action without another dialogue prompt. **Result:** a consequential pattern, not only a color change. **Next decision:** accept that career or replay with different behavior. **Limit:** the player cannot assume that a last-second benevolent intention overrides all earlier actions. The risk is an insufficiently explained system appearing to take control arbitrarily. [S9](#s9)

### E. Reuse an environment while changing the permitted toolkit

**Bonus-mission spoilers. Intention:** survive as the freed turian prisoner. **Conditions:** the bonus campaign's fixed equipment and lack of biotics. **Actions:** switch the M-37 Falcon between its available rifle and shotgun modes, manage their firing windows and use cover instead of expecting Randall's purchased powers. **Interaction:** a different capability boundary changes familiar encounter decisions. **Result:** a distinct escape attempt without an entirely new combat engine. **Next decision:** advance with the tools actually available. **Limit:** the ending is authored and bleak; greater skill does not unlock an unverified route in which the prisoner escapes every final trap. [S23](#s23)

## 7. Updates and the bonus campaign

BioWare's **April 12, 2012** update announcement documents a manual-aim alternative, improvements to tap aiming, a new playable turian mission, visual optimization and moral-state appearance changes. It also announces additional rewards for obtaining three stars at every checkpoint. Android's subsequent launch explicitly includes the 1.0.3 content. These are not features every March launch reviewer had already tested. [S19](#s19) [S11](#s11)

The captured turian's bonus scenario changes more than a portrait. Its **M-37 Falcon** has rifle and shotgun modes, but the character cannot use Randall's biotics, change into his purchased armor or buy upgrades from the store. The Director's final trap is part of a bounded narrative rather than a promise of a full second branching campaign. No native cooperative or competitive mode was added by making another protagonist playable. [S23](#s23)

The later platform releases should not be treated as a remaster that includes every subsequent operating system. A surviving promotional page's “available now” describes its publication date. Retirement also limits the practical value of recommending the application today; the evidence supports studying the documented design, not promising that a particular modern device can install it.

## 8. Art, sound, interface and tactile feel

The presentation aims for a recognizable third-person science-fiction scene rather than Galaxy's comic panels. Armor, alien prisoners and Cerberus interiors preserve franchise identity at a smaller scale. Critics could admire the visuals while finding the voice performance and narrative staging unconvincing. Visual fidelity is therefore not a substitute for character credibility or dependable action feedback. [S2](#s2) [S4](#s4)

Touch placement creates a physical design problem: moving a hand from a comfortable grip to tap a target can interrupt the player's control of the device itself. Gamezebo notices that tradeoff even while enjoying the resulting action. More freedom to aim is not universally less work, and more assistance is not universally easier when targeting chooses an unwanted surface. These observations motivate comparison between tap and manual modes rather than an assumption that the update solved every individual's problem. [S4](#s4) [S19](#s19)

Range colors, weak-point aiming, shield effects and end-of-segment scores make the rules visible, but they occupy different moments. The grade arrives after actions that may already have felt ambiguous. **Interpretation:** useful feedback should explain not only the final result but which part of the attempted action failed. No formal accessibility audit, motion-comfort experiment, audio analysis or matched-device frame-rate benchmark was performed here.

## 9. Production, distribution and commercial evidence

In Caleb Bridge's April 6, 2012 interview, IronMonkey design director **Jarrad Trudgen** explains that BioWare helped establish the character, setting and story constraints, while IronMonkey developed much of the script, casting and recording. The game reused the studio's mobile Dead Space engine. Dialogue selection was deliberately omitted in favor of shorter play sessions, with story delivered around ongoing action. These are a developer's stated production choices, not proof that all players prefer short sessions or agree with that omission. [S10](#s10)

The same-day Mass Effect 3 launch, mobile platform rollouts and the Galaxy at War connection supplied concrete promotional links. The developer's account of mobile Dead Space introducing some people to its console counterpart is an anecdote about that earlier game, not measured Infiltrator conversion. No independent influencer attribution, viral-growth estimate or title-specific marketing budget was established. [S10](#s10) [S11](#s11)

The original iOS price was **$6.99**. Vore reports optional credit purchases ranging from **$1.99 to $49.99**, while saying extra spending was not required for his completion. These are dated commercial observations, not present offers. No audited unit sales, profit, development cost or lifetime player count was found. The decision to retire the game does not itself prove a particular loss figure or establish technical incompatibility as its sole cause. [S1](#s1) [S13](#s13)

**Interpretation:** a score-to-upgrade loop can be read as rewarding mastery or as withholding enough power until repeated play. Optional paid acceleration makes that ambiguity more consequential. A useful evaluation would distinguish learning the encounter from repeating it only to buy an easier solution; a reviewer disliking the latter does not prove that every player needed to spend money.

## 11. Conditional OpenLegend research lessons

**Give repeated encounters more than a binary result.** Time, resource preservation and variety can support voluntary mastery. The dependency is transparent scoring aligned with intentional actions. A grade should not punish an interface mistake as though it were a meaningful tactical decision.

**Assist execution without erasing the plan.** Target assistance can let a player focus on threat selection and positioning. Compare levels of assistance while keeping those choices intact. The failure mode is assistance that acts on a different target or refuses an apparently valid action without explanation.

**Separate persistence layers.** A world outcome, a local inventory reward, an account entitlement and a different game's contribution are distinct states. An interface should acknowledge each at the correct moment. The same principle applies without any franchise tie-in: finishing a job does not necessarily mean its payment has been collected.

**Use constraints to make a familiar place feel different.** The bonus protagonist changes available tools rather than merely increasing enemy health. Such a scenario depends on a viable plan with the reduced toolkit, not on taking away the only solution. The narrative boundary should also be clear enough that a fixed tragedy is not mistaken for a hidden performance challenge.

**Make automatic consequences understandable.** Repeated treatment of others can shape a later action without another explicit choice, but the player needs a credible model of the connection. A research comparison could contrast automatic character continuity with a final confirmation prompt. Neither format is inherently superior; the question is whether the player experiences recognition or arbitrary loss of control.

These are conditional research hypotheses, not accepted OpenLegend requirements, implementation tasks or permission to reuse BioWare's intellectual property.
