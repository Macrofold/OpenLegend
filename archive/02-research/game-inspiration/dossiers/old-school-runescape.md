# Old School RuneScape — full research dossier

**G49 · Complete research pass, September 26, 2026.** Scope: the separately operated Old School RuneScape service, including released Sailing and the September 2026 dog quest; historical reviews are version-labelled. This is neither modern RuneScape/RS3 nor RuneScape: Dragonwilds. Research interpretations below are **not accepted OpenLegend requirements**.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Modern RuneScape](runescape.md)

## 1. Identity, promise and version boundary

Jagex launched OSRS in 2013 from a 2007 snapshot in response to community demand. Its continuing development makes it an alternative evolutionary branch, not a frozen museum server. The same account ecosystem can access modern RuneScape, but the characters' skills, objects and quest progress are separate. The first-hour promise is learning a small set of interactions in a shared world; the long-term promise is deciding which achievements deserve years of personal investment. [S5](#s5) [M18](#m18)

**Sailing is released.** The launch press page carries a November 14, 2025 publication label but a November 19 launch dateline; the dated launch notes establish November 19 as the release event. The current game has **24 skills**, not the 23 described in older reviews. Jagex lists PC, Steam, iOS and Android with cross-platform play/progression. Modern RuneScape's ability bars, Necromancy skill, rebuilt Construction 120 housing and Treasure Hunter history do not belong here. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2) [M1](#m1)

OSRS is useful for OpenLegend because its players can care about being a cook, collector or house-builder without those activities being mere delays before the only legitimate endgame. Its warning is equally important: persistence can dignify effort, but cannot make every repetitive action enjoyable.

## 2. Character identity and the full skill lattice

Appearance is customizable; combat class is not permanently selected at creation. A character can develop every skill. Equipment requirements, quest access and player-imposed restrictions create practical builds. A low-defence PvP specialist and an all-round main can therefore use the same character system without belonging to different engine-level species. The skill roster is:

| Family | Skills and ordinary functions |
| --- | --- |
| Combat | Attack, Strength, Defence, Hitpoints, Ranged, Magic and Prayer: accuracy, damage, survival, styles and supernatural support. |
| Extraction | Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing and Hunter: ores, logs, fish and captured creatures/materials. |
| Production | Smithing, Crafting, Fletching, Cooking, Herblore and Runecraft: equipment, ammunition, food, potions and runes. |
| Land/time | Farming: planted resources and return visits. |
| Movement/property | Agility, Construction and Sailing: traversal efficiency, homes and ships/oceans. |
| Specialized activities | Thieving, Firemaking and Slayer: theft, fire-related training/activities and assigned monster hunting. |

Ordinary skill progression reaches 99, while experience accumulation and collection goals can continue beyond practical unlocks. Total level, combat level and quest completion are different measures; one number does not explain an account. Skills have prerequisites and unlocks, but not one universal talent tree or mandatory class specialization. [M1](#m1) [M8](#m8)

**Interpretation:** this is a network of capabilities. A quest can give the player a reason to improve several otherwise unrelated skills. The danger is a dependency chain that becomes compulsory work rather than an invitation to explore.

## 3. Core interactions and material chains

The recurring grammar is select a destination, select an entity or item, choose an interaction, wait or intervene, collect a result, and decide whether to repeat, process, bank or travel. Context menus and item-on-item/item-on-object actions let the same modest interface cover talking, opening doors, chopping, lighting, mixing and equipping. This is broad authored interaction, **not arbitrary physical invention**. Recipes and valid targets remain designed rules. [R1](#r1) [R3](#r3)

The economy connects recognizable transformations: ore to metal equipment; logs to fires, ammunition or construction inputs; fish to food; hides/gems to wearable goods; herbs and secondary ingredients to potions. Tools, stations, skill requirements, travel and inventory capacity constrain those transformations. A sword made through Smithing is not automatically the best possible sword: monster/quest/reward sources also compete with production. [M1](#m1) [M11](#m11) [M12](#m12)

Herblore is a particularly clear composite loop. A player obtains herbs, processes a potion base and adds the appropriate secondary ingredient. Potions can temporarily improve skills, restore resources or counter hazards. Crafting efficiency equipment can conserve ingredients or improve yield. Druidic Ritual introduces the skill; higher recipes create demand for farming, monster drops and trade. This turns an apparently simple mixing interface into an economic connection among several activities. [M11](#m11)

## 4. Inventory, equipment and the meaning of a trip

The backpack's 28-slot limit is separate from worn equipment. Stackable goods, banknotes and specialized containers alter effective carrying capacity without making all objects weightless and interchangeable. Banks provide persistent storage, search/organization and bulk withdrawal/deposit. A banknote is a transport/trade representation, not an edible fish or immediately usable crafting ingredient. [M6](#m6) [M16](#m16)

Item families include tools, raw materials, food, potions, runes/ammunition, armour, weapons, jewellery, quest objects, clue-related equipment, cosmetics, pets and rare collectibles. Acquisition can come from gathering, recipes, shops, player trade, monster drops, quests or activity-specific rewards. Loss/consumption can mean using supplies, exhausting charges, spending materials, selling, dropping or encountering a death rule. Not every valuable item is tradeable, and not every upgrade is a larger numeric tier. [M1](#m1) [M7](#m7) [M11](#m11) [M19](#m19)

**Interpretation:** limited loadout space makes preparation meaningful. A spare weapon, escape option and extra food compete with the capacity to bring loot home. OpenLegend should borrow that intelligible choice, not automatically inherit dozens of bank trips as an engagement target.

## 5. Combat: apparently simple input, demanding execution

Combat combines persistent stats, equipment bonuses, attack styles, positioning, supplies and timing. Melee weapons expose stab/slash/crush suitability; a weapon's appearance or nominal tier is not the whole damage model. Ranged equipment separates accuracy and strength considerations, with bows/crossbows/ammunition and thrown weapons behaving differently. Current ranged damage types also create monster-specific preferences. [M2](#m2) [M3](#m3)

Selecting an enemy can sustain ordinary attacks, but harder encounters require changing targets, stepping away from hazards, switching protection, managing food and choosing special-attack or equipment use. Safespots exploit reach and pathing; they are not proof of sophisticated opponent planning. Bosses turn this positional/timing grammar into authored tests. OSRS is not modern RuneScape's Revolution/Evolution of Combat system. [M3](#m3) [R1](#r1)

Prayer adds an independently draining combat resource. Protection and offensive prayers can be changed during a fight; equipment affects drain, while potions, altars and suitable housing facilities restore points. Skilled flicking is timing-sensitive, and different monsters evaluate protection at different moments. It is wrong to reduce all encounters to one universal 'pray when the projectile arrives' rule. [M4](#m4)

**Interpretation:** shallow-looking verbs can support expertise through timing and composition. The corresponding accessibility risk is that precise clicks and hidden timing knowledge become the price of participating in prestigious content.

## 6. Magic is combat, logistics and production

Four spellbooks are distinct toolsets: standard, Ancient Magicks, Lunar and Arceuus. Access and individual spells can require skills, quests or other unlocks; a player normally uses the currently selected book. Switching books is itself supported by world facilities, including appropriate player-owned-house altars. [M5](#m5)

Combat spells coexist with teleportation, enchantment and utility. Runes act as material inputs, tying spellcasting to Runecraft, shops, loot and markets. Choosing magical capability can therefore change a travel route or a production routine, not just combat damage. The spellbook is a meaningful preparation constraint; it is not an OpenLegend-style promise that any described magical effect is executable. [M5](#m5) [M1](#m1)

## 7. Geography, travel and quest-gated convenience

Gielinor is navigable geography containing towns such as Lumbridge, Varrock and Port Sarim, dangerous regions, dungeons and later-added lands. Walking/running, shortcuts, transport NPCs, enchanted jewellery, spell teleports and networks such as fairy rings and spirit trees have different requirements. A Slayer assignment makes these distinctions practical: reaching a master or monster can be a repeated cost, and an unlocked route can remain useful for years. [M12](#m12)

Agility is consequently both a training activity and a logistical capability. Sailing adds navigable ocean rather than merely another instant destination menu. **Interpretation:** unlocks that change familiar routes make progress physically legible. OpenLegend can adapt this to learned paths, trusted hosts and access rights, but should avoid making every trip a test of whether the player has memorized an external wiki.

## 8. Sailing: another layer of the world, not an isolated XP bar

Pandemonium introduces captaining, docking and a starting raft/Captain's log. Boats can be upgraded and fitted with facilities; crew can operate relevant stations. Port tasks, charting, salvaging and Barracuda Trials provide different activity structures. Offshore places introduce resources and encounters that connect back to existing skills. [S2](#s2) [M20](#m20)

This is a powerful extension pattern: the new traversal system creates room for old capabilities to matter. A vessel is simultaneously transport, equipment layout, resource investment and a place from which other work happens.

The March 18, 2026 boat-combat changes illustrate iteration after launch. Fire at Will lets crew attack targets in range; Ballistic Attractor conserves cannon ammunition, with quest/skill and construction requirements. Additional sloop facility capacity changes fitting choices. These are released rules, whereas the earlier poll was only a proposal/result. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

**Interpretation:** delegation is strongest when the player still owns meaningful decisions. Assigning cannon duty can free attention for steering without making the ship a fully autonomous adventurer. Port this distinction, not the exact ammunition percentage.

## 9. Farming, time and property

Farming uses planted crops with growth stages, disease risk, harvesting and location-specific patches. Growth continues offline; fully matured crops do not simply expire because the player failed to log in at the right instant. Seeds can originate in trade, theft or combat, and harvests feed production or sale. This provides a return rhythm without requiring continuous presence. [M10](#m10)

Construction turns resources and skill into a player-owned house. Its rooms/furniture are not only decoration: pools restore selected resources; portal facilities and jewellery storage support travel; altars can support spellbook switching; displays and logs express achievements. Unlocks therefore reduce friction in other activities as well as furnish a home. This is **OSRS's house system**, not the modern game's 2026 housing rebuild. [M9](#m9)

Other management content exists, but OSRS is not a generalized settlement simulation in which every NPC household autonomously consumes the player's crops. The broader lesson is to connect property to recurring activity rather than strand it as a decorative menu.

## 10. Quests, authored character and story

**Light story spoilers.** Quests are discrete authored adventures with item puzzles, conversations, journeys, requirements and occasional demanding combat. Quest points track completed stories and can gate later access; the journal distinguishes progress. Counts change with updates, so this dossier does not freeze an old total as current. [M21](#m21)

The tone ranges from the Lumbridge cook's modest problem to conspiracies and dangerous ancient powers. Recipe for Disaster uses returning characters and food-related rescue problems to join many quest lines. One Small Favour deliberately stretches a favour chain into comedy; its irritation is part of the joke, not a universal endorsement of fetch quests. Reviewers value these named stories precisely because they are not interchangeable kill quotas. [R1](#r1)

A modern example is **A Ruff Situation**, launched September 8, 2026. It connects Varrock's stray dog to a shelter story, a modest Crafting requirement and nurtureable puppy rewards. Adult dogs can assist Treasure Trail digging. A small local story therefore creates a persistent companion with limited utility; this is not a party-RPG romance or an unconstrained conversational agent. [S6](#s6)

NPCs primarily deliver authored roles, dialogue and encounter behavior. Personal quest flags can make a player feel recognized, but there is no evidence here of generalized autobiographical memory, unscripted political planning or a fully simulated daily economy. Gods, factions and regions supply story continuity without one universal reputation slider controlling every relationship.

## 11. Activities, rewards and endgame variety

Slayer assigns monster tasks through masters with differing requirements. Task completion, streaks, equipment needs and points create a continuing reason to move among enemies rather than farm only one optimal target. Skipping/blocking and task-specific rewards add planning. Named masters can even change after story events, connecting service roles to quest history. [M12](#m12)

Minigames and raids are not one homogeneous system. Barbarian Assault gives five players differentiated roles across waves and a final queen; it has its own supplies, reward currency and safety exceptions. Tombs of Amascut supports configurable Invocations: parties choose additional constraints/mechanics that increase raid level and alter reward prospects. This makes learning and mastery more continuous than a single compulsory difficulty jump. [M13](#m13) [M22](#m22)

Treasure Trails transform drops into routes, puzzles, required items and reward caskets. Even beginner clues can arise from ordinary skilling, connecting fishing/mining/woodcutting to exploration. Higher goals also include equipment completion, skill capes, achievement diaries, combat achievements and collection hunting. Finishing one quest line is not a global ending. [M19](#m19) [M21](#m21) [M22](#m22)

**Interpretation:** optional objectives reuse familiar places and capabilities. The failure mode is endless low-probability repetition whose expected duration is unclear to the player.

## 12. Economy and business model

Most ordinary accounts can trade, allowing specialization and exchange rather than requiring everyone to self-produce everything. The Grand Exchange supplies asynchronous matching; it is not a simulated NPC market driven solely by fictional citizens' needs. Its convenience fee is currently **2%**, generally capped at five million coins per item, with exemptions/rounding. It was 1% at introduction in December 2021 and increased in May 2025. Some tax revenue supports an item sink that purchases and removes selected goods. [M7](#m7)

The durable design issue is stock versus flow: permanent items accumulate, while consumption, fees and removal systems help maintain demand. A new reward can also invalidate an old activity. This is why economy stewardship is part of content design, not a dashboard added afterward.

The service combines a free-access portion with membership for broader content. Bonds connect real-money purchase and in-game exchange/membership under supported rules. Ironman access to the Grand Exchange is narrowly limited, including bond purchase rather than ordinary trade. Avoid equating a bond with modern RuneScape's removed Treasure Hunter system. [M7](#m7) [M8](#m8) [S1](#s1)

## 13. Death, danger and ruleset variants

Death is not uniformly 'lose everything' or 'lose nothing'. Ordinary PvM recovery uses gravestone/Death's Office rules and fees; PvP, particular activities and account types have distinct exceptions. The 2020 official death overhaul establishes the recovery architecture, while subsequent changes mean its initial edge cases must not be quoted as an exhaustive 2026 rulebook. [M17](#m17)

The Wilderness and dedicated PvP worlds make other players a danger under their own eligibility and loss rules. This produces stakes and contested-resource stories, but also the player-reported conflict between PvM participants seeking rewards and PvP participants seeking prey. That complaint is not evidence that all Wilderness players share one preference. [P1](#p1)

Ironman restricts external assistance/trading; Hardcore adds status-loss consequences; Ultimate adds severe banking/storage restrictions; Group Ironman allows a bounded cooperative economy. Restrictions change the meaning of familiar objects without needing an entirely new map. Ultimate death/storage rules are sufficiently different that normal-account advice must not be copied into that mode. Temporary Leagues and Deadman variants likewise change progression/risk in explicitly bounded modes rather than silently resetting ordinary accounts. [M8](#m8)

## 14. Social systems and what is not simulated

Public/private communication, friends, cross-world chat channels, clans, trading, shared skilling, minigames and group combat create overlapping social structures. Clan controls include rank-based talk/moderation and guest eligibility. They are practical social infrastructure, not cosmetic nameplates. [M14](#m14)

The shared world enables gifts, advice, rivalry and collective routines, but participation is often parallel rather than tightly cooperative. A crowd performing the same repetitive action is not automatically a meaningful social encounter. Conversely, an activity with differentiated roles can force communication even with a small group. [M13](#m13) [P1](#p1)

There is no general recruitable tactical companion party, romance-affection system, freeform NPC dialogue engine or authored-by-player world-law language in the verified baseline. Pets and sailing crew are narrower systems. Thieving is an explicit action/skill, not proof of a general stealth/awareness simulation. These boundaries matter when comparing OSRS with OpenLegend's much broader agent aspirations.

## 15. Presentation, interface, sound and feel

The low-poly aesthetic, compact icons, textual dialogue and context menus preserve recognizability across a long-lived world. That continuity is valued by veteran reviewers but can look crude to newcomers. Small mobile controls and tiny text create a real portability tradeoff: the same persistent game is available on a phone, yet high-precision activities may remain more comfortable on desktop. [R2](#r2) [R4](#r4)

Audio also makes geography collectible and memorable. Music tracks unlock at places or events; Museum Medley explicitly recombines recognizable themes, including Sea Shanty 2, into an anniversary/location reward. The sound library therefore participates in memory and completion rather than functioning only as interchangeable background scoring. This is a documented soundtrack structure, not a claim that footage was watched or tracks were newly auditioned for this pass. [M15](#m15)

**Interpretation:** progress feedback can be modest and still potent when an icon, sound or familiar location carries accumulated meaning. The interface lesson is less 'copy the old UI' than 'preserve a stable vocabulary while improving discoverability and input access'.

## 16. Production, distribution and community development

Jagex develops and publishes OSRS. Its corporate history distinguishes the 2013 community-demand revival from the continuing modern RuneScape line. The important production pattern is preservation plus selective extension: new content must fit a recognizable existing grammar rather than require replacing the game people asked to keep. Public proposals, testing and player votes are part of that process; Sailing's 2025 alpha/beta path and 2026 post-launch adjustments are concrete examples. No proprietary server architecture or team/budget figure is inferred from the visual age of the client. [S5](#s5) [S7](#s7) [S4](#s4)

The February 2026 cannonball poll explicitly specifies a **70% threshold**; older reviews' 75% references are historical. Polling is not absolute delegation of every operational choice: integrity and service changes can follow other processes. An approved proposal and its released implementation are separate evidence states. [S3](#s3) [M7](#m7)

Desktop/mobile continuity, Steam discovery, returning veterans, community discussion, challenge accounts and visible progression all provide distribution surfaces. The five reviews give direct evidence that portability and personal history matter to some players, not a measured causal decomposition of growth. The September 2026 dog update also links an in-game story to a charity livestream; that is a documented promotional/community event, not proof that charity activity caused retention. [R2](#r2) [R3](#r3) [R5](#r5) [S6](#s6)

## 20. Worked situations

These are **constructed examples using documented rules**, not claims of personal play or observed video.

### A. A cook's ambition becomes a capability plan

A player wants to finish a demanding food-themed quest sequence. They inspect its quest/skill requirements, choose an attainable prerequisite, gather the needed materials and build toward the next unlock. Completing a subgoal changes both the journal and what the character can access. The next choice is another prerequisite or a break into an unrelated activity. **Limit:** a long dependency chain can turn aspiration into mandatory grinding. [M21](#m21)

### B. The potion is a small supply chain

A player expects a hazard or combat challenge. Instead of treating a potion as a menu buff, they obtain the appropriate herb and secondary, use the required production steps and carry the finite doses. Consumption changes the next trip's demand. The next decision is whether to self-produce, buy or choose a different activity. **Limit:** repeated manufacturing can be busywork when little changes between batches. [M11](#m11)

### C. A sailor delegates without surrendering command

A captain fits the vessel and assigns crew to cannon duty under the released boat-combat rules. In an encounter, crew fire frees the captain to position the boat and decide when to withdraw. Ammunition conservation and facility slots affect the fitting choice. **Limit:** this is a bounded station-control system, not general crew intention, negotiation or autobiographical memory. [S4](#s4)

### D. Property reduces friction elsewhere

An established player builds useful house facilities, returns to restore resources or change travel/spellbook setup, and leaves for another task. Construction investment thus changes future activity costs rather than awarding only a room screenshot. **Limit:** a home can become a utilitarian service hub that everyone builds identically; expressive layout needs independent value. [M9](#m9)

### E. A raid group chooses its own next lesson

A group can complete its current Tombs of Amascut setup but struggles with another mechanic. It selects a manageable additional Invocation, tries the changed encounter and compares the new demands with the reward opportunity. The next decision is to keep, replace or remove that constraint. **Limit:** transparent difficulty choice still requires legible consequences; optimization can collapse variety into one standard loadout. [M22](#m22)

### F. The same herb has two different stories

A normal account can purchase an ingredient on the Grand Exchange. An Ironman must obtain it through permitted self-sufficient play. The material's mechanical use can be identical, but its dependency history and perceived achievement differ. **Limit:** restrictions need to be chosen and explicit; silently denying trade is not meaningful roleplay. [M7](#m7) [M8](#m8)

### G. A local kindness remains after the quest

After the dog-shelter quest, a player nurtures a puppy and later brings the grown dog along for Treasure Trail digging. A small authored story leaves a persistent, useful companion rather than only a checkmark. **Limit:** the documented behavior is limited; it does not imply that the dog remembers individual acts or supports open-ended dialogue. [S6](#s6)

## 21. Transferable inspiration and limits

**Capabilities rather than exclusive identities.** Let a persistent person acquire roles through practice, tools, relationships and place. OSRS demonstrates the appeal; OpenLegend would still need intelligible teaching, discovery and limits to stop every actor becoming an indistinguishable master of everything.

**Logistical unlocks are real progression.** A new route, useful home facility or reliable supplier can be as satisfying as damage growth. Make that convenience visible, but do not fund engagement by preserving pointless transport friction.

**Activities should feed each other.** Resource origin, transformation, consumer and sink form a test for a proposed mechanic. A new skill is stronger when it creates new relationships among existing activities rather than a disconnected currency island.

**Constraints change meaning.** Ironman, raid Invocations and bounded temporary modes demonstrate several scales of optional law. Their lesson is explicit, inspectable contracts—not a requirement to add OSRS's particular modes.

**Authored stories and emergent biographies can coexist.** A funny local quest can produce an object, pet or access right that becomes part of a long personal history. OpenLegend could extend that with real provenance and agent memory; those capabilities are not already present in OSRS merely because players remember the game.

**Governance and trust are product mechanics.** A technically functional game can lose goodwill through pricing fears, support failures or opaque decisions. Votes require clear authority boundaries and follow-through. Do not imitate polling as decoration.

**Do not copy the grind uncritically.** Long-lived accomplishment depends on durable value, not solely on time required. A simulation with autonomous agents also changes the economics: labour that a human MMO treats as scarce attention may become cheap delegated computation.
