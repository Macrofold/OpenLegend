## Identity and current boundary

Old School RuneScape is the separately operated game descended from the 2007 RuneScape backup, not a rules toggle inside modern RuneScape. Character progress in the two games is separate. The older reviews below evaluate the pre-Sailing game or the 2018 mobile port; their references to 23 skills are historical, not the September 2026 count.

**Sailing is released, not merely proposed.** Jagex's launch announcement has a November 14, 2025 page publication label but a November 19 launch dateline; the accompanying dated launch update establishes November 19 as the release boundary. Its ocean traversal, ship progression and new islands must be included in the current full inventory. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2)

The game combines a persistent shared world with individually accumulated skills and quest unlocks. It permits goals outside combat: gathering, processing, trading, construction and long-term collections can be ends in themselves. Modern RuneScape's Evolution of Combat, Necromancy, 2026 housing rebuild and Treasure Hunter history must not be transferred into this dossier.

## Governance is a game system, not just marketing

The February 2026 Sailing Cannonball Facility poll explicitly uses a **70% passage threshold**. It proposed a facility conserving 60% of boat cannon ammunition; the results show 88.3% support among counted yes/no responses. These are evidence of a specific proposal and vote, not proof that every poll changes the game immediately. The March 18 boat-combat release notes provide the separate implementation evidence. Historical reviews referring to 75% describe the older policy. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

For OpenLegend, the research question is how a community can meaningfully shape persistent-world rules without making every individual balance adjustment a referendum. This is an inspiration, not an accepted governance requirement.

## Five independent written assessments

The fifth entry is a substantive firsthand retrospective rather than a scored launch review; that distinction matters. The first four are direct reviews. Steam player testimony is additional and not substituted for these five.

### PC Gamer — Austin Wood, original 2018 review, republished February 24, 2021

Wood values player-chosen goals, distinct quest stories and the connections between skills. His own route toward Recipe for Disaster makes preparation a personal project rather than a compulsory main quest. He also finds the grind genuinely tedious in places: repetitive Herblore, slow progression and weak initial direction are not excused by eventual satisfaction. This is a historical review, not verification of the current skill roster. [R1](#r1)

### Pocket Gamer — Harry Slater, November 1, 2018

Slater praises the mobile port for preserving the shared account and large, interconnected game rather than replacing it with a shallow mobile spin-off. His reservations concern early drudgery, ageing presentation and small-screen interaction. Portability expands where an established player can make progress; it does not automatically make the underlying game welcoming to a new audience. [R2](#r2)

### TouchArcade — Eli Hodapp, October 30, 2018

Hodapp's enthusiastic mobile review emphasizes discovery, substantial long-term activities and meaningful short sessions. He describes choosing not to optimize every decision through guides. The initial lack of direction and small touch targets remain real friction. His concert-side fishing account is attributed personal testimony, not evidence that every activity works equally well on a phone. [R3](#r3)

### Bleeding Cool — Madeline Ricchiuto, February 10, 2019

Ricchiuto finds the click-based structure naturally suited to a mobile port and appreciates continuity, but is less forgiving about tiny text, graphics and limited control/customization. Her description of the same familiar game is useful reception evidence, not a claim that a modern OSRS character directly resumes a RuneScape 3 save or that no content was added after 2007. [R4](#r4)

### GamesRadar+ — Andrew Brown, 2025 firsthand retrospective

Brown values non-combat goals that remain meaningful: money from fishing can become a house rather than another combat upgrade. He contrasts authored, often comic quests with generic kill quotas and explains how alternative training activities can transform a disliked skill. He acknowledges personal difficulty with demanding quest bosses and sharply criticizes a publisher community decision. The piece is an affectionate veteran reappraisal, not a neutral newcomer trial or a scored current-version review. [R5](#r5)

## Helpful Steam player evidence

The English most-helpful/all-time surface was inspected on September 26, 2026. These are selected accounts, not a representative survey. The lengthy positive review by **$11**, dated February 24, 2021, combines praise for goal freedom and questing with complaints about bots, account support and tensions between PvM and Wilderness PvP incentives. Historical prices, client-market-share guesses and player hours displayed today are not treated as independently verified facts.

**Aerdis117's January 16, 2025 negative review** objects to subscription increases and monetization-survey ideas. Its advertising/support-paywall complaints concern feared proposals, not evidence that those systems shipped. Other helpful positive accounts foreground childhood memories and returning after breaks. This sample therefore separates dissatisfaction with service stewardship from dissatisfaction with the core progression game. [P1](#p1)
