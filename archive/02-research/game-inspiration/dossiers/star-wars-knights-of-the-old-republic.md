# G109 — Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic

**Full research pass, September 26, 2026.** Original BioWare game, not KOTOR II, The Old Republic MMO or a remake. Research hypotheses here are not approved OpenLegend requirements. [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Library](../README.md).

Seven substantive written reviews across four editorial sites were read, spanning original and port editions. The initial Steam pass yielded a small **Most Helpful (Week), English** sample; the September 27 audit additionally recovered Steam's indexed English **Most Helpful (All Time)** surface, while retaining the weekly positive/negative bodies as contrasting current testimony. The six worked situations below are constructed from documented rules, not personally observed playtests. Major ending spoilers have their own section.

## Identity, versions and the player promise

BioWare developed the original, LucasArts published it, and Aspyr handled later ports. Xbox launched in July 2003; the Steam listing dates Windows to November 19, 2003. The setting is roughly four millennia before the films, allowing recognizable Jedi, Sith, droids and starships without requiring the player's adventures to fit between movie scenes. This is a single-player party RPG with nine recruitable companions, not a persistent multiplayer galaxy. [S01](#s01) [S24](#s24)

Original Xbox, the later Windows release, iOS and Switch are not interchangeable evidence snapshots. The Windows release included Yavin station content absent from the original Xbox disc; contemporary discussion of an anticipated Xbox Live download describes that historical distribution state, not every subsequent edition. The 2013 iPad and 2021 Switch reviews investigate ports, not new main campaigns. No mechanics or availability claim from a remake announcement is used to describe the shipped 2003 game. [S09](#s09) [S05](#s05) [S06](#s06)

The first session begins with a created protagonist escaping the Endar Spire and learning to operate on Taris. Established play alternates conversations, investigation, combat, character building and interplanetary travel. After Dantooine, the central Star Map search supplies direction while leaving much of the middle itinerary selectable. The Ebon Hawk connects destinations and becomes a recurring place to talk, prepare and encounter smaller stories. Freedom means multiple authored approaches and routes, not the ability to invent arbitrary quests or alter every object. [S25](#s25) [S30](#s30)

**Interpretation:** the promise combines a personal role with cinematic importance. The player is not simply controlling a famous film protagonist, yet the interface, sound and companions make the role immediately recognizable. Its strength depends on enough authored acknowledgment that a menu choice feels like a personal decision rather than selecting the next scene.

## Character creation and progression

Choose appearance, name, sex and a starting class, then assign attributes, skills and feats or accept assistance from the game. Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma contribute differently to physical combat, resilience, technical competence, conversation and Force use. A single attribute is not a universal measure of character worth, and a convincing physical attack animation does not guarantee that the underlying attack roll succeeds. [S02](#s02) [S12](#s12)

**Soldier** emphasizes vitality and frequent combat feats. **Scout** occupies a middle position, including automatic implant progression and defensive abilities. **Scoundrel** trades some direct durability for skills, Scoundrel's Luck and Sneak Attack. Later the protagonist adds a Jedi class: **Guardian** emphasizes martial competence and Force Jump, **Consular** stronger Force capacity and Force Focus, and **Sentinel** a mixed role with characteristic immunities. Starting expertise is therefore layered with a later identity rather than simply discarded. These are the original game's classes, not the sequel's prestige classes. [S12](#s12) [S14](#s14)

Experience from encounters and quests produces level-up choices. Skills, feats and Force powers are separate investments: improving a technical skill does not itself grant an attack feat, and a new active power still needs the resources and conditions to use it. Feats include weapon proficiencies and styles such as Dueling, Two-Weapon Fighting and Flurry. A two-weapon setup may look impressive before its penalties are adequately addressed. Auto-leveling reduces management but also delegates choices that affect a build's coherence. [S02](#s02) [S09](#s09)

Early progression focuses on surviving Taris with mundane equipment and complementary companions. The Jedi transition changes both fiction and options; the middle game develops powers, feat chains, gear and personal quests. Late encounters test the resulting build rather than opening a perpetual postgame simulation. Replay motivation comes from different characters, alignments, companions and decisions. It is not a seasonal reset, procedurally renewing settlement economy or endgame raid service. [S03](#s03) [S24](#s24) [S25](#s25)

## Actions, combat and meaningful noncombat skills

### A tactical ruleset presented as continuous action

The camera follows a directly moved character, but combat resolves through RPG rules rather than reflexively connecting every visible swing. Pause, choose targets, queue actions and switch control among party members; uncommanded companions follow scripts. The development postmortem describes personal combat rounds and coordinated attack/defense animations designed to resemble Star Wars fighting. This deliberate visual continuity caused onboarding trouble when early players expected action-game button timing. [S13](#s13)

Ordinary attacks, feats, Force powers, shields, grenades, stimulants and medical items compete for useful opportunities. Crowd control can be more valuable than immediate damage when it prevents an enemy from acting or enables a companion's special attack. A shield that counters energy is not automatically equally useful against other damage. Encounter preparation, equipment compatibility and action order matter alongside the displayed attack or defense values. Player-contributed strategies document these combinations, but their preferred builds are not universal laws. [S15](#s15) [S18](#s18)

The manual describes Default Attack, Grenadier and Jedi Support scripts, including conditional medpac and grenade use. Therefore, a reviewer's experience of an idle or unhelpful companion does not prove that those behaviors do not exist. Scripts are limited heuristics, not companions forming independent tactical plans from their biographies. At zero vitality a character is incapacitated; a party wipe requires loading saved progress. Ordinary combat defeat is not permanent deletion of the character or save. [S02](#s02)

### Skills use different kinds of causality

The eight skills are Computer Use, Demolitions, Stealth, Awareness, Persuade, Repair, Security and Treat Injury. Their outputs differ. Computer Use lowers computer-spike expenditure at terminals; Repair can reduce parts needed for disabled droids. Persuade is the protagonist's conversation skill rather than something freely borrowed from any companion. Awareness helps identify threats, Demolitions handles mines, Security opens eligible locks, and Treat Injury improves medical effectiveness. Do not force these into one generic success-percentage model. [S02](#s02) [S16](#s16) [S19](#s19)

The captured in-game Computer Use description specifically explains costs: four additional skill points lower spike consumption by one, subject to a minimum cost. A specialist may thus leave the party with more future options even when an unskilled character could perform the same transaction by spending more. This differs from an inaccessible dialogue branch or an attack missing its target. [S16](#s16)

Stealth requires appropriate equipment and investment and operates through individual control; entering combat breaks it. It is useful for reconnaissance and setup but does not make KOTOR a freeform stealth-action game with universal climbing, object physics or simulation of every witness. The control and feedback model remains a bounded RPG interface. [S02](#s02)

### Force powers and alignment

Powers cover healing, speed and protection, disabling or moving enemies, direct damage, anti-droid effects and influence over eligible conversations. Learned powers have resource costs, and armor restrictions make equipment choice more than comparing defense numbers. Light/dark orientation changes the cost of associated powers; it does not simply prohibit every ability on the other side. A neutral companion's useful power selection need not mirror the protagonist's moral identity. [S18](#s18)

The light/dark meter summarizes particular authored decisions. It is neither a complete simulation of ethics nor a universal relationship score. An economically profitable or legally successful quest resolution is not necessarily rewarded as a light-side act. The Sunry case below is a concrete counterexample to confusing reward, truth and moral acknowledgment. [S23](#s23)

## Equipment, objects and economy

The equipment vocabulary includes pistols, rifles, heavy weapons, vibroblades and lightsabers; armor and robes; belts, gloves, headgear and shields; implants; separate droid equipment; mines, grenades, medical supplies, stimulants, technical tools and Pazaak cards. Ordinary inventory is menu-managed, not a spatial survival backpack. Loot, shops, quest rewards and companions provide useful objects; retained equipment supports persistent builds, while consumables exchange stock for immediate effects. [S17](#s17)

Named starting objects connect gear to people: **Carth's Blaster**, **Mission's Vibroblade**, **Zaalbar's Bowcaster** and **Ordo's Repeating Blaster** make the roster more tangible than interchangeable stat bundles. Their names do not guarantee that keeping each item equipped forever is optimal. **T3-M4** brings technical equipment and skills; **HK-47** uses a different droid combat identity. Droid armor and attachments should not be described as ordinary human clothing slots. [S20](#s20) [S21](#s21)

Workbench upgrades modify eligible weapons and armor. Lightsaber color and power-crystal slots separate appearance from combat effects, while particular crystals encourage combinations. **Nextor** and **Opila** support a critical-oriented pairing; **Luxum** and **Firkrann** address droids. The consulted contribution contains inconsistent probability calculations, so no exact critical-chance claim is carried over. Yavin's **Heart of the Guardian** and **Mantle of the Force** alter crystal combinations and belong to the appropriate content version. This is not KOTOR II's broader components/chemicals breakdown economy. [S22](#s22) [S29](#s29)

Credits buy equipment, supplies, cards, services and access to particular solutions. They can also motivate threatening or deceptive dialogue, producing tension between better resources and the person the player wants to portray. Quest rewards and vendor purchases do not constitute a simulated macroeconomy with fluctuating supply, rival merchants or player factories. A repair skill can conserve parts; it does not imply a buildable workshop business. [S09](#s09) [S19](#s19)

Aboard the Ebon Hawk, some companions produce small quantities of supplies when inventory remains below their relevant threshold: T3 provides spikes, Zaalbar grenades, Jolee medical supplies and Canderous stimulants. Those bounded interactions give people practical value between missions without creating an autonomous production chain. The ship also contains the upgrade workbench and a galaxy map. It is a home-like hub, not player-built housing or a freely piloted space simulator. [S30](#s30)

**Pazaak**, swoop racing and turret defense diversify the action vocabulary. Pazaak combines a race toward twenty with player-supplied modifier cards and credit stakes; using a corrective card now spends a resource that may be useful later in the match. Swoop racing and turret sequences instead make movement/aiming controls important. Their port-specific reception shows why translating the main RPG interface successfully does not guarantee that its minigames translate equally well. [S10](#s10) [S26](#s26) [S05](#s05)

## Companions, relationships and the limits of autonomy

The ordinary traveling group is the protagonist plus two companions. The wider crew offers combat roles, technical skills, personal stories and context-specific interjections. Selecting someone for their remarks can be a rational player preference even when another member would improve the next fight's statistics. [S02](#s02) [S04](#s04)

| Companion | Individuality and practical role |

| **Carth Onasi** | A Republic soldier whose prior betrayal complicates trust. His blaster-oriented role and recurring conversations connect the opening escape with personal stakes. [S20](#s20) |
| **Bastila Shan** | A talented, proud young Jedi associated with Battle Meditation and the war's larger stakes. That story ability is not simply a freely selectable original-game combat command. [S20](#s20) |

Relationships develop through authored conversations, personal quests and limited romantic storylines, not a general-purpose dating or household simulation. There is no KOTOR II-style influence system that continuously pulls each companion's alignment toward or away from the player's. A choice can still produce a refusal, departure or violent rupture at an authored turning point. These are substantial consequences without being an open-ended relationship simulator. [S06](#s06) [S28](#s28)

The setting includes Republic and Sith institutions, the Jedi, local authorities, criminal organizations and commercial interests. Participation is governed by quests and story states, not player-run faction management. Town inhabitants and patrols provide encounters and dialogue; they are not documented as independently running full daily work, sleep, commerce and political agendas. Neither native multiplayer nor player settlement construction belongs to the shipped product's promise. [S24](#s24) [S25](#s25)

## World, quests and narrative consequences

Taris layers wealth, exclusion and danger vertically; Dantooine introduces Jedi training alongside local disputes; Tatooine combines frontier commerce with conflict over territory; Kashyyyk connects a companion's past with exploitation; Manaan uses a neutral city and legal procedures to complicate the war; Korriban invites passage through Sith institutions and tombs. Each world supplies a different kind of authored problem rather than only recoloring enemy arenas. The walkthrough's separate local quests and progression routes establish this variety; the interpretation is that institutional contrast makes the galaxy feel larger than the traversable map. [S25](#s25)

Some quests test observation rather than a skill number. Bolook's murdered-settlers investigation asks the player to compare witness statements and a droid's evidence. Elsewhere, conversation and battle are consecutive parts of one decision: Juhani's defeat opens a chance to understand and recruit her rather than requiring an immediate kill. A combat victory can therefore create a social opportunity. [S27](#s27)

The Ebon Hawk's stowaway and gizka episodes make the home hub receive consequences from travel. An unexpected passenger is discovered through missing supplies and unfamiliar speech; unwanted creatures can be dealt with through more than one authored route. These are modest problems whose presence changes the ship's meaning from a transport menu into a shared place. They are not evidence of procedural ecology or ongoing hunger simulation. [S30](#s30)

### Spoilers: identity, allegiance and the endgame

The protagonist's concealed earlier identity and the party's knowledge of it reframe the journey. Later, Bastila's change in allegiance forces a major choice about redemption or reclaiming dark power. The Temple Summit decision is consequential enough to change the final path and remove opposing companions; the ending is not merely the automatic sum of every earlier meter change. A formerly light-oriented protagonist can make a decisive late reversal while retaining learned powers. This juxtaposes continuity of capability with discontinuity of allegiance. [S28](#s28)

The Leviathan sequence also constrains party freedom: companions can be disabled by an authored confrontation, and Bastila's intervention changes who escapes together. Having a customizable party does not mean every encounter preserves unrestricted party selection. The final Star Forge route supplies closure rather than a freely continuing campaign after the credits. [S31](#s31) [S25](#s25)

## Six constructed situations

The examples below are rule-based illustrations, **not reports of personally played sessions**. Each includes a reason the apparent combination may not solve every problem.

### 1. A technical specialist buys future flexibility

**Intent:** neutralize a security obstacle without spending the party's last spikes. **Conditions:** a terminal offers a useful action and the group can select a better Computer Use specialist. **Actions:** inspect the cost under that character before committing. **Interaction/result:** higher competence can reduce resource expenditure even where both characters could succeed. **Next decision:** spend the saved stock on another system or keep it for an unknown obstacle. **Limit:** skill does not invent terminal commands that were never authored, and costs have a floor. This is economy through capability, not simply a random success bonus. [S16](#s16)

### 2. Control effects give another person a role

**Intent:** let Mission contribute against a dangerous opponent instead of replacing her solely for durability. **Conditions:** the group has a suitable disabling power and the opponent is susceptible. **Actions:** coordinate the disable and her attacks through pause and queued commands. **Interaction/result:** opening a vulnerability makes Scoundrel specialization relevant; the support action's value includes another character's output. **Next decision:** renew control, focus a different target or retreat if the opponent resists. **Limit:** immunities and failed effects matter. A player-contributed synergy is not proof that this party dominates every encounter. [S12](#s12) [S15](#s15)

### 3. A peaceful route requires both access and restraint

**Intent:** resolve the Sand People confrontation without simply attacking the enclave. **Conditions:** the party has appropriate disguises and HK-47's translation, and can acquire the requested moisture vaporators. **Actions:** negotiate, obtain the goods and return rather than loot indiscriminately. **Interaction/result:** language, objects and behavior jointly support peace. **Next decision:** pursue newly available information and releases or leave with the agreement intact. **Limit:** opening protected wicker containers can still turn the enclave hostile. Passing the dialogue gate does not grant blanket permission to take everything. [S32](#s32)

### 4. Truth, reward and a legal outcome can diverge

**Intent:** investigate Jolee's friend Sunry rather than assume an ally must be innocent. **Conditions:** the trial is active and the player has questioned witnesses and found the Republic's recording. **Actions:** decide what evidence to present and whether to continue defending him after learning more. **Interaction/result:** a favorable verdict and associated rewards do not necessarily track the truth of the accusation. **Next decision:** accept a compromised result or prioritize disclosure despite the cost. **Limit:** alignment changes attach to particular authored exchanges; they should not be inferred automatically from “won” or “lost” trial labels. [S23](#s23)

### 5. A component's value depends on its partner and opponent

**Intent:** prepare a lightsaber for the next destination. **Conditions:** the player owns several power crystals and can use a workbench. **Actions:** compare a critical-oriented pairing such as Nextor/Opila with a droid-oriented pairing such as Luxum/Firkrann. **Interaction/result:** the same slot can support a build plan or counter a target family. **Next decision:** carry the general setup or specialize before a known encounter. **Limit:** greater critical potential is not guaranteed damage, and guide percentages that conflict internally should not be mistaken for verified formulas. [S22](#s22) [S29](#s29)

### 6. A corrective card creates a timing decision

**Intent:** win a Pazaak set without exhausting the entire hand's flexibility. **Conditions:** the current total is awkward and a minus or flexible modifier card is available. **Actions:** weigh standing, drawing and correcting an overshoot. **Interaction/result:** a card can turn a bad draw into a workable total, but spending it removes protection from later situations. **Next decision:** accept the present risk or pay the opportunity cost. **Limit:** chance remains; owning a useful side deck does not establish guaranteed profit. Player advice involving reloads is an exploit preference, not the fictional economy's expected return. [S26](#s26)

## Art, audio, interface and feel

The over-the-shoulder camera, animated exchanges, recognizable silhouettes, voiced conversations and cinematic transitions place a numerical RPG inside a familiar audiovisual world. The player sees a duel rather than only a calculation. That same presentation can conceal why an attack missed or why a command has not yet executed. A visible animation is feedback about the fiction but not always sufficient feedback about the rule. The postmortem and contradictory player reactions both make this tension explicit. [S13](#s13) [S11](#s11)

Reviewers repeatedly value voice performances and companion delivery, while noticing repeated faces and reused alien speech. Place-specific architecture and sound help make distant worlds distinct, but long travel movies can become friction after their first emotional effect has worn off. These are reception observations, not claims that all players experience repetition identically. [S03](#s03) [S04](#s04) [S06](#s06)

Interface needs change by platform. The iPad review values large dialogue targets and a conveniently placed pause button because they protect choices against inaccurate touches, yet finds action buttons shifting under queued commands. TouchArcade separately criticizes default sensitivity. Switch criticism targets an oversized battle overlay. A large control can improve accuracy and still obscure valuable state; successful porting requires reconsidering information placement, not only mapping buttons. No accessibility, device-performance or screen-reader audit was performed here. [S05](#s05) [S06](#s06) [S08](#s08)

## Production, iteration, discovery and commercial context

The original **December 2003 development postmortem**, republished by Game Developer in 2017, credits BioWare's experience while describing difficulties with interface iteration and player expectations. Early action-like presentation encouraged repeated button presses that interrupted intended actions. The team revised the interface repeatedly, and the retrospective argues for earlier playable prototypes and a less concentrated tutorial. It also describes encounter-level balancing and the importance of recording useful test data rather than relying on impressions alone. Those are primary retrospective lessons, not proprietary code independently inspected for this dossier. [S13](#s13)

Casey Hudson's 2004 GDC talk gives the production arc more scale. He describes a project that peaked at roughly **75 BioWare team members**, spent a little over **16 months in full production**, used early Aurora/Omen prototypes to test party/camera questions, and maintained roughly three years of public-facing promotion after the 2000 announcement. He also recounts a risky public E3 2002 build that exposed the cost of demoing before systems were sufficiently final. These numbers and lessons are Hudson's retrospective account, not a reconstructed budget or source-control history. [S35](#s35)

In a **July 5, 2003 interview**, BioWare's Teresa Cotesta describes PC and Xbox work proceeding in parallel, with different input/UI needs. She emphasizes customization, alternative resolutions and a sortable journal that helps people return after interruption. She also identifies the three minigames and discusses planned Xbox Live content. These are dated pre-release intentions; broad promises about choices or power counts are not substituted for a shipped-game inventory. [S10](#s10)

The ancient setting gave BioWare space to create a cast while retaining Star Wars recognition. Distribution combined console and PC releases, then later mobile and handheld ports. These are documented access channels; their individual acquisition contribution is unknown. The GameBanshee reviewer's changed impression between an early showing and the finished game is one attributed marketing encounter, not a measurement of how the wider audience was converted. [S24](#s24) [S09](#s09)

Post-release port support also matters when comparing criticism across editions. Aspyr's January 2022 Switch patch explicitly **reduced the combat message box**, restored certain missing NPC voice lines, corrected tutorial button icons and fixed several display/crash issues. That directly addresses some defects visible in the 2021 Switch review without proving that every quest glitch or interface complaint was resolved. [S34](#s34)

The commercial structure is premium software purchase, not an intrinsic recurring subscription or randomized-item service. Contemporary reporting supplies a harder launch measure than the postmortem's superlative: Microsoft executive Robbie Bach said the Xbox version sold **250,000 copies in four days**. Hudson's 2004 GDC talk later described **more than 1.5 million copies sold worldwide** and said the title was then tracking toward roughly two million lifetime sales. These are historical snapshots, not a present-day lifetime total; the latter is a developer presentation rather than audited financial disclosure. This pass did not establish title-level budget, net revenue, profitability or retention. Later paid ports and an enduring storefront demonstrate continued distribution, not a financial return that can be calculated from review enthusiasm. [S36](#s36) [S35](#s35) [S01](#s01)

Companion quotations, personal build choices, surprising quest outcomes and the central plot revelation are plausible sharing units. The inspected reviews and player accounts establish that people discuss them; they do not identify a causal percentage of sales from word of mouth, franchise recognition, critics or particular creators. Preserving that distinction makes the lesson more useful than labeling every lasting game “viral.”

## Seven substantive written reviews

All seven main bodies were read. Four RPGFan pieces are distinct authored reviews, not four independent editorial organizations. Ports remain separate from launch criticism.

**John McCarroll, RPGFan, July 25, 2003, Xbox.** Praises cinematic places, companion individuality, performances and console adaptation. Reused faces, unskippable travel and the required Force-user transition are drawbacks. His description of five moral-response categories is not a claim that every conversation has exactly five options. [S03](#s03)

**Brian Cavner, RPGFan, November 26, 2003, Windows.** Values companions for their remarks and personalities as well as numerical strength. He enjoys cinematic combat and performances but dislikes minigames, the restrictive camera and searching inventory. His all-Jedi party preference is not a universal optimization result. [S04](#s04)

**Dave Yeager, RPGFan, June 24, 2013, iOS.** Welcomes large dialogue targets and accessible pause controls, while reporting moving action buttons, dark environments and difficult racing/turret touch controls. These are dated iPad-port observations, not a claim that all later mobile builds share the same defects. [S05](#s05)

**Audra Bowling, RPGFan, December 11, 2021, Switch.** A first-time player, she finds the cast and plot engaging despite old models and repetitive alien speech. Large combat overlays, confusing numbers, subtitles and occasional quest glitches are weaknesses. Disliking pauses does not prove that pause settings are unavailable. [S06](#s06)

**Michael Eckenfels, Armchair Dragoons, historical Windows review republished June 26, 2025.** Values emotional investment and cinema-like presentation but reports an immersion-breaking tutorial, a collision trap, delayed companion responses and puzzle interruptions. Patch 1.02 references and a still-rumored sequel identify historical criticism, not a new 2025 version test. The original publication date remains unresolved. [S07](#s07)

**Eli Hodapp, TouchArcade, June 3, 2013, iPad.** Praises the transfer of a substantial RPG and workable device performance, but finds default sensitivity high and virtual movement imperfect. He treats it as preservation of the original experience, not newly authored campaign content. His device observations are not this dossier's benchmarks. [S08](#s08)

**Applebrown, GameBanshee, historical Windows review.** Praises world variety, journal guidance, customization and voice acting; wants subtler dark-side responses, quicker weapon switching and less repetitive action selection. Occasional stutter and long transitions remain complaints. The page's September 8, 2003 metadata conflicts with its released-PC context, so the exact publication date is not silently repaired. All four main-review pages were read. [S09](#s09)

**Interpretation:** atmosphere and useful decisions can reinforce one another while their interfaces remain in tension. A pause is deliberation for one player and interruption for another. A beloved companion can justify a less efficient party. These differences are not adequately explained by assigning one timeless “good combat” or “bad UI” label.

## Transferable patterns and limits

**Make a party member matter in more than one context.** A translator, investigator or supplier can remain valuable when not winning a damage comparison. The prerequisite is varied problems with legible opportunities. A specialist who exists only to unlock a mandatory door becomes a key-shaped inconvenience rather than an interesting person.

**Separate access, cost reduction and probability.** Computer Use, Persuade and combat skills demonstrate different relationships between competence and outcome. An extensible game benefits from exposing which kind of advantage is being offered. Calling every benefit a skill check hides economically and narratively important distinctions.

**Let a place remember small consequences.** The ship's visitors, supplies and creatures make travel affect home. This need not require a complete ecosystem simulation. A few visible, coherent changes can make a hub feel inhabited, provided the game does not promise autonomy it cannot support.

**Use social decisions to change available people.** Juhani's encounter links mercy with future companionship; later allegiances can break the group. Such consequences are more tangible than a meter increment alone. The cost is authored branching and the risk of removing a player's favorite character without sufficiently clear narrative motivation.

**Do not let cinematic grammar misdescribe interaction grammar.** Continuous dueling can coexist with turn-based resolution, but the user must understand when commands execute and why outcomes occur. The correct lesson is not to abandon cinema or rules; it is to make their relationship readable and to test the first session early.

**Distinguish moral framing from payoff.** Sunry's trial demonstrates that a successful transaction can remain ethically uncomfortable. The pattern depends on meaningful evidence and acknowledgment, not on arbitrary punishment for choosing an option. These are research interpretations, not approved systems or permission to copy protected characters, dialogue, settings or visual assets.
