# G181 — Battle Chasers: Nightwar

Research accessed October 20, 2026. This dossier covers Airship Syndicate's original game, separates launch criticism from later patches and ports, and evaluates design lessons against [Open Legend's gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md). It follows [R01–R14](../research-requirements.md), the [economics/enjoyment amendment](../research-economics-engagement-and-creation.md), and the conditional R15 screen. Recommendations are research hypotheses, not accepted implementation decisions.

**Finding:** this is a strong reference for expressive party roles, temporary resources that encourage experimentation, and the tension between an excellent combat system and an exhausting campaign rhythm. It is a weaker reference for people whose lives evolve independently: its cast and relationships are authored. The available evidence does **not** establish a commercial failure. A developer describes meaningful platform demand, but project profit remains undisclosed.

## R01 — Identity, scope and player promise

Battle Chasers: Nightwar is a single-player party RPG developed by Airship Syndicate and published by THQ Nordic. Its October 3, 2017 release combines an overworld map, real-time dungeon exploration, and separate turn-based battles. The player selects three members of a six-person cast, develops their equipment and abilities, and follows an authored adventure. The Steam product supports Windows, macOS and Linux; it is neither a character-creation sandbox nor a multiplayer campaign. [S01]

| Edition or period | Relevant boundary |
| --- | --- |
| 2017 PC, PS4 and Xbox One launch | The earliest reviews describe this balance, progression and technical state. Several complaints concern slow leveling and repeated dungeons. [S09, S10, S12] |
| 2018 rebalance | The official console update removed equipment level requirements, adjusted experience and loot, and altered early difficulty, revival fees and New Game Plus. A patch announcement documents changes, not proof that every later player experiences no bugs. [S07] |
| Switch, May 15, 2018 | A later release with its own performance and control observations. Do not transfer the launch-PC experience wholesale to this version. [S13, S16] |
| iOS and Android, August 1, 2019 | HandyGames released the campaign as a premium mobile purchase, announced at US$9.99 without in-app purchases or advertisements. This is distinct from Steam sales and from subsequent subscription distribution. [S08] |

The initial attraction is immediate visual identity and recognizable party roles. Established play shifts toward learning combinations, collecting resources, adjusting builds and deciding whether a more difficult dungeon attempt justifies its rewards. Completing the finite story is a valid endpoint; indefinite retention is not the product's necessary purpose.

## R02 — What the player can actually do

### The three connected layers

On the overworld, the player follows routes and checks encounters. Inside dungeons, characters move directly, use exploration skills and encounter visible enemies. Luring an enemy away, exploiting a trap, or gathering several changes the fight before its first turn. Optional exploration areas are authored rather than generated like the main dungeons. [S05]

Combat turns offer three distinct resource decisions:

- **Actions** execute immediately and generate Overcharge. Their utility can include effects beyond damage.
- **Abilities** spend energy and have a casting delay; the initiative display helps the player judge whether an enemy will act first. Overcharge supplies temporary energy before ordinary mana is spent.
- **Battle Bursts** spend a party-wide meter, introducing a choice about which hero receives the shared opportunity. Unlike ordinary Overcharge, that meter persists between encounters. [S12, S25]

Overcharge addresses a specific design problem. Joe Madureira says the team wanted players to use interesting abilities instead of saving all their mana for a boss. A resource that normally expires at the end of a fight creates permission to spend it. This does not remove conservation entirely: health, ordinary mana, consumables and the shared Burst resource still connect successive encounters. [S04]

### Mechanics inventory and boundaries

| System | Actual player decisions and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and attributes | Six fixed protagonists with distinctive ability sets. Equipment and perks modify combat performance; the player does not choose ancestry, personality or a procedural life history. [S01, S24] |
| Party roles | Gully emphasizes protection, Calibretto healing and support, Garrison damage, Red Monika evasion and afflictions, Knolan magic, and Alumon blood-themed abilities. These are tendencies rather than a proof that only one composition works. [S24] |
| Timing and counterplay | Cast delays, slowing effects and damage over time interact with the action order. A delayed powerful attack can be less useful than an immediate action that prevents the next hostile turn. [S12] |
| Progression | Levels unlock options; perk allocation refines a hero. Two perk trees per hero permit specialization, and reassignment supports experimentation. [S11, S13] |
| Exploration and rewards | Traps, puzzles, chests, materials, lore, hunts, an arena, fishing and optional locations extend the campaign beyond compulsory battles. [S08, S11] |
| Stealth | Specific dungeon abilities support avoidance or advantageous initiation. This is not a general social-stealth or crime simulation. [S05] |
| Economy and production | Loot, buying, selling, crafting, enchanting, merchant investment and consumable preparation matter. There is no player market, factory automation, farming settlement or labor simulation. [S06, S23] |
| People and construction | Party conversations and authored quest-givers; no general household, romance-management, civic building, autonomous work schedule or faction-governance system was evidenced. [S01, S11] |

The recurring sequence is: identify an obstacle → assemble a party and supplies → make exploration and combat decisions → receive information, experience and materials → improve a build → attempt a new obstacle. Its potential weakness is equally specific: if the next improvement requires repeating solved encounters, a deep system can still produce shallow repeated decisions. This is the dossier's interpretation of the contrasting accounts in R12.

## R03 — Items, abilities and composition

Useful possessions include hero-specific weapons, protective equipment, accessories, healing and mana supplies, crafting materials, recipes and perk-granting books. Crafting stations distinguish equipment, alchemy and enchanting. Ingredient quantities are a risk choice: a shortage permits a failure-prone attempt, while a surplus can improve the resulting item. Named recipe families include Deep Iron equipment, Vented Cannon Mark II, Warforged Gauntlets, Firebrand enchantment and revival flasks. The retrieved community recipe lists establish examples, not a verified current exhaustive balance table. [S23]

Merchant investment creates a fallback when drops do not supply a needed upgrade. Steve Madureira describes the blacksmith as a way to fill equipment gaps, with upgrades improving stock and eventually enabling town crafting. Iconic possessions also expose a fiction/design compromise: the game permits replacement weapons for characters whose comic identities are tied to particular artifacts, while protecting certain original items from sale. [S06]

Composition is more than three separate damage totals. Healing, shielding, status application and turn order can make another character's action more valuable. **Attributed play account:** PC Gamer's Eric Watson describes using Calibretto to ignite an enemy, enabling Alumon's Blood Boil critical interaction and a perk that heals an ally on a critical hit. He also reports trying a Red Monika evasion tank whose protection could fail when several attacks connected. That is evidence of experimentation and its risk, not a universal recommended build. [S09]

The community mechanics guide records relationships among attack power, stamina, physical and magical defense, haste, critical chance, gear and perks. Its reverse-engineered formulas target a named older build; this dossier uses it to establish the interacting attribute families, not to certify exact current arithmetic. The guide's discussion of increasingly strong Overcharge combinations also illustrates why additional perk points can change effective play, rather than merely raise a visible level. [S24]

## R04 — Progression, economy, loss and return

### What changes over a campaign

The early campaign teaches a small familiar party; later recruits expand the possibility space. The important cost is opportunity: inactive heroes do not receive ordinary battle experience in the reviewed game. A player who already trusts a successful trio can face extra work before testing a replacement. Late recruits and their initial levels do not eliminate subsequent divergence. [S13, S22]

Gold supports purchases, supplies and merchant improvements. Materials turn exploration and enemy drops into crafted capabilities. Fishing provides another exchange route: fish parts can become Shadow Coins for the Collector's specialized stock. There is no demonstrated simulated market that changes through independent supply, labor or political action. The economy primarily regulates access to party capability. [S05, S06]

At the late-game level cap of 30, perk books and equipment still provide goals. Optional hunts and ultimate-weapon pursuits give the player reasons to inspect places and systems they could otherwise skip. The risk is overdevelopment: a completionist party can outgrow the final story challenge. The reward for exploring and the desired difficulty of the finale therefore need not remain aligned. [S10, S11]

### Loss and reset boundaries

| Boundary | Retained, lost and renewed decisions |
| --- | --- |
| End of an ordinary encounter | Temporary Overcharge normally disappears; shared Burst charge persists. This makes immediate spending and expedition-level saving different judgments. [S12, S25] |
| Party defeat | The reviewed campaign returns the party to town with a money penalty rather than deleting a protagonist or applying a permanent injury history. Dungeon progress can remain available; difficulty and version matter. [S10] |
| Re-entering or increasing dungeon difficulty | Recombination changes room/event arrangements and rewards. Repeating authored objectives or familiar enemies can still feel like replaying work already understood. [S12] |
| New Game Plus | Contemporary player reports describe returning to level one and losing most possessions and currencies while keeping abilities, perk progress and recorded knowledge. The full roster becomes available early. Higher difficulties renew build challenges; this is not continuation of an independently evolving world. Treat detailed exceptions as version-sensitive. [S20] |
| Completion | The player can finish satisfied, pursue optional mastery, or choose a new run. No sourced cohort retention or abandonment percentage was available. |

The 2018 patch matters to interpretation. It reduced progression friction while making some early encounters and New Game Plus harder. Those changes demonstrate that **less grind and greater challenge can coexist**. Removing a numerical equipment gate need not mean removing interesting tactical pressure. [S07]

## R05 — Worked interactions

The following are **constructed illustrations based on documented rules**, not claims of personally playing these encounters. They show decisions and limits rather than exact optimal builds.

### 1. Spending a temporary opportunity

**Intention:** finish an encounter without consuming expedition supplies. **Conditions:** a hero has generated Overcharge, but an enemy is preparing a dangerous move. **Action:** compare an immediate action with an ability's position on the initiative bar. **Interaction:** saving ordinary mana is useful only if the delay does not create a larger health cost. **Result:** spending the temporary resource can protect longer-lived resources. **Next decision:** reserve the shared Burst for a later emergency or use it to end this fight. **Limit:** repeatedly performing the same opening to generate energy can become routine. [S25]

### 2. Changing the encounter before combat

**Intention:** cross a room safely. **Conditions:** several visible enemies patrol near a hazard. **Action:** lead one away or draw an enemy through the trap before initiating combat. **Interaction:** movement changes both enemy count and starting condition. **Result:** preparation replaces some healing cost with observation and positioning. **Next decision:** accept a larger chained encounter for additional experience. **Limit:** a player must be able to read the environment; obscured hazards undermine this choice. [S05, S10]

### 3. Buying flexibility rather than accepting a weak slot

**Intention:** prepare a hero whose loot has lagged behind the others. **Conditions:** money can improve a merchant, while limited materials can support a craft. **Action:** compare the dependable shop route with ingredient overloading or a risky incomplete recipe. **Interaction:** gold and materials are alternative ways to repair a capability gap. **Result:** bad luck need not force another identical dungeon run. **Next decision:** preserve scarce ingredients for another party member. **Limit:** a long sequence of individually crafted consumables can make preparation itself tiring. [S06, S14, S23]

### 4. Testing an alternative party

**Intention:** explore a new combination rather than retain a familiar trio. **Conditions:** an unused hero has fallen behind. **Action:** reassign perks and take that hero through manageable content. **Interaction:** a reversible build choice collides with less reversible experience allocation. **Result:** the player may discover a satisfying role, or conclude that catch-up costs exceed its value. **Next decision:** invest further or return to the old formation. **Limit:** player reluctance here does not demonstrate that the new hero is intrinsically weak. [S13, S22]

### 5. Beginning again with knowledge instead of possessions

**Intention:** enjoy an early-game challenge with a more expressive build. **Conditions:** New Game Plus retains developed options but removes most accumulated equipment and levels. **Action:** use retained perks to approach familiar content differently. **Interaction:** lost possessions restore acquisition goals while retained knowledge shortens the path to interesting choices. **Result:** replay can become a new tactical problem. **Next decision:** test another party or higher difficulty. **Limit:** the plot and encounter vocabulary may still be too familiar for a player seeking new stories. [S20]

## R06 — People, relationships and agency

The small fixed cast makes silhouette, voice, animations and ability role reinforce a remembered person. Gully's protection, Garrison's offensive style and Calibretto's caring role let a player enact relationships through choices even when the dialogue is authored. The game can therefore create affection without a sophisticated social simulation. That is a useful distinction for Open Legend: emotional recognition does not require every possible relationship variable.

Harm's Way's inn supplies occasional party interactions, and recurring merchants and quest-givers give the expedition a recognizable destination. These scenes do not establish people independently selecting careers, remembering arbitrary player conduct or changing relationships because a particular fight went badly. Procedural dungeon assembly likewise does not make the inhabitants autonomous. [S05]

There is no native shared campaign, player economy or negotiated cooperation between human party members in the studied product. Its strongest continuity comes from returning to the same authored people and mastering their complementary abilities. For the present research question, that is useful opposing evidence: players may value sustained attachment and competence without demanding permanent injury or spontaneous personality change.

## R07 — Art, sound, interface and feel

Joe Madureira describes a deliberately economical art pipeline: hand-painted treatment and relatively simple lighting supported a small art team. The visual result derives identity from form, color and strong characters rather than maximum surface complexity. The production account is specific to this project; it does not prove the same pipeline will fit a different camera or asset volume. [S04]

RPGFan praises the contrast among environments and the music by Jesper Kyd and Clark Powell, while objecting to menu clutter, slow presentation and some sexualized character design. Nintendo Life likewise finds the port visually appealing but observes frame-rate trouble and animations that become slow through repetition. Strong initial presentation and comfortable repeated use are separate requirements. [S11, S13]

The initiative display communicates a consequential rule: a powerful ability may resolve after the threat it was meant to stop. Conversely, the fixed dungeon camera can conceal a hazard, and repeated attack flourishes can delay a decision the player already understands. Official updates addressing text contrast, text size and particularly slow enemy animations show that readability and cadence were concrete maintenance concerns. [S07, S10, S25]

**Interpretation:** retain enough animation and sound to identify the action and its consequence; evaluate the fiftieth occurrence as well as the first. A useful Open Legend test is whether a player can recognize what happened without waiting through an unchanged performance before making the next decision.

## R08 — Lore and its relationship to play

**Story spoilers follow.** Nightwar is a new adventure in the comic's setting, designed to make sense without prior reading. The comic's mix of fantasy, machines and exaggerated heroes provides a foundation; it is not evidence that the game implements a world-level technological or magical simulation. [S03]

The central relationships and island history operate at three scales:

| Scale | Narrative function |
| --- | --- |
| Personal inheritance | Gully carries powerful gauntlets associated with her missing father. Garrison and Calibretto's protective relationships give the group a reason to remain together. [S05, S21] |
| Expedition and reunion | An interrupted airship journey separates the companions on Crescent Isle. Finding them leads through bandit activity and Harm's Way into the island's deeper conflict. [S21] |
| Older violence | Alumon, the vampire Verus, the sorceress Destra and the demon C'dral connect the present adventure to older supernatural conflicts. Artifacts and access to the dead matter to the route through the campaign. The ending leaves part of Verus's story unresolved. [S21] |

The expedition also has a material motive. In the developer's account, mana powers both magic and technology but is disappearing; Knolan's discovery of an island reserve attracts the party's attention. Resource scarcity therefore helps explain the journey before the local supernatural conflict takes precedence. [S25]

The character contrasts matter more than a list of proper nouns: a war machine can perform care; inherited power can burden a child; a veteran's dangerous strength can coexist with protective duty. These are authored tensions expressed partly through roles and scenes. They are not personalities generated from combat statistics.

Lore is delivered through optional records, locations and conversations, not solely the central plot. Steve Madureira explains that found dungeon lore is removed from the remaining spawn pool, limiting duplicate discovery when room selection is procedural. That is a concrete content-delivery technique: randomized placement need not make a finite historical account arbitrarily inaccessible. [S06]

The evidence does not support reconstructing a complete religion, creation cosmology, calendar or independently functioning civic institutions from this game. Its island history serves discoveries and threats; everyday life is thinner. Reviewers also disagree over the narrative payoff: some enjoy the accumulated journals and party scenes, while others find the main plot too slight for the campaign's length. The relevant lesson is to connect world information to a present curiosity or action, without assuming more text automatically improves momentum. [S09–S11, S15]

## R09 — Production, iteration and content authoring

Airship grew from experienced developers after Vigil Games closed. Madureira's retrospective describes an extended prototype period, unsuccessful publisher pitches, and crowdfunding as a decisive opportunity to establish demand. The eventual THQ Nordic partnership expanded what the team could make. An existing comic provided recognizable characters and a public identity, but also imposed expectations about those characters. [S03]

The team experimented with the form before settling on real-time exploration and turn-based combat; an earlier card-oriented combat idea did not survive. Selecting a party RPG was partly a scope decision for a small studio. This supports prototyping the core interaction before treating its first formulation as a permanent requirement. [S04]

The launch-era interview reports a twelve-person studio using Unity and a Switch delay associated with an engine issue. The developer also says crowdfunding alone was insufficient for the game they wanted to deliver. Consequently the Kickstarter amount is especially unsuitable as a supposed total budget. [S06] An earlier interview describes additional remote contractors, personal investment before crowdfunding and informal feedback within the small team; headcount alone consequently cannot reconstruct its production costs. [S26]

Ordinary players select builds and navigate existing content; they do not receive a native world or campaign editor in the documented product. The developer supplies characters, room pieces, events, animation, writing, balancing and progression. Procedural assembly rearranges authored material rather than supplying those creative decisions. Community reverse engineering and external modification are a separate level of authorship, discussed under R15. No proprietary source workflow, team version-control practice or AI-assisted production process was verified.

[S03, S26] | **Project profit and developer/publisher return are not estimable from available evidence.** |

The estimate provider describes a review-to-ownership approach with varying ratios and adjustments for price, discounts, regional mix, platform share, refunds and tax. It does not expose sufficiently detailed game-specific inputs to reproduce a trustworthy low/base/high consumer-spending range. Its displayed confidence score is not an independently validated probability that the estimate is correct. Treat the reported dollar values as one rough partial-market estimate, not a measurement. A second site using similar review-count assumptions would not provide independent corroboration. [S18]

There is no defensible all-platform revenue range here. Steam ownership is not equivalent to directly purchased Steam copies; keys, bundles and free ownership complicate conversion. Console and mobile receipts, promotional arrangements and any subscription licensing are missing. The evidence search covered Kickstarter, developer interviews, publisher reporting, storefronts and commercial-estimate methodology. Full-price multiplication and adding crowdfunding to modeled sales would risk both price error and double counting.

### Why it attracted an audience, and where value was lost

**Documented mechanisms:** the comic creator's existing audience and experienced team's prototype made the Kickstarter pitch intelligible; successful crowdfunding then helped attract a publishing partner. The game had a distinct visual identity and a clear classic-RPG promise. This is a stronger explanation of initial discoverability than an unsupported claim that its entire market arrived through one trailer. [S02, S03]

**Developer attribution:** Stefanelli associates the successful later Switch release with an improved version, specifically reduced grind, bugs and quality-of-life problems. He describes grinding as the leading gameplay complaint. This is credible evidence about the team's interpretation; it is not a controlled comparison isolating the effect of the patch from portability, price, audience composition or release timing. [S16]

**Research judgment:** Nightwar demonstrably found a supportive audience and additional distribution opportunities. Its profit cannot be classified from the public record gathered here. Nor does the absence of a direct sequel establish failure. Commercial vulnerability plausibly came from a combination of content-production expense, a finite niche audience, and avoidable campaign friction, but the relative financial effect of each is unknown. No wishlist funnel, advertising spend, conversion cohort or title-level recoupment statement was found.

## R12 — What reviewers and players liked and disliked

### Five substantive independent written reviews

These accounts were read beyond their scores. They are selected testimony, not a survey or a measurement of feature preference.

| Review and version | Positive account | Friction, qualification and relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eric Watson, PC Gamer, October 21, 2017; PC; 81/100** [S09] | Enjoys combinations, clear combat information, art and sound. His reported build experiments make the praise concrete. | The early stretch is slow, the plot thin, and repeated enemies and grinding weaken momentum. A favorable verdict does not mean an uninterrupted positive experience. |
| **Pascal Tekaia, RPGamer, October 14, 2017; subsequently updated** [S10] | Values tactical combat, optional discoveries and unusually strong visual and audio presentation. | Inactive-party leveling and difficulty invite reruns; thorough completion can make the finale trivial. His account notes launch faults and later fixes rather than treating one state as timeless. |
| **Greg Delmage, RPGFan, November 28, 2018; PS4; 95/100** [S11] | Enthusiastic about meaningful side activities, character interactions and the accumulated setting. | Menus and lengthy animation interrupt the pleasure; some character presentation is objectionable to him. His appreciation of narrative is stronger than Watson's. |
| **Thomas Wilde, WorthPlaying, October 20, 2017; PC; 8.9/10** [S12] | Finds status effects, cast timing and dangerous ordinary battles engaging. | Explicitly had not finished after a difficulty spike. Perceives Gully and Calibretto as nearly compulsory and crafting as unnecessary overhead. That unfinished context limits claims about the whole campaign. |
| **Gavin Lane, Nintendo Life, May 14, 2018; Switch; 8/10** [S13] | Likes the approachable campaign, free perk adjustment, avoidance of weak fights and audiovisual character. | Slow animation, performance trouble and attachment to the initial trio reduce experimentation. The port's later date matters when comparing grind complaints. |

Two apparent conflicts are productive. Wilde's sense of a compulsory defensive pair coexists with Watson's experiments with an evasion tank; different build knowledge, equipment, encounter stage and risk tolerance can explain both. Delmage's enjoyment of narrative coexists with Watson's desire for stronger plot momentum: optional world detail and central dramatic progression are distinct kinds of satisfaction. Neither disagreement should be erased into a consensus score.

### Helpful Steam samples and counterevidence

The all-time helpful English review page was sampled alongside its negative-only counterpart, accessed October 20, 2026. Helpfulness rankings favor older and more visible accounts; displayed total hours can include play after the original review. These examples establish experiences, not prevalence.

- **Excuritas, Recommended, December 21, 2017:** initially stopped because of pacing, then returned using an external speed adjustment. Enjoyed builds and combat but objected to repetitive animation, thin story and the overhead of individual crafting. This is not a recommendation to install that workaround. [S14]
- **Alzorath, Recommended, October 3, 2017:** praised the cast, art, controls and side content while finding complexity and explanation less impressive than the strongest professional reviews suggest. [S14]
- **Dolyngwen, Recommended, December 19, 2017:** valued a manageable, attractive RPG experience compatible with limited leisure time. Continuity and accessibility can be the attraction even when another player demands more systemic complexity. [S14]
- **knarflook, Not Recommended, September 18, 2019:** admired the art and initial customization but stopped late, citing stagnant plotting, repetition and uneven difficulty. The account distinguishes enjoying the purchase initially from wanting to finish it. [S15]

Steam's accessed storefront reported **85% positive among 2,463 English reviews** on October 20, 2026. That language-specific aggregate is not the all-language number reported by historical analytics pages, and neither percentage establishes retention, profit or causality. [S01]

### Enjoyment, continued play and leaving

The supported appeal is competence expressed through combinations, affection for a distinctive cast, discovery and visible improvement. The supported friction is time between meaningful decisions: slow animation, catching up unused heroes, repeated objectives, and late encounters that do not repay accumulated preparation. These mechanisms explain why a person can praise combat yet abandon the campaign. They also explain why a later, better-paced version may reach a different audience. Exact completion rates and the number of players affected remain unknown.

## R13 — Open Legend hypotheses and what not to copy

| Research hypothesis | Prerequisites and a useful test | Failure case or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give players a temporary opportunity they feel comfortable spending. | A clear expiry boundary, understandable feedback, and several valuable uses. Observe whether players try unfamiliar actions earlier. | If every encounter begins with the same resource-building sequence, the mechanic has added a ritual rather than a choice. Overcharge itself need not be copied. |
| Let a capability express a remembered person. | Distinct behaviors and recognizable consequences can connect a person's identity to what they contribute. Test whether players remember someone through a specific shared event. | A rigid combat class can constrain a person's future. Independent Open Legend people need room to change work, priorities and relationships. |
| Make recovery and experimentation affordable enough to use. | Compare the cost of preparing another person or tool with the value of its new possibilities. | Catch-up chores can make theoretical variety irrelevant. Universal automatic progression could also erase meaningful differences; test the actual tradeoff. |
| Preserve challenge while removing repeated work. | Separate threat, opportunity cost and information from the time required to regain numerical eligibility. | Faster progress alone can trivialize a well-prepared finale. Encounters need decisions that survive improved capability. |
| Make procedural discovery reveal coherent authored history. | Track which records or discoveries have already been encountered; give them contextual relevance. | Rearranged rooms do not create independently evolving inhabitants or genuinely different stories. |
| Judge presentation through repeated use. | Check readability, response and player control after many familiar actions. | A memorable animation can become a frequent interruption. Eliminating all expressive presentation can also weaken attachment. |

For Mike's specific interest in lives shaped by experience, Nightwar supplies the **continuity side** of the comparison. A small persistent group can feel valuable without irreversible degradation. Open Legend could combine that attachment with causal memories and changed opportunities, but this game does not demonstrate that extension. A hypothetical injured explorer learning to prepare expeditions for others would need real alternative activities, willing collaborators and remembered events; merely assigning a penalty or a new line of dialogue would not establish the desired evolution.

Do not transfer the six-person limit, class identities, level grind, fixed party-control model, combat economy or comic lore into general engine requirements. The relevant questions are whether capability invites action, whether consequences leave worthwhile futures, and whether repeated play still offers a wanted next decision.

## R15 — Modding and player creation applicability screen

**Gate: uncertain popularity; limited external modification verified.** An identifiable mod exists, but the retrieved evidence does not establish a large sustained creation ecosystem. Nexus's counters were not populated in the retrieved page; blank values are not zero downloads. A full popular-modding-scene deep dive is therefore not asserted.

Riff1323's **Inactive Party Exp**, uploaded July 30, 2021, uses BepInEx and was tested against Steam build 24037. It assigns experience to inactive heroes, directly addressing a recurrent experimentation cost. The author restricts redistribution and reuse. This is an external code modification, not native campaign creation, and its old tested version does not guarantee present compatibility. [S22]

A separate community guide describes a combat-log and party-shuffling modification, illustrating diagnostic and rules-changing authorship. Neither example proves a supported editor, stable extension API, console mod support or creator marketplace. [S24] No modification was downloaded or executed. The useful research observation is that players created tools both to understand opaque mechanics and to reduce barriers to using the full cast.
