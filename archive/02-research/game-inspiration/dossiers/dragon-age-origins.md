# Dragon Age: Origins — full research dossier

**G51 · Complete research pass, September 26, 2026.** Scope: BioWare/EA's 2009 Origins, the 2010 Awakening expansion and the major downloadable campaigns. PC and original console interfaces differ; Ultimate Edition bundles content but does not turn every module into one continuous campaign. Design transfers below are research proposals, not accepted OpenLegend requirements.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md)

## 1. Identity and the promise of an origin

Origins is a single-player, party-based fantasy role-playing game. A created protagonist joins the Grey Wardens and seeks allies against a Blight, but first experiences a playable social background. The six origins are human noble, magi, city elf, Dalish elf, dwarf commoner and dwarf noble. They do more than change a starting statistic: familiar people and places can acquire different significance when encountered later. [R1](#r1)

The first-hour promise is being someone situated in a society. The longer-term promise is deciding what that person will do when local loyalties collide with a larger emergency. **Interpretation:** converging plot structure need not erase identity if the route into that structure continues to matter. The limitation is that the background is a finite authored branch, not a procedural biography.

## 2. Creation, classes and character growth

Race, class, appearance and origin are related choices rather than unrestricted mix-and-match fields. Warriors, rogues and mages have distinct development paths; a dwarf cannot simply choose the ordinary mage origin. The player controls the Warden and up to three active companions, while additional recruits wait outside the active party. [R1](#r1)

Six attributes—Strength, Dexterity, Willpower, Magic, Cunning and Constitution—connect combat and non-combat capability. Strength supports physical equipment and damage; Dexterity contributes to attack/defence; Willpower expands resources; Magic supports spell effectiveness; Cunning matters to rogue and social capabilities; Constitution increases durability. Defence, damage mitigation and health are different kinds of survivability. Heavy armour's **fatigue** raises ability costs; it is not a hunger or sleep simulation. [M1](#m1)

Experience from adventures produces repeated allocation decisions. **Skills** are not **talents/spells**: shared skills include Coercion, Stealing, Trap-Making, Survival, Herbalism, Poison-Making, Combat Training and Combat Tactics. Class-specific talent and spell chains provide attacks, passives, sustained modes and support. Prerequisites create opportunity costs; a character cannot acquire every useful ability immediately. [M2](#m2) [M3](#m3)

Specializations further shape a class. Examples include Champion/Templar/Reaver/Berserker, Bard/Assassin/Duelist/Ranger, and Arcane Warrior/Blood Mage/Shapeshifter/Spirit Healer. Unlocking a specialization and spending a character's specialization point are different events. Profile-level unlock persistence can outlive a particular save; the game therefore does not consistently enforce learning as irreversible in-world history. [M4](#m4)

## 3. The ordinary activity loop

The repeating sequence is travel, inspect, talk, choose, fight or find another route, acquire rewards, and reconfigure the party. Conversations and preparation can be as consequential as an individual attack. The codex, journal and quest dependencies preserve information across sessions; they do not amount to a generative NPC memory system.

Equipment includes melee weapons, shields, bows, staves, armour, jewellery, runes, consumables, gifts and quest objects. Loot, merchant purchases and authored rewards supply it. Inventory pressure makes sorting and selling recurrent activities; reviewers identify this as a significant nuisance rather than an unqualified strength. Matching gear to a party and build is the substantive decision, whereas repeatedly clearing low-value clutter is often not. [R4](#r4)

**Interpretation:** the important loop is the conversion of new information and possessions into a changed plan. A design that merely alternates exposition with identical fights can retain all the component systems while losing that sense of discovery.

## 4. Crafting and item composition

Herbalism creates poultices, lyrium potions, injury treatments and protective preparations. Poison-Making and Trap-Making support offensive coatings and placed hazards. Recipes require both competence and materials: a lesser health poultice combines elfroot with a flask; a lesser lyrium potion instead uses lyrium dust. Companions can carry different production skills, reducing the need for the protagonist to purchase every discipline. [M5](#m5)

Armour sets and runes add another composition layer. A set's bonuses can make several pieces valuable together; a distinctive item can remain useful for its effect rather than its tier alone. Material quality, stat requirements and fatigue constrain equipment choices. Base-game consumable crafting should not be confused with unrestricted forging of any imagined weapon, or with Awakening's expanded rune production. [M6](#m6)

**Interpretation:** recipes create useful relationships between exploration and preparation, but the game does not simulate a self-sustaining economy of NPC manufacturers and consumers. Merchant inventories and quest rewards are authored service/content systems, not evidence of a general market simulation.

## 5. Combat and delegated tactics

Combat is real-time with pause. The player may issue orders directly to individual members, hold positions, focus attacks and intervene when routine behavior fails. PC's overhead camera and the original consoles' closer views create materially different command experiences. The game is not a turn-based action-point system. [R1](#r1)

The tactics editor supplies prioritized condition/action rules. Conditions can concern the actor, an ally or an enemy; actions must make sense for the selected target and available resources. Presets and behavior settings reduce setup effort, while additional tactics capacity supports more elaborate routines. This is inspectable rule-driven delegation, not an LLM deciding what the companion's personality implies. [M7](#m7)

**Interpretation:** the player needs to distinguish three failures: an unsuitable policy, a valid policy that cannot currently execute, and an execution/pathfinding defect. A companion who repeatedly drinks the wrong potion should expose which of those happened. A compelling personality cannot compensate for opaque operational behavior.

## 6. Magic, control effects and battlefield composition

Spells include direct damage, area effects, healing, protection, curses and control. Sustained abilities reserve resources; active abilities consume them. A larger ability list therefore does not remove loadout and resource tradeoffs. Mana and stamina are not interchangeable for every consumable. [M3](#m3) [M8](#m8)

Combinations make status meaningful. The game's Shattering codex documents freezing or petrifying a target and then applying a qualifying impact; resistance and enemy rank constrain the result. It is not a universal instant-kill rule for every boss. Other combinations reward recognizing how one effect prepares another. [M9](#m9)

Friendly-fire rules depend on difficulty **and platform**. The Xbox manual, for example, distinguishes settings with immunity from harder settings with friendly damage; its Normal rule must not be copied into a PC guide without checking the PC rules. Positioning, doorways and control effects consequently matter beyond raw damage. [M8](#m8)

**Interpretation:** compositional combat is strongest when the player can understand the relevant state. Hidden combinations that only an external guide can explain are less transferable than readable interactions between visible properties.

## 7. Travel, exploration, stealth and interaction

Area maps reveal geography through exploration; the Ferelden world map connects larger destinations and a party camp. Travel can trigger encounters. The camp consolidates rest, conversation and reconfiguration rather than simulating a physically traversable household economy. [M8](#m8)

Locks, traps, theft, persuasion and quest-specific interactions provide alternatives to simply attacking every obstacle. These are bounded capabilities, not a universal physics sandbox. In Orzammar's political quest, for example, recovering compromising letters can involve access, persuasion and theft-related opportunities before a fighting tournament. The resulting support is contextual, not merely another point in a global morality meter. [M10](#m10)

**Interpretation:** alternative verbs matter when they change what a player knows, who cooperates or what must be fought. A lockpicking statistic that only opens another box is a narrower expression of the same potential.

## 8. Companions, approval, romance and disagreement

The recruitable cast includes Alistair, Morrigan, Leliana, Sten, Wynne, Zevran, Oghren and the mabari, with Shale and a late alternative recruit depending on content and choices. The party has authored banter, personal histories, quests and conflicting values. Taking someone along can change commentary and reactions, not only combat coverage. [R2](#r2) [M11](#m11)

Approval is an individual relationship measure. Conversations, witnessed choices and gifts affect it; personal quests and additional flags gate important developments. Romances with Alistair, Morrigan, Leliana or Zevran have their own eligibility and progression conditions. A high number is therefore not sufficient to explain every relationship state. Gifts can repair many disagreements, but should not be described as overriding every decisive plot condition. [M11](#m11)

The system does not infer arbitrary memories from every interaction. Its strength is authored specificity: people disagree for recognizable reasons. **Interpretation:** OpenLegend can borrow individual evaluators and persistent relational consequences without reducing trust, fear, desire, respect and obligation to one interchangeable affection value.

## 9. Main story choices connect local and national stakes

**Major story spoilers in this section and the worked examples.** The ally-seeking structure leads through the Circle, Redcliffe, the Dalish conflict and Orzammar. These are not four independent resource errands: local decisions alter who can help and what the Warden has accepted in exchange.

Redcliffe links preparation for a village defence to a family crisis. The possibility of obtaining Circle assistance creates another route through the possession problem, rather than making immediate sacrifice the only answer. This rewards knowledge and earlier relationships, while exposing an important design tension: urgency in dialogue does not always mean a continuously advancing simulation clock. [M12](#m12)

Orzammar mixes succession politics with the Deep Roads and the question of golem creation. Bhelen and Harrowmont offer different routes to support. Shale can give the golem question a personal face. These are authored political branches, not an autonomous electoral system running beyond the player's attention. [M10](#m10) [D1](#d1)

The Landsmeet combines gathered evidence, political support, personal decisions and confrontation. Recruiting Loghain changes the party and Alistair's relationship to the Warden; the identity of a ruler can depend on several earlier conditions. The final confrontation then connects the Grey Wardens' sacrifice to Morrigan's optional ritual. Neither outcome should be described as the single universal canon of every playthrough. [M13](#m13) [M14](#m14)

## 10. Failure, recovery, endings and replay

If someone falls in combat while another party member survives, recovery can leave an injury penalty. Injury kits or camp rest address that persistent cost; a health poultice is not equivalent to an injury cure. Total defeat normally leads back to a save rather than a continuing, fully simulated defeated-party storyline. [M8](#m8)

Narrative consequences have a different persistence contract: a departure, execution, sacrifice or rejected alliance may change later scenes. Reloading gives the player an external escape hatch, but characters do not treat the abandoned save as an experienced timeline. Endings and later imports preserve selected decisions, not every action or piece of world state.

Replay is supported by origins, classes, party choices, relationships and political outcomes rather than a compulsory infinite endgame. There is no base-game cooperative campaign, player housing construction system, farming loop or autonomous settlement management. PC mod/toolset activity is an extension ecosystem, not evidence that the unmodified campaign accepts arbitrary player-authored world laws. [M15](#m15)

## 11. Awakening — a command role and an expanded progression system

Awakening takes the Warden-Commander into Amaranthine and the Vigil's Keep region. It accepts an imported protagonist or a newly created commander. This is a separately launched expansion campaign, not a seamless additional area inserted into every moment of Origins. Import behavior has version-specific item exceptions; possessing Ultimate Edition does not guarantee that every piece of DLC equipment migrates unchanged. [A1](#a1) [A2](#a2)

Its companions are Oghren, Anders, Nathaniel, Sigrun, Velanna and Justice. Approval and personal stories remain, but romance does not. GameSpot praised the tactical continuity and some new locations while finding the new cast less memorable and the treatment of Warden recruitment less weighty. That is criticism of a particular dramatic presentation, not a rule that expansions cannot support strong new relationships. [A1](#a1)

Two additional specializations per class—Guardian/Spirit Warrior, Legionnaire Scout/Shadow, and Battlemage/Keeper—expand builds. A third specialization point arrives at level 22. Runecrafting, Vitality and Clarity add production and resource capacity; Clarity benefits the appropriate mana/stamina pool. New consumables and equipment tiers expand preparation, without making the underlying combat a different genre. [A3](#a3) [A4](#a4)

Vigil's Keep makes some responsibility institutional. Funding fortification work and finding granite or ore connect ordinary exploration to the strength of the settlement's defences. Late danger forces the player to consider the keep and Amaranthine, while the Architect and Mother complicate assumptions about the darkspawn. The keep is an authored upgrade/decision system, not freely designed city construction or a simulated tax-and-labour economy. [A5](#a5)

**Transfer:** responsibility for others can change the meaning of the same resource-gathering verb. **Limit:** the result needs to be legible; hidden quest/import bugs can make an intended command consequence look arbitrary.

## 12. Major DLC — distinct purposes, not a list of product names

### The Stone Prisoner: add a person who reframes later content

The Honnleath adventure combines combat, a trapped demon, a child, a puzzle and negotiations around obtaining Shale's activation phrase. The reward is a persistent companion with crystal-based equipment and specialized roles, not simply a weapon. Shale's personal journey into Cadash Thaig connects to the main campaign's golem history and can alter how the player experiences Orzammar. [D1](#d1)

Its design value is integration: a small expansion changes conversations and interpretation elsewhere. Its limit is dependency complexity—recruitment order, party presence and earlier choices affect which material the player sees.

### Warden's Keep: power, ethical history and practical infrastructure

Soldier's Peak reveals a contested Warden past through Sophia, Avernus and a breach in the Veil. The player can gain class-specific Power of Blood abilities from Avernus's research and must decide how to handle its practitioners. Clearing the site supplies merchants, a large storage chest and an opportunity to turn special meteorite material into Starfang. [D2](#d2)

The rewards span capability and convenience. The caution is that putting a broadly desired inventory remedy behind separate paid content can make the base game's friction feel commercially designed, whether or not that was the team's intention.

### Return to Ostagar: revisit a loss with different witnesses

A new lead opens the battlefield again. Recovering Cailan's equipment and Duncan's weapons turns former narrative props into usable objects with provenance. Companions connected to the original battle provide additional commentary; the same ruined place means something different to Alistair or Wynne than to someone who was absent. This is a revisit, not a reversal of the battle's outcome. [D3](#d3)

The mechanical structure remains a compact combat-and-loot excursion. Its distinctive contribution is remembered context, not a large new progression system.

### Darkspawn Chronicles: change the party contract

This is explicitly counterfactual: the player Warden died in the Joining. A hurlock vanguard recruits darkspawn thralls, uses their different abilities and can replace them rather than maintain a stable heroic party. Rob Bartel's developer interview describes respect/fear-based strengthening and objectives requiring particular creature capabilities. Campaign choices do not import as canonical history, although an unlocked reward weapon can cross to the main game. [D4](#d4)

The design experiment asks whether the same world becomes legible from its enemy's side. The separation between alternate narrative and transferable reward is essential; otherwise players could mistake a challenge scenario for a retcon.

### Leliana's Song: a voiced, character-focused prequel

The player controls Leliana during the events around Marjolaine and her earlier life. Political manipulation, betrayal and eventual change of direction receive more attention than a new long-form progression curve. The active party is capped at three. GameSpot found the central performances and character material effective but the supporting cast, reused spaces and battles less distinctive. [D5](#d5)

This shows how a companion can sustain a smaller protagonist-centred work. It does not imply that her full past was simulated beneath the original campaign.

### The Golems of Amgarrak: exploration changes combat readiness

An imported or new Warden enters a separate dwarven expedition. Colour-state switches change access through the location; collecting research notes adds capabilities and durability to the Runic Golem. The resulting helper can materially change the Harvester encounter. Jerrik, Brogan and the golem are the module's party context, not a continuation with whichever Origins companions the player prefers. [D6](#d6) [D7](#d7)

The useful structure is discovery feeding a later challenge. The corresponding cost is backtracking and a sharp combat-difficulty emphasis; this module should not be presented as another romance or diplomatic chapter.

### Witch Hunt: continuity expectations can exceed the delivered encounter

The module permits an imported or new protagonist and assembles a small party around the search for Morrigan. Its encounter at the eluvian gives a closing choice and points toward later mysteries. GameSpot's strongly negative assessment centred on how little Morrigan appeared, weak supporting characterization and insufficient challenge, despite some attractive environments and an interesting boss. [D8](#d8)

The warning is about promises: advertising an emotionally important reunion sets a different standard from selling a brief dungeon. Carrying a familiar name forward is not the same as satisfying the relationship it represents.

### Minor extras and promotional boundaries

Feastday Gifts and Pranks make approval unusually easy to manipulate; that changes the practical force of ordinary disagreement. Equipment promotions such as Blood Dragon Armor are reward/distribution extras, not additional stories. The short promotional A Tale of Orzammar module is also distinct from the main campaign and the later browser/social games. Bundle contents and retired delivery services require platform-specific checks; the existence of a historical announcement does not prove current availability. [D9](#d9)

## 13. Production, performance and presentation

BioWare's Mike Laidlaw described a goal of morally ambiguous choices before release. That statement establishes an intention; it does not prove that every branch avoids an obvious best answer. Companion-specific reactions give the ambition more substance than a single global good/evil score. [S1](#s1)

Voice production helps create the sense of individuality. Corinne Kempa's BioWare interview explains recording alone and relying on direction to recover emotional context. The performances are not incidental decoration: they connect discrete authored lines into the impression of a responsive person. This source does not establish a particular AI architecture. [S2](#s2)

Reviewers consistently value dialogue, voices and character work more than the uneven visual fidelity. The blood imagery, expressive conversations, codex and contrasting cultural locations support a recognizable setting, while repeated dungeon spaces and awkward interfaces can weaken it. The unvoiced protagonist permits one kind of player projection but can feel less cinematic to another player. Original PC/console differences must remain visible when discussing camera, texture quality and frame rate. [R3](#r3) [R5](#r5)

**Interpretation:** strong characterization is jointly produced by writing, acting, staging and remembered context. Replacing only the dialogue generator is unlikely to reproduce that effect automatically.

## 14. Distribution, commercial evidence and the long tail

Origins launched across PC and the contemporary Xbox 360/PlayStation 3 market; later packaging and distribution created several ways to own its content. The mix of a substantial base game, a major expansion, companion DLC, smaller experiments and promotional items is itself a production/distribution study. Bartel's 2010 interview explicitly describes experimenting with content formats, prices, development costs and platform delivery, rather than claiming the final strategy was known in advance. [D4](#d4)

EA's February 8, 2010 release reported **over 3.2 million units sold-in worldwide**. Sold-in is the publisher's channel metric, not a separately verified count of end users, active players or current owners. No current revenue, audited development budget or contemporary active-user figure was established in this pass. [S3](#s3)

Current player reviews show a long tail of replay and attachment, alongside compatibility complaints. Their suggested patches and mods were not installed or tested here; an anecdotal repair recommendation is not a verified guarantee for all current machines.

## 17. Worked cross-system situations

These are constructed examples of documented rules, not personal play or watched footage.

### A. An origin returns as personal context

A city-elf Warden reaches the alienage during the later political investigation. Familiar social context and a family-linked reward make the scene different from a stranger's visit. The next choice concerns how to handle the slavers and evidence, not merely where to collect loot. **Limit:** the variation is authored; it does not prove that every citizen remembers every action. [M13](#m13)

### B. Replace a crude routine with a conditional policy

A healer is spending resources at unhelpful moments. The player revises the ordered tactics conditions, tests the result and retains manual control for unusual danger. **Limit:** the policy still needs valid targets, resources and successful execution. An order editor should expose why a rule did not fire rather than imply that more rules always solve the problem. [M7](#m7)

### C. Manufacture another attempt instead of simply reloading

The party survives a difficult fight but has injuries and depleted supplies. A crafter converts gathered ingredients into recovery items, and the player compares continuing with returning to camp. **Limit:** healing health and removing an injury are different services. Repeated supply manufacture is only interesting when it changes a genuine expedition decision. [M5](#m5) [M8](#m8)

### D. Knowledge opens another moral option

At Redcliffe, the player knows the Circle may provide help. They pursue that relationship rather than accepting the apparent immediate dilemma. The available resolution changes because of another part of the campaign. **Limit:** an apparently urgent crisis with no advancing clock can encourage implausibly leisurely optimization. [M12](#m12)

### E. A political compromise costs a companion

At the Landsmeet, a Warden decides that recruiting a former enemy is strategically useful. That changes who is available and damages a valued relationship. The next choice is not just a different ending slide: the party and its future conversations have changed. **Limit:** a few decisive flags can feel abrupt if prior conversations did not establish the companion's boundary. [M13](#m13)

### F. Add an interpreter, not just a tank

The player recruits Shale before exploring the golem question. A combat-capable party member now also interprets the place through a personal history. **Limit:** important reactions can be missed through party selection, so a design needs a considered policy for later discussion of events someone did not witness. [D1](#d1)

### G. Exploration becomes responsibility for a settlement

An Awakening commander finds ore and funds fortification work rather than spending everything on personal gear. The reward is tied to the place and people entrusted to them. **Limit:** this is a bounded upgrade quest; a general settlement system would also need labour, supply, maintenance and communication rules that Awakening does not establish. [A5](#a5)

## 18. What OpenLegend should learn—and not assume

**Background should remain operational.** A person's origin can affect access, interpretation and obligations after an introductory sequence. Convergence is not inherently bad; forgetting the route to it is.

**Separate personality from execution policy.** Opinions, relationship history, goals, combat routines and navigation failures should not be collapsed into one opaque 'AI' category. A believable person still needs reliable action execution.

**Model several kinds of consequence.** Temporary injury, resource depletion, relationship damage, changed authority and death do different design work. One universal penalty cannot replace all of them.

**Make social boundaries legible.** A companion can tolerate disagreements yet refuse a particular act. That is richer than either unconditional obedience or a single approval threshold, provided players can understand the distinction.

**Let one system supply an alternative inside another.** A prior alliance changing a family crisis is a stronger cross-system connection than simply adding a combat bonus. For generative worlds, the alternative must remain grounded in actual capabilities and authority.

**Companions can be interpreters of places.** Bringing different people should change which history, danger or opportunity becomes apparent. OpenLegend would need perception and knowledge boundaries so an absent actor does not mysteriously know everything.

**Do not copy authored branching and call it universal agency.** Origins' powerful moments are carefully constructed. A more open simulation needs rules for analogous consequences when no writer has pre-enumerated the branch, plus safeguards against incoherent or irreversible outcomes.
