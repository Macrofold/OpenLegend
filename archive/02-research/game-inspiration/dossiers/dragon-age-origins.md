## Identity and scope

Origins is BioWare's 2009 party role-playing game, published by EA. A created protagonist enters one of six origin stories and eventually joins the Grey Wardens. The shared main campaign does not erase the origin: returning characters and changed context can make the same later encounter mean something different to another protagonist. This dossier covers the base game plus Awakening and major DLC, with separate campaign/import boundaries rather than treating Ultimate Edition as one uninterrupted adventure. [R1](#r1)

The six origins are human noble, magi, city elf, Dalish elf, dwarf commoner and dwarf noble. Race/class choices constrain eligibility. The active party contains the protagonist and up to three companions. The PC version offers an overhead tactical view; the original console interfaces differ. These distinctions matter when comparing reviewers' experiences of the same nominal game. [R1](#r1) [R5](#r5)

## A central research tension: authored individuality and delegated tactics

Origins combines relationships that make the player care about companions with configurable combat automation that can reduce routine orders. Those are different systems. A companion's dialogue, approval and authored quest history should not be mistaken for the reasoning mechanism that selects an attack. Likewise, failure of a pathfinding or tactics rule is not necessarily evidence that the character's narrative personality is shallow.

The reviews disagree on how well that combination works. GameSpot celebrates the controllable party and conditional tactics; Destructoid finds unreliable execution and frequent micromanagement. Platform, encounter, difficulty and player preference must remain visible rather than collapsing this into a claim that the AI was universally excellent or universally broken.

## Five substantive independent written assessments

All five review bodies were read. These are different publications, not multiple platform scores from a single review substituted for independent sources.

### Kevin VanOrd — GameSpot, November 3, 2009

VanOrd praises the world, emotionally engaging supporting cast, quest choices and the PC's flexible party control. He finds equipment progression and tactical combinations satisfying. His criticism includes glitches and substantial platform differences: console texture quality, frame rate, camera and occasional audio/pathfinding issues affect the experience. This is a launch-era review, not a current compatibility audit. [R1](#r1)

### James Stephanie Sterling — Destructoid, November 3, 2009

The Xbox 360 review values the setting, morally ambiguous decisions, humour and characters, especially the memorable party personalities. Its criticism is much sharper about unreliable tactics, pathfinding, sudden difficulty spikes and the amount of direct intervention needed to keep a party alive. Inventory/navigation and technical roughness also interrupt enjoyment. The review's resource scarcity complaints are a firsthand experience, not a complete accounting of all available crafting supplies. [R2](#r2)

### John P. Hussey — RPGFan, January 4, 2010

Hussey's PlayStation 3 assessment emphasizes how replay changes the significance of familiar people and places. He values the universe, choices, audio and character development. Controls and radial-menu navigation can become cumbersome, while frame-rate drops and uneven graphics contrast with the narrative strengths. His enthusiastic verdict does not negate those specific reservations. The review is not used as authoritative chronology for fictional historical events. [R3](#r3)

### Mike Moehnke — RPGamer, August 3, 2014

This PC Ultimate Edition retrospective values the companions, encounter planning, codex and overall bundle. It finds familiar plot ingredients redeemed by the detail of the journey and appreciates terrain and traps creating tactical problems. Inventory management, uneven visuals and loading are persistent irritants. It evaluates a later package, not an independently tested 2026 build. The original PC game already had an overhead camera; retrospective wording must not be misread as saying Ultimate introduced it. [R4](#r4)

### Wesley Yin-Poole — VideoGamer, historical review; page updated June 6, 2021

Yin-Poole finds a compelling, replayable world and particularly strong companions. The absence of a global good/evil score leaves decisions to the player's judgment and to individual characters' reactions. He is less impressed by the generic visual presentation and warns that frequent pausing, terminology and a largely unvoiced protagonist will not suit everyone expecting a more streamlined cinematic RPG. The page update date is not a new 2021 playthrough. [R5](#r5)
