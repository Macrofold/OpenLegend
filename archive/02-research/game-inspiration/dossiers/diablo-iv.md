# Diablo IV — full research dossier

**G12 · In progress, September 26, 2026.** Substantive checkpoint, not completed research. Remaining: the original five classes and ordinary combat/world loop, full equipment/Paragon and recovery account, campaign/presentation/audio, production/distribution/commercial history, contrasting direct reception, and R01–R14/preservation/link review. The existing [three-game comparison](../games/path-of-exile-path-of-exile-2-and-diablo-iv.md) remains intact. [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md).

This is Diablo IV, including explicitly separated expansion and seasonal systems. Diablo II, Diablo III, Immortal and an announced successor do not establish its rules. Historical introductions explain development; their numerical tuning is not automatically current. No personal gameplay or full-video viewing is claimed.

## 1. A character project exists inside several different release contracts

Blizzard's 2024 release account separates **Vessel of Hatred** ownership—Spiritborn, Nahantu, its campaign and companion/endgame systems—from simultaneous base-game progression changes. [D01](#d01) The April 2026 **Lord of Hatred** update then changed the skill trees, maximum level and item system again. The current level cap is 70, with twelve Torment tiers; an old guide's level 50/60 or four-tier description is not the same progression contract. [D02](#d02)

**Interpretation:** a player may return to a familiar class while finding that the route to a functioning build has changed. An expansion purchase, a permanent free-system update and a temporary seasonal activity are not interchangeable forms of content. Researching only the launch campaign would miss much of the current activity; researching only the latest seasonal power would misdescribe the permanent game.

The April update also removed passive nodes from the ordinary skill tree, moving many effects into equipment and making active skills more configurable. Not every option is free: non-owners receive two of the three final-branch Bonus Skill Variants, while Lord of Hatred owners can choose among all three. [D02](#d02) That is a documented, specific entitlement boundary—not independent confirmation of every historical reviewer allegation about a paywall.

## 2. A familiar skill can become a different operator

The redesigned skill branches can change an ability's category, allowing a new interaction with equipment, class rules and other tag-dependent effects. The Druid example is particularly concrete: changing **Trample** from Werebear to Werewolf changes its animation into a leap. Necromancer **Sacrifice** no longer necessarily eliminates summoned minions; it can reduce their number or damage while retaining another bonus. [D02](#d02)

**Constructed situation:** a player wants to retain a particular transformed identity while using a useful movement action. Changing the action's supported form can serve that goal, but other bonuses must actually recognize the resulting tags. A new animation alone would not establish compatibility. Similarly, accepting fewer helpers for another benefit is a different decision from permanently prohibiting all helpers.

**Interpretation:** preserving a recognizable ability name while altering its operation is an approachable form of composition. It also creates a migration burden: someone who learned the old name may infer an old restriction that no longer applies. The documentation must describe the actual action and edition rather than rely on recognition alone.

## 3. Spiritborn: a chosen identity can alter the whole skill set

The **Spiritborn** debuted with Vessel of Hatred in October 2024. Its four Spirit Guardians—**Jaguar, Gorilla, Eagle and Centipede**—supply different combat vocabularies. The **Spirit Hall** selects a primary and secondary relationship; the primary adds its Guardian's tag to all skills. [D03](#d03)

Blizzard's introduction illustrates **Gorilla primary plus Jaguar secondary**: defensive/thorns-related Gorilla effects can combine with Jaguar's attack-speed-oriented Ferocity. The exact original values are historical; the structural example is how a broadly applied tag allows another family of bonuses to participate. [D03](#d03)

**Constructed choice:** specialize deeply in one Guardian or combine the useful behavior of two. The interesting operation is not merely equipping a second animal icon. The choice changes which actions qualify for effects and how frequently those effects matter. A limitation follows: compatibility does not prove the resulting build has enough resources, survivability or damage for the selected challenge.

**Interpretation:** a small vocabulary of recognizable identities can support a larger number of useful configurations. The player needs a visible account of why an ability qualifies, especially when its original visual identity differs from its newly acquired tag.

## 4. Warlock: a summon can be a tool, a defense or a changing resource cycle

**Warlock**, released with Lord of Hatred, separates **Wrath** for skills from **Dominance** for summoning and demon abilities. Its authored fiction frames demon control as dangerous subjugation, not a friendly creature-collection relationship. [D04](#d04)

The action vocabulary includes **Dark Prison**, which restrains enemies in an area; **Wall of Agony**, which makes demons into an obstruction; **Tyrant's Grasp**, which pulls enemies; and **Sigil of Summons**, which prolongs summons. These have spatial and temporal functions beyond a larger attack number. [D04](#d04)

**Constructed interaction:** place a restraining effect where it can keep enemies near a summon, then decide whether to remain in a useful ritual area or move away from danger. A stationary benefit can encourage a bad position. Neither naming a demon nor giving it an animation establishes an independently reasoning social character.

The class's Soul Shards select different archetypes. In the documented **Legion** design, greater-demon use temporarily assists lesser-demon casting, while enough lesser-demon deaths can remove the resource cost of a later greater summon. [D04](#d04) **Interpretation:** a helper's disappearance can be part of a planned cycle, not only lost progress. That emotional contract should not be imported unexamined into a world where inhabitants are intended as valued persons.

### An attributed build discovery

In his April 21, 2026 review, **Tyler Colp** describes changing **Rampage** from repeated resource-consuming summons into one large repeatedly resummonable demon. He then found pleasure in preparing enemies and supporting it, despite usually disliking summoner play. His build became satisfying before the post-campaign hunt for exceptional equipment. [D05](#d05)

**Interpretation:** delegating damage can leave meaningful work in positioning, setup and coordination. The relevant distinction is not manual versus automated action, but whether the remaining decisions still feel authored by the player. This is Colp's review-build experience, not a measured typical progression time or a current optimal build.

## 5. Paladin: an oath changes the meaning of a sequence

**Paladin** was playable through Lord of Hatred pre-purchase before the expansion's full launch. Its **Oaths** supply different ways to organize actions. **Judicator** applies Judgement through basic skills and lets appropriate core skills detonate it; **Disciple** connects supported cooldown skills to an angelic **Arbiter** form. Other routes emphasize Fervor or consuming Resolve for stronger, larger attacks. [D06](#d06)

**Constructed comparison:** immediately use a large attack, or first apply the condition that makes the next attack more valuable. A player pursuing transformed uptime has another reason to care about when a cooldown is used. The same equipment statistic may support these two plans differently.

**Interpretation:** a class-defining rule gives otherwise ordinary attacks a relationship. Its value depends on readable setup and payoff, not just a more elaborate skill list. The introduction's old percentages are deliberately omitted because later patches changed Oath tuning; the named structure is the research object, not an optimized current rotation.

## 6. Runewords are trigger–resource–effect compositions

Vessel of Hatred introduced two rune families: **Ritual** runes generate **Offering** when an action meets a condition, and **Invocation** runes spend it for an effect. Blizzard's original **Bac/Jah** example connects movement with replacing a later Evade by a Sorcerer-style teleport. Overflow can improve supported effects. [D07](#d07)

**Constructed situation:** choose a trigger that ordinary play actually supplies, then pair it with a useful result. A high-output trigger is not useful if the intended build rarely performs its condition. The resource threshold also means that one qualifying action need not immediately produce the desired effect.

**Interpretation:** this is a compact reusable-construct grammar: eligibility, evidence of activation, accumulated resource and an effect. It can borrow a recognizable class action without giving the user that class's entire identity. A system advertised as creating abilities still has explicit rune and socket limits; it is not unrestricted natural-language invention.

The cited 2.0 page is a **historical PTR explanation**. D01 corroborates the feature's subsequent release. It is not used to assert that its original numerical thresholds or every test rune remain unchanged.

## 7. Companions can have a useful role without general autonomous conversation

Vessel of Hatred's named Mercenaries are **Raheir**, **Varyana**, **Aldkin** and **Subo**. Their quests and backgrounds distinguish them, while their combat roles include protection, kill-streak support, demonic transformation and marking targets. Solo play can hire one as a continuing companion; a different mercenary can provide a chosen **Reinforcement** when an **Opportunity** condition occurs. [D08](#d08)

**Constructed interaction:** choose a reinforcement that responds to losing control, rather than simply selecting the ally with the most appealing damage description. It can provide help when the player cannot conveniently issue another command. An unsuitable trigger, however, can leave the assistance irrelevant to the actual problem.

**Interpretation:** identity, availability and execution policy are separate design decisions. A named person can matter through a bounded reliable action. This does not establish open-ended planning, belief revision or dialogue memory. The solo hire's continuity is also different from a short triggered intervention in a human party.

The expansion's **Dark Citadel** adds cooperative puzzles, while **Kurast Undercity** uses a timed run with **Tributes** targeting rewards and **Attunement** improving their quality. A stronger Tribute raises the challenge as well as the potential reward. [D08](#d08)

**Constructed choice:** spend a useful Tribute when prepared to complete its challenge, rather than assume possessing the access item guarantees its reward. Preparation, successful execution and the resulting loot are separate commitments. The detailed current group and recovery rules remain part of this dossier's unfinished work.
