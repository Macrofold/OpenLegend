# G80 — Final Fantasy III

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Full-category research pass: September 26, 2026.** This completes the G80 checkpoint while preserving its review disagreements, production testimony and player evidence. The subject is the **1990 Japanese Final Fantasy III**, not Final Fantasy VI's original North American SNES title. Story, world-map and late-job spoilers follow. No firsthand gameplay, full-video viewing or proprietary implementation inspection is claimed. Constructed examples and transferable hypotheses are not accepted OpenLegend designs.

## 1. Identity, promise and version boundaries — R01

The fundamental promise is a four-person expedition whose members can change professions when the next problem demands it. The player explores, prepares a party, fights menu-driven battles, finds new capabilities and revisits the world with different means of movement. This is an authored single-player RPG, not a continuously simulated civilization or multiplayer campaign. [J10]

| Version | What must remain distinct |
| --- | --- |
| **Famicom original, April 27, 1990, Japan** | Four initially interchangeable Onion Knights; changing jobs spends Capacity Points. Historical command lists, equipment rules and numerical formulas need their own labels. [J6][J12][J14] |
| **Nintendo DS 3D remake, Japanese release August 24, 2006** | Rebuilt presentation, the named protagonists Luneth, Arc, Refia and Ingus, revised job access/balance, and temporary adjustment penalties after changing jobs. This is a DS remake, not a 3DS remake. Later 3D ports are not presumed identical to either the DS release or Pixel Remaster. [J4–J6] |
| **Pixel Remaster, Steam release July 28, 2021** | A newly presented 2D version following the original's party/world structure, with revised jobs and no Capacity Point or job-sickness barrier. It does not incorporate every 3D addition merely because it is newer. [J3][J10] |
| **Later Pixel Remaster releases and updates** | PS4/Switch arrived April 19, 2023. Subsequent PC patches added options beyond the launch reviews, including a separate job-level growth boost. Dated changes are summarized in §8, not projected backward. [J8][J31] |

**Interpretation:** the game sits between FFI's initial class commitment and FFII's use-shaped characters. It makes changing the group a recurring answer. Its central tension is whether the player experiences this as experimentation or as complying with a succession of prescribed solutions.

## 2. The action and job inventory — R02, R03

### Decisions within and between rounds

Battle commands are selected for the party and then resolved according to the combat rules. Attack, magic, item use, defense and retreat share the interface with job-specific actions. There is no real-time parry requirement. Between encounters, the player changes jobs, reallocates permitted equipment and spells, restores resources and plans the route. [J3][J13]

The following is the **22-job 2D/Pixel Remaster unlock inventory**, not the 3D unlock schedule. Some translated names vary; Dark Knight is called Mystic Knight in the historical database. [J11][J12]

| Availability | Jobs and practical roles |
| --- | --- |
| **Beginning** | **Onion Knight:** an initially limited generalist, not a blank slot that automatically learns every other job's commands. |
| **Wind Crystal / Altar Cave** | **Warrior:** armored physical offense. **Monk:** unarmed or nunchaku offense. **White Mage:** recovery, protection and utility magic. **Black Mage:** offensive and disabling magic. **Red Mage:** restricted access to both schools alongside weapon use. |
| **Fire Crystal / Molten Cave** | **Ranger:** ranged attacks and, in Pixel Remaster, Barrage. **Knight:** durable offense and protection of endangered allies. **Thief:** theft, escape and field utility. **Scholar:** enemy information and, in Pixel Remaster, enhanced item use. |
| **Water Crystal / Cave of Tides** | **Geomancer:** environment-dependent attacks without MP expenditure. **Dragoon:** spear-oriented offense and Jump. **Viking:** defensive physical role. **Black Belt:** advanced martial offense. **Dark Knight:** specialized blades and group attacks. **Evoker:** summons with variable outcomes. **Bard:** songs and support. |
| **Earth Crystal / Ancient Labyrinth** | **Magus:** advanced Black Magic. **Devout:** advanced White Magic. **Summoner:** the stronger, determinate summon effects. |
| **Eureka** | **Sage:** broad magic access. **Ninja:** broad physical equipment access and powerful thrown consumables. |

### A job is a package, not a permanent learned sub-command

Character level, job selection and job proficiency are separate. Changing roles does not erase the character's overall experience, but a newly chosen job can be less practiced. FFIII is not FFV's system of combining a learned command from one job with another active job. A player should not expect to retain every useful verb merely by briefly wearing its costume. [J3][J13]

The original's Capacity Point cost decreases with familiarity in the target job. The DS remake instead imposes a temporary statistical adjustment. Pixel Remaster removes both impediments, but does not make equipment ownership or depleted spell charges irrelevant. A directly read player discussion specifically warns that switching a mage through a non-casting job can leave charges empty; that is a reported remaster interaction, not a proprietary-code audit. [J14][J34]

### Commands whose names conceal edition changes

**Ranger:** the original has limited White Magic. The 3D remake and Pixel Remaster use **Barrage**, a distributed multi-hit attack. Arrows are consumable in the former but not in Pixel Remaster. Importing the old ammunition bill would therefore misdescribe the newer job's economy. [J21]

**Scholar:** the original separates reading HP and weakness. Pixel Remaster's **Study** reports both; **Alchemy** highlights applicable items and increases their potency with job proficiency. This is enhanced item use, not a recipe-based crafting system. The 3D Scholar's low-tier spell access and passive Item Lore are different arrangements. [J20]

**Bard:** the 3D version's song depends on the equipped harp. Pixel Remaster instead unlocks **Paeon** for party healing at job level 1, **Minuet** for offense at 10, **Elegy** for damage reduction at 20, and **Requiem** for randomized enemy ailments at 30. Harp possession and song availability should not be conflated. [J23]

**Geomancer:** Terrain selects effects based on the combat environment without spending spell charges. It does not terraform the map. Some launch-era guides carry forward the original's Backfire penalty, whereas the dedicated version reference explicitly removes it for Pixel Remaster. This dossier follows that version-specific account and records the disagreement instead of silently presenting all guide text as compatible. [J22][J35]

**Physical specialists:** Knight's protection of critically injured allies, Dark Knight's **Bladeblitz**, Black Belt's **Boost/Kick**, and Viking's **Draw Attacks** offer more than differing attack totals. The community guide's claims about redirecting magic are not sufficiently cross-verified here; the worked interpretation is limited to a physical-defense role. A role's unlock also does not guarantee immediate access to equipment that makes it attractive. [J17][J18]

## 3. Resources, equipment, spells and objects — R03

### Growth has several owners

Experience raises character level. Job practice improves effectiveness within a role. Strength, Agility, Vitality, Intelligence and Mind/Spirit describe different capabilities; physical hit count and accuracy are not simply synonyms for damage per hit. **HP is a consequential exception to effortless rebuilding:** leveling under different Vitality can leave a persistent capacity difference. Exact original formulas are not adopted as a current all-platform calculator. [J13]

**Interpretation:** the important player question is which investments survive a change. Reversible role selection can coexist with irreversible historical growth. That combination is not inherently bad, but advertising only the reversible part can lead a player to discover long-term consequences much later than intended.

### Equipment and spatial formation

Weapon families include conventional blades, spears, axes and staves, but also books, bells, harps, bows and thrown weapons. Their permissions connect inventory to the currently selected role. Head, body and hand protection, shields and dual wielding create defensive/offensive choices; an empty weapon slot can be intentional for martial jobs. [J12][J17][J20–J23]

The original systems reference shows why choosing two weapons is not automatically better than carrying a shield: shields contribute defensive properties while ranged equipment can preserve useful offense from the back row. Its exact multipliers, discounts and escape penalties remain historical rules, not unqualified remaster advice. [J14]

Named equipment makes the allocation problem concrete. **Excalibur**, **Ragnarok** and **Moonring Blade** have more than one plausible recipient. A practiced Knight can remain useful even after Ninja unlocks; Sage's broader spell coverage need not always replace a specialist's larger reserve. Those comparisons contradict the strongest “only one correct endgame party” claims without requiring the dossier to invent an optimal build. [J18][J19]

Dark Knight access illustrates a different constraint: the job can be available before convenient access to the dark blades and armor sold in **Falgabard**. The player is choosing a role, a supply route and a practice investment together. Opening a menu entry is not the same as delivering its mature power. [J18]

### Magic is equipped capability plus a tiered reserve

Spells are obtained, assigned and limited by the current job's casting permissions. White, Black and summon magic use **eight tiers of charges**, not FFII's single pool with individually trained spell levels. A charge from a higher tier cannot simply be spent on an exhausted lower tier. A spell appearing on a character's list does not prove the active job can cast it. [J13][J16]

Named effects cover recovery (**Cure, Cura, Curaja, Raise/Arise**), elemental damage, disabling conditions, protective support, navigation and summons. **Mini** and **Toad** are particularly important because a condition that weakens the party can also grant passage. This does not make every battlefield status an unrestricted overworld transformation tool. [J16][J24]

Evoker and Summoner do not merely apply different damage multipliers to an otherwise identical animation. The original spell reference distinguishes the Evoker's alternative outcomes from the Summoner's fixed effect. Acquiring a summon and selecting the caster therefore answer different questions. Hidden or earned summons should not be mistaken for automatically unlocked spells whenever a new casting job appears. [J16]

A **Golem Staff** can supply an item-use petrification effect and an **Elder Staff** an item-use healing effect, conserving charges at the cost of a battle action. Ninja's **Throw** instead spends a **Shuriken**, turning stored wealth or treasure into immediate power. Repeatable equipment use and consumed ammunition are different economies. [J19]

### Recovery and key-object inventory

Potions and Hi-Potions restore HP; Phoenix Downs address KO. Gold Needles, Echo Herbs, Maiden's Kisses and other remedies target particular conditions. Offensive objects such as **Antarctic Wind**, **Zeus's Wrath** and **Bomb Fragment** supply effects without requiring a learned spell. Magic Keys address doors; Gnomish Bread supplies a map function; Gysahl Greens have a chocobo-related use. The historical inventory distinguishes field-only, battle-only and shared uses rather than assuming every object works everywhere. [J15]

That Chocobo use is more concrete than the original pass recorded. In the 2D/Pixel Remaster lineage, using **Gysahl Greens at a Chocobo Woods calls the Fat Chocobo**, which stores spare inventory for later retrieval. [J36] This is a small but meaningful logistics primitive: an item can unlock **external storage tied to a recurring world entity/location** instead of merely healing or opening a door. It should not be confused with the ordinary rideable Chocobo or with a player-built warehouse.

FFIII does **not** have the ordinary Ether-and-Tent recovery pattern a veteran of other installments might expect. Inns, restoration points and rare Elixirs therefore matter to sustained spell use. Calling this “no MP” is misleading shorthand: it lacks the common pooled meter, not a finite magical resource. [J9]

The **Wheel of Time**, **Noah's Lute** and four **Fangs** advance transport, awaken a necessary ally or open a route. They are authored access objects, not replaceable equipment tiers. Treasure, a quest object, a job unlock and a vehicle are all rewards, but they change different parts of the possibility space. [J24]

## 4. World, travel and relationships — R02, R06, R08

### A map that changes meaning

**World-reveal spoilers:** the initial land is a floating continent, not the whole planet. Reaching the flooded world changes the scale of the journey; restoring the Water Crystal opens a different geography. Earlier towns and landmarks remain meaningful rather than being replaced by a completely unrelated second campaign. [J9][J24]

The **Nautilus** handles powerful winds, lands on ordinary ground and later dives underwater. The **Invincible** offers a slower mobile service base and the ability to hop suitable mountain barriers. Acquiring the latter does not remove the former: some destinations still call for the Nautilus. This is complementary access, not a simple sequence of faster mounts. [J26][J27]

The Invincible combines recovery, shops and transport. Boarding its interior is not a settlement-construction mechanic; the player does not freely design its rooms, recruit an economy or invent new engines. Its services change the cost of an expedition without making every dungeon itself safe. [J27]

**Interpretation:** the most interesting traversal upgrade changes how the player reads geography. A ridge, a current and a patch of seabed become questions about capability. An apparent visual obstruction only becomes satisfying when the required verb and the permitted locations are understandable.

### NPCs and companions have bounded agency

Sara, Cid, Desch, Aria, Alus, Doga and Unei connect particular regions, problems and capabilities. In the 2D version, temporary companions follow and comment rather than becoming a fifth player-controlled combat slot. The original designer explicitly preferred that separation to inventing a reason every narratively powerful guest fought at the protagonists' current strength. [J24][J28]

The 3D remake gives its core protagonists names and more personal framing. Its historical **Mognet** letter exchanges could unlock optional material, including interactions involving other DS players. That is not cooperative control of the campaign, nor evidence that every original online function is currently available. Pixel Remaster does not gain the DS social surface by association. [J4][J5]

The optional-content boundary is materially larger than “some letters.” In the DS remake, Mognet-linked sidequests can unlock the remake's **secret Onion Knight job**, a wandering Legendary Smith/Ultima Weapon chain, and the hidden **??? Dungeon / Iron Giant** superboss after the relevant letter conditions. [J37] Those are 3D-remake systems layered on top of the 1990 structure. The 2D Pixel Remaster uses Onion Knight as the **starting job** and does **not** inherit the DS mail-gated secret dungeon simply because both releases are called Final Fantasy III.

This difference is exactly the kind of remake boundary the audit is meant to preserve: “newer version” does not mean cumulative union of all earlier extras.

There is no general relationship-rank economy, marriage, romance selection, faction reputation, player trading, city management or authored-world editing in the studied core. The 3D development interview's proposed favorability-based dialogue system was **discarded**; it must not be cited as a shipped social mechanic. [J6]

### Human-scale adventures inside a larger cosmology

Ur supplies the protagonists' ordinary beginning; Kazus and Sasune give the journey an immediate curse to break; Saronia links a political crisis to the loss of an airship and a new way forward. Miniaturized routes, underwater locations and the Crystal Tower give regions different demands, not merely stronger versions of the previous monsters. [J9][J24]

The late antagonists **Xande** and the **Cloud of Darkness** scale the threat beyond local trouble. The world also complicates the equation of light with uncomplicated goodness: the Invincible's ancient builders belong to a history in which excessive use of Light caused catastrophe and Warriors of Darkness restored balance. That is a story about dangerous imbalance, not evidence of a simulated alignment meter. [J25][J27]

**Interpretation:** the cast's appeal does not depend only on dialogue volume. A companion can be remembered because they explain a strange place or leave the party with a new route. Conversely, sparse characterization puts extra pressure on those encounters: when a person only delivers the next object, the journey can feel like an errand chain rather than a relationship.

## 5. Economy, difficulty and return play — R04

Gil buys equipment, spells and supplies. Job practice costs battles and actions. A long expedition spends recovery capacity and exposes the party to uncertainty. The player's resources therefore include money, build familiarity and the margin for another encounter—not just a level number. [J13][J15]

The relevant preparation question is often “what capability is missing?” rather than “how much more experience is needed?” Goldor resists a magic-heavy response; Hein rewards identifying a changing weakness; Garuda strongly rewards the Dragoon's defensive/offensive timing. Guides recommend these answers, but recommendations do not prove that every alternative is mechanically forbidden. [J25]

A lost attempt can expose a poor build, insufficient supplies, bad luck or an unexpectedly punishing recovery rule. DS criticism repeatedly connects difficulty to the absence of convenient permanent saves inside dungeons. Pixel Remaster's autosaving changes the cost of retrying; it does not by itself rebalance every enemy or make every role equally useful. [J1][J4][J5]

**Interpretation:** challenge and punishment need separate treatment. An encounter can demand an interesting change while a long repeated approach makes discovering that change unattractive. Removing a recovery penalty may encourage more experimentation without changing the underlying solution space at all.

Eureka and optional summon encounters provide late goals and equipment beyond simply following the next town instruction. Bestiary and treasure completion create a second reason to inspect places, but some encounters or regions can be missed. Replaying with different jobs is voluntary variation, not a live-service season. The premium campaign has no native daily stamina system, gacha recruitment or purchasable battle-pass progression. [J10][J24]

## 6. Worked interactions — R05

These are **constructed illustrations of documented mechanics**, not firsthand runs, measured optimal strategies or claims of guaranteed victory.

### A. Use a harmful condition as a traversal ability

**Intention:** pass through a route sized for tiny travelers. **Conditions:** possess Mini, suitable casting jobs and restorative access nearby. **Sequence:** prepare spells and charges, shrink the party, then enter the route with a magic-oriented plan. **Interaction:** the same status grants access while making ordinary physical offense ineffective. **Result:** travel changes combat preparation. **Next decision:** retain the setup for another small passage or restore ordinary form. **Limit:** merely changing everybody into mages does not buy their spells or refill their reserves. [J24]

### B. Spend an action to reduce uncertainty

**Intention:** damage Hein without guessing his current weakness. **Conditions:** a Scholar and an ally able to exploit the information. **Sequence:** Study, read the result, choose the corresponding attack, and reassess when the enemy changes. **Interaction:** one character's informational action improves another's resource expenditure. **Result:** a low-damage turn can still improve the group's outcome. **Next decision:** repeat the observation or exploit what is known. **Limit:** old information can become stale; Study does not itself guarantee survival. [J20][J25]

### C. Read a region's equipment as a tactical clue

**Intention:** survive Garuda's dangerous attacks. **Conditions:** unlocked Dragoon, suitable gear and a prepared party. **Sequence:** recognize the usefulness of available Dragoon equipment, change the chosen members, and use Jump while managing recovery. **Interaction:** temporary absence from the battlefield and landing damage serve the same plan. **Result:** a different party structure changes exposure, not just damage totals. **Next decision:** keep the role or rebuild after the fight. **Limit:** the widely recommended all-Dragoon approach is not established here as the sole legal victory condition. [J25]

### D. Develop a support verb instead of buying a new instrument

**Intention:** add a defensive song in Pixel Remaster. **Conditions:** Bard is unlocked and the character can gain job proficiency. **Sequence:** practice the job, reach Elegy's availability, then select it when reducing incoming damage is more useful than another action. **Interaction:** practice opens a new command; buying the harp associated with an older version's song is not the unlocking mechanism here. **Result:** job experience expands the available response. **Next decision:** heal, buff or attack on the following turn. **Limit:** having the command does not make all enemies or all damage patterns harmless. [J23]

### E. Keep the older vehicle because it solves another problem

**Intention:** reach an underwater destination after obtaining the Invincible. **Conditions:** the Nautilus has its diving capability and remains available. **Sequence:** select the appropriate craft, travel to the relevant sea area and submerge. **Interaction:** two vehicles partition access rather than one strictly dominating the other. **Result:** an older reward remains useful. **Next decision:** continue the underwater route or return to the larger ship's services. **Limit:** the Nautilus is not a portable inn simply because another airship is. [J26][J27]

### F. Convert stored wealth into a shorter dangerous fight

**Intention:** end a late encounter before repeated heavy attacks exhaust recovery. **Conditions:** a Ninja, Shurikens and a reason to value immediate damage over saving them. **Sequence:** compare the remaining threat with the supply, then choose Throw. **Interaction:** a purchased or found consumable becomes a battle action rather than a permanent equipment upgrade. **Result:** the player spends future flexibility for present safety. **Next decision:** replenish, conserve the remainder or use ordinary attacks. **Limit:** hoarding forever and spending indiscriminately are both choices with costs; no fixed optimal count is claimed. [J19]

## 7. Presentation, controls and the production of identity — R07

Koichi Ishii's retrospective describes giving jobs distinct commands, poses and visual personalities. Palette restrictions encouraged substitutions rather than simply adding colors. He also explains designing wounded poses to make players want to help the visible party. This is named artistic intention, not a measured emotional response. His imagined costume stories are not extra gameplay rules. [J29]

**Interpretation:** a class can become recognizable through a small combination of silhouette, posture and verb. That economy of expression makes switching roles readable even when the character has little authored dialogue. A visually different costume without a useful behavioral difference would not produce the same effect.

The DS reviews disagree about pace: GameSpot finds the 3D presentation slower than the 2D predecessors, while RPGamer finds its battles quick and enjoys the optional stylus controls. Those are experience reports, not frame-time benchmarks. A second screen or touch input should not be treated as an automatic improvement simply because the hardware permits it. [J4][J5]

Pixel Remaster's revised sprites, music, maps and collection features support a different kind of accessibility. Siliconera's criticism of unused screen area and inconsistent typography shows that attractive assets do not guarantee efficient information display. Later font and audio choices must be distinguished from the launch presentation. [J2][J8][J10]

The critical-hit flash is a particularly concrete dated issue: the official history adds the effect in August 2022, while a January 2023 negative player review objects to it. No claim is made that a particular complaint caused a later decision, or that every present platform lacks a mitigation. [J7][J8]

## 8. Production, discovery and commercial context — R09–R11

Tanaka's original-development retrospective describes keeping organized design materials and recalls the problem of players converging on Ninja and Sage despite the aim of flexible jobs. That retrospective is a 2020 interview republished in 2025, not contemporaneous production minutes. [J28]

The 2025 Tanaka/Asano conversation identifies **Matrix Software** as the 3D remake developer and describes a ten-point direction document. Named protagonists were a deliberate remake decision. It also records debate over job balance and a save point in the final tower; the save point was not added. A development proposal is evidence of a considered option, not a shipped feature. [J6]

TOSE separately credits its work on the mobile/Steam Pixel Remaster series. This prevents reading the store's publisher/developer field as a complete workforce list. The research does not establish a verified remaster budget, proprietary engine implementation or per-title profit. [J30]

The commercial positioning connects historical access with refreshed presentation and convenience. The 2D worldwide release can appeal to players who previously knew only the 3D remake; individual games and bundles offer different entry points. This is visible positioning, not an independently measured attribution of sales to nostalgia. [J2][J10]

| **January 30, 2024, v1.1.0** | PC receives additional experience/gil, encounter, font, music and bestiary options. Not all were present in the 2021 review builds. [J8] |

No reliable individual FFIII Pixel Remaster sales split was established. Dividing a combined series figure by six would manufacture precision rather than supply evidence.

**Sharing hypothesis:** unusual party compositions, a remembered difficult boss, recognizable job costumes and the floating-world reveal are compact subjects for discussion. Such stories can sustain interest without a service-driven retention schedule. No specific influencer campaign, conversion rate or viral origin is claimed without evidence.

## 10. Transferable patterns and failure modes — R13

**Switching roles is only meaningful when roles change useful decisions.** Dependency: distinct verbs and comprehensible situations. Failure mode: cosmetic differences or a single dominant final package make the apparent variety superficial.

**An access condition can connect exploration to party composition.** Dependency: preparation is available before the restriction traps the player. Failure mode: repeating the same transformation puzzle turns discovery into forced equipment administration.

**Information can be a combat contribution.** Dependency: uncertainty is consequential and the discovered fact can be acted upon. Failure mode: a scanner is mandatory for one encounter and has no worthwhile role thereafter.

**A later vehicle need not replace an earlier one.** Dependency: their abilities remain distinct and switching is understandable. Failure mode: retaining old transports creates logistical friction without meaningful access differences.

**A free menu change can still contain hidden long-term costs.** Dependency: separate character growth, job practice, HP history and current resources. Failure mode: players interpret reversible job selection as a guarantee that every consequence is reversible.

**Reduced retry cost can preserve challenge while encouraging experiments.** Dependency: distinguish difficult decisions from repeated travel or lost progress. Failure mode: defending every inconvenience as necessary difficulty prevents selective improvement.

**A recognizable visual role benefits from an equally recognizable verb.** Dependency: costume, action and feedback reinforce one another. Failure mode: beautiful differentiation masks mechanically redundant roles, or numerical changes erase the role fantasy.

These are research interpretations, not requests to copy protected characters, assets, dialogue, names or exact formulas into another game.
