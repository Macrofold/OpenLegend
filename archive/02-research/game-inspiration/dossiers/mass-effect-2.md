## The distinctive structure

Mass Effect 2 turns preparation for a dangerous expedition into most of the campaign, rather than treating recruitment as a brief opening act. Shepard investigates disappearing human colonies while working with the controversial Cerberus organization, assembling specialists aboard the Normandy SR-2. Recruitment, personal missions and equipment investment feed the final assault. This is a single-player authored action RPG, not a cooperative campaign or a freely simulated galactic government. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

**Interpretation:** the structurally interesting question is not merely whether the player likes a character. It is whether the player has learned what that person can do, resolved the distractions that affect their commitment, and assigned them a suitable responsibility. A friend, a qualified specialist and a well-equipped expedition are different things.

## Combat and capability relationships

Enemies can possess health, armor, shields or barriers. The colored defense layers are practical information: many disabling effects cannot operate normally until protective layers are removed. A fight can therefore have a preparatory phase in which the squad makes a target vulnerable, followed by a control or damage phase. Difficulty changes how commonly protections appear. Shepard and squadmates regenerate health and shielding after avoiding damage, but a temporary shield restoration is not the same as restoring lost health. [S6](#s6)

Overload is particularly useful against shields and synthetic opponents. Its additional ranks can interfere with weapons or disable vulnerable synthetics; its maximum-rank choice trades concentrated effect against wider coverage. A visible shield is consequently a reason to consider a technical specialist rather than only a bigger gun. This is the second game's power behavior; the broader chaining and combo interactions of Mass Effect 3 must not be backported. [S7](#s7)

Warp addresses armor and biotic barriers, inhibits health regeneration and can detonate a target already suspended by an appropriate biotic effect such as Pull or Singularity. The interaction requires a compatible target state, not simply casting any two powers consecutively. **Interpretation:** a second person's contribution can change what the first person's power accomplishes, rather than merely doubling independent damage. [S8](#s8)

Cover, aimed shooting, limited thermal clips, weapon switching, powers and separate squad orders produce a more direct action vocabulary than the original. The RPG tradeoff is real: fewer equipment comparisons and a smaller set of individually consequential power upgrades replace much of the earlier item churn. Mission rewards, rather than individual enemy kills, supply experience; Destructoid's reviewer specifically missed that immediate per-kill feedback. [S2](#s2)

## Loyalty is not a universal success flag

A successful personal mission makes a companion loyal, unlocks a power and offers an alternate appearance. Some missions can be completed without gaining loyalty; later conflicts can remove it. The acquired power and appearance need not disappear when loyalty is subsequently lost. Advanced Training can teach Shepard one unlocked bonus power at a time. The distinction among **mission finished**, **ability unlocked**, **relationship currently secure** and **qualified for this task** matters. [S9](#s9)

The approach to the Collector Base checks major Normandy preparations: armor, shielding and the Thanix cannon. Missing these upgrades can kill team members before the player reaches the main ground operation. The final mission then evaluates assigned specialists and leadership, while a later defensive stand depends on the group left behind. A loyal technician is not thereby the strongest biotic, and taking all the strongest defenders with Shepard can leave the rest exposed. [S10](#s10)

The guide evidence requires careful handling. Some simplified trophy guides imply that loyalty guarantees survival; others confuse the combat squad with the abducted support crew. Neither simplification should replace the separate checks. The final dossier will retain the distinction without presenting a brittle all-purpose walkthrough or silently importing Mass Effect 3's war assets into this game's own progression.

## Five written reviews — preliminary synthesis

**Kevin VanOrd, GameSpot, January 26, 2010.** The review values distinctive companions, visual direction and more reliable action, while finding the main plot safer and more predictable than the personal stories. It also identifies scanning fatigue and occasional movement bugs. The analysis is useful precisely because the compelling whole and its uneven activities coexist. [S1](#s1)

**Brad Nicholson, Destructoid, January 26, 2010.** Nicholson praises faster navigation, expressive scenes and sharper combat. His qualifications include rigid movement, weak enemy hit feedback and the loss of experience from individual kills. The maximum score should not be mistaken for an absence of criticism. [S2](#s2)

**Ashton Liu, RPGFan, January 31, 2010, Xbox 360.** Liu appreciates tactical weapon and companion variety, but explicitly recognizes that streamlining can feel like oversimplification to the original game's audience. He finds planet scanning repetitive and artificially lengthening. His observation of an assignment involving surveillance is particularly useful alongside another critic's less favorable reaction to noncombat sequences. [S3](#s3)

**GamesRadar, original-release review.** The surviving article values imported decisions and companion-specific stories that make selecting only two teammates emotionally difficult. It criticizes mining, intrusive helmet presentation and equipment management reduced too far. Its examples demonstrate why a synopsis of the Collector threat would miss much of the campaign's actual appeal. Byline/date verification remains pending; the article's substantive body, not only its verdict bullets, was inspected. [S4](#s4)

**Gus Mastrapa, Wired, January 26, 2010.** Mastrapa describes deliberately declining a compassionate interrupt as a memorable role-playing act. He applauds the reduced administrative burden but warns that players who enjoy traditional RPG management may feel underserved. His reported inaction is attributed testimony, not a play session performed for this dossier. [S5](#s5)

## Commercial and Steam evidence already established

EA's January 29, 2010 announcement says **more than two million units sold-in** during the first release week. That means distribution to the retail channel, not a verified count of two million consumers or completed playthroughs. The release also claims launch-day Twitter and Google News prominence; those are publisher-attributed marketing observations rather than independently reconstructed rankings. [S11](#s11)

Steam review attempts for apps **2362420** and **24980**, using top-rated review URLs, both returned internal errors in the web reader. Search did not yield a usable ranked review sample. These failures are recorded, not converted into a claim that reviews do not exist. Direct-player alternatives and the distinction between the original Steam listing and later bundled SKU remain to be finalized.
