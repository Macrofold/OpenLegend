# G102 — Mass Effect 2

[Research roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Mass Effect](mass-effect.md) · [Library](../README.md)

**Research date:** September 26, 2026. **Pass:** R01–R14 complete with the access limitations below. Documentary research; no gameplay, benchmark or video-viewing claim. **Spoilers:** recruitment, loyalty, Tali's trial, the Reaper IFF and final-mission sections disclose consequential rules. The opening premise itself reveals the first mission's outcome.

**Central finding — interpretation:** preparation is the campaign, not administrative work before the campaign. Recruiting a person, understanding their specialty, helping with a personal problem, funding an expedition and assigning responsibility all contribute to a final operation. The corresponding warning is that friendship, qualification, equipment and group capacity are different variables: reducing them to one “loyalty” score misrepresents both the game and its strongest lesson.

## 1. Identity, editions and the player promise

BioWare and EA released Mass Effect 2 for Windows and Xbox 360 on January 26, 2010 in North America and January 29 in Europe. The PlayStation 3 version followed in January 2011. Legendary Edition includes a revised version of this campaign in its May 2021 trilogy remaster. The current Steam listing labeled **Mass Effect 2 (2010) Edition** packages substantial DLC; its presentation should not be mistaken for the contents of every original retail disc. [S12](#s12) [S13](#s13) [S14](#s14)

This is a **single-player third-person action RPG**. Shepard directly controls movement, aim and conversation; two AI squadmates accompany most field missions. There is no native co-op campaign, player trading, settlement building or independently playable squadmate campaign. The Normandy SR-2 is a persistent home and mission-selection hub rather than a freely piloted combat spacecraft. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

The opening destroys the original Normandy and reconstructs Shepard through Cerberus. This creates a fictional reason to reconsider appearance and class when importing an earlier character, while keeping selected history. A newcomer can create a Shepard; the interactive Genesis recap available in relevant packages supplies selected prior decisions, not every possible original-game flag. Neither route imports the first game's entire equipment and talent system unchanged. [S13](#s13) [S15](#s15)

First-session play establishes immediate survival, new colleagues and a colony investigation. Established play alternates between recruitment, personal missions, optional assignments, conversations, scanning, shopping and research. The Collectors' abduction of human colonies supplies urgency, but most ordinary expedition time is not governed by a continuously advancing simulated invasion. Specific mission-state triggers later create genuine time pressure; those should not be confused with a universal calendar. [S1](#s1) [S23](#s23)

## 2. What the player can do

### Classes and characteristic powers

The six familiar class names now express more sharply differentiated action styles. **Soldier** uses Adrenaline Rush to improve a shooting opportunity. **Adept** controls space with biotics such as Singularity. **Engineer** deploys a combat drone. **Infiltrator** uses Tactical Cloak. **Vanguard** uses Charge to close distance aggressively. **Sentinel** combines disciplines with Tech Armor. These are Mass Effect 2 capabilities; they must not be backdated to the original game or supplemented with Mass Effect 3's later movement and combo rules. [S16](#s16) [S17](#s17)

Leveling supplies points for a relatively compact power set. Investment is increasingly costly across ranks, and maximum-rank evolutions offer alternatives such as stronger individual effects versus wider coverage. Choosing a small number of developed powers can be more consequential than spreading points thinly. A signature class power, an ammunition power and a learned bonus power are different types of access, not an unrestricted spellbook. [S3](#s3) [S7](#s7) [S9](#s9)

| Activity | Decisions and prerequisites | Costs, feedback and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shooting and cover | Select an available weapon, aim, reload, move between firing positions and use melee when appropriate. | Thermal clips and heavy-weapon ammunition constrain sustained fire. Cover does not guarantee safety from flanking or aggressive enemies. |
| Squad command | Choose two companions; position them, select targets and trigger available powers. | A member's class, current cooldown, incapacitation and line of attack affect what can happen now. |
| Technical minigames | Interact with eligible terminals and containers through the game's hacking or bypass interface. | These are player-facing interaction challenges, not the original game's universal party Decryption/Electronics rank checks. |
| Conversation | Select a stance, investigate optional topics and sometimes accept or decline a timed interrupt. | The short label does not expose every consequence. Inaction during an interrupt is itself a choice; not every scene offers one. |
| Expedition and investigation | Select destinations, inspect hubs, scan planets and pursue detected anomalies or mission leads. | Most spaces are authored routes. The galaxy map is not permission to land everywhere or simulate arbitrary civilian occupations. |
| Research and commerce | Find or buy upgrades, gather required resources, use the Normandy laboratory and configure equipment. | Research can require a prior discovery or upgrade tier. Fictional credits and minerals are separate from paid DLC access. |

This table combines the manual, reviews and upgrade references; it is an inventory of supported actions, not a promise that every location accepts every action. [S2](#s2) [S16](#s16) [S20](#s20)

### Defense layers make target state legible

Health, armor, shields and barriers are distinct combat states, displayed with different colors. Many control effects do not work normally through protective layers. A plan can therefore require stripping a defense before lifting, freezing or hacking the target. Difficulty affects how commonly enemies possess protection; a tactic that feels effortless on one setting may have an additional prerequisite on another. Health and shielding can recover after avoiding damage, but special powers and enemy types complicate a universal regeneration rule. [S6](#s6)

Overload is particularly valuable against shields and synthetics. Its higher ranks add interference effects; its final evolution can favor concentration or coverage. Warp addresses armor and biotic barriers and can detonate a target suspended by a compatible biotic effect. These are not interchangeable forms of generic damage. In particular, the second game's biotic interactions should not be redescribed as the broader fire, cryo and tech combination vocabulary of the third game. [S7](#s7) [S8](#s8)

**Interpretation:** the interesting cooperative unit is often a sequence of state changes. One person's action makes another person's ability appropriate. This differs from a party in which everyone simply contributes independent damage to the same health bar.

## 3. Items, research and the economy

Weapon families include heavy pistols, submachine guns, shotguns, assault rifles, sniper rifles and heavy weapons. Class restrictions and later training choices make availability part of the build. Ammunition powers replace much of the original game's ammunition-modification inventory. Shepard configures armor pieces and their bonuses, while companion appearance changes are not a full return to managing each squadmate's individual armor inventory. [S6](#s6) [S13](#s13)

Named weapons such as the Carnifex pistol, Mantis or Widow sniper rifle, Mattock rifle and Collector-ship alternatives distinguish equipment by handling and role rather than only a continuously rising tier number. A Soldier guide's preferred loadout is one player's recommendation, not proof that its combination is optimal for every difficulty or class. The crucial comparison is whether the weapon supports the intended range, defense matchup and ammunition budget. [S17](#s17) [S21](#s21)

Recruiting Mordin enables the Normandy's research laboratory. Discovered plans and purchased upgrades are not identical: some purchases apply immediately, while research projects consume minerals and can require earlier tiers. Iridium, palladium, platinum and element zero support different upgrade families. Probes extract resources from scanned planets, and deposits deplete. Fuel and probe expenditure makes searching an activity with costs rather than an unlimited free menu action. [S18](#s18) [S19](#s19) [S20](#s20)

This is **research-based upgrading**, not open-ended crafting from arbitrary objects. There is no deep production economy of mines, labor, workshops and autonomous trade routes. Shops, mission rewards, recovered credits and research exist chiefly to support the expedition. A purchase can improve a personal combat role or contribute to ship preparation; those are different benefits from the same limited pool of time and resources. [S18](#s18) [S20](#s20)

Legendary Edition changes some acquisition paths: equipment associated with old promotional DLC can require an in-game purchase or research rather than simply appearing under the original entitlement rules. A modern bundled store package also changes what content a purchaser receives. The dossier therefore separates **owning an add-on**, **having its equipment available in the campaign**, and **paying the fictional cost to obtain it**. [S14](#s14) [S20](#s20)

**Design interpretation:** fewer, more recognizable items can reduce clerical comparisons while preserving build identity. The danger is overcorrection: players who valued extensive equipment management may perceive clarity as impoverishment. Likewise, making minerals relevant to survival does not automatically make the act of collecting them enjoyable.

## 4. Progression, loss and return loops

Experience is awarded through mission and activity completion rather than as a continuous reward for each enemy killed. Power allocation, weapon acquisition, research and recruited companions all expand capability, but at different rhythms. A mission can yield narrative progress without a new gun; a loyalty mission can alter final-operation readiness without expanding the protagonist's level cap. [S2](#s2) [S9](#s9)

Ordinary combat failure leads to a reload, and temporary squad incapacitation is distinct from story death. The final operation can permanently remove companions from the chosen save history. Under sufficiently poor conditions Shepard can also die; that ending is not equivalent to a continuing survivor career that can be imported into the next game. [S10](#s10) [S22](#s22)

Unlike the original game's normal post-finale structure, a surviving Shepard can continue undertaking remaining content after the main operation. New Game Plus is another route: it restarts the story while carrying selected character development and equipment, not every research upgrade, loyalty state or moral record. A same-game restart and an import into Mass Effect 3 are separate transformations of saved state. Exact bonus amounts and leveling details should be checked against the edition rather than copied between original and remastered releases. [S22](#s22)

The late campaign introduces **mission-sequence pressure**. Acquiring the Reaper IFF starts a progression toward the Normandy's abduction event. After the support crew is taken, spending additional missions elsewhere can cost lives. The exact pre-abduction allowance depends on mission state; a blanket “there are always exactly two missions” rule is unsafe. This is not a simulation in which every minute spent reading the codex advances a hidden galactic clock. [S23](#s23)

## 5. People, loyalty and social boundaries

The base game's recruitable team includes Miranda, Jacob, Garrus, Mordin, Grunt, Jack, Thane, Tali, Legion and Samara or her alternative Morinth. Kasumi and Zaeed are additional DLC companions. Naming both Samara and Morinth does not mean they occupy two simultaneously available positions in an ordinary playthrough. The Normandy also contains noncombat colleagues whose continuity matters, including Joker and the ship's AI, EDI. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3) [S9](#s9) [S14](#s14)

A successful personal mission generally secures loyalty, a bonus power and an alternate outfit. However, a mission can be completed without gaining loyalty, and a later confrontation can undermine a relationship. Losing loyalty does not necessarily revoke an already unlocked power or appearance. Advanced Training lets Shepard acquire one unlocked bonus power at a time. Consequently **content completed**, **ability unlocked** and **person presently committed** should not be collapsed into one flag. [S9](#s9)

Companions have authored histories, differing values and recurring conversation states. This is not evidence that they run unrestricted daily schedules, form arbitrary off-screen romances or solve their own missions without the player. The game creates individuality through performances, personal assignments, contrasting priorities and responses to selected decisions. Its character AI in combat can still make poor positioning decisions; narrative intelligence and tactical reliability are different qualities. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4)

Native romance routes are limited and gender-specific: male Shepard can pursue Miranda, Jack or Tali; female Shepard can pursue Jacob, Garrus or Thane. Imported first-game relationships provide continuity, with Liara receiving additional treatment in Lair of the Shadow Broker. Flirtation or a limited encounter is not automatically equivalent to a full companion romance arc. Mods and unused content do not establish native availability. There is no universal gift, marriage or family-management system. [S24](#s24)

Paragon and Renegade remain distinct accumulated approaches. The original second game does not retain Charm and Intimidate as separately trained talents; some persuasive options depend on how much available morality the player has earned, with relevant character bonuses. A visually impressive bar therefore need not guarantee every response. These mechanics can pressure players toward consistency even when a mixed response would better express their imagined Shepard. That last tension is our interpretation, not a claim of universal player behavior. [S25](#s25)

## 6. Story, places and preparation for the finale

Cerberus supplies Shepard's resurrection and resources while remaining an organization whose methods and agenda invite distrust. The Illusive Man's direction does not turn Shepard into the manager of Cerberus's entire economy. Colony disappearances, the Collectors and the approach to the Omega-4 relay provide the central operation; recruitment and personal stories supply much of the actual campaign's substance. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

The contrasting social settings matter. Omega supplies criminal and mercenary life; Illium connects corporate power with personal business; the quarian fleet makes Tali's problem institutional as well as familial. Returning characters and imported decisions can appear through major scenes, minor acknowledgments or messages. Those callbacks differ in scope: recognizing a past action is meaningful without implying a completely independent campaign branch. [S4](#s4)

**Finale spoilers.** The approach to the Collector Base checks important ship preparations, including upgraded armor, shielding and the Thanix cannon. Once on foot, the operation asks for specialists and leaders. A suitable technician, a credible fire-team leader and a sufficiently capable biotic are not interchangeable, even when all are loyal. Later, the group left to defend itself has a different survival problem from Shepard's immediate combat team. Loyalty is relevant but insufficient as a universal explanation. [S10](#s10)

**Interpretation:** the climax can feel earned because many earlier choices acquire a concrete use. Its fragility is the same mechanism in reverse: if a casualty appears to result from a hidden formula rather than an understandable assignment, players can experience it as a quiz or a guide-dependent trap. The strongest transferable pattern is legible preparation, not opaque multiplication of flags.

## 7. Six worked interactions

These are **constructed rules-based illustrations**, not observed play sessions. They assume the stated edition, trained powers and mission conditions. They illustrate dependencies and counterexamples, not a guaranteed optimal walkthrough.

### A. Make a target vulnerable before combining powers

**Intention:** stop a protected enemy before it overwhelms the squad. **Conditions:** a shielded target and available technical and biotic capabilities. **Actions:** remove its shield with an appropriate attack, suspend the now-vulnerable target with a compatible biotic effect, then use Warp. **Interaction:** the defensive state gates control, and the controlled state enables a further effect. **Result:** coordinated abilities accomplish more than unplanned casts. **Next decision:** spend another cooldown on the next opponent or reposition while powers recover. **Limit:** casting Pull into intact protection is not the same setup; any two powers are not automatically a valid combination. [S6](#s6) [S7](#s7) [S8](#s8)

### B. Turn a mineral deposit into expedition readiness

**Intention:** prepare the ship rather than maximize an arbitrary resource total. **Conditions:** a known research project, an available laboratory and insufficient required minerals. **Actions:** scan a promising planet, spend probes on meaningful deposits, return to research and fund the intended upgrade. **Interaction:** a traversal activity supplies an input to a preparation system. **Result:** a specific capability or protection, not merely a larger inventory number. **Next decision:** stop collecting or fund a different upgrade. **Limit:** exhausting every planet is not inherently required; the cost of unnecessary collection can exceed the value of more reserves. [S18](#s18) [S19](#s19) [S20](#s20)

### C. Win a trial without winning the person's trust

**Tali spoilers. Intention:** protect both Tali's legal standing and her family commitment. **Conditions:** the trial and evidence concerning her father's experiments. **Actions:** decide whether to present that evidence, accept exile without it, use an available persuasive response or mobilize an eligible supportive crowd. **Interaction:** legal exoneration and loyalty are separate outcomes. Earlier treatment of Veetor and survival of Kal'Reegar can contribute to the crowd route when its other conversation conditions are satisfied. **Result:** a technically successful defense can still betray Tali's wishes. **Next decision:** accept the consequence or seek an alternative route already made possible by previous actions. **Limit:** “mission complete” does not certify loyalty, and a helpful acquaintance is not sufficient without the route's prerequisites. [S26](#s26)

### D. Give a task to the right loyal person

**Finale spoilers. Intention:** get the team through a dangerous split operation. **Conditions:** appropriate ship upgrades, surviving companions and the relevant loyalty states. **Actions:** select a genuine technical specialist for the technical assignment and an appropriate leader for the other team; later consider who remains to defend the group. **Interaction:** qualification, commitment and remaining group composition all matter. **Result:** preparation is tested through responsibility, not just a final gunfight. **Next decision:** balance Shepard's preferred squad against the needs of people left behind. **Limit:** picking a favorite loyal character for every role is not safe, and taking all the strongest defenders can weaken the rear group. No single loyalty check guarantees all outcomes. [S10](#s10)

### E. Recognize when optional work has acquired a deadline

**Intention:** rescue the Normandy's abducted support crew. **Conditions:** the abduction event has occurred. **Actions:** compare immediate departure with doing another unrelated mission. **Interaction:** the story now evaluates mission delay in a way ordinary earlier exploration did not. **Result:** extra preparation after this point can carry a human cost. **Next decision:** proceed with the available team or accept that cost. **Limit:** this is not proof that every earlier conversation was timed; the important information is the transition into a different rule regime. [S23](#s23)

### F. A heist can vary activity without becoming a universal stealth simulation

**Kasumi spoilers. Intention:** enter Hock's protected vault. **Conditions:** access to the party and Kasumi's mission-specific assistance. **Actions:** investigate security, obtain the required identity information and choose a supported approach to the guarded space. **Interaction:** observation and prepared credentials precede the later combat, changing the rhythm of the mission. **Result:** a compact infiltration fantasy, followed by a personal decision concerning Keiji's stored memories. **Next decision:** weigh the companion's attachment against the information's implications. **Limit:** this is an authored set of solutions, not a simulation that validates any invented disguise or social engineering plan. [S27](#s27)

## 8. Art, audio, interface and feel

The defense colors and visible power effects communicate target state quickly enough for action combat. Cinematic close-ups and a compact dialogue wheel shift attention toward performance, while timed interrupts make a response feel situated in a moment rather than selected from a detached questionnaire. This can also create ambiguity: the player chooses a summary, not the exact line, and equipment such as a helmet can interfere with the intended intimacy. [S4](#s4) [S6](#s6)

In Wayne Santos's interview, Casey Hudson describes an art direction influenced by older science-fiction illustration and cinema, led by Derek Watts's team. Familiar forms combined with an unusual detail help objects and spaces remain readable. He also describes casting and writing as an iterative relationship rather than merely recording a finished script. The article's surviving text has transcription artifacts; its general production account is usable, but it is not quoted as a clean verbatim transcript. [S28](#s28)

RPGFan particularly values the character performances, including Mordin's delivery, and the integration of action with presentation. Other player testimony is less impressed by the dated faces or by the score in isolation. These are differences in experience, not contradictory frame-rate measurements. No formal accessibility evaluation, audio mix analysis or input-latency test was performed. [S3](#s3) [S31](#s31)

**Interpretation:** the interface makes a smaller set of powers feel more usable, but the presentation cannot fully conceal repeated enemy arenas or mechanical scanning. Strong cinematic framing and satisfying activity remain separate obligations.

## 9. DLC and edition-specific experience

**Zaeed and the Normandy crash site** extend different parts of the fantasy: another recruit and personal mission versus a return to the destroyed ship's history. Their distribution through the original Cerberus Network is not the same as buying all later paid expansions. **Firewalker** adds the Hammerhead hover vehicle and vehicle-focused missions, not the original Mako's entire planetary-exploration structure. [S14](#s14) [S32](#s32)

**Kasumi: Stolen Memory** mixes party infiltration, vault access and combat around Hock and Keiji's memories. It adds a companion and equipment, but the critic notes the compressed characterization compared with a full recruitment-plus-personal-mission arc. This is useful variety with a bounded amount of relationship development, not a new open-ended stealth career. [S27](#s27)

**Overlord** combines a Cerberus experiment, disturbing audiovisual framing, Hammerhead traversal and action sequences. Its ethical horror gives the institutional question behind Cerberus a personal focus. VanOrd finds the atmosphere and final revelation effective while noting that the vehicle is underused and technical roughness remains. Those criticisms concern the add-on's execution, not the absence of new weapons alone. [S33](#s33)

**Lair of the Shadow Broker** returns Liara to the center of an investigation involving Feron and Tela Vasir. It changes pacing through a skycar chase, a storm-battered exterior and character-focused scenes, then supplies an intelligence-center return loop and a continuation of a possible relationship. VanOrd praises the setting and emotional presentation while retaining reservations about stiff vehicle handling and allied combat behavior. A familiar relationship gains new context without pretending Liara has become a permanently selectable base-game squad member. [S34](#s34) [S24](#s24)

**Arrival** follows Shepard without a normal squad into a rescue involving Dr. Kenson and evidence of the Reapers' approach. Its bridge to the third game imposes consequential story movement more than it offers a range of outcomes. VanOrd's criticism is that removing squad banter and meaningful choice leaves the retained shooting insufficient; he still acknowledges atmospheric settings and some enjoyable action. This is an especially clear counterexample to treating the game's combat as an isolated explanation of its appeal. [S35](#s35)

Weapon, armor and appearance packs change loadouts or presentation rather than adding equivalent story campaigns. The PS3 package and later Steam bundle include substantial material that original purchasers obtained separately. Legendary Edition further revises acquisition and usability, including increased ammunition availability in the second game and a trilogy-wide interface context. These changes must be labeled rather than silently used to describe a 2010 launch experience. [S14](#s14) [S15](#s15) [S20](#s20)

## 10. Production, distribution and sharing

Hudson's interview describes extensive work on feedback categories such as performance, visual loading and how optional content connects to the main experience. It supports a picture of deliberate revision from the original, not an inference that the team merely removed systems to reduce cost. Publicly visible behavior alone does not disclose the game's proprietary code or exact staffing budget. [S28](#s28)

BioWare's 2013 retrospective gives a particularly useful account of the finale. Cinematic lead Parrish Ley describes coordinating many possible character states and iterating through broken versions before the sequence worked. This is a developer's retrospective description of production complexity, not an independently counted number of branches. The same article recalls watching a player hesitate over Tali's feelings: an attributed account of audience engagement, not footage watched for this dossier. [S29](#s29)

EA promoted the game through a major sequel launch, character-focused positioning and the Cerberus Network. That network used a single-use code included with a new copy; other purchasers could buy access. It delivered news and selected downloadable material. It did **not** mean every future expansion would be free, and the surviving 2010 announcement is historical distribution evidence rather than verification that every old activation path works today. [S12](#s12) [S32](#s32)

**Marketing interpretation:** the game gives players compact stories to share: whom they trusted, which friend survived and what they refused to do. The finale makes differences between careers particularly legible. Developer recollections show interest in watching those responses, but this dossier does not establish a particular streamer's sales contribution, a viral coefficient or a measured attribution breakdown. [S29](#s29)

## 11. Commercial context

EA's January 29, 2010 press release reports **more than two million units sold-in during the first release week**. Sold-in means supply into the distribution channel; it is not an independently verified count of consumer purchases, unique players or completed campaigns. The same release claims prominent launch-day Twitter and Google News activity. These remain attributed publisher claims, not independently reconstructed rankings. [S11](#s11)

The business model combined a premium game, selected entitlement-gated bonus content, paid expansions and later bundles/remasters. Fictional resource collection is separate from those commercial entitlements. No title-level development budget, net profit, retention curve or current active-player total was established here. A review count or the revenue of a trilogy collection cannot fill those gaps. [S14](#s14) [S32](#s32)

## 13. Transferable research hypotheses and limits

**Separate commitment from qualification.** A trusted person can still be wrong for a technical, diplomatic or leadership role. A useful experiment would make both the relationship and the relevant capability legible before assignment. The failure mode is a hidden aptitude quiz whose answer is available only from a guide.

**Let preparation become part of the story.** A final crisis can give earlier personal assistance, research and recruitment a visible use. This requires causal clarity: players should understand how the preparation helped. Copying a long checklist without emotional or mechanical relevance would reproduce the administrative burden rather than its payoff.

**Keep multiple outcomes separate.** Tali's trial distinguishes legal success from respect for a person's wishes. Comparable situations could distinguish public reputation, private trust and material safety. A single global approval meter would obscure the interesting conflict; unlimited invisible state would make it unreadable.

**Communicate changes in the time regime.** Optional exploration and urgent rescue can coexist, but the transition must be understandable. A player should not need to infer a new deadline solely from an unexplained casualty. Alternatives include explicit briefings, observable deterioration or a clearly marked commitment point.

**Prefer meaningful equipment decisions to constant replacements.** A small, recognizable arsenal may support planning better than frequent marginal upgrades. However, there must still be enough role differentiation for choosing a tool to matter. The opposite failure is a perfectly tidy inventory with no expressive decisions.

**Treat a companion as more than an ability slot.** Personal missions, recurring conversation and constrained responsibilities can reinforce one another. They depend on reliable presentation and enough individual attention; expanding the roster without supporting distinct relationships risks diluting the very attachment the structure seeks to create.

These are research interpretations, not accepted OpenLegend requirements or authorization to change code. BioWare's characters, terminology and authored events remain reference examples, not proposed assets.
