# Applying the Phantom Blade Zero study to Open Legend

**Research interpretation, 31 October 2026.** These are ways to use the evidence within existing Open Legend work. They are not new accepted mechanics, a paid campaign, a feature-delivery claim or a replacement roadmap. The [gameplay priorities](../../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) continue to select the complete survival-adventure experience; [ND26](../../../../../docs/maintainers/needs-design.md#nd26--a-bounded-commercial-test-and-repeatable-product-demonstration) owns the existing demonstration and validation scope.

## 1. Make the distinctive promise recognizable, then prove it

The [campaign study](04-marketing-business.md) connects an immediately legible action fantasy with repeated demonstrations and independent hands-on access. The transferable idea is to reduce uncertainty about a desirable experience. The research does not establish a universal trailer length, advertising budget or number of events that produces sales.

For Open Legend, choose an ordinary player's wanted activity and show the meaningful difference its systems make. The existing opening already requires useful making, a defensible home, exploration, opposition, practical invention and people with their own aims. One coherent sequence can establish intention, an attempted approach, an actual response and a consequence worth continuing. It need not force every capability into the same scene.

A short clip should earn curiosity. A longer sequence should establish how the result was reached, including ordinary movement, interface use, waits, failure and recovery. A playable session should establish that someone else can produce meaningful outcomes under the same rules. These forms answer different questions. A polished montage cannot substitute for all of them.

**Existing owners:** [Showcasing the game](../../../../../archive/06-marketing/showcasing-the-game.md), [marketing experiments](../../../../../archive/06-marketing/ideas-channels-and-experiments.md), and [FPV05 audience materials](../../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-validation.md#fpv05--audience-materials-and-recruitment).

**Useful observation:** ask a newcomer what they expect to do after seeing the material, then compare that answer with what they actually try and enjoy. A different outcome is acceptable when the advertised freedom is real; a completely different kind of game or hidden prerequisite is an expectation problem.

## 2. Separate spectacle, control and genuine consequence

The [reception chapter](02-reception.md) records disagreement about how cinematic action feels to the player. The lesson is not that elaborate animation is inherently shallow, or that difficult input is inherently deep. Presentation and decision-making can reinforce each other, but each needs evidence.

Open Legend's relevant question is whether an input produces a recognizable, responsive action whose result follows from the situation. A player should be able to explain what they attempted, why it succeeded or failed, and what they might try next. Satisfying feedback matters, including ordinary actions used repeatedly. It should support the player's choice rather than hide a canned result.

This fits the existing [ND38 execution design](../../../../../docs/maintainers/needs-design.md#nd38--player-skill-during-action-execution). That owner already separates ordinary attacks, optional timed input, character competence and authoritative consequences. This research supplies a contrasting experience to examine; it does not adopt Sha-Chi, automatic combinations, universal parries or a new timing minigame.

**Useful observation:** compare a novice's initial impression with a player who has learned a reliable technique. Can both explain the relationship between input, opponent behavior and consequence? Do they voluntarily keep using the interaction when its visual novelty is familiar? Distinguish an assistance preference from poor feedback or a broken rule.

## 3. Let rewards change a wanted activity

The [mechanics study](01-mechanics-world.md) illustrates equipment as a change in action vocabulary and access. Its broader interpretation is that a reward can be understood through what it enables, including something the player previously encountered.

For Open Legend, a useful tool, method, piece of knowledge or willing relationship can connect activities. An expedition might reveal an obstacle; a practical improvement can make a later attempt worthwhile; the resulting achievement can matter to a person or to the player's home. The interesting question is the resulting possibility, not whether every discovery awards another statistic.

The alternative matters too. An attractive place, performance, conversation or keepsake may be a satisfying reward without a combat advantage. Connecting systems should not turn every quiet activity into mandatory preparation or a completion checklist.

**Existing owner:** [Playable game first](../../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md), especially its complete activity strands and continuing-play questions. Existing inventory, world content and progression owners retain their authority; the dossier creates no parallel loot system.

**Useful observation:** after acquiring an improvement, can the player identify something they want to do with it? Follow that intended use through to its result. If the supposed reward only adds inventory management or a higher future requirement, its practical value has not been established.

## 4. Preserve recovery choices without importing another world's reset rules

The [failure and replay account](01-mechanics-world.md) makes recovery part of the game's overall pacing. Its tradeoffs are particularly useful because critics can value the same persistent progress differently.

Open Legend's continuing world has different requirements from a finite authored action campaign. A boss phase checkpoint or permanently cleared encounter is therefore an example to examine, not a universal rule to copy. What matters is what a setback costs, what is retained, how soon an interesting decision returns, and whether success is allowed to remain meaningful.

A failed hunt, lost opportunity or refused request can lead to an understandable next approach. Repetition becomes a concern when the player must reproduce already-mastered administration before reaching another worthwhile decision. Conversely, removing every consequence can erase the reason to prepare or care.

**Existing owners:** the selected world and [FPV07 danger and returning play](../../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-validation.md#fpv07--danger-unscripted-contact-and-returning-play).

**Useful observation:** record the complete time from failure to the next wanted action, including travel and recovery. Ask whether the player chose to continue and why. A satisfied stopping point is a valid outcome; the goal is worthwhile participation, not maximizing compulsory return.

## 5. Give lore a present use and let people remain particular

The [world and narrative account](01-mechanics-world.md) and [contrasting story reviews](02-reception.md) show why lore quantity and narrative engagement require separate judgments. Curiosity about a person or place can carry context; interruption, opaque terminology or a withheld conclusion can work against it.

Open Legend's opportunity is to let a person have a concrete want, a reason for acting and a relationship to something the player can encounter. A past event becomes useful when it changes interpretation or choice: who might help, why someone refuses, what a place means, or what an object makes possible. Background descriptions alone do not establish an independent person or a consequential history.

This is already compatible with [narrative guidance](../../../../../docs/narrative/README.md) and [characters and play](../../../../../docs/production-workflows/characters-and-play.md). The latter's production route connects intended character behavior with actual situations and consequences. The research does not introduce a prescribed martial-arts setting, new factions or an exhaustive lore encyclopedia.

**Useful observation:** ask what the player remembers about a person and why it matters to their next decision. Distinguish remembering a name from caring about an unresolved situation. Do not require a predetermined sympathetic reaction; dislike, mistrust, rivalry and indifference can all be informative.

## 6. Find the audience for the actual experience

The [business chapter](04-marketing-business.md) distinguishes measured purchase geography from inferred motives and documents several routes to discovery. Its evidence does not show that one generic action audience, one social platform or existing franchise followers alone explain sales.

Open Legend should preserve its existing separation between people who want the survival-adventure, people who want to author worlds, developers who want to contribute, and people attracted to supporting the idea. These interests can overlap, but a successful authoring demonstration is not direct evidence that people want to play the adventure or pay for the same offer.

Likewise, a large creator's reach is not automatically a useful audience. Coverage of comparable choices, exploration, systemic situations or character-driven stories may provide a more informative initial audience than unrelated popularity. This is a selection hypothesis, not an approved contact list or outreach action.

**Existing owners:** [candidate channels and measures](../../../../../archive/06-marketing/ideas-channels-and-experiments.md#candidate-channels), [FPV05](../../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-validation.md#fpv05--audience-materials-and-recruitment), and [ND26](../../../../../docs/maintainers/needs-design.md#nd26--a-bounded-commercial-test-and-repeatable-product-demonstration).

**Useful observation:** retain the source of an invitation or visit, the expectation it created, actual participation and voluntary further interest. Compare these steps rather than treating views, wishlists, signups, play, payment and renewal as interchangeable.

## 7. Use repeated evidence, without creating a promotion treadmill

A coherent update gives an interested person a new reason to reconsider: a playable improvement, a useful capability, a resolved friction or a richer situation. The campaign's [dated sequence](04-marketing-business.md) supplies examples; it does not prove that a small team should imitate its scale, celebrity agreements or event calendar.

For Open Legend, the economical version is to connect genuine development progress with representative material and feedback. Reuse existing capture and explanation work. Avoid making public spectacle a competing product that requires continually building unrepresentative scenes.

Paid distribution can increase exposure, but it cannot by itself establish whether the underlying activity is enjoyable, understandable or affordable to deliver. The [marketing experiment owner](../../../../../archive/06-marketing/ideas-channels-and-experiments.md#measures-that-connect-marketing-to-the-product) already separates these questions.

**Useful observation:** identify the specific uncertainty an update addresses, then check the response from relevant people. If multiple campaign changes happen together, preserve that ambiguity rather than assigning every later response to the favored change.

## 8. Keep the production lesson proportional

The [studio history](03-studio-production.md) shows accumulated experience, commercial financing, specialist production and substantial concentration of resources. Its most transferable idea is sustained coherence between a recognizable promise and the capabilities needed to deliver it.

Open Legend has its own strengths and limits. A browser-first, continuing world of useful invention and independent people need not compete on cinematic animation volume, celebrity casting or a large fixed campaign. Nor should “small scope” remove the very qualities that make it distinctive. The correct comparison is whether the selected playable experience is complete and credible at the available scale.

No new needs-design item is created by this dossier. ND26, ND38, gameplay priorities, narrative/production owners and FPV already cover the meaningful questions above. A later selected change can use the evidence in its own design; this research neither reopens completed design automatically nor marks outstanding player qualification complete.
