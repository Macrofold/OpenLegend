## Identity and edition boundary

The canonical subject is the 2003 Game Boy Advance title released internationally as **Fire Emblem**, known in Japan as **Rekka no Ken** and now officially identified as **The Blazing Blade**. It is the first internationally released Fire Emblem, not the first game in the series. Nintendo's June 16, 2023 announcement establishes the US Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack release on June 22; the Australian announcement uses June 23. These are regional dates, not conflicting original GBA release dates. [S01–S02]

The campaign follows Lyn, Eliwood and Hector. Its continuing characters and permanent combat losses differ from disposable armies built anew for each map. Native Link Arena competition must be distinguished from the single-player campaign; modern suspend points/rewind, where provided by an emulator, must not be described as an original Casual mode. Mechanics and rerelease verification remain underway.

## Five independent written reviews already read

The complete substantive bodies below were inspected, including both pages of GameSpy. This is not a count of aggregate snippets. Their assessment dates remain distinct from their archive captures or later republication.

- **GameSpot, Bethany Massimilla, November 10, 2003, 6:24 PM PST:** values characters, tactical accessibility and presentation, but criticizes item allocation and reduced freedom. Describes native suspend versus a permanent mid-battle save and finds the Link Arena less compelling than the campaign. Its casual claim that Marth and Roy are absent must not erase Roy's epilogue cameo or imply he is a regular playable lord. [C01]
- **GameSpy, Christian Nutt, December 3, 2003:** praises how Lyn's story teaches the game and how story, artwork and maps reinforce one another. Criticizes awkward shopping and replay costs after permanent losses. The review's speculation about releasing an earlier GBA entry and its incorrect association of that entry with both Roy and Marth are not production facts. Both archived pages were read. [C02]
- **Eurogamer, Tom Bramwell, July 7, 2004:** strongly values the integration of tactical play with authored narrative, particularly reunions with Lyn-era characters during Eliwood's story. Finds some difficulty spikes but considers replay useful learning. Calls the stripped-down multiplayer tedious. Its imprecise tutorial count and broad descriptions of withdrawal are not used as exact rule references. [C03]
- **Nintendo Life, Jowi Meli, originally 2015, updated/reissued June 23, 2023:** praises the tutorial's alignment of a new person, a new mechanic and a story event, while acknowledging early pacing costs and harshness relative to newer options. The mistaken reference to cavalier shields is not a claim about an equipable shield system. [C04]
- **RPGFan, Brian Mackenzie, February 3, 2023:** values maps, pixel art and supports but criticizes repetitive unlock playthroughs, opaque side-chapter requirements, support grinding, weak niches for some classes and story inconsistencies. Distinguishes optional side chapters from the preceding game's ending requirements. [C05]

**Early synthesis:** a tutorial can simultaneously build tactical understanding and affection for its cast. The conflicting assessments of its replay burden show why a strong first-play experience does not settle the design of repeat access. Likewise, permanent loss can make individuals matter without ensuring that players actually accept losses instead of restarting.

## Mechanical details already verified

The physical weapon triangle is Sword over Axe, Axe over Lance, Lance over Sword; the magical triangle is Anima over Light, Light over Dark, Dark over Anima. Attack speed subtracts weapon weight exceeding constitution, and a four-point attack-speed lead enables a follow-up. These are different checks from weapon advantage; exact damage and regional effectiveness boundaries still need completion. [M01]

Support activation requires eligible characters to spend turns adjacent, accumulating pair-specific points before a conversation becomes available. Detailed caps, range, affinities and paired outcomes are still being verified. [M02]

The specialist FAQ explicitly says a unit lost during Lyn's story returns with default later-campaign stats, while inventory does not transfer from Lyn's story. Consequently, a blanket statement that every defeat in every mode permanently deletes the person would be wrong. Lord promotions also depend on story and route; ordinary class-promotion advice should not be applied indiscriminately. [M03]
