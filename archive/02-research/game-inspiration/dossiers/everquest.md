# EverQuest — full research dossier

**G198 · October 28, 2026 — researched and independently reviewed.** Original PC _EverQuest_: Live and official progression servers, with historical eras identified. _EverQuest II_, _Next_, _Legends_ and independent emulator services have different contracts. No client, editor, server, account or payment was used; audiovisual observations below are attributed written evidence.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Packet provenance](../references/packet-provenance.md)

EverQuest's useful proposition is that a particular capability can make someone valuable to others: controlling danger, preparing supplies, finding a route or preserving a group through a mistake. The cost can be waiting, obscure instructions or dependence on absent people. OpenLegend should test which relationships produce interesting choices and remembered accomplishments; longevity alone does not justify copying every friction.

**Reading route:** §§1–4 establish rules and continuity; §5 works through decisions; §§6–8 connect setting, presentation and creation; §§9–10 separate commercial evidence from player experience; §11 routes the findings to existing design owners.

## 1. Identity and version boundaries

Launched in **1999**, EverQuest is a persistent, zoned fantasy MMORPG. Its setting, **Norrath**, includes cities, wilderness, dungeons and other planes. Character continuity coexists with resettable encounters and instances; the service contracts below determine which world a person actually enters. [E01](#e01) [R02](#r02)

**Shattering of Ro** went live December 2, 2025, raising the cap to **130** and adding planar quests, group missions, raids, collections and new abilities. The October 6, 2026 **Favors of Fortune** announcement concerns preorder/beta access, not a delivered replacement at this cutoff. The distinction matters when judging new promises against proven play. [S12](#s12) [P03](#p03)

## 2. Character identity, roles and progression

A class selects a substantial capability vocabulary. This compact map uses a publisher-hosted **2008 community guide** for enduring role identities, not its contemporary balance rankings. [S01](#s01)

| Class family                             | Practical contribution                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight          | Tanking; the latter two add different healing, control or attrition spells.                                       |
| Cleric, Druid, Shaman                    | Healing alongside distinct buffs, debuffs, nature/travel or offensive tools.                                      |
| Rogue, Monk, Berserker                   | Melee damage with different stealth, pulling, positioning and weapon demands.                                     |
| Ranger, Beastlord, Bard                  | Hybrid utility through ranged/nature abilities, a warder and spells, or songs.                                    |
| Wizard, Magician, Necromancer, Enchanter | Burst spells/portals; conjured pets/items; damage over time/undead tools; or control/charm/resource regeneration. |

A character's role can therefore include stopping an extra enemy from acting or preserving others' resources. Large damage numbers are only one expression of competence. [S01](#s01)

Race and deity also locate a person socially. Indexed extracts of the historical **Trilogy manual** explain faction as other groups' disposition, affected by identity and actions: an Innoruuk worshipper is unwelcome at a Qeynos paladin temple. Current starting choices should not be inferred from that example. [S14](#s14)

Levels unlock eligibility and capacity; practice improves relevant skills, while trainers supply another route. **Alternate Advancement** adds earned points spent on ranked general, archetype and class abilities. The publisher's 2008 redesign consolidated repeated lines and exposed prerequisites through purchase filters: specialization creates decisions, but navigating an expanding list is itself work. Exact old catch-up thresholds are not imported. [S09](#s09) [S13](#s13)

Current acquisition differs from old “buy every spell” guides. The **February 2026 update** added optional subscriber spell/combat-ability auto-grants at level-up, with a four-expansion lag and exclusions; eligibility depends on owned content on Live and unlocked era on progression servers. Knowledge of which tool to use remains separate from receiving it. [S05](#s05)

**Alternate Personas** let one named character develop different class/race/deity combinations. Inventory, bank, crafting and keys are shared; levels and spellbooks remain separate. Swapping requires suitable safe locations outside combat. Shared achievements and lockouts also prevent treating each persona as a fresh quest identity. [S06](#s06)

## 3. Actions, combat and cooperation

Move, select a target, start weapon attacks or activate a prepared ability, then respond to resources, position and other participants. Spellcasting requires a target/prepared spell; casting time, interruption, resistance and depleted mana can defeat the intended effect. The historical manual separates acquiring a spellbook entry from memorizing a usable gem; its old slot limit is not a current specification. [S14](#s14)

The **2008 Berserker guide** connects two-handed attacks and thrown axes to endurance-costing stun, snare and threat reduction; some abilities consume particular summoned axes. Damage and aid generate enemy attention, so an aggressive player can draw attacks away from the tank. [S08](#s08)

Pulling separates a manageable fight from nearby enemies; control and timing determine whether the group can sustain it. Group play joins complementary capabilities, while raids coordinate several groups around an encounter and an agreed reward policy. A publisher-hosted **2007 raid guide** assigns scouting, pulling, tanking, loot distribution and backup leadership, and explicitly treats an unexpectedly extended event as a cost imposed on everyone. That is valuable historical coordination advice; its authoritarian rhetoric is not an OpenLegend social model. [S18](#s18)

**Pets and mercenaries are different relationships.** Class abilities provide pets (§2). Mercenaries are hired NPC group members, taking a slot and XP share, following broad stance/assist instructions rather than direct control. Hire/upkeep and suspension shape their use; the 2010 guide excludes them from raids. Its two-type list is obsolete: later support includes melee and caster damage alongside tank/healer roles. The May 2022 update simplified ranks to Apprentice/Journeyman; June 2026 notes confirm both damage types remain supported. [S15](#s15) [S16](#s16)

The positive opportunity is continuity when a human partner is absent. The limit for OpenLegend is equally significant: a paid, obedient support NPC is not evidence that a freely motivated inhabitant will choose, refuse or remember helping. CP10/CE05 must evaluate that separately.

## 4. Items, crafting, markets, time and loss

Possessions serve as equipment, consumables, ingredients, collectibles or display. The Persona FAQ makes custody concrete: changing class can return inapplicable equipment to bags, while a shared Lore item does not grant another copy. [S06](#s06)

**Crafting** combines ingredients in an appropriate container/workstation. Recipes can be learned or discovered; missing components, skill and failure affect the attempt. Smithing, tailoring, pottery, baking, brewing, fletching and jewelcraft serve different outputs; research produces spells, while alchemy, poison-making and tinkering have class/race constraints. The 2007 primary guide distinguishes a recipe becoming trivial for skill gain from guaranteed success, and warns that vendor resale may not recover inputs. Fishing/gathered or looted resources, intermediates and player demand make the chain broader than a combine button. Its old skill caps are not current limits. [S04](#s04)

**Advanced Loot**, introduced in 2015, separates personal collection from group allocation. A master looter can assign an item, permit a first-claim grab or use need/greed rolls; filters remember recurring choices. An item can be visible or awarded yet still locked until the recipient reaches/unlocks its corpse. Interface visibility, entitlement and physical collection are distinct states. [S20](#s20)

Do not assume one trade rule across servers. **Lethar's 2026 rules** add personal rolls for qualifying grouped players, bind tradable equipment when equipped, remove class/race/deity equipment restrictions and treat specified No Drop categories as Heirloom. Unclaimed personal drops expire rather than becoming public loot. These are named-server rules; they do not redefine every Live item. [S21](#s21)

The **Bazaar** supplies searchable player trade. August 2026 opened it before Luclin on progression servers; September's letter also records stat/expansion filters and selected Hero's Journey quest drops made available to the whole group. Those are targeted reductions in transaction and cooperation friction, not evidence that all scarcity or competition disappeared. [S07](#s07)

Augments fit compatible item slots; removal requires the appropriate distiller. Modern death retains possessions, while a corpse supports recovery of lost XP through resurrection. Historical equipment-recovery stories therefore describe a different penalty contract. [E01](#e01)

Travel remains a capability and knowledge problem. The **2007 teleportation guide** connects bind/Gate return points, Druid circles, Wizard portals, Plane of Knowledge books and other networks. Landing points can expose a traveler to hostile factions or creatures. Current content still grants travel tools through achievement: February 2026 added Ruined Relic teleports unlocked by a Partisan achievement. [S10](#s10) [S05](#s05)

Time is consumed by preparation, movement, recovery, finding companions and repeated attempts, as well as combat itself. Raids/missions and their lockouts are bounded commitments; the stored character continues beyond them (§1). The **Shattering of Ro** launch explicitly cleared stored corpse XP when raising the cap, illustrating why “persistent” cannot mean every recoverable state lasts forever. [S12](#s12)

### What payment changes

**Free play** includes expansion content through _Laurion's Song_ following the June 17, 2026 unlock. That does not grant every original edition's promotional item or all later expansions. [E05](#e05) **All Access** unlocks broader AA progression, full spell ranks and mercenary tiers, additional character slots, fuller market/guild facilities and progression-server access. The membership page lists optional Perks as an additional charge, not included benefits. Payment therefore changes usable character and organizational capability. [E03](#e03)

**Krono** converts purchased membership time into a tradeable asset: a cash purchaser can sell it for existing player currency/items; an earner can buy it and redeem membership time. The sale transfers existing wealth rather than creating platinum. Published rules prohibit player cashout. The March 30, 2026 FAQ says redemption grants All Access time only when All Access is already active; otherwise it grants time in the consuming game. It also says a traded/listed Krono becomes tied to that game and warns of special-server restrictions. This dossier follows that dated FAQ where older support wording is broader. [E02](#e02)

Purchasable **level 115 Heroic Characters** include gear, spells and AAs, subject to the _Shattering of Ro_ unlock. The anniversary's free subscriber claim ended in April. Separate **Heroic Experience Draughts**, released May 2026, grant a specified level without that equipment/spell/AA package, with availability tied to server progression. These remove preparation time; they do not teach coordination or class judgment. [E09](#e09) Optional Perks alter play too: the Fangbreaker FAQ explicitly permits Challenger's protection against losing a level. It is not blanket immunity from death's consequences. [E06](#e06)

## 5. Six worked situations

These are **constructed situations** derived from the cited rules, not claimed playtests. Each isolates a choice worth testing rather than recommending its entire historical implementation.

1. **A damage dealer saves the attempt by doing less damage.** In the 2008 Berserker rule context, a fleeing enemy risks reaching allies while the tank is under pressure. The player spends a prepared axe/endurance on snare or stun, then reduces threat instead of maximizing attacks. If control holds, the healer gains time and the group avoids an extra fight; if resources are missing, retreat becomes attractive. The next preparation includes supplies and an agreed control role. [S08](#s08)

2. **A newcomer chooses a useful errand.** In the documented Crescent Reach opening, hailing Emissary Tinvinn connects a player to trainers and local quest givers. The player uses Find and reward preview rather than accepting every task blindly; a non-mana class can reject an irrelevant regeneration reward. A completed useful errand teaches both navigation and self-directed selection. The next question becomes which capability or place to pursue, rather than which marker must be cleared. [S09](#s09)

3. **A craft attempt changes the plan.** An aspiring supplier gathers ingredients, prepares intermediate goods and attempts a recipe. Failure consumes resources, while practice can still improve skill; success does not guarantee profitable resale. The player compares another attempt with buying the missing product or finding someone who wants it. The interesting decision is the purpose and recipient of the work; repeated failure alone does not establish enjoyable mastery. [S04](#s04)

4. **A shortcut has an arrival problem.** A traveler asks a Druid for a route to a distant goal. In the historical teleport framework, the port is available but nearby guards may dislike that traveler. Choosing a safer approach, protection or another destination changes the plan. Arrival succeeds only if the character can continue from there; the next choice is whether to explore, establish a return point or withdraw. Useful travel knowledge includes what happens after transport. [S10](#s10)

5. **An awarded object is not yet collected.** A group assigns a drop to someone who was out of range. The recipient sees the reward but its lock prevents collection; reaching and unlocking the corpse resolves the problem. The group must decide whether to wait, escort them or proceed. The system can make the reason and remedy legible without eliminating negotiation. Persistent loot filters also need an understandable way to undo an accidental refusal. [S20](#s20)

6. **A familiar identity learns another role.** A player creates a different Persona to fill a missing capability. Shared resources reduce repetition, but class-specific advancement and safe-location swapping prevent an instant mid-fight replacement. Existing achievement/lockout state also limits replay rewards. The next decision is to practice the new role, recruit another participant or change the planned activity. Continuity helps returning players only if it preserves a comprehensible route to competence. [S06](#s06)

## 6. Connected history, cultures and motives — spoilers

The publisher's **2007 creation account** begins with the Nameless and elemental gods shaping existence. **Veeshan** claims Norrath and places dragons in Velious; **Brell Serilis** secretly seeds the depths, then brokers a pact to restrain dragon dominance. Dwarves, Tunare's elves and Prexus's aquatic Kedge embody competing divine claims. Excluded **Innoruuk** turns kidnapped elven rulers into ancestors of the dark elves as revenge. **Rallos Zek's** creations build an empire and invade the Plane of Earth; divine retaliation destroys that power and scatters its peoples. Solusek Ro's heat later displaces elves, while human settlement produces Qeynos and Freeport. These conflicts make displacement, divine allegiance and unfamiliar cities intelligible. They are authored history, not a continuously simulated creation myth. [S03](#s03)

That history reaches the player through faction reception (§2), routes (§4) and individual aspirations. Berserker **Keras**, exiled after killing a sparring partner, seeks control; his epic quest makes disciplined fury an achievement. [S08](#s08) Crescent Reach's Drakkin heritage leads to a breath-weapon quest rather than remaining only a selection label. [S09](#s09)

The delivered **Shattering of Ro** premise reconnects divine motives: an explosion kills **Druzzil Ro**; unconscious **Firiona Vie** is the survivor, and **Solusek Ro** blames mortals. **Innoruuk** and **Rallos Zek** join his assault on Tunare's Plane of Growth for their own advantage. **Mithaniel Marr** defends Tunare, while **Quellious** treats both sides and seeks an alternative to war. Damaged magic becomes hazardous storms as well as a story problem. This creates a question—what happened, whom to aid, and whether escalation is warranted—around the expansion's activities. The promotional premise does not prove every alignment produces a branching, lasting world outcome. [S11](#s11)

OpenLegend's transferable ambition is a world whose inhabitants' reasons connect history to present choices. A named motive should affect an offer, refusal, place or relationship that the player can encounter; a pantheon list alone does not accomplish that.

## 7. Presentation and interface

The historical manual documents first-person, overhead and rear camera views, including fixed/rotating options and pan/zoom. Changing viewpoint changes spatial awareness; this is a documented original interface, not a current screenshot or observed camera test. [S14](#s14)

Koh's 2007 review contrasts smooth, natural Drakkin movement with choppier older zones. Rivervale's buoyant music stood out, but sound quality varied between zones and footsteps did not change with terrain. These are attributed written observations, not footage inspected for this research. [R04](#r04)

Hotbars make practiced actions accessible: the publisher's 2008 guide documents dragging a memorized spell or social onto a button, activating it by mouse/shortcut, and saving, loading or deleting sets. A social can sequence supported commands; key conflicts are marked. Those mechanisms support personal workflow, but the guide's exact old bar counts are historical. [S19](#s19)

Modern inventory filters (§4), safe loot handling and customizable windows (§8) show how legibility can reduce administrative effort while leaving the underlying decision intact. For OpenLegend, the useful test is whether a player can distinguish a failed action, unavailable ability, wrong location and inaccessible object, then act on that distinction without a required external explanation.

## 8. Native housing and external creation

The **2010 House of Thule guide** documents native housing; its old numerical limits are not assumed current. Buy a plot, place a house, then position, rotate or resize eligible possessions using allowed/blocked previews. Collision, wall and free modes support arrangements. The door leads to an interior. Permissions separate visiting, interacting, moving/removing items and administration. Decor includes useful workshops and trophies. Revise through the item window; crates pack possessions, and picking up a house preserves its interior arrangement. This is a documented author→visit→revise→remove path, not an executed test. Upkeep and transfer boundaries are in §1. [S02](#s02)

### Representative world-authoring lifecycle

The following is a documented path through **EQEmu/PEQ**, not a reproduced tutorial or an official Daybreak world SDK.

1. **Prepare and author.** The Windows installer documents server binaries, MariaDB, quest runtimes, ProjectEQ content/maps and the Spire administration tool. [M05](#m05) The project also supports Linux. [M04](#m04) The PEQ editor tutorial creates **Salutation_Model_XIV**, a greeter in Gloomingdeep: choose its model/attributes, assign a spawn group and position, then build a patrol grid. A separate Perl file supplies reactions to speech and waypoints, including an offered exit from the tutorial. The editor's data and the script are distinct deliverables. [M06](#m06)
2. **Preview and test.** Run the private server and connect the matching client. The tutorial documents refreshing NPC data, reloading quests and repopulating the zone, then observing the patrol and trying the greeter's dialogue. Recheck the offered teleport and client-visible appearance after changes; these are proposed verification steps based on that tutorial. A model missing from that client's zone can appear incorrectly. [M05](#m05) [M06](#m06)
3. **Understand dependencies and conflicts.** Quest lookup selects files by zone, instance version, NPC ID/name and fallback location. Lua takes precedence over Perl with the same name; global and local scripts may both execute. NPCs sharing an ID/name can also share script-variable context. Thus filename, scope and identity are behavioral dependencies, not merely packaging details. [M07](#m07)
4. **Package and share.** Scripts can be distributed through a repository. The documented database approach keeps custom SQL changes as repeatable, versioned patches over a known base. The backup tool distinguishes content from player, login and other state tables. A reviewable distribution therefore needs matching scripts, content changes, base/version requirements and any required client-side data; the latter sentence is an inference from those separate mechanisms, not an existing one-click pack standard. [M06](#m06) [M09](#m09) [M10](#m10)
5. **Recipient installation and play.** The play guide requires a compatible historical client build, the selected service's login configuration and any server-specific files such as spell data. Its supported-client list is not today's official Live download. The project does not supply Daybreak's proprietary client assets and notes acquisition constraints on several old builds. A recipient then connects to the chosen world; character continuity belongs to that service. [M08](#m08)
6. **Update and remove.** The version-patching example can rebuild a content database from its base and reapply changes. That does not establish automatic safe removal of a quest, item or NPC referenced by ongoing player state. Back up and inspect affected content/state, resolve conflicting IDs/scripts, and test the revised combination before making a published-service promise. This is a maintenance implication; no general migration guarantee was found, and none is imposed on OpenLegend's development saves. [M07](#m07) [M09](#m09) [M10](#m10)

**Permission and evidence limits.** EQEmu's repository carries GPLv3 code licensing; that does not grant rights to Daybreak assets or make every server's content interchangeable. [M11](#m11) Official support for UI customization is not blanket approval of gameplay automation or third-party servers. The practical lesson for OpenLegend is to make a creator's supported recipient path, dependencies and revision consequences understandable; it is not a decision to adopt EQEmu, its technical stack or a donation business model.

## 9. Production, marketing and commercial evidence

Darkpaw Games develops the current game; Daybreak publishes it within the EG7 group. [P04](#p04)

Brad McQuaid's 2012 interview traces the design to John Smedley's graphical online-world proposal and McQuaid/Steve Clover's experience with MUDs. UO strengthened their preference for PvE and prompted adding crafting. Development moved from software to hardware rendering when crowds threatened performance; model optimization and a reliable UDP framework addressed different technical constraints. McQuaid describes a relatively inexperienced team working through trial and error. These participant recollections explain choices and production problems, not the current engine's implementation or a controlled proof that difficulty caused retention. [P01](#p01)

In a 2015 interview, McQuaid recalls the **1998 GDC playable demonstration** attracting visitors who repeatedly returned to its roughly ten machines. He also describes launch problems involving many small network packets, router capacity and regional bandwidth. The demonstration is an actual exposure mechanism; his claim that the rocky launch did not materially hurt the game is retrospective judgment without cohort data in the interview. [P02](#p02)

Current promotion includes **October 6, 2026 Favors of Fortune preorders and beta access**, with weapon ornaments and a participation-based later beta reward. That is a purchase-and-participation offer, not evidence of conversion performance. It does not turn the beta expansion into delivered Live content. [P03](#p03)

**Outcome inference:** historical receipts and continued publisher-reported performance support durable demand, without identifying which individual friction or payment rule caused success. EverQuest's useful positive proposition is that learned roles and coordinated preparation can create achievement people remember together. For OpenLegend, test whether another participant's contribution opens interesting choices and becomes recognizable; retain independent inhabitants' motives and refusal. Persistent identity need not require a single global economy, mandatory raid schedules or copied subscription gates.

## 10. Reception and player disagreement

Five independent accounts explain different kinds of pleasure and friction. These are qualitative observations, not representative retention evidence.

| Account and play stage                                                                        | What appealed, what resisted, and actual behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Greg Kasavin, GameSpot; early original-game review, page dated May 1, 2000** [R01](#r01)    | Large, varied places and consequential race/class choices made exploration attractive. Grouping added tactics and companionship to otherwise slow combat and recovery. He also praised trades as an economic alternative. Weak instructions, long travel/loading, corpse recovery and repetitive early combat made the opening demanding. The payoff was acquiring abilities and equipment that changed what the character could do. This is a play-based assessment and recommendation, without a documented personal departure or later return.                                               |
| **GR Staff, GameRevolution; April 1, 1999** [R02](#r02)                                       | The firsthand monk narrative turns initiation into a concrete achievement: after an orc killed him, the reviewer practiced, overcame the required enemies, completed his order's collection task and earned a white sash. A new combat technique and recognized rank made progress tangible. He praised depth, visuals and playing with others, while criticizing confusing beginnings and lengthy loading. Wanting the next sash is an expressed future goal; the first sash and post-death continuation actually occurred in the account.                                                     |
| **Rick Fehrenbacher and Al Wildey, GamesFirst!; archived August 28, 1999 review** [R03](#r03) | The authors actually progressed into their teens, joined guilds, traveled and completed quests after launch troubles improved. Exploration, distinctive places and generous players sustained that play. They found spawn camping dull and low/mid-level crafting costly, failure-prone and awkward. They explicitly rejected poor documentation as a good way to create community: a rich world already supplied useful things to ask other people. Their crafting judgment disagrees with Kasavin's; neither establishes every profession's profitability or today's rules.                   |
| **Carolyn Koh, MMORPG.com; May 7, 2007 return/re-review** [R04](#r04)                         | Koh resumed after solving hardware-related trouble; the voiced tutorial helped her relearn an intimidating interface. She enjoyed small-group coordination, evolving spell roles, risky pulls and the chance to save a partner with one remaining heal. The review describes actual renewed play and earlier regular groups. Clunky quest tracking, uneven old/new presentation and sparse low-level companionship constrained the appeal. Her conclusion favors entering with friends; it does not demonstrate that newcomers reliably find them or that she subsequently stayed indefinitely. |
| **Andy Patrizio, Ars Technica; June 6, 2019 veteran retrospective** [R05](#r05)               | Patrizio still played regularly, valuing raid companionship with a smaller weekly schedule. He criticized empty older zones and cumbersome looting. Interviewees Mike and Michelle Shea had left around 2007 as raiding became dominant and did not want to return; affection survived departure. This combines firsthand continuation with attributed exit testimony, not average retention.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

**Helpful Steam contrasts.** The retrieved most-helpful surface displayed **BlueMichelle**, positive, October 24, 2014, **175.2 hours / 86 helpful**: an actual return after a ten-year interruption, enjoying familiar places, workable laptop performance and mercenary help, while finding empty starting areas and group-dependent quests frustrating. **Wereboar**, negative, December 1, 2025, **1,825.4 hours / 73 helpful**, valued atmosphere, distinct classes and completed collection goals, but found rare-spawn waiting and an inactive guild increasingly obstructive. Walking away was contemplated, not confirmed. Displayed hours are the retrieved totals, not publication-time hours. These selected accounts do not measure prevalence. [R06](#r06)

A separate recent Steam surface displayed **vpilot**, negative, **August 6 (year not shown), 47.4 hours, zero helpful**: a nostalgic return followed by trying several classes and uninstalling, citing perceived quest bugs and a bland shared opening. That is reported behavior; it does not verify a bug rate or every race's starting options. [R07](#r07)

**Interpretation for OpenLegend.** Preserve dependence that gives someone a useful contribution and a shared event worth remembering. Do not mistake unclear basic instructions, waiting for a spawn or an absent partner for that contribution. A small earned symbol can connect practice to belonging; later access still needs to work when friends leave or play schedules shrink. These accounts support testing that distinction, not importing EverQuest's class system, compulsory grouping or death penalties. Existing FPV06–08 and CP10 can observe willing participation, meaningful help and stopping reasons; CE05 can ask whether a particular earned object or remembered interaction changes later choice.

## 11. Application, critique and existing task routes

The research supports **recognizable usefulness, chosen commitments and earned belonging** as design hypotheses. It does not show that inconvenience creates attachment, that forced specialization is necessary, or that old communities reliably replenish themselves. Later conveniences are evidence of specific problems being addressed; they do not invalidate every earlier account or justify a new patch-driven design doctrine.

**Review decision:** no new design document or needs-design item is warranted. These owners already contain the relevant questions. Classes, skill rules, factions, loot, housing upkeep, travel and reset policies belong to particular worlds; the engine should provide necessary capabilities without prescribing EverQuest's contract globally. OpenLegend's accepted finite free and paid authoring remains compatible with these lessons: the research asks for a usable, transparent creator path, not unlimited free compute or a copied subscription economy.
