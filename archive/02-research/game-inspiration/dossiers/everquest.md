EverQuest's useful question for OpenLegend is how a person's particular capability can make them valuable to a group. That promise includes costs: coordination, comprehension and finding compatible company. This dossier will distinguish the enjoyable relationship from the waiting or dependency that can prevent someone reaching it, and will treat any proposed transfer as a world-specific design hypothesis.

The setting is **Norrath**, a world of competing peoples, gods, cities, wilderness and other planes. ## 2. Character identity, roles and progression

A class selects a substantial capability vocabulary; race, deity and starting choices further situate the character. A publisher-hosted **2008 community guide** illustrates the following role identities. Its contemporary damage rankings and race restrictions should not become an October 2026 balance guide. [S01](#s01)

| Class family                             | What it contributes                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight          | Taking and directing enemy pressure; the latter two combine tanking with different spell tools.                |
| Cleric, Druid, Shaman                    | Healing plus distinct buffs, debuffs, travel or offensive capabilities.                                        |
| Rogue, Monk, Berserker                   | Melee damage, with different scouting, pulling, control and weapon demands.                                    |
| Ranger, Beastlord, Bard                  | Hybrid utility through ranged/nature tools, a warder and spells, or songs that improve a group's capabilities. |
| Wizard, Magician, Necromancer, Enchanter | Burst spells/portals; conjured pets/items; attrition/undead tools; or control, charm and regeneration.         |

The distinction is practical: a useful contribution can be preventing an additional enemy from fighting, maintaining the group's resources, or creating a route out. It need not appear as the largest damage number. The detailed current progression, equipment and resource constraints remain under research. [S01](#s01)

## 6. Connected history, cultures and motives — spoilers

The publisher's **2007 creation account** begins with the Nameless and elemental gods shaping existence. **Veeshan** claims Norrath and places dragons in Velious; **Brell Serilis** secretly seeds the depths, then brokers a pact to restrain dragon dominance. Dwarves, Tunare's elves and Prexus's aquatic Kedge embody competing divine claims. Excluded **Innoruuk** turns kidnapped elven rulers into the ancestors of the dark elves as revenge. **Rallos Zek's** creations expand into an empire and invade the Plane of Earth; divine retaliation destroys that power and scatters its peoples. Later, Solusek Ro's heat drives elves from their forest, while human settlement produces Qeynos and Freeport. These motives explain conflict, displacement and culturally distinct places; they are an authored historical framework, not proof that the server continuously simulates its creation myth. ## 8. Native housing and external creation

The **2010 House of Thule guide** documents the native housing foundation; its old numerical limits are not assumed current. Buy a plot, place a purchased house, then select eligible inventory items for positioning, rotation and resizing. Green/red previews show allowed placement; collision, wall and free modes support different arrangements. The house door leads to its interior. Access settings separate visiting, interacting, moving/removing items and managing permissions. Decor can include workshops and trophies with useful effects. Revise through the real-estate item window; storage crates can pack possessions for moving, and picking up a house preserves its interior arrangement. This is a documented author→visit→revise→remove workflow, not an executed editor test. [S02](#s02)
