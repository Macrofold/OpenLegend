# Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire — research dossier

**G55 · Original research pass September 26, 2026; identified operational gaps addressed September 27.** Scope: Obsidian's 2018 computer RPG, its three major expansions, the 2019 turn-based/5.0 changes and subsequent console edition. This incorporates and preserves the five-review and Steam-evidence checkpoint at `1fd2a4a`. The [expedition and relationship rules supplement](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md) supplies the subsequently audited naval, romance and recovery gaps. No firsthand game execution, watched footage, audio audition or current-platform performance benchmark is claimed.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Library](../README.md) · [Previous: Veilguard](dragon-age-the-veilguard.md) · [Operational supplement](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md)

**Spoilers:** sections 1 and 5 explain the opening; the faction, weapon and expansion examples reveal later possibilities. The key research question is how a game makes a traveling party consequential to a region without making that party omnipotent.

## 1. A captain, a witness and a person in a world larger than themselves

Deadfire follows the Watcher from the first Pillars of Eternity into a maritime region of Eora. Eothas animates the enormous statue beneath Caed Nua, destroys the Watcher's home and leaves their survival entangled with his journey. A created history or imported prior history establishes selected consequences; it does not carry every previous physical object and private thought into a continuous simulation. The premise gives pursuit a personal reason while exposing conflicts among gods, trading powers, pirates and the people already living in the archipelago. [S1](#s1) [R1](#r1)

The original computer release was **May 8, 2018**; the PS4/Xbox One Ultimate Edition followed in **January 2020**. The announced Switch version was subsequently cancelled, so old store/review platform tags must not be mistaken for a shipped port. Native play is single-player. The 2019 alternative combat mode is a major rules/pacing change, not a multiplayer expansion. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3)

**First play:** reconstruct identity and history, choose a class/build, survive the opening and obtain the means to leave Port Maje. **Established play:** sail to a destination, investigate local problems, recruit or reconfigure people, gather resources and negotiate access to the next objective. **Late play:** reconcile relationships and faction commitments, face difficult optional encounters, decide who will accompany the Watcher into the region's future and complete the pursuit. Its strongest activities often happen while following a curiosity rather than the critical path. That freedom also creates a tension when an urgent supernatural crisis waits through extensive side work. [R2](#r2) [R3](#r3)

**Interpretation:** a ship is more than transport when it carries people, obligations and preparation. The player does not simply choose a map coordinate; they choose what kind of expedition can reach it and what costs the crew will bear. This is a useful fantasy even when the voyage is represented abstractly rather than by steering a physical hull through a continuous ocean.

## 2. What the player actually does

| System family | Actual verbs and decisions | Boundary that matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creation and identity | Choose appearance, ancestry/culture/background, attributes, class/subclass and selected prior history. | Social identity, imported history and combat class are different sources of reactivity. A preset history is not proof that the player personally played those events. [S1](#s1) |
| Party and progression | Recruit full companions or sidekicks, hire custom adventurers, choose available class configurations, allocate abilities and skills, retrain supported allocations. | Five active people including the Watcher; named companions have preset class choices, not unlimited identity rewriting. [M1](#m1) |
| Combat | Position, attack, cast, interrupt, switch tools, apply statuses, protect vulnerable people, retreat or spend Empower. | Real-time recovery and the later turn system value timing differently. Neither is a first-person action game. [M2](#m2) [S3](#s3) |
| Expertise | Use active and passive skills in appropriate checks, consumables and scripted interactions; benefit from Party Assist where eligible. | Assistance is a diminishing contribution, not the sum of everybody's skills applied universally. [M3](#m3) |

There is no native cooperative campaign, universal player-run commodity exchange, unrestricted construction system, comprehensive farming/fishing career or generational household simulator in the scope established here. Food and ship upkeep are real but do not establish a universal hunger model for all NPCs. World-map time, attack recovery, rest and an authored story's pacing are also not interchangeable clocks.

**Operational depth added after audit:** [supplement §§1–2](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md#1-a-vessel-is-a-loadout-and-a-workplace) explain fitted ships, jobs, naval commands, range, ammunition, repair and boarding; [§4](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md#4-knockout-injury-healing-and-death) explains individual recovery and difficulty boundaries. These are the detailed owners for the ship/recovery rows, not claims that the original short inventory already contained those rules.

### A build is a set of tradeoffs, not merely a role label

The class system combines familiar combat archetypes with different resource loops. A caster's slots, a cipher's Focus, a chanter's accumulating phrases and a monk's Wounds make different actions productive. Multiclassing connects two vocabularies while giving up access to the highest single-class ability tiers. It is not free access to every benefit of both complete progressions. [R5](#r5) [M2](#m2)

Attributes affect several kinds of output rather than mapping one-to-one onto a fantasy job. Active/passive skill investments separately support out-of-combat expression. The resulting space invites experimentation, but initial explanations must distinguish a useful thematic choice from an irreversible limitation. An overpowered guide build and a narratively coherent character are not necessarily the same research objective. Exact damage optimization is outside this dossier's scope.

### Hit chance and armor answer different questions

Accuracy is tested against the relevant defense; penetration is then compared with the armor rating for the damage type. Being accurate therefore does not guarantee doing useful damage. The documented system penalizes underpenetration in steps and rewards a sufficiently large advantage. A target may have a weaker defense against a different damage type, making a weapon or spell change more useful than adding another ordinary damage bonus. [M7](#m7)

**Interpretation:** layered defense is valuable when it produces distinguishable responses—improve the chance to connect, change the damage type, reduce protection or increase penetration. It becomes opaque when all four situations merely display a disappointing number. A combat log should expose the relevant stage without requiring the player to reverse-engineer every modifier.

### Status effects create a language of responses

Afflictions and inspirations are grouped by the attribute they affect. Applying an appropriate opposing status can remove an existing condition instead of simultaneously supplying its ordinary buff. Resistance is checked before the effect participates in this cancellation. Thus protection from a low-tier condition can preserve an inspiration that would otherwise be consumed. These details matter more than memorizing a list of intimidating names. [M8](#m8)

**Interpretation:** a status vocabulary is especially useful when it explains both prevention and repair. It should not silently promise that any healing spell removes any debilitating condition. Equally, a failed application may be a successful cancellation rather than “nothing happened”; feedback should say which rule resolved it.

### Resources have both encounter and expedition boundaries

Empower can strengthen an eligible action or restore class resources. Its expendable pool is not the same limitation as its once-per-encounter use. Equipment that refunds a point need not remove that encounter gate. Healing a person during combat likewise does not automatically erase the injuries acquired through repeated incapacitation. [M2](#m2) [M9](#m9)

The result is a layered preparation economy: what can be done now, what can be repeated during this fight and what must be restored across the expedition. A useful adaptation would name those boundaries in plain language. “You still have a charge” is not a sufficient answer when the current encounter disallows another use.

## 3. Items that change decisions

### The grimoire is a portable repertoire, not an editable spell notebook

A Deadfire wizard knows learned spells and can gain additional options from an equipped grimoire. The books' contents are fixed; switching books changes available spells but has a recovery cost, and an incompatible school remains incompatible. The player can therefore prepare complementary repertoires instead of repeatedly buying identical permanent bonuses. A new book matters partly because it contains what the person did not choose to learn. [M10](#m10)

**Interpretation:** person-held knowledge and tool-supplied capability should remain separate. A stolen, unequipped or incompatible tool need not erase personal learning, while personally knowing one spell need not grant every related tool's contents. This distinction transfers well to research, medicine, instruments and machinery, not only magic.

### A unique item's upgrade is a commitment to its particular behavior

Most unique items can acquire predefined improvements; some upgrades exclude alternatives. Vion-ceth's Wood Splitter branch, for example, offers different successor effects rather than permitting all upgrades simultaneously. Reagents and money connect exploration to improvement, but the existing item remains the object being modified. This differs from treating crafting as a universal source of freshly manufactured equipment. [M4](#m4)

A quality increase is one kind of value. An upgrade that changes targeting, replenishment or resistance can be more transformative even without the largest displayed number. The interface should explain whether an upgrade adds, replaces or prevents another effect; “better” conceals all three possibilities.

### Modwyr turns ownership into a story

Modwyr is a talking, initially Watcher-bound sword with a progression and a previous owner, Yngfrith. Its initial “Emotional” effect changes added damage according to the wielder's health. Finding its owner can change its binding/unique-item state. The weapon therefore exists at once as a combat tool, a remembered relationship and a problem of ownership. It is an authored talking object, not proof that every sword contains a deliberating agent. [M11](#m11)

**Interpretation:** an object's history becomes valuable when it changes what the player can do with it or what keeping it means. A thousand-word lore entry that never affects an interaction is a weaker form of integration. The danger is treating a person-like object as a reward that cannot refuse or remember; a genuinely autonomous adaptation would need explicit rules for consent and allegiance.

### Sasha's Singing Scimitar connects an item to a class rhythm

Its Refreshing Finale upgrade returns an Empower point and chanter phrases after the qualifying Empowered invocation. The distinction between an invocation, ordinary weapon attack and another class's ability is essential. An apparently broad promise of unlimited empowerment is false because the normal encounter restriction still matters. [M9](#m9)

**Interpretation:** a class-specific resource interaction can make a weapon desirable for more than its attack damage. This gives acquisition a build-specific meaning while avoiding the claim that rarity alone determines the best loadout. The counterexample is important: a character without the matching action cannot obtain the same benefit merely by holding the item.

### Food crosses personal and collective preparation

Silverfin has a personal Intellect benefit and a positive crew-morale contribution; poultry is both food and an ingredient in more elaborate dishes. Yolk Bowl combines rice, eggs and poultry into a different preparation effect. These are bounded recipes, not an emergent cuisine model. The same supply can be valuable as an ingredient, a prepared meal or support for the crew. [M12](#m12) [M13](#m13)

**Interpretation:** an ordinary object acquires interesting scarcity when two legitimate uses compete. The player should be able to reserve supplies intentionally instead of discovering that an automated system consumed a carefully saved ingredient. Whether a crew and expedition party share a pantry is a substantive rule, not an inventory-screen detail.

## 4. Ten concrete interactions and their limits

These are constructed examples of documented rules unless explicitly identified as a critic's observed play. They are not fabricated accounts of the researcher's own sessions. [Four additional operational cases](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md#5-four-additional-constructed-cases) extend this set with named cannon ranges, injured staffing, Maia/Ishiza and individual injury recovery; the original cases below are preserved.

### C01 — A diplomat benefits from people who never speak

**Intention:** pass a difficult conversation check. **Conditions:** the Watcher has a relevant skill and other active members have invested in it. **Action:** select the eligible response. **Interaction:** Party Assist converts their combined supporting skill into a diminishing bonus. **Result:** the group may qualify where the speaker alone would not. **Next decision:** invest in one specialist, several supporting people or a different route. The same assistance does not automatically apply to consumable scaling or checks that separately test every member. [M3](#m3)

**Lesson:** collaboration can matter without giving everyone a separate dialogue turn. But a simulation must still decide whether the helpers are present, communicating and permitted to advise; an abstract party bonus should not silently become telepathic global expertise.

### C02 — Remove fear without obtaining the advertised buff

**Intention:** restore a terrified ally's usefulness. **Conditions:** an existing Resolve affliction and an available opposing inspiration. **Action:** apply that inspiration. **Interaction:** cancellation consumes the new application to remove the old condition. **Result:** the person recovers without necessarily retaining the ordinary buff. **Next decision:** use another action for further protection or spend the resource elsewhere. Resistance ordering can change whether cancellation occurs at all. [M8](#m8)

**Lesson:** report successful removal separately from successful addition. A player should not have to infer that a missing buff icon means the action helped.

### C03 — A spell book solves the wrong-protection problem

**Intention:** damage an enemy that resists the current approach. **Conditions:** another grimoire supplies an eligible alternative spell. **Action:** switch the book, endure its recovery and attack the more useful defense/damage channel. **Interaction:** equipment changes repertoire, while target protection determines whether the substitution matters. **Result:** a small preparation decision changes the encounter's viable actions. **Next decision:** preserve safety during the switch or use an already known fallback. A forbidden school remains forbidden. [M10](#m10) [M7](#m7)

### C04 — Refunding a charge is not resetting the encounter

**Intention:** improve a chanter's expedition endurance. **Conditions:** the relevant Sasha upgrade and an eligible invocation. **Action:** Empower the invocation. **Interaction:** the item restores the point and phrases. **Result:** a future resource burden is reduced, but the current encounter's Empower limit still applies. **Next decision:** spend the recovered phrases on another invocation rather than repeatedly attempting a disallowed Empower. [M9](#m9)

**Lesson:** resource quantity and permission to act are separate state. A fluent assistant describing “infinite resources” would be misleading if it omitted the remaining admission rule.

### C05 — The sword's story changes who can wield it

**Intention:** obtain an unusual weapon's full value. **Conditions:** Modwyr has been found and bound; its dialogue points to its former owner. **Action:** follow the associated story to Yngfrith. **Interaction:** resolving the relationship can change the item's binding status while retaining a distinct weapon. **Result:** narrative work alters equipment eligibility. **Next decision:** keep, equip or interpret the object differently. This is one authored item's path, not a universal automatic emancipation system for all equipment. [M11](#m11)

### C06 — Better meals compete with a longer expedition

**Intention:** prepare the party for a difficult fight without neglecting travel. **Conditions:** a finite stock of useful food and ingredients. **Action:** reserve some for a dish and allocate some for the crew. **Interaction:** personal preparation and morale/supply planning compete for goods. **Result:** a route or shopping decision becomes relevant before the battle starts. **Next decision:** buy more, shorten the trip or accept a different preparation benefit. The illustration does not assume real-time spoilage or unverified automatic ingredient selection. [M12](#m12) [M13](#m13)

### C07 — Preparing the dungeon can change a later obstacle

**Attributed review example:** Vitale's Forgotten Sanctum play identifies opportunities to pacify spiders or prepare fungicide in a subterranean forest before later encounters. **Intention:** reduce risk rather than repeatedly win direct fights. **Conditions:** explore and use the relevant local options. **Action:** prepare the environment. **Interaction:** one part of the dungeon changes another's obstacle. **Result:** traversal and investigation have tactical consequences. **Next decision:** spend effort on preparation or accept the direct encounter. This is an authored set of interactions, not unrestricted chemistry applied to all plants. [D3](#d3)

### C08 — An intuitive theft meets a scripted alarm

**Attributed launch review example:** Kelly steals a guard's alarm horn at Fort Deadlight, yet the alarm still occurs. **Intention:** remove the physical means of calling help. **Condition:** the horn exists as a stealable object. **Action:** take it. **Interaction:** inventory ownership and the authored alarm sequence fail to compose as expected. **Result:** reinforcements still arrive. **Next decision:** use a supported diversion or fight. This is a dated observation, not a personally reproduced current-build defect. [R1](#r1)

**Lesson:** giving a prop a systemic affordance creates a reasonable expectation that other systems consult it. An inaccessible decorative horn is less misleading than a stealable horn whose supposed owner can still blow it. The smallest test is not whether the object can be stolen; it is whether the dependent action checks possession.

### C09 — A missed click becomes an apparent tactical mistake

**Attributed launch review example:** Vitale describes UI slowdown where selecting an ability does not register before clicking an enemy. **Intention:** cast at range. **Action actually accepted:** move toward the target. **Result:** a fragile caster approaches danger. **Next decision:** pause and verify selection rather than assuming the command was accepted. [R4](#r4)

**Lesson:** command acknowledgement is part of tactical fairness. A system that silently drops the first half of an action can make its perfectly coherent simulation feel arbitrary. The remedy is not smarter narration of the resulting mistake, but an inspectable accepted command.

### C10 — Changing the time model changes the desired build

**Intention:** make combat more deliberate. **Conditions:** start a separate turn-based campaign. **Action:** choose the alternative mode and evaluate actions one actor at a time. **Interaction:** recovery/initiative and per-turn opportunity now structure pacing differently. **Result:** a strategy valued in real-time play need not retain the same relative value. **Next decision:** rebuild around the new action economy rather than copying a real-time recommendation unchanged. The supported mode choice is made for a new campaign; console commands or mods are not presented as a normal reversible UI feature. [S3](#s3)

**Lesson:** “turn-based” is not a camera preference. A change in scheduling can require different encounter density, duration, resource and balance assumptions even when all the same named abilities remain present.

## 5. People, factions, knowledge and narrative scale

The main companions are **Edér, Aloth, Pallegina, Xoti, Serafen, Maia Rua and Tekēhu**. Returning people's availability can depend on imported history. Their alternative class configurations let a preferred personality fill more than one combat role. Sidekicks add distinctive bodies and some contextual material without promising every full companion's relationship depth. The DLCs also show why a launch-era statement that a sidekick has nothing further to say should not be applied indiscriminately to later content. [M1](#m1) [D1](#d1) [D3](#d3)

The relationship interface records reactions to topics and conduct. Importantly, people react to **each other**, not only to the protagonist. This allows a party to feel like a group whose members have standards independently of whether they approve of the Watcher. Public disposition, faction standing and an individual relationship remain separate abstractions. Their visibility helps the player understand change, but does not establish that every offscreen conversation is independently simulated. [M6](#m6)

**Romance and continuity:** the [dedicated relationship explanation](../mechanics/deadfire-expedition-and-relationship-rules.md#3-romance-is-not-synonymous-with-party-approval) distinguishes the four sustained routes, Serafen's casual encounter, non-romanceable companions/sidekicks, personal quest conditions, companion-to-companion relationships and political loyalty. A relationship meter alone does not discharge those separate systems.

The regional political contest involves the **Huana**, **Royal Deadfire Company**, **Vailian Trading Company** and **Principi**. Indigenous sovereignty, caste hierarchy, colonial ambition, trade and piracy intersect rather than dividing neatly into good and evil colors. A trading concession or a local intervention can carry a different meaning to the people who need it, the institution financing it and a companion tied to that institution. The best interpretation is not that all sides are equally justified, but that each has concrete interests the player can investigate. [R2](#r2) [R3](#r3) [R4](#r4)

Neketaka is especially useful as an example of social geography: elevation, districts, architectural differences and access distinguish groups before the player learns every proper noun. A city can make inequality navigable, not only describe it in a codex. Elsewhere, a small island can give a tightly bounded dispute more room to be remembered than another enormous capital. The risk is uneven density: a wonderful city sets expectations that thinner destinations cannot meet. [R3](#r3)

The main story eventually asks what mortals should do about a transformation they cannot simply veto through combat. Eothas is not merely a large enemy health bar. The player's consequential territory includes interpretation, alliances and the aftermath, not unlimited power to make the crisis never happen. Obsidian's 5.0 additions to Eothas/Woedica dialogue are direct evidence that the presentation of this critical path needed further explanation for some players. They are not evidence that the entire ending was replaced. [S5](#s5)

### Visible routines are not proof of a universal society simulation

The 2017 developer interview proposed schedules, weather and environmental reactivity. It also documents the rationale for a five-person party: fewer simultaneous combatants improve readability. Those are primary statements of intent at a particular time. This research has not verified every proposed schedule or storm interaction in the released game, and does not treat the preview as a complete shipped specification. [S1](#s1)

The custom combat AI is more concrete: official 1.1 notes explicitly add or categorize conditions for consumables, modals, weapon switching and basic attacks. It is an inspectable command-policy surface, not a claim of general private thought. The player can automate a routine and intervene when the situation changes. A believable non-player person would additionally need rules for knowledge, refusal, ownership and obligations; the existence of an AI-editor row does not establish those layers. [S4](#s4)

## 6. Presentation, interface and atmosphere

The game combines an elevated party view and painted-looking environments with animated characters, effects and environmental movement. Its maritime architecture and color distinguish it from merely repeating the first game's familiar fantasy landscape. Portraits and illustrated interactions let writing supply gestures and large events that a small figure on the map cannot communicate alone. This is not an FPS requirement, nor is a free camera necessary for the sense of place. [R4](#r4) [R5](#r5)

Inline explanations of unfamiliar names and concepts reduce the cost of dense lore. A reader can ask for context without leaving the conversation to search an external wiki. That is a different intervention from simplifying every culture into familiar stereotypes. The better question is which information the current decision requires and which can remain an invitation for curiosity. Interface clarity can preserve depth instead of replacing it. [R4](#r4)

Five characters, enemy formations, spell shapes, cast/recovery time, defenses and status icons still create substantial visual load. The target-selection and missed-input observations in section 4 show that clarity is causal, not cosmetic. In a smaller or top-down adaptation, preserving accepted-command feedback, readable status families and differentiated silhouettes may matter more than increasing rendering detail. No measured frame-time, GPU cost or universal accessibility success is claimed.

Justin E. Bell composed the soundtrack, with contributions from Frölich Geschray and sea shanties arranged/performed by Nils Brown and Carrie Shaw. The publisher's soundtrack listing supplies these credits. Singing associated with travel can make the crew perceptible during an otherwise abstract journey; that is an analytical explanation of its potential function, not an auditioned musical analysis. [S6](#s6)

## 7. Three expansions, three uses of additional scope

### Beast of Winter — visit history rather than only hear about it

Released in August 2018, this is a high-level side journey during the main campaign, not an epilogue. Harbinger's Watch, Rymrgand's influence and a dangerous dragon lead into spaces that revisit significant historical events. Vatnir joins as a sidekick. Vitale's August 3 review values the visualized history, writing and some encounters but finds puzzles obvious and the package isolated; his own sub-four-hour completion is not a distribution of player runtimes. [D1](#d1)

**Interpretation:** a supernatural space can turn lore into something the player moves through and acts on. The risk is that a beautiful history tour has no practical relation to the present party or politics. A bounded historical encounter is stronger when it changes a person's interpretation of an existing commitment.

### Seeker, Slayer, Survivor — a challenge structure must defeat the default plan

The September 2018 expansion brings the Watcher to Kazuwari and the Crucible through an invitation connected to a severed head. Galawain's three aspects organize combat challenges. Vitale finds many fights solvable through the same routine and the artifact collection too close to scavenger-hunt padding. Suzie Ford, by contrast, values the tactical demands and encounter variety while also criticizing pacing. Their disagreement concerns experienced adaptation, not the mere number of enemies or arenas. [D2](#d2) [D4](#d4)

**Interpretation:** naming three challenge types does not create three strategies. A good test asks which previously successful plan fails, what evidence explains the failure and which alternative becomes attractive. A later community mod explicitly removes the nine distant artifact hunts; its authorial complaint is evidence of a desired pacing change, not proof that every player dislikes those hunts. The mod is not a native update. [P3](#p3)

### The Forgotten Sanctum — reconnect a dungeon to the wider work

Released in December 2018, the final expansion involves archmages, Wael and a strange archive/dungeon. Vitale's January 3, 2019 review is much more positive about its connections to earlier quests and characters. He also values tactical difficulty that makes affliction management matter and choices such as pacifying spiders or preparing fungicide. These are meaningful local alternatives within authored spaces, not infinite procedural dungeon solutions. [D3](#d3)

**Interpretation:** added scope is most valuable when it changes the meaning or usefulness of what the player already knows. A returning acquaintance, a neglected sidekick's expertise or a new interpretation of an older event can make the base game feel deeper without multiplying its map size. More content and more connected content are not the same investment.

The Ultimate Edition packages the major expansions and free additions. Cosmetics, extra merchants, challenge settings and large narrative expansions should not be treated as identical units of content. This dossier does not infer undisclosed future DLC from the continued presence of a store page. [S2](#s2) [S5](#s5)

## 8. Production, release, promotion and commercial evidence

### A sequel that renegotiates nostalgia

The 2017 Messner interview with Josh Sawyer and Feargus Urquhart explicitly contrasts the first game's traditional setting expectations with a desire for a different region. It also describes changes to party size, combat legibility and environmental presentation. These are creator rationales, not independent proof that every change succeeded. The later release differs from the interview's original March target, illustrating why preview dates and specifications need version labels. [S1](#s1)

Obsidian's **February 2, 2017** update reports **$2 million raised on Fig after one week**, with companion AI customization and Spanish localization as attained goals. This is a dated crowdfunding milestone, not copies sold, the final budget or profit. The same announcement uses a Josh Sawyer multiclass explanation/video to make a systems change legible to backers. [S7](#s7)

Official patches show feedback turning into concrete interventions: 1.1 expands AI conditions and supplies a ship-resupply convenience; 5.0 adds dialogue to the critical path, changes access for an unaffiliated route and completes the turn-based release. These are stronger production lessons than the unsourced generalization that a sequel simply became more polished over time. Different complaints received different kinds of response—interface, rule access, exposition and an alternative scheduling model. [S4](#s4) [S5](#s5)

### Turn-based mode was a separate design intervention

The January 2019 account identifies designer Nick Carver and programmer Brian Mackintosh as central to the mode's development. The beta arrived on January 24 and required a new campaign; 5.0 took it out of beta in May. The same abilities can change strategic meaning when they enter a turn structure. The **April 2026** announcement on the same Pillars website instead concerns the **first game**, whose switching behavior must not be imported into Deadfire's specification. [S3](#s3) [S5](#s5) [S8](#s8)

### Marketing, participation and what was not measured

The documented participation surfaces include Fig rewards/updates, developer explanations, a backer beta, review access and a later Ultimate challenge. Builds, imported histories, faction outcomes and unusual object stories are plausible sharing units because two players can meaningfully compare them. That last claim is a research hypothesis, not an attribution model proving which streamer, community or channel produced sales. Reviewer disclosures such as Vitale's Fig backing also help distinguish a reviewer's starting expectations from a neutral demographic sample. [S1](#s1) [S5](#s5) [S7](#s7) [R4](#r4)

### Weak initial sales do not establish permanent unprofitability

The accessible **Origin Story interview transcript** contains Sawyer's retrospective account of weak initial sales followed by eventual profitability after several years. The relevant passage is around the transcript's **11:55** segment. The page displays “May 31” without a year in its visible heading, so this dossier does **not** repeat secondary pages' unverified “by 2025” dating as the original interview date. The statement is attributed to the director, not treated as an audited financial disclosure. No lifetime units, net revenue, development budget or title P&L were independently recovered. [S9](#s9)

A 2018 revenue estimate extrapolated from one crowdfunding investor's return would not be a reliable substitute for those missing figures. The correct comparison is between separately dated and defined observations, not a narrative in which either “flop” or “beloved classic” proves every commercial claim. The business model is a premium campaign plus expansions/editions, not a multiplayer population whose continued concurrency defines viability.

## 9. Reception: five independent critics and bounded player evidence

All five substantive written review bodies were inspected in the original pass, and their checkpoint summaries are preserved here. Launch, pre-release and 2019 experiences are not interchangeable. A reviewer's own completion time is not the game's universal length. The September 27 supplement does not claim five fresh review readings.

**Andy Kelly — PC Gamer, May 8, 2018.** Kelly values descriptive writing, Neketaka, varied quests and customization. Crew needs give travel consequences, but map sailing and resupply can feel routine. He is more positive about ship combat than several other critics. His stolen-horn example exposes an intuitive interaction that the scripted scene does not support. [R1](#r1)

**Jerry Williams — RPGFan, June 20, 2018.** Williams praises philosophical dialogue, skill/reputation reactivity and companion disputes. Chasing a god is less immediately compelling to him than the first game's crisis. Familiar strong builds can become repetitive; naval combat interrupts the better activities. Pathing/input issues coexist with appreciation for writing and performances. [R2](#r2)

**Hayden Dingman — PCWorld, May 8, 2018.** Dingman likes story set pieces, smoother combat, naval encounters and distinctive equipment. He finds the middle aimless, some locations thin and companion material prematurely exhausted. His pre-release build has serious technical and quest-state failures. ## 10. Proposed OpenLegend experiments and what not to import

**A helper's expertise should have an owner.** Party Assist suggests a low-friction way for specialists to matter during another person's task. A small experiment could require an adviser to be present, able to communicate and willing to help. Record the supported part of the task and what remains beyond the group's skill. Do not grant absent people automatic knowledge or treat a whole party as one omniscient character.

**A prop's affordance should bind dependent actions.** The horn example is a minimal cross-system integrity test: taking the object should affect actions that require it, or the world should establish another means of sounding the alarm. A generative explanation should not invent a replacement horn after the fact merely to preserve a scripted outcome.

**A policy editor can make repeated work explainable.** Deadfire's condition/action AI suggests a routine that the player can inspect, interrupt and revise. Begin with one helper, a small condition vocabulary and visible reasons for inaction. The assistant should distinguish “no target qualified,” “resource unavailable” and “order refused.” None of that requires claiming that the original game uses a language model or the same architecture.

**Knowledge-bearing equipment can connect discovery and capability.** A fixed grimoire, a learned technique and an enchantment are different ownership models. Test losing or lending the tool, a forbidden compatibility, interruption during use and the later recovery of the object. A system that preserves these distinctions can support non-magical manuals and instruments as well.

**A home can move.** A ship-shaped hub can carry a familiar group into new geography, making travel emotionally continuous. It still needs meaningful choices about crew, supplies and risk. Automating routine restocking may improve the experience without removing the important commitments, but only after identifying which shortages actually change a plan.

**A powerful protagonist can remain bounded.** Influence over aftermath, institutions and relationships can be meaningful even when the player cannot reverse a cosmic event. The scope of agency must be communicated early enough that the ending feels like a consequence of the world's scale rather than an arbitrary confiscation of control.

These are proposed research experiments, not accepted engine requirements or claims about current OpenLegend implementation. Private-campaign pause, abstract faction states and authored time travel are especially poor candidates for copying unchanged into a shared autonomous world. Preserve the useful decision; redesign its world and concurrency assumptions explicitly.
