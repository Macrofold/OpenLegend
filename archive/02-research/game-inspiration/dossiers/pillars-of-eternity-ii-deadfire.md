## Five independent written reviews — substantive bodies inspected

### Andy Kelly, PC Gamer, May 8, 2018

[Review](https://www.pcgamer.com/pillars-of-eternity-2-deadfire-review/). Kelly values descriptive writing, Neketaka, varied quests and tactical customization. Crew needs give travel social consequences, but he finds the map's actual sailing unconvincing and resupply repetitive. He is considerably more positive about ship combat than several other critics. His Fort Deadlight example also exposes a boundary: stealing an alarm horn does not stop a guard's scripted alarm. That is a specific attributed launch observation, not a reproduced current-build result or proof that every object is non-systemic.

### Jerry Williams, RPGFan, June 20, 2018

[Review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire/). Williams praises philosophical dialogue, skill/reputation reactivity and companion disputes. The central god-chasing plot feels less immediately compelling than the first game's crisis. Familiar powerful builds can make combat repetitive, though he still enjoys its rhythm. Ship combat feels shallow and disruptive. He also reports pathing/input friction and distinguishes strong performances from the idea that literally every bit of narration is voiced. This is a Windows-era experience, not a current cross-platform diagnosis.

### Hayden Dingman, PCWorld, May 8, 2018

[Review](https://www.pcworld.com/article/401937/pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-review.html). Dingman likes central story encounters, smoother combat, text-based naval battles and equipment with histories. He finds the middle less purposeful, many locations thin and some companion arcs prematurely exhausted. His pre-release build produced crashes, memory-related slowdown and contradictory quest/navigation states. Those findings explain launch uncertainty without establishing that all later versions retain them. His enthusiasm for naval encounters contrasts with Williams and Hanley rather than supplying a universal verdict.

### Bryan Vitale, RPG Site, May 8, 2018

[Review](https://www.rpgsite.net/review/7139-pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-review). Vitale finds the setting and systems unusually cohesive. Party Assist makes non-combat team composition matter; inline lore explanations reduce disorientation. Faction flexibility lets him roleplay without immediately losing most quest access, though it can feel convenient. He reports a departed companion appearing during boarding and missed UI inputs that turn an intended spell into movement. His overall technical assessment is less negative than Dingman's. He discloses backing the Fig campaign and receiving a review copy.

### Joe Hanley, RPGamer, May 7, 2019

[Review](https://rpgamer.com/review/pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-review/). Hanley values multiclass tradeoffs, alternative quest solutions and replay customization. His later review explicitly covers turn-based play and the changed injury system. He finds crew upkeep and ship battles disruptive and prefers boarding or avoidance. The setting's sounds and visual identity work for him, but the main story, characters and ending feel weaker than the first game. His suggested attribute builds are preferences, not universal class requirements. His May 7 review also precedes the May 8, 2019 5.0 release by one day.

## Primary mode/version correction

Obsidian's [May 8, 2019 Patch 5.0 account](https://eternity.obsidian.net/news/pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-update-61---patch-50---the-return-of-the-ultimate) was read in full. It documents turn-based mode leaving beta, a revised ship encounter interface, added recorded Eothas/Woedica dialogue responding to critical-path feedback, changes to the unaffiliated ending route and The Ultimate challenge. The latter combines multiple hard-mode constraints; its historical prize offer is not automatically still open.

The [April 6, 2026 turn-based announcement](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/eternity/turn-based-mode-has-fully-come-to-pillars-of-eternity) concerns **Pillars of Eternity I**, despite being on the same branded site. Its freely switchable mode rules must not be credited to Deadfire. Likewise, a Switch platform tag on an old review is not proof that the announced Deadfire Switch port shipped.

## Detailed mechanics and expansion evidence underway

The documented [Empower rules](https://pillarsofeternity.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_%28Deadfire%29) distinguish resource recovery from strengthening an eligible action and retain a once-per-encounter restriction. [Refreshing Finale](https://pillarsofeternity.fandom.com/wiki/Refreshing_Finale) on [Sasha's Singing Scimitar](https://pillarsofeternity.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha%27s_Singing_Scimitar) restores an Empower point and chanter phrases after the appropriate action; restoring the point is not removing the encounter restriction. The [affliction/inspiration guide](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ps4/271748-pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-ultimate-edition/faqs/76599/afflictions-and-inspirations) explains cancellation and resistance ordering. These are community-observed rules, not proprietary implementation claims.

Bryan Vitale's [January 3, 2019 Forgotten Sanctum review](https://www.rpgsite.net/review/8135-pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire-the-forgotten-sanctum-review) was read in full. He values its connections to prior characters and quests, difficult affliction-management encounters and alternative dungeon routes. He describes pacifying spiders or preparing fungicide as examples of routes influencing later obstacles. His much weaker assessment of the other two DLCs is not a substitute for reading their individual reviews.
