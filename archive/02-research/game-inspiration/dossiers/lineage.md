# Lineage — original and Remastered PC dossier

**Game:** G201, Lineage's original 1998 PC lineage and its Remastered continuation.  
**Status:** Research in progress; expanded core checkpoint, not completed or independently reviewed.  
**Research date:** October 28, 2026.

## 1. Scope and player promise

NC's official retrospective dates the original service to September 1998. This dossier studies that PC lineage, including its Remastered continuation; Lineage II, the mobile M/W/2M products and the separate 2026 Classic service require distinct labels. [S01](#s01)

The current Korean download page directs players through PURPLE to the Remastered client and lists Windows 10 or later, 64-bit, among its requirements. That verifies the documented regional PC access route; other regions' operation, payment and continuity are being researched separately. No client was installed or run. [S02](#s02)

The useful OpenLegend question is how familiar places, economic roles and collective authority can give a persistent world meaning beyond individual advancement. The eventual comparison must distinguish players' freely chosen cooperation from institutions that become obligatory, and shared geography from the different lifetimes of characters, possessions and encounters.

## 2. Foundational conflict and a concrete collective stake

The official artbook places **Deforge**, a survivor of the royal family's tragedy, on Whispering Isle; **Kurtz** and the Black Knights pursue him. **Ken Rauhel** is the opposing ruler. Elves originate around the Mother Tree and are drawn from relative isolation into surrounding conflict. Later, Ken Rauhel seeks **Dantes** and the Dark Elves after defeat; their desire to return to the surface helps drive another war. These motives connect political legitimacy, exclusion and territorial conflict. [S01](#s01)

Historically, castle sieges gave player alliances an analogous stake: taking a throne granted authority to adjust and collect taxes. [S01](#s01) The current siege guide explicitly says tax-rate adjustment, mercenary hiring and soldier management ended in May 2019. Territorial rewards survive, but the older governance interface must not be presented as current. [S10](#s10)

**OpenLegend hypothesis:** a shared accomplishment is more meaningful when it changes a place or service people use. That does not establish a need for mandatory sieges, player taxation or winner-take-all politics. The full dossier will test this possibility against other roles and firsthand accounts of enjoyment, effort and departure.

## 3. What players actually do

The original English manual describes pointing and clicking to move, selecting an enemy to attack, double-clicking equipment or consumables, assigning spells to shortcuts, talking to NPCs, combining requested materials through NPC exchanges and trading with another character. Strength, dexterity, constitution, intelligence, wisdom and charisma influence different combat, recovery or follower capacities. Its four classes were Knight, Wizard, Elf and the Prince/Princess who could establish a blood pledge; its Knight had no magic. These are the **2001 rules**, not the modern roster. Local, whisper, party and pledge channels supported different conversation scopes; bookmarks enabled magical return where permitted. [S05](#s05)

The current Korean class guide describes the following broader role families. These are overlapping capabilities, not a compulsory tank/healer/damage party formula. [S17](#s17)

| Role family | Distinct choices and capabilities |
| --- | --- |
| Leadership and support | Royal creates pledges and combines weapons, party buffs and leadership abilities. |
| Physical frontline | Knight emphasizes health and stun; Warrior has reactive counters; Fencer changes modes and chains skills; Holy Knight combines greatsword/shield with anti-undead/demon tools. |
| Reach and adaptation | Elf combines bows, general and elemental magic; Lancer changes between melee and ranged spear forms. |
| Magic and disruption | Wizard uses advanced general magic, area attacks and protective buffs; Illusionist combines its Kiringku weapon, magical attacks and party support. |
| Targeted pressure | Dark Elf uses dual blades/claws and concealment; Dragon Knight combines weakness exposure with Foe Slayer; Fighter charges skills. |

The page also describes the chakram-using **월영사** but gives a November 7, 2026 date, after this research cutoff. Its presence in a guide is not sufficient evidence that the class was already playable on October 28. [S17](#s17)

## 4. Delegation, resources and the player's attention

Current **Play Support System (PSS)** documentation distinguishes full repeat hunting, assistance while the player controls combat, and hunting near a chosen position. Settings govern buffs, attack order, return conditions, shopping, storage and travel routes. HP, MP, carried weight and hostile-player attacks can trigger different responses. Return and random teleport depend on carrying their respective scrolls; a configured intention without its consumable does not execute. Settings can be saved to a server repository. [S06](#s06)

The developers' post-Remaster **2019 Talk Day** Q&A acknowledged that PSS convenience could diminish the satisfaction of hands-on fighting. Their proposed response included manual hunting spaces, instances and teamwork, with strategic decisions beyond simply disabling automation. These were stated intentions, not evidence that all the promised experiences shipped or solved the problem. [S16](#s16)

**OpenLegend interpretation:** delegation is useful when players can choose the work they enjoy doing themselves. The relevant design question is what remains interesting to notice, decide and change after routine execution is delegated. Automation's quality, interruption rules and recoverability require validation alongside the activity; neither constant clicking nor unattended accumulation is automatically the desired experience.

## 5. Items and native composition

**Magic dolls** are timed companions that follow and supply buffs without taking experience. Only one is summoned at once. Double-clicking consumes crystals; cancelling does not refund them. An Elder doll supplies periodic MP recovery, while a Bugbear doll increases carrying capacity. Equipment status and active summoning are separate states. Ordinary synthesis combines two to four dolls of the same grade: more inputs increase the success chance, while failure preserves one and deletes the others. Potential stones add another configurable layer. This is prescribed item composition, not authoring a new creature or autonomous personality. [S14](#s14)

The official Shield entry documents a concrete acquisition route: buy the spell from **Simon** in Talking Island village using Knight Order coins, subject to class-specific learning levels. A spell therefore has an acquisition and eligibility boundary as well as a combat purpose. Its entry's 2019 learning table should not be extended to subsequently introduced classes without another source. [S18](#s18)

Crafting chains, ordinary travel, death and recovery are still being completed from the retrieved primary documentation. Paid power and housing custody are being researched separately from these native item rules.

## 6. A blood pledge is an institution

The current guide reserves pledge creation to a Royal and supports instant, application-based or password admission. Rank determines authority; shared-warehouse access requires a grant. Members contribute to pledge growth and can open different group encounters through contribution, Adena or entry items. Alliances identify friends but do not merge castles, halls or warehouses. Leaving removes pledge chat and warehouse access; possession and succession conditions constrain dissolution or leadership transfer. The guide contains inconsistent buff-level thresholds, so no single current threshold is inferred from it. [S09](#s09)

**OpenLegend interpretation:** belonging becomes substantive when people can make arrangements, delegate responsibility and use shared resources. Those powers also require comprehensible boundaries. An alliance need not erase distinct ownership; a social invitation need not silently authorize withdrawal from communal storage.

## 7. Worked situations — partial set pending full pass

These are causal reconstructions from documented rules, not observed playtests. Their transfer lessons are design hypotheses.

### A. Configure a repeat trip and discover a missing dependency

A player records a route beginning near the warehouse in the town to which the hunt returns, travels to the destination and saves it. They set supplies and return conditions before enabling repeated hunting. If the return scroll is absent, low health does not produce the intended escape; choosing a mismatched return town can add travel costs or disrupt repetition. The result depends on the configuration **and** inventory. [S06](#s06) A useful delegation interface should make those dependencies visible before the task starts.

### B. Attempt a castle capture

The attacking Royal declares war. During the scheduled siege, the surrounding area becomes a combat zone and pledge emblems identify participants. Attackers break the outer gate and destroy the guardian tower; only a Royal can take the exposed crown. Capture sends outsiders back to the starting position, but ownership at the siege's end determines the winner. The holding pledge receives territorial rewards. [S10](#s10) This produces successive shared objectives and a changed place, while the deadline and monopoly also concentrate participation pressure and power.

### C. Improve a companion at a real opportunity cost

A player who values the Elder doll's mana support must weigh keeping that useful item against committing same-grade dolls to synthesis. Extra inputs improve the chance; failure still consumes all but one. The proposed stronger companion is not a guaranteed upgrade. [S14](#s14) For OpenLegend, distinguish meaningful composition choices from loss that merely pressures further acquisition; reusable behavior should not inherit this gamble by default.

### D. Organize a bounded group expedition

A pledge's authorized participants choose a soul-stone encounter, pay its opening cost and enter the portal within its three-minute window. Up to fifty can enter; defeating the summoned boss adds contribution and yields the documented rewards. [S09](#s09) The institution converts accumulated cooperation into a shared occasion, but a narrow entry window can exclude people who helped earn it.
