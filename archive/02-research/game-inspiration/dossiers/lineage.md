# Lineage — original and Remastered PC dossier

**Game:** G201, Lineage's original 1998 PC lineage and its Remastered continuation.  
**Status:** Researched and independently reviewed; R01–R14 and applicable R15 covered.  
**Research date:** October 28, 2026.

## 1. Identity, scope and player promise

NC's official retrospective dates the original service to September 1998. [S01](#s01)

This dossier studies that PC lineage and its Remastered continuation. Lineage II, the mobile M/W/2M products and the separate 2026 Classic service retain distinct labels throughout.

The current Korean download page directs players through PURPLE to the Remastered client and lists Windows 10 or later, 64-bit, among its requirements. This is the regional PC access route, not a global platform or service promise. No client was installed or run. [S02](#s02)

**Persistent-world finding:** shared fields coexist with instances and cross-server areas, while the Taiwan Remastered transition explicitly retained characters and servers with exceptions. Persistence is therefore real but service-specific, not one worldwide shard or an assurance of unchanged state. [S15](#s15) [E05](#e05)

The useful OpenLegend question is how familiar places, economic roles and collective authority can give a persistent world meaning beyond individual advancement. The comparison distinguishes players' freely chosen cooperation from institutions that become obligatory, and shared geography from the different lifetimes of characters, possessions and encounters.

## 2. Foundational conflict and later motives

**Story spoilers:** named conflicts and later developments follow.

The official artbook places **Deforge**, a survivor of the royal family's tragedy, on Whispering Isle; **Kurtz** and the Black Knights pursue him. **Ken Rauhel** is the opposing ruler. Elves originate around the Mother Tree and are drawn from relative isolation into surrounding conflict. Later, Ken Rauhel seeks **Dantes** and the Dark Elves after defeat; their desire to return to the surface helps drive another war. Their earlier exclusion and religious shift from Einhasad to Grankain give that alliance a cultural history. Elsewhere, the **Ice Queen** seeks revenge after her father dies in a campaign against **Valakas**; her transformation freezes Oren. Motives thus shape alliances and the places players enter. [S01](#s01)

Historically, castle sieges gave player alliances an analogous stake: taking a throne granted authority to adjust and collect taxes. [S01](#s01)

The current siege guide explicitly says tax-rate adjustment, mercenary hiring and soldier management ended in May 2019. Territorial rewards survive; the older governance interface is historical. [S10](#s10)

**Story developments.** The 2019 developer Q&A recalls a Fencer event in which Deforge sought forgiveness for his ancestors at Blood Mountain and **Shuken** joined the Red Knights. Its future-story outline then positioned **Halpas**, who had escaped **Balrog's** prison and imprisoned his former captor, behind the next conflict. Defeating Balrog for a key to Halpas was a proposed sequence at that date, not proof of its later implementation. This separates an experienced episode from an announced narrative-to-encounter link. [S16](#s16)

**OpenLegend hypothesis:** a shared accomplishment is more meaningful when it changes a place or service people use. That does not establish a need for mandatory sieges, player taxation or winner-take-all politics. The cases and firsthand accounts below test that possibility against ordinary actions, effort and departure.

## 3. Actions, character identity and combat roles

The original English manual describes pointing and clicking to move, selecting an enemy to attack, double-clicking equipment or consumables, assigning spells to shortcuts, talking to NPCs, combining requested materials through NPC exchanges and trading with another character. Strength, dexterity, constitution, intelligence, wisdom and charisma influence different combat, recovery or follower capacities. Its four classes were Knight, Wizard, Elf and the Prince/Princess who could establish a blood pledge; its Knight had no magic. These are the **2001 rules**, not the modern roster. Local, whisper, party and pledge channels supported different conversation scopes; bookmarks enabled magical return where permitted. [S05](#s05)

The Korean class guide describes overlapping roles. [S17](#s17)

## 4. Travel, progression, time and failure

**A shared world with different activity boundaries.** The official beginner guide lists ordinary fields and multilevel dungeons, timed locations, party instances and cross-server areas. Gludio Dungeon uses an Adena entry charge; the Tower of Insolence uses floor scrolls or charms and connects through a rift to the Tower of Domination. Hidden hunting grounds share a charged time allowance, with different regular/special-server limits. Instances consume a Time–Space Key and are labeled no-penalty combat areas. The Abandoned Land's inter-server variant explicitly mixes players from different servers. These are distinct admission, time and population rules, not one uniformly seamless map. [S15](#s15)

**Progression changes the routine.** The publisher's guide introduced in April 2024 organizes advancement from Talking Island to mainland play around level 90. Quests supply equipment, spell access and resources, then direct players toward different hunting grounds. It warns that the starter consumables stop working at 90 and directs players to replace them through Giran's vendors. Carrying weight also matters: the guide says natural HP/MP recovery stops at 50% and attacks/spells at 83%. Selling, dissolving or deleting items reduces load; bringing excessive healing supplies can therefore interfere with the activity they support. These are the retrieved guide's rules, subject to later revision. [S04](#s04)

**Death and return are versioned, not a single universal punishment.** An October 28, 2020 publisher update, preserved by Inven, added recovery lists for experience loss and vanished equipment, up to twenty entries retained for twenty-four hours. Players accessed them through the death icon or **Agatha**; the item list concerned vanished items, not every dropped possession. Recovery costs varied by level. The notice also limited affected inventory categories to weapons, armor and accessories. This documents a change from the much harsher early review accounts; it does not independently certify every 2026 server's current loss table. [S20](#s20)

**Progression interpretation:** returning can mean resuming a useful build, rejoining companions, revisiting a place, changing a resource route or preparing for a shared occasion. The systems above also create pressure to optimize time and protect prior investment. Those are competing experiences, not proof that greater loss or slower advancement makes attachment stronger.

## 5. Items, crafting and native composition

**Magic dolls** are timed companions that follow and supply buffs without taking experience. Only one is summoned at once. Summoning consumes crystals without a cancellation refund. An Elder doll supplies periodic MP recovery, while a Bugbear doll increases carrying capacity. Equipped and summoned states differ. Ordinary synthesis combines two to four dolls of the same grade: more inputs increase the success chance, while failure preserves one and deletes the others. Potential stones add another configurable layer. This is prescribed composition, not a newly authored creature. [S14](#s14)

The official Shield entry documents a concrete acquisition route: buy the spell from **Simon** in Talking Island village using Knight Order coins, subject to class-specific learning levels. A spell therefore has an acquisition and eligibility boundary as well as a combat purpose. Its 2019 learning table does not establish eligibility for later classes. [S18](#s18)

**Crafting** exchanges materials through NPCs or an integrated menu usable in Safety Zones. It exposes categories, required material conditions, alternatives, favorites and, for applicable recipes, probabilities. A player can choose substitute inputs rather than assume one recipe route. Certain results can be blessed through a great success; the Sacred Magic Defense Helmet is a named example, with a visible effect and server announcement. This describes authored recipes and output variations, not construction of arbitrary new equipment behavior. [S26](#s26)

**Enchantment** is a separate progression risk. The 2024 system guide distinguishes ordinary failures that destroy equipment from specific protective scrolls that preserve it. Artisan weapon/armor scrolls apply to gear already at least +9; Orim accessory-scroll failure can reduce the enhancement, while its blessed form preserves that value. The exact scroll changes the consequence, so “upgrading destroys gear” is an incomplete rule. [S19](#s19)

The elemental guide describes fire, water, earth and wind damage added alongside ordinary enhancement. Failed elemental attempts consume the scroll without destroying the weapon. Reapplying another element can replace the former progression; separately described conversion scrolls preserve the elemental stage. Enemy resistance changes the usefulness of the result. The guide's 2015 thresholds are historical documentation, not a universal promise for every contemporary item. [S22](#s22)

## 6. Delegation, resources and the player’s attention

**Play Support System (PSS)** offers repeat hunting, assistance during player-controlled combat, and hunting near a chosen position. Settings govern buffs, attack order, return, shopping, storage and routes. HP, MP, weight and hostile attacks can trigger responses. Return and random teleport require their respective scrolls. Settings can be saved to a server repository. [S06](#s06)

The developers' post-Remaster **2019 Talk Day** Q&A acknowledged that PSS convenience could diminish the satisfaction of hands-on fighting. Their proposed response included manual hunting spaces, instances and teamwork, with strategic decisions beyond simply disabling automation. These were stated intentions, not evidence that all the promised experiences shipped or solved the problem. [S16](#s16)

**OpenLegend interpretation:** delegation is useful when players can choose the work they enjoy doing themselves. The relevant design question is what remains interesting to notice, decide and change after routine execution is delegated. Automation's quality, interruption rules and recoverability require validation alongside the activity; neither constant clicking nor unattended accumulation is automatically the desired experience.

## 7. Institutions and shared activity

The current guide reserves pledge creation to a Royal and supports instant, application-based or password admission. Rank determines authority; shared-warehouse access requires a grant. Members contribute to pledge growth and can open different group encounters through contribution, Adena or entry items. Alliances identify friends but do not merge castles, halls or warehouses. Leaving removes pledge chat and warehouse access; possession and succession conditions constrain dissolution or leadership transfer. The guide contains inconsistent buff-level thresholds, so no single current threshold is inferred from it. [S09](#s09)

**OpenLegend interpretation:** belonging becomes substantive when people can make arrangements, delegate responsibility and use shared resources. Those powers also require comprehensible boundaries. An alliance need not erase distinct ownership; a social invitation need not silently authorize withdrawal from communal storage.

A pledge's authorized participants choose a soul-stone encounter, pay its opening cost and enter the portal within its three-minute window. Up to fifty can enter; defeating the summoned boss adds contribution and yields the documented rewards. [S09](#s09) The institution converts accumulated cooperation into a shared occasion, but a narrow entry window can exclude people who helped earn it.

## 8. Seven worked situations

These seven situations reconstruct documented rules; they are not observed playtests. Transfer lessons are hypotheses.

### A. Configure a repeat trip and discover a missing dependency

A player records a route beginning near the warehouse in the town to which the hunt returns, travels to the destination and saves it. They set supplies and return conditions before enabling repeated hunting. If the return scroll is absent, low health does not produce the intended escape; choosing a mismatched return town can add travel costs or disrupt repetition. The result depends on the configuration **and** inventory. [S06](#s06) A useful delegation interface should make those dependencies visible before the task starts.

### B. Attempt a castle capture

The attacking Royal declares war. During the scheduled siege, the surrounding area becomes a combat zone and pledge emblems identify participants. Attackers break the outer gate and destroy the guardian tower; only a Royal can take the exposed crown. Capture sends outsiders back to the starting position, but ownership at the siege's end determines the winner. The holding pledge receives territorial rewards. [S10](#s10) This produces successive shared objectives and a changed place, while the deadline and monopoly also concentrate participation pressure and power.

### C. Improve a companion at a real opportunity cost

A player who values the Elder doll's mana support must weigh keeping that useful item against committing same-grade dolls to synthesis. Failure consumes all but one; the stronger companion is not guaranteed. [S14](#s14) For OpenLegend, distinguish meaningful composition choices from loss that merely pressures further acquisition; reusable behavior should not inherit this gamble by default.

### D. Make a shared house useful to others

After acquiring a clan house, members can return there, recover HP/MP faster, decorate and use its services. A member can open the door for outsiders and expel unwanted nonmembers; access does not transfer ownership. [E09](#e09) The place acquires value through repeated use and hospitality. OpenLegend's shelter work can test that value without importing the upkeep rule described in §9.

### E. Rework an existing rune instead of replacing an identity

The **Elixir Rune** begins with a level-55 quest and has strength, dexterity, constitution, intelligence and wisdom variants. To change its attribute, a player visits the laboratory north of Gludin, buys the appropriate remover from **Poi**, applies it to produce a depleted rune, then chooses the desired result with **Riddle**. Upgrading is another exchange: **Seirun** combines the depleted rune, the applicable book and Adena; the level-70 recipe specifies 500,000 Adena and a faded book. Only one rune slot is available. [S23](#s23) This is a named preparation–transformation–recipient chain. Its positive opportunity is comprehensible reconfiguration; its cost and level gates should be evaluated separately from that flexibility.

### F. Turn a peaceful outing into useful supplies

A character of sufficient level buys a rod and bait from **Marco**, asks **Paul** to enter the Gludin fishing area, chooses a waterside point and starts fishing. The interface shows bait and reel status; catches consume bait. Reels alter available catches and timing, but a different reel cannot replace one with remaining uses. Fish can be sold to **Hang Berry** for Berry currency, then exchanged for supplies such as crystals, equipment or elemental scrolls; some fish instead open for random rewards. [S25](#s25) This provides a quieter alternative with economic consequences. It does not establish deep active fishing skill, and substituting another unattended accumulation loop would not by itself broaden enjoyment.

### G. Prepare for a place, then gain a new way to perceive it

**Eva Kingdom** can be entered through its waterway stairs or a paid teleport. Without underwater breathing, the character loses HP; Eva's Blessing, mermaid scales or underwater boots supply it. A monster-dropped pufferfish grants the temporary condition needed to speak to the hungry whale shark. It sends one player to a timed boss or treasure room. Success can supply **Captain Jack's goggles**, whose temporary vision reveals otherwise hidden nearby creatures. Death or timeout can fail the boss room, with death returning the character to Heine. The pufferfish and goggles also expire after acquisition. [S24](#s24) Preparation changes access, discovery changes perception, and the reward opens another activity. Preserve that relationship while separately testing whether short expiry makes an exploration opportunity feel compulsory.

## 9. Persistence, payment and commercial outcomes

**Persistence belongs to an actual service.** Gamania’s Taiwan Remastered FAQ retained existing accounts, characters and their server after the update, while acknowledging removed items, systems and maps and retaining the subscription-server model. This is continuity with specified exceptions, not an untouched 1998 world or a promise that every regional version has identical rules. [E05](#e05)

The original North American service is a different outcome: NCsoft’s May 2011 announcement, reproduced in full by GameBanshee, scheduled permanent shutdown for June 29, citing regional financial viability. It stopped new accounts and recurring charges, opened the remaining period to existing eligible accounts, and promised unused-time refunds. The reason is an attributed publisher judgment; it does not describe global Lineage failure. [E04](#e04)

**Korean Remastered sells functional advantages.** NC’s April 2019 notice scheduled access without an access pass from May 2 and the introduction of Einhasad’s Favor. This historical change is documented in a full reproduction of the publisher’s announcement. [E07](#e07) The current Einhasad guide describes shop-bought, character-specific 30-day Favor: it adds 100% experience, improves item-drop chances, and adds damage reduction, 100 HP and carrying capacity. Its clock runs while logged out. Separate consumable Blessing is earned through activities or events, or replenished with dragon gems; it supplies stronger benefits, including PvP damage reduction, and depletes with earned experience. The 2025 revision permits additional experience benefit from using both. Cash access, earned alternatives, combat power and time substitution therefore require separate evaluation; this is not a cosmetic-only model. [E08](#e08)

**Regional services differ.** Gamania’s 2026 maintenance notice separately lists Taiwan subscription, free and international services. [E06](#e06) The Classic announcement specified Korea/Taiwan, preliminary free access on February 7 and paid monthly play from February 11, 2026 at KRW 29,700. This is not a worldwide Remastered price. [E12](#e12)

- **Property can outlast a session and still be lost through absence.** The current Korean clan-house article specifies monthly 500,000 Adena upkeep, due by the 10th with grace through the 15th. Nonpayment removes ownership and sends the house to auction without refunding its purchase price. A collectively useful place therefore depends on resources and attendance. [E09](#e09)
- **Movement is a bounded operation.** Taiwan’s July 29–September 2, 2026 migration window linked standing rules charging 399 Beanfun points, with selected server groups and limited slots. Carried equipment and backpack items move; unwanted belongings can remain in the source account warehouse. Successful migration requires a new name, and clan conditions can block it. This expired window does not establish unrestricted current migration or the transfer of every stored asset and relationship. [E10](#e10)
- **Inactive and lost state have specific policies.** Korea’s current operating policy permits deletion of characters below level 15 when both the last login and access-pass expiry were at least six months ago, with advance notice. This is conditional, not an automatic deletion of every character after six months. The recovery table excludes inactivity-policy deletions and failed-enchantment losses. [E13](#e13)

**OpenLegend inference:** retain the appeal of useful collective places and consequential cooperation; test whether those activities remain enjoyable and resumable without copying upkeep schedules or paid combat modifiers. PT05/PT06 and CO01–CO03 already own continuity, travel, payment and lapse; SH12/DG13 own practical shelter. This case adds evidence, not a second persistence roadmap or a development-save compatibility obligation.

## 10. Production, presentation and audience access

**Origins and regional assumptions.** In an August 2001 interview, NCsoft CEO Taekjin Kim described two years of development and ten months of pilot testing before September 1998 commercialization. He emphasized accessible controls, communication and coordinated conflict. He also acknowledged that US content and business arrangements needed adaptation. These are the publisher's contemporary account and intentions, not proof that the US service subsequently met those needs. The interview's staffing figure concerns the new American organization, not the original game's entire development team. [P01](#p01)

Designer/programmer **Jake Song** connected Lineage to his enthusiasm for **NetHack** and Il-sook Shin's manhwa: dungeon exploration became part of a shared feudal world. In his 2006 recollection, establishing local support and industry relationships mattered to the American expansion. He also recalled working with **Patrick Doane** on the Macintosh port. This is a useful distinction between transporting software and building an operation that understands its audience; the latter lesson is our interpretation, not a measured explanation of regional performance. [P02](#p02)

**Actual promotion and service.** Former sales leader **Jeon Dong-jin's** 2006 interview describes targeting PC cafés when home connectivity was costly. The accompanying report identifies outreach around Sinchon and cooperation with café associations, plus blood-pledge events and communication with pledge leaders. Jeon recounts creating a customer-service process after unresolved complaints brought players directly to the office. These were concrete distribution, support and community activities. His claim that accumulated relationships and shared history kept people attached is attributed testimony, not a retention study. The article's introductory 1997 service wording is not used to replace its own September 1998 commercialization timeline. [P03](#p03)

**Maintaining recognizable play while changing its presentation.** The official second Remastered test notice in January 2019 records feedback about awkward UI, small characters and sliding-looking movement. It proposed classic/simple interfaces, zoom options, movement adjustments and improvements to PSS automation. It separately acknowledged the emotional response to automation and problems stabilizing replaced engines and renewed graphics. The announced second test was delayed from January 29 to January 31. These are documented problems and proposed test changes; the notice alone does not verify their eventual success. [P05](#p05)

For 2024's **UNLIMIT**, design director **Jo Su-gon** described about a year of preparation alongside weekly updates. Character and environment artists **Kim Seong-hwan** and **Kim Jin-uk** worked with design, effects, animation and programming. **Girtas's** size challenged the quarter-view camera; entrances and battle cutscenes helped reveal him. Artists retained recognizable dragon colors while revising forms, and repeated effects work made new avoidable ground hazards legible in 2D. The team connected **Antharas's** retreat to the story, rather than showing a permanent death that contradicted his return. This provides a concrete production pattern: art, behavior, camera and narrative were revised together. Their favorable account of player response remains developer testimony. [P04](#p04)

**OpenLegend interpretation.** A world can keep a recognizable identity while changing how people reach, understand and enjoy it. Preserve useful cues and familiar relationships; qualify the complete activity after changing presentation or delegation. Café distribution, promotion and customer support also show why audience access and ordinary service belong beside the feature itself. These observations strengthen existing discovery, first-playable and operating-cost work; they do not select a particular engine or introduce a marketing-service feature.

**Audio evidence is historical.** Wiley described sword/arrow attacks, creature groans and lightning as basic effects, with movement and battle music. He found sound sparse, but less consequential than companions and play. [R02](#r02) No contemporary listening or live-client presentation test was conducted.

## 11. Reception and continuation

### Historical reception: what the social world did and did not overcome

These five assessments concern the early English-language original service. They expose different experiences of its entry barrier and pledge play; they do not measure the later Korean/Taiwanese audience or 2026 Remastered sentiment. Dates and actual actions matter more here than a pooled score.

**Ron Dulin, GameSpot — original English service; surviving page stamped May 17, 2006.** Dulin found repeated clicking slow and hard to read, with animation sometimes continuing after a death message. Pets improved combat, but weakening an animal and then dragging meat and entering its quantity during the fight made acquiring one cumbersome. He valued expanding areas, equipment assembly and pledge sieges, yet argued that early frustration obstructed those attractions. Random item loss could erase something expensive, while near-identical character models weakened visible progress. His warning that readers might quit during the trial is a prediction, not a reported cancellation or retention measurement. [R01](#r01)

**Galen Wiley, Inside Mac Games — August 28, 2003; Mac client in the original desktop service.** Wiley actually moved from solo questing into the OSX pledge. Companions enabled harder monsters, better experience and valuable equipment; this changed his assessment of otherwise simple play. He distinguished beautiful painted artwork from jerky movement and low-resolution presentation. Limited appearance choices disappointed him, and soloing became stale. His recommendation depended on finding a group and committing time; promised months of entertainment are a value judgment, not documented months of his own retention. This is a concrete account of social access improving play, rather than proof that every newcomer could find that access. [R02](#r02)

**kdribbs, Everything2 — May 30, 2002; one month of original-service trial play.** The author explicitly said they were not subscribing after the trial. Helpful players and the siege idea appealed, but the siege actually observed at Kent involved prolonged waiting at its entrance. Newly purchased boots were promptly lost on death; experience loss made advancement feel reversible. Finding hunting partners was difficult, while pets became practically important for solo hunting. In this account, killing lawful characters made the killer chaotic. An attacker provoked the author’s dog to make its owner liable for a kill, so another character could attack without penalty; the author escaped by teleporting both away. The account supplies a stated subscription decision and concrete incidents, without proving the frequency of either grievance across the service. [R03](#r03)

**fayzex, GameFAQs — December 12, 2002; three days of PC trial play.** The reviewer liked the possibility of coordinated castle warfare but doubted access to a sufficiently organized group. Repetitive fighting, indistinguishable characters and poor animation dominated the actual early impression. The essay recommends avoiding the game, but does not establish a completed trial, a paid subscription cancellation or experienced endgame mastery. Its reported price and nationality generalizations are not reliable market evidence. Read it as a limited newcomer reaction: the attractive group activity was not enough to justify the perceived entry cost. [R04](#r04)

**Asgald, Neoseeker — June 7, 2003; PC.** Asgald disliked both repeated clicking and the Ctrl-assisted automatic attack alternative. Shared character appearances and sluggish animation made advancement feel visually monotonous. Unlike Wiley, the author did not find siege combat a compensating pleasure: standing, attacking and being attacked from behind felt uninteresting. They report turning off sound and music after five minutes, an actual adaptation to dissatisfaction. The review supplies no playtime total or explicit quit/return history; its negative verdict should not be converted into either. [R05](#r05)

The disagreement concerns whether relationships and collective goals redeem the moment-to-moment actions. It is not resolved by popularity. A short supplemental account from **Antman8272, November 11, 2002**, reports almost a year of play and values community, changing surroundings and varied interactions despite weak graphics. That is actual self-reported continuation, but its brevity excludes it from the five substantial assessments. [R06](#r06) **Design interpretation:** make cooperation improve what a player can do, make joining that cooperation practicable, and verify whether ordinary actions remain enjoyable before collective prestige arrives. Loss and political consequence require legible responsibility; community appeal does not by itself excuse confusing feedback or involuntary reversal.

**Steam access limit.** The original title has a historical Steam record, app **29520**, identified as _The Blood Pledge_. [R07](#r07) On October 28, 2026 its store URL redirected to Steam's front page. The accessible English “Most Helpful (Week)” review panel returned no review bodies; all-time/all-language attempts were inaccessible. No helpful-positive/negative sample, playtime or Steam sentiment percentage is asserted. [R08](#r08)

## 12. External creation: a sustained legacy-server scene

**R15 applicability: yes, for the original PC game's legacy emulation ecosystem; current Remastered client modification is not established.** The l1j-en project traces maintained forks and translations back through its 2008 naming and later revisions. [M01](#m01) Its historical server catalog records several differently tuned communities. [M02](#m02) L1Justice publishes its own modified rules and dated 2026 maintenance. [M03](#m03) [M04](#m04) This combination demonstrates sustained authorship and use beyond a lone editor. The operator's displayed online-account counter is corroborating self-report, not audited unique-player measurement. The repository name `classic` must not be confused with NC's separate 2026 Classic service.

### What creators change

**l1j-en is community server code, not NC's original server source or an official SDK.** Its README targets the final US client and a separate 3.63 branch. [M01](#m01) Configuration and database edits change rules/data; deeper changes require Java. Ant builds the server with a MySQL-compatible database. Server portability does not establish client support. [M05](#m05)

The project history identifies **Smitty** with the 3.63 effort and **Tricid** with optimization and client work; these dated descriptions include unfinished projects, not a claim that a replacement client shipped. The 2021 About page explicitly says the team no longer runs an official public server, superseding the older server list's wording. [M07](#m07)

L1Justice illustrates a server operator's separate authorship: altered advancement and loot rates, selected changes to spells, and web market/reference tools. Its staff describes spending more than a year developing and testing its variant. That is their account, not an audited budget. The project says it does not trade donations for items. [M08](#m08) A separate guide documents **runes** obtained through Hardin's quest: a sealed reward requires Hugrint's Breath of Magic before expiry, and the equipped rune then has its own use timer. This is a concrete customized progression loop, not a rule imported into Remastered. [M10](#m10)

The **l1j-en launcher** is a distinct C# project connecting a compatible client to a selected server. A launcher, server implementation and customized world's definitions solve different problems; none alone delivers a complete playable creation. [M11](#m11)

### A creation-to-play workflow

**Constructed example based on the published tools:** an operator makes a small practice world with faster early advancement and a scheduled castle siege. This illustrates authoring and delivery; it was not reproduced here.

1. **Choose a compatible foundation.** Select an exact l1j-en revision and client target. [M01](#m01) Build the Java server and initialize its matching database. The setup guide packages the executable JAR with configuration, data, maps, libraries and startup scripts. Older Java instructions conflict with the newer README; an edit date does not validate every paragraph. [M05](#m05)
2. **Author a bounded change.** `RateXp52` distinguishes early progression from the general experience multiplier. `WarTime` and `WarInterval` expose siege duration and spacing. The FAQ explains enabling wars through `checkwartime` and setting the castle's `war_time`; server timezone affects the schedule. A change therefore spans rules and stored event state, rather than just a cosmetic file. [M06](#m06) [M09](#m09)
3. **Preview and investigate failures.** Start an isolated test world and check progression, announcements and the encounter with compatible characters, including late arrivals. This proposed check was not performed. Documented failures include outdated database definitions, misplaced maps and incompatible clients; a build log alone cannot establish correct play. [M05](#m05)
4. **Package and deliver.** Publish a revision-tagged change with configuration/database differences, attribution and client dependency. Contributors submit focused SQL changes, not whole database dumps. [M06](#m06) Recipients configure their connection, launch the matching server and use compatible clients/connectors. This proposed package follows the documented dependencies; it is not a one-click marketplace. [M05](#m05)
5. **Update or remove deliberately.** An operator must reconcile overlapping configuration/code changes and keep database definitions aligned with Java behavior. There is no universal plugin load order that resolves those conflicts. Removing the faster rate changes future advancement; it does not by itself undo experience or castle ownership already earned. That latter consequence is an inference from changing rules separately from stored world state. [M09](#m09) [M12](#m12)

Variant-specific problems belong with the operator; reproducible upstream defects go to l1j-en’s issue tracker. [M06](#m06)

A particularly revealing compatibility defect is documented in the current server configuration: older clients can fail with contemporary dates, so `GameTimeShift` changes the time reported to the client while leaving actual server time alone. This is a narrow legacy workaround, not evidence that these clients support Remastered or that OpenLegend should undertake comparable development-save compatibility. [M13](#m13)

### Distribution, trust and incentives

Discovery runs through repositories, documentation, server sites and forums. L1Justice's **BeanBags** published an October 5, 2026 update fixing dragon-raid admission and changing pets; the terse note is evidence of maintenance, not a measured performance result. The older l1j-en catalog rejected pay-for-power listings and accepted voluntary hosting donations, while the project's About page describes limited volunteer support. These are distinct community policies, not universal creator-market rules or verified income. [M02](#m02) [M04](#m04) [M07](#m07)

The README and checked `COPYING` header identify GPL version 2, while older wiki prose refers to version 3. [M01](#m01) [M06](#m06) [M14](#m14) A recipient must inspect the actual artifact's licensing; this dossier does not resolve every contribution's provenance. Neither an open-source emulator nor a server calling the original game “abandonware” establishes permission to redistribute NC's client, artwork or trademarks. Source access, asset rights and permission to operate a service remain separate. [M03](#m03)

The Korean operating policy effective April 15, 2026 classifies modifying or adapting client/server programs among unauthorized-program activities. Community availability therefore does not establish official-service approval. [E13](#e13)

**Transfer to OpenLegend.** Make the smallest useful creation deliverable with its dependencies, authority and destination requirements; let someone else actually use it and understand changes. Keep supported world definitions separate from engine behavior and make bug ownership intelligible. This strengthens DG12/PACK01–03 and the existing authored-world boundary. It does not adopt executable plugins, third-party emulation, unlimited hosting, or legacy development-save migration. The [current development policy](../../../../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy) remains controlling.

## 13. Transfer to OpenLegend and critique

The strongest opportunity is **a world worth using together**: ordinary places and equipment can support chosen work, exploration and relationships, while collective action can change a service or shared objective. The caution is equally concrete: interesting institutions do not rescue unpleasant controls, opaque losses or inaccessible groups. Historical reception supports that disagreement; current commercial strength does not settle it.

**Engine and world boundary.** Safe authority, state ownership, delivery and observability are reusable requirements. Tax rates, siege calendars, destructive enhancement, clan upkeep and paid combat bonuses are this game's choices. Its prescribed PSS behavior does not establish general-purpose autonomous people, and legacy Java server editing does not establish a supported OpenLegend executable-plugin interface. Population, simulation and operating costs require measurement through [existing scale qualification](../../../../docs/maintainers/product-scalability.md#ps08--broader-experience-and-scale-qualification); neither concurrency claims nor decades of service prove a cheap general simulation.

**Review judgment.** Keep the positive links between preparation, perception, cooperation and useful places. Retain the regional failure and historical frustration evidence without turning them into a universal “Lineage failed” or “payment explains everything” story. No extra feature specification or new task family is needed: the existing owners above already provide the route. Public service commitments and customer recovery policies remain separate from OpenLegend's [development-save policy](../../../../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy).
