## Identity, maps and delivered scope

Medieval Dynasty combines personal survival and crafting with settlement management and family succession. Render Cube develops the game and Toplitz publishes it. The original Valley is a solo map; Oxbow supports solo or online cooperative play. The player personally gathers, hunts, travels and builds, then recruits residents and organizes production while preparing a successor. This is a different perspective from directing households solely through a strategy-game camera. [Current Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1129580/Medieval_Dynasty/).

The latest public PC patch verified before the cutoff is **2.7.0.4, dated 7 October 2026**. Among its fixes are Hunting Set crafting access for clients when the host owns it and removal of Exquisite/Legendary items from Market Stalls. These notes matter when discussing the newest paid gear and selling systems. September's Wild Game update, version 2.7.0.2, already delivered fifteen legendary animals, their trophies and trophy wall decorations. These encounters belong to the free update; the Hunting Set is a separate purchase. [Public patch 2.7.0.4](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/25776186/), [Wild Game release](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/25343507/).

The Hunting Set supplies two outfits and decorated hunting weapons crafted using materials from legendary animals. The Exquisite Pack, released in December 2025, concerns appearance and settlement presentation through hairstyles, clothing and emblems. They should not be confused with the original game's core survival, village or dynasty systems. [Hunting Set](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4997500/Medieval_Dynasty__Hunting_Set/), [Exquisite Pack](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4159030/Medieval_Dynasty__Exquisite_Pack/).

The announced Backwood is an additional map with Creative Mode scheduled for **2027**, beyond this dossier's cutoff. Its proposed freedom from money, resources and reputation requirements cannot be presented as an available mode in November 2026. [Official announcement archive](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Medieval+Dynasty&appids=1129580&enddate=1781542931&feed=steam_community_announcements), [developer roadmap](https://rendercube.pl/roadmap-update-2026/).

## Shared settlement and multiplayer boundaries

The developer's maintained FAQ specifies **four participants total**, a common village and host-controlled permissions. This resolves the store's potentially confusing “up to 4 friends” phrasing. The host owns the save and ending the host session ends the server; no dedicated servers, LAN or split-screen are established. The maps are separate starts rather than regions connected by in-game travel. An Oxbow save can move between offline and online play; Valley remains the separate solo campaign. [Developer FAQ, revised June 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4027970229978377158/).

## Work simulation and visible activity

The developer distinguishes ordinary production from field work: most assigned workers produce during scheduled work hours even when their displayed character is still traveling. Farmers actually perform field operations, so home, farm shed and field distance affects their available working time. This prevents a misleading interpretation of every visible walk as a physical delivery bottleneck. [Developer FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4027970229978377158/), [corroborating community explanation, December 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4030222834110711121/).

June 2026's Full Stock update adds visible filling of food/resource storage and residents using decorative workstations and selected furniture. Its laundry, tanning, lumber and butter-churning activity strengthens the appearance of everyday village life. The release distinguishes these decorative uses from a claim that every animation is an independently simulated production or logistics process. [Official Full Stock announcement and changelog](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Medieval+Dynasty&appids=1129580&enddate=1781542931&feed=steam_community_announcements).

## R09 — Production history, resources and constraints

Render Cube's origins matter more than a small-team success slogan. In producer/designer Michał Nowak's April 2021 interview, development began in late May 2019 with six people and had reached sixteen members almost two years later. The studio brought prior AA/AAA graphics outsourcing experience and Unreal experience from *Monster League*. Its medieval subject deliberately emphasized ordinary people's livelihoods; Polish history informed its appearance without assigning the fiction to one documented country, tribe or event. Marketplace assets supplied starting material that the team often altered. Nowak identified profiling, terrain/memory management and level-of-detail work as production tools, and explicitly connected performance to limits on buildings and inhabitants. These are developer descriptions of decisions and tradeoffs, not independent proof of performance on every machine. [Epic Games, original developer interview, 2021-04-13, full article read](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/medieval-dynasty-offers-an-incredibly-deep-open-world-survival-experience?lang=en-US).

The initial concept was not purely an unsolicited independent pitch: CEO Damian Szymański told SII that Toplitz approached Render Cube with it and funded production; the team expanded a simpler simulator proposal into survival, settlement building and RPG systems. In the same July 2021 interview he said Render Cube had acquired the IP in May 2021. This describes an evolving publisher/developer partnership rather than establishing every later contract's terms. [SII, original CEO interview explicitly labeled promotional investor material, 2021-07-28, full relevant body read](https://www.sii.org.pl/14634/aktualnosci/ze-spolek/odwazny-mix-gatunkowy-uczynil-z-medieval-dynasty-globalny-hit-spolka-jest-w-drodze-na-newconnect-wywiad-z-damianem-szymanskim-prezesem-render-cube.html).

Current support is a continuing production commitment. The June 11, 2026 publisher announcement delivered the free *Full Stock* update for PC and current-generation consoles and published a roadmap extending to Q2 2027. Its studio biography dates Render Cube to 2012 and describes the earlier outsourcing business, including work commissioned by CD Projekt, Techland and Platige Image. The biography's imprecise “over 30” employee count should not be turned into an exact current staffing allocation. The document does not provide the number of staff assigned to Medieval Dynasty, an update budget, or a complete contractor list. [Toplitz/Render Cube press release, 2026-06-11, full body read](https://www.gamespress.com/New-Full-Stock-Update-For-Medieval-Dynasty-Adds-Fresh-Options-To-Enhan).

**Production interpretation:** an art-services background, reusable engine knowledge and a tightly recognizable setting plausibly reduced the difficulty of producing an attractive world. They did not eliminate simulation costs. Building/inhabitant limits expose the practical boundary between the fantasy of an expanding settlement and the cost of running it. Subsequent multiplayer and platform support broaden the number of configurations requiring maintenance. This is an interpretation of the documented staffing and constraints, not a measured explanation of sales.

## R10 — Positioning, launch sequence and distribution

The initial pitch combined a medieval life fantasy with family, work and settlement growth. Toplitz's August 20, 2020 announcement set Early Access for September 17 and explicitly invited player feedback to influence development. The publisher presented the game as another entry in its Dynasty publishing series. That branding connects its sales channel to an existing publishing identity; it does not establish shared developers, technology or a shared narrative universe with the other Dynasty games. [Toplitz, original announcement, 2020-08-20, indexed article body read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-soon-to-launch-into-early-access.html?page_n167=15).

Version 1.0 arrived September 23, 2021 on Steam, Epic, GOG and Microsoft Store, including PC Game Pass. The launch announcement advertised a thirty-hour Steam-page livestream and described existing Let's Play activity. Its historical PC list price was £26.99 / €29.99 / US$34.99. A Digital Supporter Edition added soundtrack, map, artbook and other digital extras. These are specific marketing and packaging mechanisms, not evidence of how many viewers bought the game or of realized average price. [Toplitz, original 1.0 launch release, 2021-09-23, indexed full article text read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/begin-your-life-in-the-middle-ages-with-medieval-dynasty-out-today-on-pc.html?clear_privacy_settings=1%3Fclear_privacy_settings%3D1&page_n167=11).

The October 6, 2022 announcement marked the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S release. Toplitz called it its largest console release to that date, which is a publisher characterization rather than an independently ranked industry fact. The same statement reported more than one million Steam units sold before this platform expansion. The evidence supports a staged rollout from PC to current-generation consoles, not a simultaneous original launch. [Toplitz, original console launch release, 2022-10-06, indexed article text read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-release-date-for-console-versions-announced-kopie.html?page_n167=5).

Further platform/co-op/VR chronology is being verified for the completed draft. Neither an announced future map nor a separately developed VR title should be counted as shipped content in the native game.

## R11 — Historical commercial evidence and limits

The July 2021 CEO interview reported more than 600,000 copies and over 850,000 wishlists, and attributed roughly PLN6m of developer entitlement to the PC Game Pass agreement. It also described profit participation rising from 10% to 25% and then 50%, with the latter effective from December 2020. These are dated management statements with a promotional-investor context. Copies, wishlist entries and a subscription contract are different quantities; none should be added into a single sales count. The interview's claim of recovering investment in two days does not disclose a complete auditable development-and-marketing budget. [SII, original CEO interview, 2021-07-28](https://www.sii.org.pl/14634/aktualnosci/ze-spolek/odwazny-mix-gatunkowy-uczynil-z-medieval-dynasty-globalny-hit-spolka-jest-w-drodze-na-newconnect-wywiad-z-damianem-szymanskim-prezesem-render-cube.html).

Later regulatory sales and company results follow below, with their exact reporting periods and recognized-revenue/consumer-spending distinction. A release-day list price multiplied by lifetime copies would not be a defensible estimate of revenue: discounts, regions, platform shares, refunds, subscription deals and publisher contracts are not known in that calculation.

## R15 — Conditional creation/modding gate: provisional uncertain

An official editor or SDK has not been established. A pinned Steam moderator explanation, updated September 2026, states that official mod support is absent and only a possibility after active development, whose roadmap extends into 2027. It attributes that decision to the original scope and to support/debugging work. The moderator also asks users reporting bugs to identify external editors and changed values. This is a current community-support explanation, not a binding promise that an SDK will arrive. [Steam, pinned moderator post originally dated 2025-08-07, full body read](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/591778884098320977/).

Community tools and content nevertheless exist. Nexus catalog snapshots returned 75 and 80 entries, with zero collections, and the indexed October 2026 catalog includes recently updated Lua/UE4SS utilities alongside saved-game presets and other changes. Those cache-dependent figures describe a small visible catalog, not an exact live total or an installed-user count. They show creation activity; they do not alone prove sustained popular adoption of substantive mods. A named loader and newer script projects are being inspected before setting the final gate. [Nexus Mods catalog, retrieved cached snapshots, accessed 2026-11-10 UTC](https://www.nexusmods.com/games/medievaldynasty).

## Commercial evidence: disclosed developer revenue and returns

**The defensible conclusion is a commercially successful game with sustained sales and a profitable developer, not merely a promising launch.** The latest verified regulatory milestone is **more than 3.5 million gross copies sold through September 30, 2026**, reported on October 14. The issuer attributes its count to publisher and distribution-platform reports and separately records **1.68 million outstanding Steam wishlists**. Gross copies are not net-of-refund units, subscription users or concurrent players; wishlists are not orders. Promotional headlines about “players” should not silently replace the filing's metric. [Render Cube ESPI 8/2026, reproduced in full by Bankier](https://www.bankier.pl/wiadomosc/RENDER-CUBE-S-A-Osiagniecie-3-5-mln-sztuk-brutto-sprzedazy-gry-Medieval-Dynasty-9212801.html).

### A bounded cumulative calculation

The issuer's [Q1 2026 report, p. 43](https://rendercube.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/zal01_RENDER_CUBE_-_raport_za_I_kwartal_2026.pdf) reports historical game-related revenues of PLN **7.449m (2020), 13.400m (2021), 13.943m (2022), 15.459m (2023), and 17.179m (2024)**. Adding the unrounded source amounts to the separately identified 2025 and H1 2026 game-revenue lines gives approximately **PLN 92.54m through June 30, 2026**. This is this dossier's arithmetic sum of reported developer revenues, not an additional company disclosure, cash-flow total, gross retail-sales estimate or profit figure. Later 2026 sales are outside that period.

### Contracts, investment and limits

The Q1 filing describes Render Cube receiving **50% of net game proceeds after distribution costs and recovery of the publisher's production/marketing expenditure**, including the console arrangement. It also records the **€2.5m acquisition of the trademark/IP**, with the final payment in October 2022, and says the game recovered its production cost. These are disclosed contractual/investment facts; the IP purchase is not the original production budget. Earlier contract terms and recoupment timing prevent simply doubling all historical developer revenue into publisher receipts. [Q1 report, pp. 40–43](https://rendercube.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/zal01_RENDER_CUBE_-_raport_za_I_kwartal_2026.pdf).

The inspected accounts do not establish a single all-in lifetime development-and-marketing budget, a complete retail price/channel/refund history, or consolidated lifetime profit across publisher and developer. The current developer revenue share, company margins and acquisition price answer different questions. A precise title ROI or a worldwide consumer-spending range would require missing inputs; no review-based model is substituted for these actual reported results.

**Interpretation:** recurring revenue years after release supports an enduring product business. It does not isolate how much each free update, co-op launch, discount or creator campaign caused. Dependence on one successful title remains a business risk even when current accounts are profitable. A useful OpenLegend lesson is to budget for maintaining and explaining a lasting experience; the evidence does not promise that adding every requested system will reproduce these returns.

### Production-scale update from the accounts

The half-year report records **49 employees / 48.75 full-time equivalents at June 30, 2026** and a transition from B2B contracting toward employment beginning in December 2025. This is company staffing, not a claim that all employees worked exclusively on Medieval Dynasty. A historic interview's small starting team must not be presented as the current production organization. [H1 report, pp. 27 and 33](https://rendercube.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/zal01_Render_Cube_SA_raport_okresowy_polroczny_2026.pdf).

## Five substantive independent written reviews

Full bodies of all five reviews were opened and read. Four cover the PC full-release period in 2021; the fifth reviews the 2022 console release. This is longitudinal publication coverage, not a controlled comparison of the same build. None establishes current co-op behavior or late-generation completion rates.

| Review and context | Specific enjoyment, friction, and continuation evidence |
|---|---|
| [PC Gamer — Jason Coles, 27 September 2021](https://www.pcgamer.com/medieval-dynasty-review/), **74/100**. PC, Windows 10; full-release period; hours and build unreported. | Small tangible improvements made nightly sessions comforting: another building or resident created visible progress. Repetition could also become advancement grind, exemplified by crafting many spoons before earning a more useful tool. Managing residents added purpose beyond individual survival, and adjustable survival pressures supported different pacing. The knowledge menu and scattered information weakened teaching. His positive response rests on modest cumulative achievements rather than excitement or a major story payoff. |
| [GamePressure — Giancarlo Saldana, 22 September 2021](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/medieval-dynasty-review-to-heir-is-human/z84a3), **7.5/10**. PC, launch-period assessment; hours/build unreported. | Building and recruiting made direct labor rewarding, and controllable pacing supported a personal approach. Repetitive errands and long trips weakened that rhythm; dialogue and largely unresponsive NPC presentation failed to make the inhabitants equally engaging. Re-equipping tools and getting stuck added separate interface/technical friction. His generational discussion describes the intended possibility of succession, not evidence that he completed an heir transition. |
| [WellPlayed — Edward Darling, 26 September 2021](https://www.well-played.com.au/medieval-dynasty-review/), **8/10**. PC publisher code; approximately **25 hours**, about three in-game years, with a newborn child; exact build unreported. | Creating a village from nothing delivered pride, and delegation eventually freed time for personal goals. Long travel and demanding production dependencies felt laborious. Residents and spouse became socially uninteresting, weakening his motivation to expand after the initial fascination. He anticipated returning for relaxed growth, yet had not reached succession: his infant still needed to mature. Floating NPCs, interaction failure, and disappearing items were contemporary reports, with reloads resolving some issues. |
| [MMORPG.com — Emily Byrnes, 19 October 2021](https://www.mmorpg.com/reviews/not-so-mmo-medieval-dynasty-review-2000123384), **7/10**. PC full-release period; hours/build unreported. | Short seasons created useful preparation pressure while tending land and providing for residents could be calming. Guidance was poorly prioritized, leaving basic water provision to encyclopedia research and outside help. Repeated journeys, long unvoiced exposition, and shallow romance produced tedium rather than attachment. She wanted more protagonist choice. This is historical Valley-era reception: limited character choice and solitary play must not be applied to later Oxbow/co-op options. |
| [Dexerto — Sam Smith, 11 October 2022](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/medieval-dynasty-review-1953961/), **7.5/10**. **Xbox Series S**, console launch; hours/build unreported. | The transition from personally gathering everything to managing a settlement generated accomplishment. Broad settings could make the experience comfortable, but removing pressures also risked monotony; maintaining an established town excited him less than creating it. Conversations soon became skippable and villagers felt functional rather than memorable. Performance and presentation impressed him on his specific console setup. This is a solo console assessment before later multiplayer delivery, not evidence about current console co-op performance. |

### What this critic sample can establish

The sources disagree in useful details: Darling enjoyed the central mystery, while Saldana and Byrnes tired of its delivery; several welcome repetitive work when it produces visible growth, while criticizing repeated errands that interrupt it. Those are preferences about purpose, timing, and presentation rather than a single verdict on whether repetition is good. In particular, the title's generational promise can arrive much later than the early settlement reward: Darling's stated play stage provides a concrete observation, not a measured average time to succession. [WellPlayed](https://www.well-played.com.au/medieval-dynasty-review/) · [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/medieval-dynasty-review-to-heir-is-human/z84a3) · [MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/reviews/not-so-mmo-medieval-dynasty-review-2000123384)
