## Identity, maps and delivered scope

Medieval Dynasty combines personal survival and crafting with settlement management and family succession. Render Cube develops the game and Toplitz publishes it. The original Valley is a solo map; Oxbow supports solo or online cooperative play. The player personally gathers, hunts, travels and builds, then recruits residents and organizes production while preparing a successor. This is a different perspective from directing households solely through a strategy-game camera. [Current Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1129580/Medieval_Dynasty/).

The latest public PC patch verified before the cutoff is **2.7.0.4, dated 7 October 2026**. Among its fixes are Hunting Set crafting access for clients when the host owns it and removal of Exquisite/Legendary items from Market Stalls. These notes matter when discussing the newest paid gear and selling systems. September's Wild Game update, version 2.7.0.2, already delivered fifteen legendary animals, their trophies and trophy wall decorations. These encounters belong to the free update; the Hunting Set is a separate purchase. [Public patch 2.7.0.4](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/25776186/), [Wild Game release](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/25343507/).

The Hunting Set supplies two outfits and decorated hunting weapons crafted using materials from legendary animals. The Exquisite Pack, released in December 2025, concerns appearance and settlement presentation through hairstyles, clothing and emblems. They should not be confused with the original game's core survival, village or dynasty systems. [Hunting Set](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4997500/Medieval_Dynasty__Hunting_Set/), [Exquisite Pack](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4159030/Medieval_Dynasty__Exquisite_Pack/).

The announced Backwood is an additional map with Creative Mode scheduled for **2027**, beyond this dossier's cutoff. Its proposed freedom from money, resources and reputation requirements cannot be presented as an available mode in November 2026. [Official announcement archive](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Medieval+Dynasty&appids=1129580&enddate=1781542931&feed=steam_community_announcements), [developer roadmap](https://rendercube.pl/roadmap-update-2026/).

## Shared settlement and multiplayer boundaries

The developer's maintained FAQ specifies **four participants total**, a common village and host-controlled permissions. This resolves the store's potentially confusing “up to 4 friends” phrasing. The host owns the save and ending the host session ends the server; no dedicated servers, LAN or split-screen are established. The maps are separate starts rather than regions connected by in-game travel. An Oxbow save can move between offline and online play; Valley remains the separate solo campaign. [Developer FAQ, revised June 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4027970229978377158/).

## Work simulation and visible activity

The developer distinguishes ordinary production from field work: most assigned workers produce during scheduled work hours even when their displayed character is still traveling. Farmers actually perform field operations, so home, farm shed and field distance affects their available working time. This prevents a misleading interpretation of every visible walk as a physical delivery bottleneck. [Developer FAQ](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4027970229978377158/), [corroborating community explanation, December 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/4030222834110711121/).

June 2026's Full Stock update adds visible filling of food/resource storage and residents using decorative workstations and selected furniture. Its laundry, tanning, lumber and butter-churning activity strengthens the appearance of everyday village life. The release distinguishes these decorative uses from a claim that every animation is an independently simulated production or logistics process. [Official Full Stock announcement and changelog](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appgroupname=Medieval+Dynasty&appids=1129580&enddate=1781542931&feed=steam_community_announcements).

## R09 — Production history, resources and constraints

Producer/designer Michał Nowak dated development to late May 2019: six people initially, sixteen by his April 2021 interview. Render Cube brought AA/AAA graphics-outsourcing experience and Unreal knowledge from *Monster League*. Its setting emphasizes ordinary livelihoods, drawing on Polish history without representing one documented location or event. The team altered Marketplace assets, used profiling, terrain/memory management and level-of-detail work, and constrained buildings and inhabitants for performance. These are developer descriptions, not independent performance measurements. [Epic Games, original developer interview, 2021-04-13, full article read](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/medieval-dynasty-offers-an-incredibly-deep-open-world-survival-experience?lang=en-US).

In July 2021, CEO Damian Szymański said Toplitz brought the preliminary concept and funded production; Render Cube expanded the simulator into survival, settlement building and RPG systems. He dated the IP deal to May 2021. The regulatory account below distinguishes that agreement from completion of payment in October 2022. [SII, original CEO interview explicitly labeled promotional investor material, 2021-07-28, full relevant body read](https://www.sii.org.pl/14634/aktualnosci/ze-spolek/odwazny-mix-gatunkowy-uczynil-z-medieval-dynasty-globalny-hit-spolka-jest-w-drodze-na-newconnect-wywiad-z-damianem-szymanskim-prezesem-render-cube.html).

His December 2023 retrospective emphasized another part of the relationship: *Monster League* failed financially but demonstrated capability, and Toplitz liked the team's medieval concept and gave it creative freedom. The two accounts describe a collaboration; they do not establish exclusive authorship of the idea. He recalled unpaid outsourcing invoices threatening the studio, learning marketing through failure, and later financing a sound studio, motion-capture equipment and hiring. He stressed cautious growth. His generic budget examples concern the industry, not Medieval Dynasty's disclosed production cost. [MamBiznes, Natalia Kieszek's original founder interview, 2023-12-15, full interview body read](https://mambiznes.pl/porady/mozna-wyprodukowac-gre-minimalnym-kosztem-osiagnac-sukces-damian-szymanski-render-cube/).

Current support is a continuing production commitment. The June 11, 2026 publisher announcement delivered the free *Full Stock* update for PC and current-generation consoles and published a roadmap extending to Q2 2027. Its studio biography dates Render Cube to 2012 and describes earlier outsourcing commissions from CD Projekt, Techland and Platige Image. The release does not disclose the number of staff assigned to Medieval Dynasty, an update budget or a complete contractor list. Use the dated company staffing disclosure in the financial section for current scale, rather than treating the six-person starting team as today's organization. [Toplitz/Render Cube press release, 2026-06-11, full body read](https://www.gamespress.com/New-Full-Stock-Update-For-Medieval-Dynasty-Adds-Fresh-Options-To-Enhan).

September's anniversary release confirms that the free *Wild Game* update and paid *Hunting Set* were delivered on PC and current-generation consoles. It still describes *The Backwood* as forthcoming in 2027. Thus the development record contains both shipped additions and a future commitment; the future map does not expand the current game's assessed content. [Toplitz/Render Cube anniversary press release, 2026-09-22, full body read](https://www.gamespress.com/en-US/Medieval-Dynasty-Celebrates-6th-Anniversary-With-Fresh-Wild-Game-Updat).

**Production interpretation:** prior art and engine experience plausibly supported efficient world creation. Population/building limits nevertheless expose the cost of simulating expansion. Later multiplayer and platform support add configurations to maintain. These are implications of the documented choices, not a measured explanation of sales.

## R10 — Positioning, launch sequence and distribution

The initial pitch combined a medieval life fantasy with family, work and settlement growth. Toplitz's August 20, 2020 announcement set Early Access for September 17 and explicitly invited player feedback to influence development. The publisher presented the game as another entry in its Dynasty publishing series. That branding connects its sales channel to an existing publishing identity; it does not establish shared developers, technology or a shared narrative universe with the other Dynasty games. [Toplitz, original announcement, 2020-08-20, indexed article body read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-soon-to-launch-into-early-access.html?page_n167=15).

The 1.0 campaign included an August 2021 Future Games Show trailer and Gamescom Studio appearances. Toplitz's event announcement specifically credited player and streamer feedback with steering updates during Early Access. This is evidence of a public presentation and feedback strategy; it does not establish an influencer budget, paid sponsorship roster or conversion rate. The linked videos were not watched in this research pass. [Toplitz, original Gamescom announcement, 2021-08-26, article body read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-besieges-gamescom-with-an-exclusive-new-trailer-and-more.html).

Version 1.0 arrived September 23, 2021 on Steam, Epic, GOG and Microsoft Store, including PC Game Pass. The launch announcement advertised a thirty-hour Steam-page livestream and described existing Let's Play activity. Its historical PC list price was £26.99 / €29.99 / US$34.99. A Digital Supporter Edition added soundtrack, map, artbook and other digital extras. These are specific marketing and packaging mechanisms, not evidence of how many viewers bought the game or of realized average price. [Toplitz, original 1.0 launch release, 2021-09-23, indexed full article text read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/begin-your-life-in-the-middle-ages-with-medieval-dynasty-out-today-on-pc.html?clear_privacy_settings=1%3Fclear_privacy_settings%3D1&page_n167=11).

The October 6, 2022 announcement marked the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S release. Toplitz called it its largest console release to that date, which is a publisher characterization rather than an independently ranked industry fact. The same statement reported more than one million Steam units sold before this platform expansion. The evidence supports a staged rollout from PC to current-generation consoles, not a simultaneous original launch. [Toplitz, original console launch release, 2022-10-06, indexed article text read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-release-date-for-console-versions-announced-kopie.html?page_n167=5).

An adapted PS4/Xbox One edition followed on April 20, 2023. The publisher's page was posted April 21 but its dated release body says April 20; retaining that distinction avoids moving the launch by a day. Its then-future co-op plan named PC and current-generation consoles. An older console release is therefore not evidence that the later multiplayer updates shipped there. [Toplitz, original last-generation console release, 2023-04-20 body / 2023-04-21 page, full article body read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-available-now-on-playstation-4-and-xbox-one.html?page_n167=2%3Fclear_privacy_settings%3D1).

The Oxbow/co-op update shipped on Steam on December 7, 2023, with a new map and character creator supporting male and female avatars. Its release statement called co-op a community-requested feature and deferred other platforms. [Toplitz/Render Cube release reproduced by Saving Content, 2023-12-07, full body read](https://www.savingcontent.com/2023/12/07/long-awaited-co-op-mode-arrives-in-medieval-dynasty-today/). Crossplay arrived June 27, 2024 across PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Steam, Epic, GOG and Microsoft Game Pass. [Official update text archived by SteamDB, 2024-06-27](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/14860278/). This produced a second substantial launch proposition years after the original purchase: friends could build together, while the new location also served solo players. The change's exact incremental sales effect is not disclosed.

**VR scope boundary:** *Medieval Dynasty: New Settlement* launched March 28, 2024 for Meta Quest 2, 3 and Pro, with Spectral Games responsible for the VR project. [Toplitz, original VR launch release, 2024-03-28, full article body read](https://www.toplitz-productions.com/news-2388/medieval-dynasty-goes-virtual-out-now-all-new-standalone-vr-experience.html?clear_privacy_settings=1&page_n167=19). Spectral describes a separate game with a new map and story rather than a port. Its undated investor page retains some old future-tense text, so it supports the project's identity, not a reliable current feature timetable. [Spectral Games, developer investor page, body read](https://www.spectralgames.com/for-investors/). VR mechanics, map size and audience figures should consequently not be credited to the native title.

**Why the positioning plausibly works:** the documented pitch makes a broad systems game legible through a concrete aspiration: build a home and sustain a family and community. A village's visual development can supply material for a long Let's Play series, while co-op gives owners an intelligible reason to invite friends. The sequence of showcases, live broadcasts, roadmap releases and platform launches creates recurring occasions to explain the product. This is causal interpretation, not an attribution study. Publisher access, storefront visibility, pricing and platform expansion are competing or complementary explanations for commercial strength. No measured marketing return, campaign acquisition cost or creator-attributed sales total was found.

## R11 — Historical commercial evidence and limits

The July 2021 CEO interview reported more than 600,000 copies, over 850,000 wishlists and roughly PLN6m of developer entitlement from the PC Game Pass agreement. It described profit participation rising from 10% to 25%, then 50% from December 2020. Its claim of recovering investment in two days does not disclose a complete development-and-marketing budget. These are dated management claims, not interchangeable sales measures. [SII, original CEO interview, 2021-07-28](https://www.sii.org.pl/14634/aktualnosci/ze-spolek/odwazny-mix-gatunkowy-uczynil-z-medieval-dynasty-globalny-hit-spolka-jest-w-drodze-na-newconnect-wywiad-z-damianem-szymanskim-prezesem-render-cube.html).

The founder later reported 1.5 million copies across available platforms as of September 30, 2023, explicitly withholding an updated count after co-op's December launch. That distinction prevents attributing the entire milestone to co-op. [MamBiznes, original founder interview, 2023-12-15](https://mambiznes.pl/porady/mozna-wyprodukowac-gre-minimalnym-kosztem-osiagnac-sukces-damian-szymanski-render-cube/).

The financial evidence below preserves the distinction between recognized developer revenue, cash and consumer spending. A release-day list price multiplied by lifetime copies would not be a defensible estimate of revenue: discounts, regions, platform shares, refunds, subscription deals and publisher contracts are not known in that calculation. The historical changes in participation also explain why applying today's contractual percentage retrospectively would misstate receipts. Current regulatory disclosures take precedence over loose promotional uses of “players.”

## Commercial evidence: disclosed developer revenue and returns

**The defensible conclusion is a commercially successful game with sustained sales and a profitable developer, not merely a promising launch.** The latest verified regulatory milestone is **more than 3.5 million gross copies sold through September 30, 2026**, reported on October 14. The issuer attributes its count to publisher and distribution-platform reports and separately records **1.68 million outstanding Steam wishlists as of October 14, 2026**. Gross copies are not net-of-refund units, subscription users or concurrent players; wishlists are not orders. Promotional headlines about “players” should not silently replace the filing's metric. ### Contracts, investment and limits

The Q1 filing describes Render Cube receiving **50% of net game proceeds after distribution costs and recovery of the publisher's production/marketing expenditure**, including the console arrangement. It also records the **€2.5m acquisition of the trademark/IP**, with the final payment in October 2022, and says the game recovered its production cost. These are disclosed contractual/investment facts; the IP purchase is not the original production budget. Earlier contract terms and recoupment timing prevent simply doubling all historical developer revenue into publisher receipts. [Q1 report, pp. 40–43](https://rendercube.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/zal01_RENDER_CUBE_-_raport_za_I_kwartal_2026.pdf).

The inspected accounts do not establish a single all-in lifetime development-and-marketing budget, a complete retail price/channel/refund history, or consolidated lifetime profit across publisher and developer. The current developer revenue share, company margins and acquisition price answer different questions. A precise title ROI or a worldwide consumer-spending range would require missing inputs; no review-based model is substituted for these actual reported results.

**Interpretation:** recurring revenue years after release supports an enduring product business. It does not isolate how much each free update, co-op launch, discount or creator campaign caused. Dependence on one successful title remains a business risk even when current accounts are profitable. A useful OpenLegend lesson is to budget for maintaining and explaining a lasting experience; the evidence does not promise that adding every requested system will reproduce these returns.

## R15 — Conditional creation/modding gate: uncertain popular adoption; community tooling verified

An official editor or SDK has not been established. A pinned Steam moderator explanation, updated September 2026, states that official mod support is absent and only a possibility after active development, whose roadmap extends into 2027. It attributes that decision to the original scope and to support/debugging work. The moderator also asks users reporting bugs to identify external editors and changed values. This is a current community-support explanation, not a binding promise that an SDK will arrive. [Steam, pinned moderator post originally dated 2025-08-07, full body read](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1129580/discussions/0/591778884098320977/).

Community tools and content nevertheless exist. Nexus catalog snapshots returned 75 and 80 entries, with zero collections; the richer indexed October snapshot included new UI/gameplay utilities as well as save files. It showed 275 downloads for *SW_Minimap* and 525 for *SW_SpawnItems*. These are cache-dependent catalog/download observations, not a live total or unique installed players. Their short observation window does not demonstrate sustained broad adoption. [Nexus Mods catalog, cached October 2026 snapshots retrieved on access date](https://www.nexusmods.com/games/medievaldynasty).

Stulu's **Medieval Dynasty Mod Loader (MDML)** is an actual community fork of RussellJerome's UnrealModLoader. Its repository documents Windows 64-bit and DirectX 11 requirements, C++ DLL/core mods, Unreal actor/logic mods and file-overwrite mods. It places per-mod folders and an INI manifest under the game's content directory. The repository's SDK/examples and creation guide are community tooling, not a Render Cube editor. [MDML maintainer repository, README and file tree read](https://github.com/stulu08/Medieval-Dynasty-Mod-Loader). Nexus records an original December 2022 upload and a January 23, 2025 update. That demonstrates multi-year tool maintenance; it does not certify compatibility with the October 2026 game or widespread downstream use. Its download counters were unavailable in the retrieved page. [MDML author listing, indexed metadata read; direct page returned 403](https://www.nexusmods.com/medievaldynasty/mods/30).

The newer **Soleware SW_Minimap**, uploaded by Frotonis in September 2026 and updated October 9, supplies a contrasting real project: an in-game minimap built on UE4SS and shared `SW_Common` code. Its documents distinguish a bundled first install from mod-only updates and warn that bundling again can overwrite existing UE4SS settings. Its changelog records a Valley-position correction and a thread-related crash fix, illustrating actual compatibility work. Modification/reuse requires the author's permission, and redistribution to other sites is prohibited. These are project-specific rules, not a blanket license for all Medieval Dynasty mods. [Author's Nexus project page and indexed installation text, body/changelog/permissions read](https://www.nexusmods.com/medievaldynasty/mods/102).

**Gate judgment:** verified creation and maintenance, but **uncertain sustained popular mod adoption**. A few download observations, many heterogeneous files and one maintained loader do not establish a robust audience for substantial content mods. Accordingly this dossier records the real tools, examples and constraints without asserting a mature creator economy or expanding an unproven ecosystem into a full SDK/workflow case study. Revisit if current unique-user/use data, substantial project histories and independent evidence of repeated community use become available. The transferable issue is the cost of designing support boundaries early; it is not evidence that OpenLegend needs to acquire or execute these tools.

### What this critic sample can establish

The sources disagree in useful details: Darling enjoyed the central mystery, while Saldana and Byrnes tired of its delivery. Their differing responses concern purpose, timing, and presentation rather than establishing one verdict on repetition. Darling's explicit play stage also distinguishes the early settlement reward from the much later possibility of succession; it is not a measured average time to reach an heir. Opened every individual permalink, read the complete selected bodies, and checked its displayed metadata. ## Cross-source findings and implications for Open Legend

### The reward is a place taking shape—and a changing job

The strongest positive through-line in this sample is recognizable progress: a structure, a household, a more effective settlement, or a place with a personally chosen layout. Management becomes attractive when it changes the player's role from doing every task to choosing what the community should do. dog deified enjoys precisely that transition. AccidentalLuddite deliberately delays it; personal work is itself enjoyable. The design implication is to support both styles and make delegation an earned option, rather than assume everyone wants to automate as quickly as possible. These are observed preferences, not a proposed new Open Legend requirement. [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/medieval-dynasty-review/) · [dog deified](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198018579272/recommended/1129580/) · [AccidentalLuddite](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199140598383/recommended/1129580/)

### Separate worthwhile effort, repetitive administration, and broken execution

VincentVaguely accepts carrying and work because progress provides relief; Datucis saw a low-tier money routine outcompeting more elaborate investment. The Mighty Swash's complaint is different again: choosing seasonal priorities may be interesting, while repeatedly entering percentages is burdensome. Those concerns call for distinct investigation—reward balance, policy controls, and readable dependencies. Terpoma's broken-save report belongs to reliability and recovery, not challenge tuning. A researcher should not combine them into a single claim that the game is “too grindy,” or carry their older defects forward without patch evidence. [VincentVaguely](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198010111637/recommended/1129580/) · [Datucis](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198054343648/recommended/1129580/) · [The Mighty Swash](https://steamcommunity.com/id/themightyswash/recommended/1129580/) · [Terpoma](https://steamcommunity.com/id/terpoma/recommended/1129580/)

### A dynasty requires an emotional bridge to the next generation

Darling's newborn after approximately 25 hours and SirChancelot's departure before succession expose a pacing question: what keeps a player invested between founding a successful village and taking over as its heir? The latter still values the experience; leaving before succession is not equivalent to regretting the purchase. intorpere wanted a world that changed meaningfully across generations. The research hypothesis is that succession needs intermediate relationships, changes, and choices that matter before the transfer itself. This sample contains **no verified account of completing an heir handover**, so it cannot evaluate the handover experience or estimate its completion rate. [WellPlayed](https://www.well-played.com.au/medieval-dynasty-review/) · [SirChancelot](https://steamcommunity.com/id/LuckyChance7/recommended/1129580/) · [intorpere](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198007987235/recommended/1129580/)

### Co-op changes the social reward, while scope still matters

Ekko_One describes companions transforming the same slow work into fun; VincentVaguely enjoys both modes. SirChancelot nevertheless wanted strategic conflict after the shared village stabilized, while Viper's return did not meet expectations formed by other survival games. Co-op can supply conversation, shared accomplishment, and divided labor without replacing a missing reason to continue for a particular audience. That is an inference from these contrasted accounts, not evidence that multiplayer inherently fixes pacing. It also leaves host availability, network behavior, and current platform parity to the primary-source technical section. [Ekko_One](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198013958551/recommended/1129580/) · [VincentVaguely](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198010111637/recommended/1129580/) · [SirChancelot](https://steamcommunity.com/id/LuckyChance7/recommended/1129580/) · [Viper4115](https://steamcommunity.com/id/viper4115/recommended/1129580/)

### Atmosphere needs inhabitants and encounters that fit it

The scenic world does not automatically create attachment to residents: the historical critics distinguish beautiful environments from thin social responses. Likewise, Onion Crust's objection is primarily tonal—the animals feel incompatible with the grounded setting—not simply a request for lower difficulty. These observations suggest evaluating whether routine interactions and hazards reinforce the intended daily-life fantasy. They do not establish that all current NPC dialogue, wildlife behavior, or combat is defective. [GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/medieval-dynasty-review-to-heir-is-human/z84a3) · [MMORPG.com](https://www.mmorpg.com/reviews/not-so-mmo-medieval-dynasty-review-2000123384) · [Onion Crust](https://steamcommunity.com/id/OnionCrust/recommended/1129580/)

## Source register and boundaries

- A [GearsRealm article displaying 2026 publication/update dates](https://gearsrealm.com/medieval-dynasty-review/) was also read in full. It does not identify platform, build, hours, or a tested update, so its date alone cannot establish a current-build re-review; it is not used to certify current feature availability.
- [Opium Pulses](https://www.opiumpulses.com/article/767/medieval-dynasty-review) displays a 2024 date but describes multiplayer as forthcoming in December. This timing mismatch makes it unsuitable as clear evidence of post-co-op reception; it is not counted among the five reviews.
- Critic references to bandit behavior, sleep consequences, obsolete travel options, or historical production recipes are not used as authoritative current rules. The dossier's official mechanics and update record take precedence.
- Steam individual bodies and metadata were retrieved from their public pages. Research captures are intermediate working evidence; comments and developer replies were not counted as extra independent player reviews. No aggregate review score is used to infer how common any theme is.
- Limits: helpfulness ranking favors visible older accounts; English-only sampling misses other communities; unknown settings, maps, mods, platform paths, and review edits prevent controlled comparisons. Current total hours cannot verify continuous engagement, an heir transition, or absence of problems. The critics do not provide a current co-op benchmark, and recent Steam opinions do not establish current bug incidence.

## OpenLegend applications and boundaries

The strongest research question is **when useful personal work becomes a life worth continuing**. Building a house, feeding others and establishing a workshop can give a gathering action a visible purpose. Maintaining a large settlement or waiting for an heir can also lengthen the interval between meaningful decisions. These are candidate lessons from the systems and contrasting reception above, not adopted OpenLegend requirements. The [playable-game priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) remain the project scope.

| Pattern to investigate | Concrete OpenLegend question | Existing owner and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work produces something personally wanted | Can a player complete a useful meal, tool or shelter improvement soon enough to appreciate the result, then choose a more ambitious project? Repeating an action is more defensible when its product changes available plans. | [First-playable survival](../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-survival.md), [practical competence](../../../../docs/worlds/base/practical-competence.md). Numerical unlocks should not be assumed to justify arbitrary repetitive crafting. |
| Help changes the player's use of time | Can a willing person contribute a complementary task, freeing the player to explore, invent or spend time with someone? Independent people are not automatic worker slots; this comparison does not adopt ownership or universal obedience. |
| A home holds accumulated consequences | Does returning reveal useful changes, signs of care, and opportunities to act with other residents? Can decoration express a person rather than only raise a settlement statistic? | [Editable shelters](../../../../docs/projects/editable-shelters-feature-spec.md), [persistent objects](../../../../docs/maintainers/persistent-objects.md). Place attachment can emerge at a household scale without a full village-management layer. |
| A shared world needs clear continuity | Do participants know who controls the session, what persists, and what happens when someone leaves? | Long horizons need near-term meaning | Does a durable project provide intermediate rewards before its eventual legacy? Is choosing to finish, settle down, or begin anew a satisfying option? | [Game priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md), [playtest plan](../../../../docs/projects/first-playable-playtest-plan.md). **Visible activity and actual causality must be distinguished.** Medieval Dynasty's documented difference between ordinary abstract production and physically performed farming is a useful comparison with Manor Lords' logistics emphasis. A lively village can be valuable even when every animation is not the economic calculation. For OpenLegend, show the real cause of success or interruption and make independent characters' meaningful actions consequential. This dossier does not establish either game's proprietary implementation or OpenLegend's simulation capacity.

**Offer a reason to use prosperity.** Delegation can become rewarding freedom, but a fully supplied village can also exhaust the initial goal. A useful follow-on activity might be exploration, hospitality, experimentation or relationships; it should be valuable in its own right. The reception evidence supports asking what the player wants next, not asserting that an endless technology tree or rising upkeep will solve continuation.

**Treat inheritance as one form of continuity.** A player's craft, relationships, place and knowledge can outlast one immediate challenge without requiring the entire Medieval Dynasty family model. OpenLegend's [development-save policy](../../../../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy) remains distinct from any eventual fictional persistence or succession rules. This research creates no promise of save compatibility, demographic simulation or implementation work.
