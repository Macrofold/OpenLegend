# Path of Exile 2 — full research dossier

**G11 · Full pass completed September 25, 2026.** Major systems and R01–R14 are addressed below. This is the sequel, not a relabeling of the [original game's dossier](path-of-exile.md). [Preserved comparison](../games/path-of-exile-path-of-exile-2-and-diablo-iv.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md). Early-access changes, future promises and historical criticism are distinguished. No personal gameplay, complete-video viewing or private code audit is claimed.

## 1. An unfinished release has a current game and a future promise

The reproduced developer FAQ identifies **Forbidden Rites / 0.5.5** as the September 4, 2026 event league. Joining creates a character in a fresh economy; Runes of Aldur continues separately. **Duelist and 1.0 are planned for December 11**, not already delivered on this research date. [Q01](#q01)

The player explores hostile areas, fights, chooses equipment and skills, improves a character and then pursues an endgame build. The campaign begins with an Exile escaping execution amid corruption spreading through Wraeclast. Alessandro Fillari's launch-era review distinguishes satisfying build construction from encounters whose abrupt difficulty interrupts that progress. [Q09](#q09)

**Interpretation:** the immediate promise is purposeful action, while a longer project gives acquired objects meaning. A new league does not erase every old character. Likewise, a public database containing an unreleased class or area is not proof players can use it. Research the available activity and label the future promise separately.

## 2. Combat emphasizes commitment and combinations

In Ken Allsop's 2023 interview, Jonathan Rogers explains that dodge-canceling and moving or retargeting during supported casts let the team design longer actions without surrendering all player control. He also describes Uncut Gems as a way to choose an eligible skill rather than wait for precisely that gem to drop. These are original design explanations, not a claim that every skill has identical cancellation rules. [Q10](#q10)

**Worked decision:** begin a powerful action during a plausible opening, then cancel it when the threat changes. The player gives up the anticipated payoff but preserves another opportunity to act. Responsiveness need not mean every useful attack is instantaneous; it can mean being able to revise a costly commitment.

Allsop's launch play account supplies a concrete **Warrior/Titan** composition: **Volcanic Fissure** lines erupt when struck by slams; **Leap Slam** supplies movement and a trigger; aftershocks can repeat surrounding effects. He also values the short skill demonstrations and the ability to leave recommended supports for a broader palette. [Q11](#q11)

**Interpretation:** an ability has a role in a sequence, not only isolated damage. A movement action can become an activation method, and a persistent effect changes where another action is valuable. The published example is an attributed historical build, not an independently reproduced current optimum.

## 3. An action is composed in the Skills panel

Support gems sit on the skill they modify rather than inheriting the original game's equipment-link arrangement. Current game-text references distinguish category restrictions within a skill from narrower global restrictions on exact Lineage supports and certain persistent-buff supports. Meta gems can contain skills and supports but cannot contain another Meta gem. An old universal one-copy-per-character description is incomplete. [Q02](#q02)

**Unleash** accumulates Seals and repeats a supported spell when cast, with reduced repetition damage and exclusions for skills with cooldowns or an existing Seal mechanism. [Q03](#q03)

**Worked choice:** let a spell prepare while moving or doing something else, then use its accumulated repetitions during an opening. Continuous use and delayed use have different value. More repetitions are not unconditional free damage; an ineligible spell does not acquire the behavior because its description sounds compatible.

**Interpretation:** acquisition, compatibility and composition are separate steps. A larger palette helps only when the user understands which operation applies to which action. A nesting restriction can preserve comprehensibility while leaving considerable room for combinations.

### Weapon sets change more than the held model

Two sets can carry different items, dedicated passive allocations and amounts of available **Spirit**. Skills automatically select a required set or use an assigned one. Spirit maintains supported persistent effects; it is not merely another name for mana. [Q04](#q04)

**Worked consequence:** triggering an action can change equipment and therefore the budget supporting another effect. Owning the necessary items is not enough; the active configuration matters. This permits specialization but creates an explanation problem when an apparently enabled effect stops working.

In a September 2025 thread, **Chillyjam** reported a Spirit skill not functioning. **nil0tan** described resolving a similar issue by correcting its weapon-set assignment, and the original poster acknowledged the help. This is a reported diagnosis, not proof every Spirit problem has that cause. [Q05](#q05)

## 4. Recovery can be automatic without being unplanned

**Charms** trigger under specified conditions and consume charges. A **Thawing Charm** responds to freezing; other charms address different threats. Limited slots require choosing protection rather than preparing every response. [Q06](#q06)

Life and mana flasks are reusable, charge-limited recovery tools replenished through supported sources such as checkpoints and wells. The current reference also documents checkpoint travel within an area. [Q07](#q07)

**Worked distinction:** the player chooses a defensive response beforehand, then need not perform another reflex input when it activates. Protection still fails if its charges are unavailable or it addresses the wrong threat. A boss and ordinary groups may impose different demands on the same recovery economy.

**Interpretation:** this relocates agency rather than removing it. The important preparation is choosing what to protect against and arranging reliable replenishment. Automatically triggering a sensible effect is different from an agent deciding the entire character's ambition or strategy.

## 5. A familiar currency name conceals a different operation

The sequel's **Chaos Orb** removes one modifier and adds another to a rare item, unlike the original game's full rare-affix reroll. Greater and Perfect variants restrict replacement modifier levels, with exceptions where excluding every tier would otherwise remove a modifier type entirely. [Q08](#q08)

**Worked choice:** risk a useful existing property to improve an item, or preserve the functioning equipment while developing a replacement. Raising a minimum roll threshold does not guarantee the resulting property fits the build. Currency is an executable tool as well as something that can fund another acquisition.

**Omen of Whittling** changes which property is removed: the modifier with the lowest item-level requirement, not necessarily the lowest displayed tier or the one the player values least. The Omen is consumed when used. [Q12](#q12)

**Interpretation:** narrowing randomness is useful only if the selection rule matches the player's intention. A technically correct operation can damage a valued item when the interface encourages a different interpretation. Importing the original game's semantics by name would compound that problem.

## 6. Campaign, specialization and trials create different destinations

The **Third Edict** added Act Four in August 2025: Doryani directs the player toward an ancient weapon, with a ship-based journey through the **Ngamakanui archipelago**. Its islands can be visited nonlinearly. Three temporary Interludes replaced repeating the campaign in Cruel difficulty before endgame. [Q13](#q13)

**Interpretation:** progression can reveal more of a world instead of demanding a repeat of the same route. Nonlinear destinations also allow a player to pursue a preferred opportunity while retaining an authored reason for traveling. The eventual final acts remain distinct from these temporary bridges.

Ascendancy Trials add specialized tests. **Trial of the Sekhemas** uses Honour alongside ordinary survival; repeated hits can end an otherwise survivable attempt, and relics modify the challenge. [Q14](#q14) **Trial of Chaos** accumulates chosen difficulties, but the September 2026 update permits leaving and resuming at the same room and supplies per-room reward chests. [Q15](#q15)

**Worked comparison:** a heavily protected build can still be poorly suited to a test that penalizes repeated contact. A player may need a different approach rather than more of the same defense. Allowing a pause removes a real-life scheduling cost without removing the encounter's tactical problem.

## 7. Endgame items configure risk, access and another attempt

**Return of the Ancients / 0.5**, released May 29, 2026, rebuilt the Atlas around additional storylines, an allocatable Atlas tree and specialized progression. Older launch descriptions cannot be assumed to describe that entire endgame. [Q16](#q16)

**Waystones** select area level and can be modified for difficulty and rewards. The current text ties more modifiers to fewer permitted revivals. Most endgame areas distinguish death limits from ordinary exit/re-entry; after a boss death the map owner can restart the encounter for the party when retries remain. Landmark areas have separate access rules. [Q17](#q17)

**Worked decision:** add more reward-generating danger or preserve enough retries to learn an unfamiliar encounter. A build that can handle ordinary enemies may still be ill-suited to a particular modifier. The map item expresses a chosen risk contract, not merely a key opening a fixed room.

Forbidden Rites adds boss clusters whose defeated bosses appear again in later Ritual waves. **Sacred Blooms** introduce Wildwood areas, where collected Wisps increase later monsters' difficulty and reward. Party members now receive their own limited Ritual reward options. [Q15](#q15)

**Interpretation:** a familiar encounter becomes an ingredient in another encounter. Choosing further amplification can be interesting when the player can understand what will recur. A reward shared through participation also need not be identical to giving every person unrestricted control of the host's progression.

## 8. Group play, trade and loss have explicit boundaries

The console design provides shared-screen couch co-op, cross-play and cross-progression. Both players can use their respective side of the inventory interface rather than wait for a single full-screen menu. The initial announcement's planned content counts are not the current shipped inventory. [Q18](#q18)

**Interpretation:** simultaneous access to the same game is not sufficient cooperation. A second player needs a workable interface during ordinary preparation, not only during combat. These companions are other humans; controllable or persistent minions do not establish an independent social AI system.

**Merchant's Tabs** support asynchronous trade through **Ange**, not the normal stash interface. GGG's support replies distinguish the feature's Act Four prerequisite and its absence in Solo Self-Found. [Q19](#q19)

**Worked contrast:** sell an item through a prepared listing, directly negotiate with another person, or play without trade in SSF. A desirable drop becomes a different opportunity under each arrangement. Shared currency names do not erase the separate progression and entitlement rules of the two Path of Exile games.

The original early-access FAQ promised that characters would persist in a separate Early Access environment rather than enter the eventual launch economy. It also warns that balance changes may disrupt existing builds. The current Forbidden Rites Hardcore variation migrates a dead character to its corresponding Standard event league. [Q20](#q20) [Q15](#q15)

**Interpretation:** keeping the character record, preserving its exact effectiveness and keeping access to the new economy are different continuity promises. A user can truthfully be told that nothing was deleted while still losing the practical method they had learned. Good update communication addresses both.

## 9. Art, performance and authored people make the combat particular

Fillari's review connects the grim environments and grotesque encounters with the satisfaction of a functioning loadout, while finding some progression blocks excessively harsh. [Q09](#q09) **Interpretation:** atmosphere can make danger compelling but cannot by itself make an unclear defeat understandable. Visual severity and reliable attack feedback do different jobs.

Russell Adderson's **0.4 Druid** account praises the force and combination possibilities of its bear form and **Walking Calamity**. His enthusiasm for that embodied fantasy coexists with criticism of a confusing Vaal Temple room-building system and unwanted loss of accumulated rooms. Those are historical impressions, not proof every described defect survived later updates. [Q21](#q21)

**Interpretation:** a transformed body earns its identity through action, movement and feedback. The pleasure differs from merely displaying a bear portrait while retaining an unchanged attack. Equally, a compelling class can coexist with another subsystem that weakens investment.

### A recurring musical idea becomes someone's song

Composer **Kamil Orman-Janowski** identifies **Una's “A Siren's Call”** as a return to the earlier Merveil theme, now with lyrics by **Kane Bennett**, performance by **Hester Ullyart** and production by **Michael Collier**. [Q22](#q22) GGG's Third Edict soundtrack post separately highlights **Tavakai** and the **Well of Souls** as contrasting musical settings. [Q23](#q23)

**Interpretation:** continuity can be emotional and cultural, not only a copied inventory. A person singing a recognizable theme makes the town and the franchise's history relevant without requiring another exposition screen. These are attributed production facts; no complete listening analysis was performed here.

## 10. Production and distribution: the sequel's independence had a cost

The 2023 Rogers interview records the decision to make PoE2 a standalone game rather than the originally planned shared endgame. It links that freedom to changes the team felt unable to impose on the original. [Q10](#q10)

The early-access date later moved from November 15 to December 6, 2024 because account and server infrastructure needed more work, including purchase compatibility and load testing. That is the developer's reported explanation, not evidence the team spent the entire delay changing combat. [Q24](#q24)

**Interpretation:** preserving paid history across products is different from sharing every character, item or rule. A common account can reduce entry friction while creating complex compatibility obligations. A sequel can also justify a new mechanical contract without silently rewriting the old game's audience out of existence.

The original FAQ describes paid early-access entry with points and optional cosmetic/storage purchases, while some longstanding supporters qualified through earlier spending. It limits availability of old cosmetics and relevant stash types instead of promising every asset worked everywhere immediately. [Q20](#q20)

The Third Edict launch campaign combined a coordinated platform update, preload instructions, supporter products and Twitch rewards. [Q25](#q25) **Interpretation:** promotion supplies a shared return occasion, but incoming players still need reliable access and a worthwhile activity. A stream showing a spectacular combination is not a measured conversion rate or evidence that every newcomer can build it immediately.

The existing chapter retains the **578,569 Steam peak on December 8, 2024** and its September 2026 review capture. Those are historical Steam measures, not new synchronized observations, audited unit sales or profit. No private budget, comparable retention series or source-attributed share of purchases was established.

## 12. Transferable inspiration, completion and preservation

The strongest transferable patterns are **a skill as a composable object**, **a preparation choice that enables automatic execution**, **a resource budget that changes with active configuration**, and **a difficulty modifier that also changes recovery opportunities**. The corresponding limits are equally important: better-shaped output is not automatically a compatible action, and preserving an object is not the same as preserving its usefulness.

For OpenLegend, expose which stage failed: missing resource, incompatible support, invalid target, unsuitable active set or a technical defect. Let an invented capability alter a real approach. Do not import periodic economic resets, extreme punishment or enormous loot breadth without testing whether they serve the intended persistent-world experience.
