## Current retail, historical retail and announced services

Midnight is released, and its Devourer specialization changes the previously two-specialization Demon Hunter. Its housing system is no longer merely a proposal. Season 2's August schedule identifies Venomous Abyss, a dungeon rotation, Delves, Prey, PvP and Tidebound Grotto Lair, with staged unlocks and reset differences. Those dates do not establish permanent properties of every dungeon. [S01](#s01) [S02](#s02)

The September 4 weekly update identifies 12.1.5 as PTR content, including Labyrinths and a one-boss raid. **World of Warcraft: Forever** was subsequently announced September 12 as a separate permanent service, with beta beginning September 17 and planned North American release November 4, 2026. It is not already released retail, a mod or simply another name for Classic. The announcement contains inconsistent daylight/standard-time labels, so this dossier uses the planned date without manufacturing a precise corrected launch hour. [S03](#s03) [S09](#s09)

## Character, account and social progression have different owners

Warbands expand account-region sharing through achievements, collections, a bank and eligible reputations. Warbound-until-equipped gear can move before use, then binds to its wearer. Collecting a plate appearance on another class does not grant a Priest the ability to wear plate. Sharing progress and sharing combat capability are different. [S04](#s04)

**Interpretation:** account sharing can make an alternate character's session feel useful without abandoning the player's wider goals, while character-specific equipment preserves a reason to play that class. A complete description must name these boundaries rather than claim everything is shared.

Class and specialization talent trees have different purposes: one supplies shared utility and the other develops a particular role. The War Within's Hero Talents add another selected identity layer. Its original announcement's Demon Hunter count is historical, not a valid current count after Midnight. Named specialization examples in the official class pages show different resource grammars: Rogue Energy/combo points, Death Knight runes/Runic Power, and Druid forms are not merely differently colored mana bars. ## A home connects activities and audiences

Housing and decor are Warband-wide; the February explanation describes access without a housing lottery or onerous upkeep. Neighborhood Endeavors combine residents' tasks, change presentation and unlock themed decor access, with participation requirements scaled to neighborhood size. This is a structured collective activity, not a general political simulator. [S05](#s05)

August's Blueprints save/share a room, interior, exterior or entire home. Importing shows budget needs, owned and missing decor. Sharing a layout transfers a plan, not possession of every object in it. **Interpretation:** decoration offers a reason to revisit activities whose combat rewards are obsolete; a neighborhood supplies an audience, and reusable plans make creative work portable. [S06](#s06)

## Encounters can come to the traveler

Prey is opt-in: Astalor supplies a hunt, and its target can ambush the player during other activities. Higher difficulty changes assistance and encounter conditions. The original preview establishes design vocabulary; the Season 2 notice establishes that the system continues, not that every old payout remains current. [S07](#s07) [S02](#s02)

The updated Delves introduction distinguishes Midnight's Valeera from The War Within's Brann, and staged availability from day-one completeness. It includes outdoor layouts and ground-mount use. An NPC companion can support solo value without removing the player's route, positioning and encounter decisions. [S08](#s08)

## Reception already read: disagreement is useful evidence

**Greg Kasavin, GameSpot, original-game review.** He values approachable controls, quest-guided exploration, useful short sessions, coherent art and audio. He also identifies immediate launch stability trouble and unfinished PvP features. The current page displays August 5, 2015, but GameSpot's own anniversary retrospective explicitly dates the original review to November 29, 2004. Its twenty-quest limit and anticipated PvP implementation are therefore historical evidence, not current rules. [S13](#s13) [S14](#s14)

**Luke Winkie, PC Gamer, December 13, 2022, Dragonflight.** He values active dragonriding, restored talent choices and richer professions, but dislikes efficient, silent leveling-dungeon runs that remove the need to communicate. His criticism is about an experienced subset of content, not evidence that high-end dungeons contain no decisions. [S15](#s15)

**Harvey Randall, PC Gamer, March 26, 2026, Midnight.** He praises atmosphere, redesigned familiar places and promising new systems, while criticizing overexplained story beats and gaps in the new combat UI. These are launch observations rather than an exhaustive September bug audit. [S16](#s16)

**Alex Van Aken, Game Informer, March 20, 2026, Midnight.** His account connects decor rewards to older content and describes kitbashing objects into new furnishing forms. He is more positive about the central story than Randall. He explicitly has not finished assessing all endgame activities, so this is not a full-season verdict. [S17](#s17)

## Production evidence: iteration before apparent inevitability

John Staats's interview describes an EverQuest-influenced aim to remove pain points, a collaborative working culture and uncertainty about dungeon size while spaces were already being built. He also recalls trying inappropriate Quake-style BSP tooling early. These are attributed participant recollections, not deductions about the modern engine. **Interpretation:** the released world's apparent cohesion should not erase the provisional decisions, tooling changes and editorial work that preceded it. [S18](#s18)
