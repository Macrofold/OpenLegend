## Identity and declared boundaries

This dossier studies Obsidian's 2010 single-player RPG and its released add-ons, not Fallout 3, Fallout 4, a fan remaster, or a modded combined campaign. Its resemblance to Fallout 3 is an explicit historical design choice, not evidence that every predecessor rule carries over. Senior producer Jason Bergman's October 14, 2010 Q&A describes a differently structured story with several paths, first-/third-person play, and optional Hardcore rules. The original level cap was 30. The base campaign ends with consequences presented at its conclusion rather than permitting continued play in the resolved world. Later add-on and mod boundaries still need independent treatment below. [Producer Q&A](#s-producer)

## Primary production and systems evidence

In BuckGB's July 1, 2010 GameBanshee interview, Chris Avellone says he joined midway through a project whose goals were established by Josh Sawyer and art director Joe Sanabria. A western-US setting constraint led the team toward Las Vegas, with its lighting, signage and casinos offering visual possibilities. They retained open exploration while changing weapon skills, modifications and companion interactions. Avellone describes a mandate to make every skill useful early: lower-level weapons for different specializations and conversations where Barter expresses an economic argument rather than merely adjusting shop prices. This is a stated design intention, to be checked against actual quest examples, not a claim that every skill is equally powerful throughout the released game. The interview's later Alpha Protocol discussion is a separate topic and is not evidence for New Vegas production. [Interview](#s-avellone)

Bergman distinguishes Karma from faction reputation: both exist, but reputation drives much of the response to decisions. His Hardcore description adds food, drink, sleep, weighted ammunition, slower Stimpak healing and mortal companions. It can be switched off; doing so forfeits the uninterrupted-mode trophy. Fast travel remains possible unless the journey's needs would kill the Courier. His pre-release limb-healing and vault-count wording requires corroboration rather than uncritical repetition. [Producer Q&A](#s-producer)

## Reception — five substantive written reviews read

These are individual accounts, not a representative survey. Their technical findings concern their reviewed 2010 builds/platforms; they do not establish how every later PC configuration performs.

### Craig Pearson — PC Gamer, October 19, 2010

Pearson values the stronger central story, moral situations and adaptive faction choices, while finding the interface, models and combat dated and exploration less consistently rewarding than Fallout 3. Speech and Barter feel unusually valuable. He advises against Hardcore for an initial playthrough, a useful disagreement with GamesRadar's veteran-oriented recommendation. His artillery anecdote illustrates why a promised safe passage does not necessarily protect against the consequences of another character's presence; it will be separated from verified general rules in the final worked examples. [Review](#r-pcg)

### James Stephanie Sterling — Destructoid, October 18, 2010; page updated October 9, 2024

Sterling's Xbox 360 account praises writing, humor, companions and the density of optional quests, while noting that this breadth can overwhelm. Trait tradeoffs, skill-dependent options and equipment variety strengthen role expression. Stutters and crashes make frequent saving important. The enthusiastic overall judgment does not excuse the reliability problems. An unsupported assertion about the impossibility of a no-kill playthrough is not adopted here as a mechanic. The page's current byline is used. [Review](#r-destructoid)

### Alex Donaldson — RPG Site, November 16, 2010

Donaldson sees familiar exploration/combat foundations improved by companion controls, crafting and modifications. Character motives, faction choices and voice performances are central strengths. Poorly communicated commitment points and serious bugs—crashes, disappearing characters and save trouble—undercut those strengths. His account of ongoing patches belongs to 2010. His impression that Karma matters little is not converted into a claim that the system has no effects. [Review](#r-rpgsite)

### Michael A. Cunningham — RPGamer, October 21, 2010; updated March 21, 2019

Cunningham values factions, companions and replayability but finds some pieces shorter or shallower than their Fallout 3 counterparts. Abrupt forgiveness by major factions can cheapen the perceived consequences of earlier behavior. He reports only two freezes in roughly thirty hours, a contrast with more troubled reviews rather than a refutation of them. Desert-to-Strip presentation and audio receive praise; rigid character presentation and repetitive radio material remain reservations. [Review](#r-rpgamer)

### Tyler Nagata — GamesRadar, 2010 launch-era review, all three pages

Nagata appreciates the political possibilities and companions, and recommends Hardcore to returning veterans. Loading interruptions, scripting oddities and crashes across reviewed platforms are substantial objections; a final-battle crash is especially disruptive. He still finds a worthwhile experience but less of Fallout 3's original discovery shock. The accessible page does not clearly display an exact publication day, so none is invented. Its simplified statement about having only one companion must be checked against the humanoid/non-humanoid distinction rather than repeated literally. [Review, pages 1–3](#r-gamesradar)
