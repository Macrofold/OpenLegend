# G112 — Fallout: New Vegas

**Full research pass — September 26, 2026.** [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md). Reference research, not an implementation specification. Five independent written reviews, an additional full review, creator interviews, concrete quest documentation and identified Steam accounts inform this study. No gameplay execution, source-code audit or video playback is claimed. **Quest resolutions, faction paths and expansion premises below contain spoilers.**

## Identity, scope and the player promise

Obsidian's single-player RPG, published by Bethesda, launched in October 2010 for Windows, Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3. The Courier survives an attempted murder and follows a personal grievance into a contest over the Mojave and New Vegas. Familiarity with Fallout 3's exploration and fighting is an intended starting point, not evidence that every predecessor rule carries over. First- and third-person views support the same character, rather than separate campaigns. [Launch review](#r-destructoid), [producer](#s-producer)

The Ultimate Edition bundles the base game with **Dead Money, Honest Hearts, Old World Blues, Lonesome Road, Courier's Stash and Gun Runners' Arsenal**. The first four are substantial adventures; the latter two add equipment-oriented content. The GOG version offers offline, DRM-free access; its preservation-program compatibility claims are not a guarantee that every old scripting defect has disappeared. Mods, fan remasters and combined-game projects are separate from this scope. [GOG](#s-gog)

This is not a settlement builder, conventional spellcasting RPG, multiplayer sandbox or continuously simulated post-ending state. Its strongest kind of agency is choosing **how a particular person with particular abilities intervenes in authored situations**. The opening town is already a miniature political decision, rather than a neutral corridor before the “real” role-playing begins. The last sentence is our interpretation of the Goodsprings quest structure. [Goodsprings](#s-goodsprings)

## Character identity and advancement

### Attributes, skills and traits do different jobs

The seven SPECIAL attributes are **Strength, Perception, Endurance, Charisma, Intelligence, Agility and Luck**. They affect different capacities: carrying and weapon handling, detection and associated skills, health/resilience, social aptitude, learning, action-point-related performance, and critical/luck-dependent outcomes. Selecting a capable shooter is therefore not identical to selecting a capable negotiator or resourceful technician. Attributes are a persistent foundation, not simply seven interchangeable experience bars. [Attributes](#s-attributes)

The thirteen skills are **Barter, Energy Weapons, Explosives, Guns, Lockpick, Medicine, Melee Weapons, Repair, Science, Sneak, Speech, Survival and Unarmed**. They give a build more specific meaning. Repair supports equipment and ammunition work; Science supports terminals and technical crafting; Survival supports field food preparation; Medicine affects healing. Lockpick and Science provide different access routes, while Sneak changes how the character approaches detection and theft. Speech is not named “Speechcraft,” despite that wording in one review. [Skills](#s-skills)

Doc Mitchell's introductory questions can suggest three tagged skills, but the player can override the suggestion. Leaving the opening area permits reconsideration of character choices. Avellone describes this as accommodating different player approaches, not requiring the player to accept a personality quiz's judgment. [Avellone](#s-avellone)

Traits impose distinctive tradeoffs rather than simply awarding a level-up bonus. **Small Frame** improves Agility but increases limb vulnerability; **Built to Destroy** exchanges faster equipment degradation for more critical potential; **Good Natured** improves noncombat skills while reducing combat skills. **Fast Shot** and **Trigger Discipline** pull speed and accuracy in opposite directions. **Wild Wasteland** changes the availability of deliberately strange encounters. These options help define a character's liabilities as well as strengths. [Traits](#s-traits)

Experience and level gains supply skill investment; ordinary level-up perks arrive every second level. Perk prerequisites combine level, attributes and skills, so planning is more than choosing the next item on a universally available tree. [RPGamer](#r-rpgamer) **Jury Rigging**, for example, requires level 14 and Repair 90; **Chemist** requires Medicine 60; **Light Step** uses Perception and Agility requirements. The distinctions can turn an apparently noncombat investment into a durable logistical advantage. [Perks](#s-perks)

## What the player actually does

### Combat, movement and tactical time

The Courier explores on foot, talks, scavenges, sneaks, steals, trades, interacts with containers and terminals, and fights with guns, energy weapons, explosives, melee weapons or unarmed attacks. **V.A.T.S. pauses the action to select body targets and queue attacks**, using the character's action-point budget; real-time combat remains an alternative. This differs from treating the game as a turn-based party RPG or importing a later Fallout game's V.A.T.S. timing. [GOG](#s-gog), [skills](#s-skills)

Iron sights make manual aim a more direct option. Weapon choice involves handling, ammunition, condition and target protection, not only the highest displayed damage. Environmental danger also structures travel: Pearson describes discovering that a tempting northern route out of the opening area contains enemies beyond his initial capability. An open map does not mean uniform danger or equally practical routes. [Producer](#s-producer), [PC Gamer](#r-pcg)

The action inventory includes close-range and long-range attacks, mines and thrown explosives, crouched approaches, aimed body damage, companion support and retreat. Damage, ammunition expenditure and equipment wear connect combat to later supply decisions. Sterling's examples include a **Recharger Rifle**, a grenade machinegun and alternate ammunition; these offer different resource and encounter rhythms, not simply cosmetic skins. [Destructoid](#r-destructoid)

### Equipment, storage and crafting

Weapons, armor, ammunition, chems, food, magazines, ingredients and miscellaneous salvage share inventory attention. Carry capacity makes loot selection meaningful. The player can equip, consume, sell, repair or retain an object for a later recipe; “junk” is therefore contextual rather than universally worthless. Skill magazines provide temporary boosts, distinct from permanent character investment. Weapon condition also means finding another gun can be useful even when its combat role is already covered. [Attributes](#s-attributes), [Destructoid](#r-destructoid), [RPG Site](#r-rpgsite)

Weapon modifications can change scopes, magazine capacity or firing behavior. Avellone's base-game account explicitly distinguishes ordinary modifiable weapons from unique weapons that do not accept those modifications; later add-on-specific equipment should not be flattened into that pre-release rule. [Avellone](#s-avellone)

**Workbenches, reloading benches and campfires** organize different production activities. Science, Repair and Survival matter to different recipes rather than one generic crafting level. Useful salvage can become ammunition, technical supplies or meals; required skills and ingredients limit access. This is menu-based recipe production and repair, not freeform object construction. It has no Fallout 4-style player settlement economy. [Skills](#s-skills), [RPG Site](#r-rpgsite)

A repaired **ED-E** provides a particularly useful connection between inventory and character relationships: spare electronics or sufficiently strong technical skills can turn a broken object into an ally. **Jury Rigging** changes which equipment can serve as repair material, widening useful salvage without making all objects interchangeable. Both are worked through below. [ED-E](#s-ede), [perks](#s-perks)

### Stealth, access and social routes

Sneaking, pickpocketing, lock access, terminals, disguises and skill checks provide alternatives to direct combat. Their opportunities are authored and conditional. A skill does not permit arbitrary natural-language persuasion of every NPC, and a disguise does not erase all consequences of violence. In **Beyond the Beef**, access can depend on Speech, Repair or an appropriate White Glove appearance; kitchen access and control offer still other skill-dependent approaches. [Skills](#s-skills), [Beyond the Beef](#s-beef)

The important analytical distinction is between a **character capability** and a **recognized opportunity to apply it**. An unlocked skill matters when the game supplies doors, recipes, people or disputes that acknowledge it. New Vegas's recurring use of noncombat skills is therefore more informative than simply counting how many skills appear on its character sheet.

## Progression, economy, time and failure

### Several resources, not one universal progression score

Early play develops basic equipment, money, food, technical access and a chosen skill identity. Midgame links towns, faction introductions, companion recruitment and specialized gear. Late play emphasizes commitment to a political outcome, companion resolutions, high-level perks and optional expansion adventures. Choices can close routes; accumulating capability does not guarantee preserving every alliance. [RPG Site](#r-rpgsite), [GameBanshee review](#r-gb)

Bottle caps and casino play supply monetary goals, but quests also reward experience, faction standing, equipment, access and relationships. **Caravan** is a separate card game, while casinos offer another use for money and luck. Brother None criticizes the abundance of money and the eventual lightness of resource pressure. That is evidence of a particular experienced economy, not proof that every build or difficulty faces no scarcity. [GameBanshee review](#r-gb)

Repairs, purchases, medical help and consumables create sinks. Selling salvage can finance immediate needs while sacrificing a repair component. A temporary skill boost can enable an immediate conversation; permanent skill investment can improve repeated access. These are different time horizons for value. The salvage/repair and temporary-magazine behaviors are documented; the time-horizon framing is our analysis. [Perks](#s-perks), [Destructoid](#r-destructoid)

### Hardcore is a ruleset, not simply a larger health multiplier

Bergman's description adds food, water, sleep, ammunition weight, slower Stimpak healing and mortal companions. The mode can be disabled, but that forfeits its uninterrupted-completion trophy. Fast travel is not categorically removed; it is restricted when the resulting needs would be fatal. The original base-game level cap was 30, and the base ending does not allow continued play in the resolved world. These are pre-release producer statements corroborated where applicable by the launch reviews; his oversimplified limb-healing wording is not reproduced as an exclusive rule. [Producer](#s-producer)

Normal companion defeat and Hardcore companion death are materially different attachment risks. The Courier's death returns play to a save rather than founding a persistent multiplayer corpse economy. A successful quest can nevertheless leave a named civilian dead or an organization hostile. Saving and reloading can explore alternatives, but technical crashes are not intended dramatic consequences and should not be romanticized as “emergent difficulty.” [RPGamer](#r-rpgamer), [Goodsprings](#s-goodsprings), [GamesRadar](#r-gamesradar)

The main story's finite conclusion and expansion content supply return purposes, rather than a native season-reset loop. The full expansion sequence supports characters reaching level 50; that does not change the original 30-level launch boundary. [Lonesome Road review](#s-lr)

## People, factions, routines and narrative

### Political choice is broader than a morality meter

The main resolution paths involve the **New California Republic, Caesar's Legion, Mr. House, or an independent route associated with Yes Man**. Smaller communities and organizations also matter: Boomers, the Brotherhood of Steel, Followers of the Apocalypse, Kings, Powder Gangers and casino families supply local interests rather than one homogeneous quest faction. Endings can acknowledge smaller groups alongside the central outcome. [GameBanshee review](#r-gb)

Karma and faction reputation coexist. Helping one group need not make another friendly; early opportunities to cooperate do not imply that all eventual allegiances remain compatible. Reputation is more local and relational than a single global good/evil number. Cunningham nevertheless finds some major-faction forgiveness too abrupt, and Donaldson finds certain commitment points insufficiently clear. The system's strengths and those failures should be considered together. [Producer](#s-producer), [RPGamer](#r-rpgamer), [RPG Site](#r-rpgsite)

It would be misleading to describe the factions as equally sympathetic or the campaign as a fully simulated military economy. Brother None questions the Legion's comparatively blunt villainy and observes the limits of the war presentation. Our interpretation is that a rich set of political options does not require moral equivalence, but does require understandable interests and credible consequences. [GameBanshee review](#r-gb)

### Companions are characters and usable capabilities

The base roster is **Arcade Gannon, Boone, Cass, ED-E, Lily, Raul, Rex and Veronica**. [Companion index](#s-companions) The player can normally travel with one humanoid and one non-humanoid companion, not just one follower total. The companion wheel adjusts behavior and permits equipment/inventory interaction. Different personal quests and preferences give these allies identities beyond their damage output. Their combat AI can still frustrate; an order interface is not a guarantee of perfect execution. [RPGamer](#r-rpgamer), [GameBanshee review](#r-gb)

Boone's recruitment connects an investigation to trust and revenge. Veronica's Brotherhood role creates tension between loyalty and exposure to the outside world; her interest in particular formal clothing becomes a small personal callback. ED-E begins as a repair problem with an alternate material-based solution. These examples demonstrate multiple relationship-entry mechanisms rather than a universal approval meter. [Boone](#s-boone), [Veronica](#s-veronica), [ED-E](#s-ede)

This is not a general romance-tree game, dynasty simulator or cooperative campaign. NPC routines can matter locally: Jeannie May's movement and nighttime absence create an opportunity to investigate her safe. That documented behavior should not be inflated into a claim that every NPC possesses a fully simulated occupation, family economy or offscreen life. [Boone](#s-boone)

### Authored stories and systemic consequences meet unevenly

The personal search leading through settlements gives way to decisions about authority and resource distribution. HELIOS One turns technical repair into a political allocation problem. The Ultra-Luxe turns an elite institution's hidden practices into investigation, disguise and moral choice. Novac ties a companion's private tragedy to a local person's secret. These are authored situations with multiple recognized interventions, not procedurally generated plots. [HELIOS](#s-helios), [Beyond the Beef](#s-beef), [Boone](#s-boone)

**A useful limit:** the Beyond the Beef walkthrough explicitly notes that a threatened trade blockade does not become an actual simulated blockade. A line of dialogue can express a character's intention without the world executing a complete economic model. Preserving this distinction prevents the reference's narrative richness from being mistaken for unlimited simulation depth. [Beyond the Beef](#s-beef)

## Eight concrete situations

Unless attributed to a reviewer, these are constructed explanations of documented quest rules, not claims of hands-on reproduction. Each includes an available intention, necessary conditions, actions, resulting interaction and a subsequent choice.

### 1. Prepare Goodsprings through several professions

**Intent:** defend Ringo. **Conditions:** Ghost Town Gunfight is underway. **Actions:** ask Trudy for support with Speech 25 or Sneak 25; obtain Easy Pete's dynamite with Explosives 25; use Barter 25 for Chet's equipment or Medicine 30 for additional help from Doc Mitchell. **Interaction:** different competencies prepare the same confrontation. **Result:** a better-supported defense, followed by local fame and Powder Ganger hostility. **Next:** assess casualties and whether to continue antagonizing that group. **Limit:** winning does not guarantee Sunny or Trudy survives. Siding against the town is a different quest path. The analytical lesson is that early skills become identities when several different people recognize them. [Goodsprings](#s-goodsprings)

### 2. Repair a future companion instead of buying one

**Intent:** activate ED-E in Primm. **Conditions:** access to the broken eyebot. **Actions:** use Repair 65, Repair 35 plus Science 55, or gather three Scrap Metal, two Sensor Modules and one Scrap Electronics. **Interaction:** knowledge and materials are alternative admission routes. **Result:** recruitable assistance and the Enhanced Sensors benefit. **Next:** decide how the robot complements a humanoid ally. **Limit:** generic goodwill alone does not repair it; the technical/material requirements matter. Our interpretation is that recruitment can reward a non-social build without making the ally merely a purchased stat upgrade. [ED-E](#s-ede)

### 3. Investigate before enabling revenge

**Intent:** help Boone identify his wife's betrayer. **Conditions:** his Novac request and the beret instruction. **Actions:** follow No-Bark's clue, obtain the Bill of Sale from Jeannie May's safe, and lead the implicated person into Boone's firing line. **Interaction:** evidence, access and a visible signal combine. **Result:** Boone acts on the target presented. **Next:** explain the choice and seek recruitment. **Limit:** the mechanism can be abused to frame another person; a Speech 55 lie is not the same moral action as presenting evidence. Not every plausible response, such as blackmail, is supported. [Boone](#s-boone)

### 4. Replace a cannibal feast with a deception

**Intent:** save Ted without merely attacking the whole Ultra-Luxe. **Conditions:** access to the kitchen and banquet chain. **Actions:** remove or distract Philippe through a suitable conversation, then prepare the imitation meal with Survival 75 or Intelligence 6 and his recipe. Let Mortimer reveal himself before exposing the deception. **Interaction:** cooking and timing become investigative tools. **Result:** an authored noncombat exposure route. **Next:** decide how to report responsibility to Ted's father. **Limit:** quest sequence matters; not every earlier action preserves every later route. The documented blockade threat is not a simulated supply-chain event. [Beyond the Beef](#s-beef)

### 5. Decide who receives repaired infrastructure

**Intent:** restore HELIOS One. **Conditions:** plant access and its technical obstacles. **Actions:** repair the generator with Repair 35 plus Scrap Metal, or use the maintenance robot through a technical/credential route. Choose the destination of the plant's output and activate the final reflector controls. **Interaction:** engineering success enables a political decision rather than automatically serving the quest giver. **Result:** different communities or organizations benefit. **Next:** accept the associated reputation and reward consequences. **Limit:** ARCHIMEDES I's local defense and ARCHIMEDES II's orbital-weapon route are distinct; “activate the laser” is insufficiently precise. [HELIOS](#s-helios)

### 6. Make an old weapon useful again

**Intent:** maintain favored equipment cheaply. **Conditions:** Jury Rigging, level 14 and Repair 90. **Actions:** use a compatible item from a broader repair category rather than requiring an identical duplicate. **Interaction:** a perk changes the meaning of future salvage. **Result:** previously unappealing loot becomes maintenance supply. **Next:** compare its repair value with its sale value. **Limit:** compatibility categories remain; arbitrary trash cannot repair everything. Our interpretation is that a good logistics perk changes repeated decisions rather than merely increasing a damage number. [Perks](#s-perks)

### 7. Give a gift that recognizes a particular person

**Intent:** respond to Veronica's interest in a dress. **Conditions:** she is a companion and an eligible formal outfit is available. **Actions:** give the appropriate formal wear. **Interaction:** a personal preference meets a specific item category. **Result:** her response can grant Scribe Counter. **Next:** consider the difference between helping her personally and resolving her larger Brotherhood conflict. **Limit:** ordinary pre-war dresses do not all satisfy this particular preference. The design interpretation is that a small precise callback can convey individuality without a huge generic relationship meter. [Veronica](#s-veronica)

### 8. Discover that safe passage is not blast immunity

**Attributed actual play:** Pearson pays George for an artillery route, gains the Boomers' acceptance, then attacks George. George runs into the artillery zone; shelling directed at him also kills the Courier. **Intent:** retaliate after obtaining safety. **Interaction:** another target's movement and area damage defeat that expectation. **Next:** reload or reconsider retaliation. **Limit:** this is one reviewer's reported event, not a controlled test or universal guarantee. Our interpretation distinguishes an organization's intention toward the player from immunity to its physical effects. [PC Gamer](#r-pcg)

## Expansions: different pressures, not interchangeable extra maps

### Dead Money

The Sierra Madre heist temporarily strips familiar equipment, imposes dangerous radios/speakers and poisonous areas, and requires dealing decisively with enemies that can otherwise rise again. Three temporary companions have agendas and cannot simply be imported into the Mojave party. The DLC initially raised the cap from 30 to 35. Steven Carter's March 18, 2011 PC review values the slower, resource-conscious pace but dislikes repetitive collection challenges and warns that the emphasis may disappoint combat-first players. Initial Xbox release and later PC/PS3 availability are distinct. **Reference value:** scarcity can renew attention to neglected objects, but forced deprivation also interrupts a cherished build. [Dead Money](#s-dm)

### Honest Hearts

A caravan trip to Zion permits limited equipment rather than stripping everything. An ambush leads into the lives of Follows-Chalk, Waking Cloud and competing tribal/community interests. Daniel's evacuation-oriented position contrasts with Joshua Graham's violent response. Eric Schwarz's May 25, 2011 review appreciates the landscape and promising characters but finds too much fetching and insufficient use of the setup and noncombat possibilities. **Reference value:** a beautiful, distinct region needs activities that express its moral conflict; geography and a premise alone do not deliver the depth implied by them. [Honest Hearts](#s-hh)

### Old World Blues

Big MT converts the Courier into the subject of grotesque scientific experimentation. The Think Tank, Dr. Klein and Dr. Mobius support a deliberately absurd tone. New tools such as the Sonic Emitter combine combat and access functions; the Sink's eccentric appliances turn exploration and recovered personalities into a useful home. Eric Schwarz's July 23, 2011 account values the comedy and interlinked utility but acknowledges lengthy dialogue and a tone that can alienate those seeking grounded Fallout. **Reference value:** a home becomes more meaningful when exploration changes what its inhabitants or devices can do. [Old World Blues](#s-owb)

### Lonesome Road

The Divide and Ulysses concentrate attention on the Courier's past and responsibility. Traversal through wreckage and frequent combat produce a more linear journey than the base game's broad faction web. Eric Schwarz's September 23, 2011 discussion values the confrontation but identifies limited noncombat approaches and the burden of high-health enemies. Its high-level context belongs to the expanded game, not the original cap. **Reference value:** an authored reckoning can gain focus by constraining routes, but can also clash with an identity the player believed was self-authored. [Lonesome Road](#s-lr)

These are substantive edition contrasts, not four additional independent reviews counted toward the base game's five-review requirement. Courier's Stash and Gun Runners' Arsenal belong to equipment/provisioning scope rather than four more full campaigns. [GOG](#s-gog)

## Art, audio, interface and production

The Mojave's worn desert settlements and New Vegas's lighting create a legible contrast between scarcity and concentrated power. Cunningham praises that contrast while finding character presentation stiff and radio material repetitive. The visual identity is not merely “post-apocalyptic brown”: casinos, signage, local costumes and recognizable institutions distinguish social spaces. The last clause is an interpretation of the reviewed setting, not a claim of a separate art-team study. [RPGamer](#r-rpgamer)

Bergman identifies Inon Zur's score, a string quartet used for location music, and several radio stations, including Radio New Vegas with Wayne Newton. Music can therefore function as both atmosphere and an in-world broadcast. [Producer](#s-producer) The Pip-Boy-style interface, dense inventories and loading transitions supply information but also interrupt flow; Nagata and Pearson do not find familiarity sufficient to excuse the friction. No dedicated accessibility or present-day performance benchmark was run here. [GamesRadar](#r-gamesradar), [PC Gamer](#r-pcg)

Avellone says he joined midway through goals established by Josh Sawyer and art director Joe Sanabria. A western-US constraint helped lead to Vegas, whose lighting and signage offered opportunities. His stated mandate was to give every skill useful early applications, including economic arguments for Barter. This is a creator intention, illustrated by the documented opening quests, not proof of perfect balance. The interview's Alpha Protocol discussion is a separate game's production history. [Avellone](#s-avellone)

Reviews repeatedly report scripting faults, crashes and save problems, with very different experienced severity. These are dated observations, not a diagnosis of proprietary engine internals. GOG's preservation changelog records a November 13, 2024 compatibility validation for modern Windows; that is a storefront's stated maintenance action, not independent confirmation that all quests work on every configuration. [RPG Site](#r-rpgsite), [GOG](#s-gog)

## Distribution, promotion and commercial context

This is a paid game with separately released add-ons and a later collected edition, not a subscription or free-to-play service. Producer Q&As and interviews position it as a full new story with optional Hardcore play, companions and consequential choices. PC modding is a distinct community extension route; it is not evidence that the original console or PC release contains native multiplayer. [Producer](#s-producer), [GOG](#s-gog)

Our interpretation of its continuing sharing appeal is that a quest can be retold as a compact disagreement: whom the player trusted, which skill they used, and who benefited. The evidence demonstrates examples of such stories and disagreements, but does not quantify creator-driven acquisition, recommendation rates or causal marketing attribution.

**Dated commercial evidence:** WIRED's November 8, 2010 report relays Bethesda's announcement of **five million copies shipped** and **over $300 million in retail sales**. Shipments are not verified consumer sell-through; retail value is not publisher net revenue or profit. Digital performance was described without a usable separate count. The original press release was not recovered here, so this is explicitly secondary reporting. No current lifetime total, budget or profit estimate is invented. [WIRED](#s-sales)

## Reception: five independent reviews and direct player evidence

### Craig Pearson — PC Gamer, October 19, 2010

Pearson praises the stronger central story, morally interesting situations and faction flexibility while finding models, interface and combat dated. Speech and Barter feel valuable; exploration is less consistently rewarding than Fallout 3. He discourages Hardcore for a first playthrough. His artillery account above remains attributed rather than universalized. [Review](#r-pcg)

### James Stephanie Sterling — Destructoid, October 18, 2010; updated October 9, 2024

Sterling's Xbox 360 review values writing, humor, companions and abundant side quests, but notes that breadth can overwhelm. Traits and equipment strengthen role expression. Stutters and crashes require defensive saving. The current byline is used; an unsupported claim that no-kill play is impossible is not adopted as a rule. [Review](#r-destructoid)

### Alex Donaldson — RPG Site, November 16, 2010

Donaldson sees improved crafting, companion controls and modifications atop familiar foundations. Character motives, political choices and performances are strengths; unclear commitment points and serious bugs undermine them. His discussion of ongoing patches concerns 2010. His impression that Karma is marginal is not converted into a claim that it has no effects. [Review](#r-rpgsite)

### Michael A. Cunningham — RPGamer, October 21, 2010; updated March 21, 2019

Cunningham values factions, companions and replayability but finds some elements shallow or short. Abrupt faction forgiveness can cheapen previous decisions. His two freezes in roughly thirty hours differ from other reviewers' experiences without disproving them. He praises desert/Strip atmosphere and audio while criticizing rigid presentation and repetition. [Review](#r-rpgamer)

### Tyler Nagata — GamesRadar, launch-era 2010; three pages read

Nagata appreciates political possibilities and companions and recommends Hardcore to returning veterans, unlike Pearson's first-run advice. Loading interruptions, scripting problems and crashes—including during the final battle—are substantial objections. The exact publication day was not established from the visible page. His simplified “one companion” wording is corrected by the humanoid/non-humanoid distinction. [Review](#r-gamesradar)

### Additional critical context

Brother None's November 23, 2010 GameBanshee review supplies further disagreement about war simulation, economic pressure and faction portrayal. Its full body was read, but neither its armor simplification nor its incomplete enumeration of combat skills overrides the specific mechanics references. [Review](#r-gb)

### Steam, captured September 26, 2026

The accessible app **22380** page displayed **Most Helpful (Week), All, English**. Filtered positive/negative and API routes failed; the bare page worked. **lakerprime** recommends the game while criticizing age and unfinishedness even with mods. **paisah** praises quests and skill/reputation checks despite combat and technical age. **DaphneLynx** briefly endorses persevering through crashes. Their displayed hours—28.4, 125 and 118.4—are capture-time values, not proven hours at publication. Helpful votes were only two, one and three respectively; this is not a prevalence study. The negative-flagged **Jam** fragment supplies no meaningful criticism and is excluded. [Weekly page](#s-steam)

A separately retrieved negative review by **Cobra**, May 9, 2024, criticizes the environment, main quest, graphics and gunplay. It has nine displayed hours and one helpful vote at capture. Crucially, it belongs to **PCR regional app 22490**, not 22380; its global ranking is unknown. Duplicate rendering counts as one account. [Cobra](#s-cobra)

**Synthesis:** praise centers on authored choice, characters and replayability. Criticism separates into reliability, interface/combat age, unclear consequence signals and dissatisfaction with the content itself. Those are different problems; “players forgive bugs for writing” does not explain the reviewer who dislikes the writing or world too.

## Transferable patterns and counterexamples — interpretation

**Give a profession several early witnesses.** A doctor, trader and explosives specialist should encounter different recognitions before a build feels like a spreadsheet. This depends on authored opportunities distributed across early play. One isolated skill check followed by hours of irrelevance is not equivalent. A smaller alternative is fewer skills with more consistent applications.

**Make noncombat investment change recurring economics.** Repair compatibility and technical recruitment can reshape what loot means and whom the player can bring. Such benefits need understandable prerequisites and boundaries. Otherwise hidden categories make the system feel arbitrary. A straightforward repair fee is simpler, but offers less build-specific identity.

**Separate knowledge, proof and persuasion.** The player may suspect a culprit, possess evidence, or convince an ally; those should not automatically be the same state. This enables morally different paths without needing every conversation to be unconstrained. The failure mode is treating an information-gathering quest as a skill-number bypass with no downstream distinction.

**Let restoring a resource precede deciding its beneficiary.** HELIOS-style situations separate competence from politics: successful engineering does not determine whose interests should win. This depends on legible stakeholders and actual differentiated outcomes. A dialogue promise without world consequences can still support narrative, but should not masquerade as simulated economics.

**Offer commitment without pretending all options remain open.** Faction exclusivity makes decisions consequential, but commitment points need communication. Sudden lockouts feel unfair; arbitrary pardons can devalue earlier choices. A reversible probation stage is an alternative, provided it is understood as such rather than silently erasing the past.

**Do not mistake technical failure for dramatic risk.** A companion's authored mortality can intensify attachment; a disappearing NPC or corrupted save breaks trust. Both remove access, but they have different causes and design meanings. Preservation and reliability are part of sustaining a choice-rich game, not separate from the quality of its choices.

These hypotheses neither authorize OpenLegend implementation nor propose copying Fallout's names, settings, assets or particular political factions.
