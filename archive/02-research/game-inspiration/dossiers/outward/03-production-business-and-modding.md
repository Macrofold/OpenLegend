# Outward — production, business, marketing and community creation

Research cutoff: **7 November 2026**. Part of the [Outward dossier](../outward.md). This synthesis records public evidence and explicit limits. It is not a play-test or a financial audit. Linked videos were identified through metadata and surrounding text, not watched; soundtrack pages and credits were inspected, but the music was not auditioned. The mod creation and installation workflow is constructed from documentation and **was not executed**.

## 1. Findings that matter most

Outward provides evidence that a distinct, demanding RPG can support a small studio over several years. The strongest commercial evidence combines dated sales disclosures with the founder's later description of continuing cash flow. It does **not** establish an exact development budget, revenue, profit, return on investment, customer acquisition cost or publisher split. These quantities should remain unknown in the dossier. Sources: [December 2020 sales announcement](https://www.gematsu.com/2020/12/outward-sales-top-one-million-dlc-the-three-brothers-launches-december-15-for-pc-in-2021-for-ps4-and-xbox-one), [CEO retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/guillaume-boucher-vidal-671b8827_celebrating-3-million-players-for-outward-activity-7374118148507181056-5e0O), [March 2024 studio interview](https://gamesbeat.com/nine-dots-launches-3rd-party-publishing-with-friendly-terms/).

Three distinctions are essential:

- **Copies and players:** the CEO explicitly described passing three million copies sold. A separate September 2025 promotional release and video celebrated three million players. Preserve both disclosures with their own attribution; neither gives the other metric's counting methodology.
- **Original game, Definitive Edition, ports and sequel:** their release dates, features, tools, reception and audience exposures are different. A modern mod package or Switch review should not be assigned to the original 2019 launch.
- **Commercial outcome and causal explanation:** sales, continued cash flow, editions and ports support a success conclusion. They do not isolate how much demand came from cooperative play, difficulty, music, reviewers, creators, discounts or modding.

The modding ecosystem qualifies for a full dossier treatment: it has a substantial catalog, dependency tooling and recent content/tool activity. Its diversity is better established than its penetration into the total player base. The research supports discussing installation, authoring, compatibility, distribution, trust, rights and creator maintenance; it does not support claiming that mods are necessary, universally compatible or a proven driver of sales. Sources: [Outward catalog](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/), [SideLoader](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/sinai-dev/SideLoader/), [Outward Construction Kit](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Blue_Chamber_Collective/Outward_Construction_Kit/).

## 2. Production history, financing and scope

### 2.1 An unsuccessful crowdfunding route preceded the successful product

Nine Dots promoted an Outward Kickstarter in April 2015 with a **C$150,000 goal**. The campaign is marked canceled. A May 2015 report reproducing the developer's cancellation explanation describes inadequate press reach and the time cost of the campaign, with alternative financing pursued through the Canada Media Fund, a publisher, investors and income from GoD Factory. This is evidence about financing and discovery constraints before launch; it is not evidence that the eventual game lacked a market. Sources: [Nine Dots announcement, 10 April 2015](https://www.indiedb.com/games/outward/news/outward-is-now-on-kickstarter), [cancellation report, 8 May 2015](https://indie.rpgwatch.com/news/outward--kickstarter-canceled-31751.html), [canonical campaign](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/271033429/outward-the-adventurer-life-sim).

Execution Labs reported in July 2015 that its alumnus Nine Dots had secured **over $960,000 in Canada Media Fund production funding** for Outward. The inspected page does not specify the currency, and the amount should not be silently labeled US dollars or converted. Nor is a funding award equivalent to the project's entire cost. The linked original CMF material was not successfully recovered during this research. Source: [Execution Labs, 13 July 2015](https://www.executionlabs.com/nine-dots-studio-kitfox-games-receive-cmf-investments/).

**Interpretation:** Crowdfunding tests a particular proposition, presentation, audience reach and timing, while also being a financing mechanism. Failure on that route does not isolate which factor failed. Outward's later outcome is a useful counterexample to treating a canceled campaign as a reliable estimate of lifetime demand. It also does not prove that ignoring campaign feedback is generally wise: the relevant lesson is to separate a product thesis from the economics and reach of the channel used to test it.

### 2.2 Small internal team, long development, expensive feature interactions

A February 2019 original interview describes ten internal developers at interview time, as few as six earlier, and more than four years of development. It connects independent split-screen roaming to Unity memory/terrain problems and says a region was reworked four times. These are internal-team and development-history statements, not a complete contractor census or an audited cost account. Source: [Gaiscioch interview, 25 February 2019](https://gaisciochmagazine.com/articles/outward_an_extraordinary_adventure_set_in_ordinary_lives.html).

**Interpretation:** A differentiating feature can impose costs across systems. Separate cooperative players potentially need separate visible areas, combat situations and interface states while sharing persistence and progression rules. The business risk is therefore larger than the programming estimate for “add a second player.” In a new project, the relevant prototype would exercise the combined world/cooperation/persistence promise under realistic load. This is a transfer hypothesis, not a claim that Open Legend should adopt Outward's technology or split-screen implementation.

Public material gathered here does not establish original outsourced headcount, person-years, wage costs, marketing spend, localization spend, certification cost, engine payments, office overhead or the division of work between studio and publisher. A small headcount should not be converted into a budget using an invented average salary. A multi-year project may have changing staffing, contractors, subsidies and substantial unpaid or deferred founder labor; this dossier has no reliable accounting for those possibilities.

### 2.3 Post-launch work and engagement are part of the production story

In December 2020, GamesBeat's original interview reported a development period in which the Stadia version and second expansion stretched the team. The founder also described roughly 30 hours of average play and fewer than 10% of players completing any of the four campaigns, alongside some players spending hundreds of hours. These are historical developer-reported figures; they are not a current cohort analysis or a complete satisfaction measure. The same interview described the US as the largest market, followed by Germany and Canada, with Japanese localization then arriving. Source: [GamesBeat, 3 December 2020](https://gamesbeat.com/outwards-freedom-and-challenge-helped-the-rpg-sell-over-1-million-copies/).

**Interpretation:** Story completion, perceived value and commercial performance can diverge. A player might leave satisfied after several memorable expeditions, stop because of frustration, or repeatedly restart without finishing. Aggregate play time and completion cannot distinguish those experiences. For Open Legend research, useful questions would pair completion with abandonment reasons, willingness to return, memorable incidents and whether the player felt the journey justified its cost.

The production lesson is similarly conditional. Ports and expansions can extend a product's earning life while drawing on the same people who fix bugs or build the next release. Outward's public history supports considering that tension; it does not supply enough staffing or cost data to quantify the optimal release cadence.

### 2.4 Later studio sustainability

A March 2024 original interview describes Nine Dots as having 28 staff and quotes the founder explaining that the studio was financially secure and still receiving Outward cash flow as it entered third-party publishing. This is meaningful evidence of continued business value several years after release, though it remains a management account rather than audited game-level profitability. The article's terms for Nine Dots' newly announced publishing business are **not** the terms of Outward's earlier Deep Silver agreement. Its discussion of prospective publishing budgets also does not establish Outward's budget. Source: [GamesBeat, 4 March 2024](https://gamesbeat.com/nine-dots-launches-3rd-party-publishing-with-friendly-terms/).

### 4.2 Expectations are informative, but they are not audited results

At launch, the founder described 100,000 copies as a good outcome and 200,000 as probably very profitable for the involved parties. He also explained that proceeds involved the store, publisher, studio, Canada Media Fund and an accelerator. These were prospective judgments, not audited profitability thresholds. A later lifetime aspiration can coexist with those launch scenarios without being treated as a contradiction. Source: [GamesBeat launch interview, page 2, 26 March 2019](https://gamesbeat.com/outward-interview-unlearn-what-you-have-learned-about-role-playing-games/2/).

**Interpretation:** The comparison between expectations and eventual disclosed scale supports saying Outward exceeded the founder's stated ambitions. It does not allow calculating the magnitude of excess profit. Unit economics may change as sales move from launch pricing to deep discounts, bundles, new territories or new editions, while ongoing ports and content add costs. Continuing cash flow several years later is stronger evidence of durable business value than a multiplication of launch list price by lifetime copies.

### 4.3 A Steam estimate, and why total revenue and profit remain unresolved

**Observed third-party model:** Raijin's fully inspected app 794260 page on **7 November 2026** estimated **719,100 Steam copies and approximately US$17.3 million in lifetime gross Steam revenue**, labeled medium confidence by the provider. This is a model of that Steam app, not disclosed receipts, a separate DE-only sales total, franchise revenue or developer income. Earlier retrieved snapshots differed; those are dated revisions, not the endpoints of a confidence interval. [Provider page](https://raijin.gg/app/794260/Outward_Definitive_Edition).

The provider combines review-based unit estimates with verified-purchase weighting and ownership-sample checks. Its detailed revenue explanation is **estimated units × US base price × a price-bracket discount factor**. It does not reconstruct Outward's actual lifetime discount history or deduct store share, refunds and taxes. Its wording about regional pricing is inconsistent across sections; game-specific realized pricing is not established. The confidence label is the provider's assessment, not an independent audit. [Methodology, updated 5 August 2026](https://raijin.gg/methodology).

**Total cross-platform revenue, developer receipts and profit: not estimable from the available evidence at a defensible low/base/high confidence range.** The research searched developer/publisher milestones, original financial interviews, crowdfunding and institutional funding disclosures, continuing studio cash-flow accounts, and public Steam estimators. [Gamalytic's app page](https://gamalytic.com/game/794260) exposed no game-level number through retrieval; [VG Insights' app route](https://vginsights.com/game/794260) was inaccessible. The accessible Raijin estimate supplies a partial-market comparison, with the limitations above. No convenient average price, publisher share or console multiplier is substituted for missing evidence.

The distinction between these amounts can be traced through a dependency map:

1. **Consumer spending** requires paid units by platform, territory, date and edition, multiplied by actual realized prices, with taxes, refunds, bundles and subscription arrangements handled consistently.
2. **Receipts to the commercial parties** require store deductions, local taxes and any distribution or subscription terms.
3. **Developer receipts** require the actual publishing agreement, recoupment waterfall, rights allocation, funding repayment obligations and timing of cash remittances.
4. **Developer profit** additionally requires original development costs, content and port costs, support, allocated overhead and any other relevant obligations.

These are distinct quantities. An estimate of consumer spending would not be a studio revenue estimate; a studio revenue estimate would not be a profit estimate. The funding award is a financing disclosure, not a substitute for the fourth step. A third-party Steam sales model, even if methodologically useful, would not become a verified cross-platform financial result.

## 5. Marketing, commercials, discovery and word of mouth

The research identifies official announcements, trailer routes, developer interviews, release events and community programs. It does not contain an advertising account, impression logs, spend, conversion data, influencer contracts or a complete media plan. Consequently, “commercials” can be documented as promotional assets and messages; paid media effectiveness remains unavailable.

The canceled Kickstarter's reported difficulty obtaining press attention is useful evidence of an earlier discovery constraint. Source: [campaign cancellation report](https://indie.rpgwatch.com/news/outward--kickstarter-canceled-31751.html). **Interpretation:** A useful question is how the communication problem changed as the game became playable and acquired an audience. A founder's strategy or a later commercial milestone cannot establish the actual word-of-mouth share; that requires separate attribution evidence.

### 5.3 An observable sequence of communication occasions

The financing campaign, launch, sales celebrations, expansions, Definitive Edition, Switch port and sequel reveal each offered a reason to cover the franchise again. This is an interpretation of the dated release record. The communication advantage is a series of concrete news hooks rather than a single launch moment. Its effectiveness still depends on audience reach, relevance and product satisfaction, none of which can be calculated from the existence of press releases alone.

For the sequel, the current official news archive includes playtest announcements and recaps, FAQs, art/system articles, contests and beta-related material. The October release-date post embeds a III Initiative trailer and explicitly asks for YouTube engagement, then directs interested people toward a developer livestream, Discord and Early Access information. These are visible community/discovery mechanisms. The underlying video and livestream were not watched here. Sources: [Nine Dots news archive](https://www.ninedotsstudio.com/news), [October 2026 announcement](https://www.ninedotsstudio.com/news/outward-2-new-release-date).

**Open Legend transfer hypothesis:** The strongest communication material for a systems-heavy adventure may be a concrete player situation whose consequence is understandable: what preparation enabled, what went wrong and what the player learned. That hypothesis should be tested against actual trailer comprehension and purchase motivations. A trailer that conveys atmosphere without making the interaction legible may attract the wrong expectations; a systems explanation without human stakes may fail to convey why the preparation is enjoyable. This is proposed analysis, not a claim that the inspected Outward videos demonstrably achieved either result.

No conclusion is drawn about creator sponsorship versus organic coverage. A creator's enthusiasm, a large view count or a fan thread cannot establish compensation status or attributable sales without additional evidence. The user-supplied Reddit thread belongs in the [reception chapter](02-reception.md), where its date, participant selection and context can be preserved.

## 6. Art, music and presentation

The soundtrack is a documented production contribution in its own right. Jean-François Racine's original album is dated 25 March 2019 and contains 25 tracks, with day/night and regional or settlement associations alongside combat/dungeon material. Its credits identify live instrumental contributors, including guitars, flute, violin, cello and handpan, as well as the composer's work. Separate official albums cover The Soroboreans and The Three Brothers. Sources: [original OST](https://jeanfrancoisracine.bandcamp.com/album/outward-ost), [Soroboreans OST](https://jeanfrancoisracine.bandcamp.com/album/outward-the-soroboreans-ost), [Three Brothers OST](https://jeanfrancoisracine.bandcamp.com/album/outward-the-three-brothers-ost).

The composer also has a university profile and an ABC program text identifying the music and collaborators. These provide independent editorial routes to the work without replacing direct listening. Sources: [Université de Sherbrooke profile](https://www.usherbrooke.ca/culture/actualites/nouvelles/details/39981), [ABC Game Show program](https://www.abc.net.au/listen/programs/game-show/music-of-outward/12256546).

**Interpretation:** Region-linked and day/night music provides a plausible mechanism for place recognition and emotional continuity during travel. The existence of that structure is supported by the album; the effect on a particular player must come from listening or player testimony. Self-selected Bandcamp comments connect music with exploration and shared memories, which is useful qualitative evidence of possible pleasure, not a representative satisfaction survey. Source: [original OST and comments](https://jeanfrancoisracine.bandcamp.com/album/outward-ost).

For the dossier, presentation should therefore be assessed as more than a list of visual compromises. A game may be technically modest while its landscape, color, sound and recurring musical associations contribute strongly to the adventure fantasy. Conversely, appealing atmosphere does not excuse unreadable threats or confusing interaction cues. Those two dimensions should be evaluated separately against footage or firsthand reports. This research did not inspect enough original-game art-production evidence to attribute a specific visual pipeline, shader strategy, animation budget or outsourcing arrangement. The sequel's art articles should not be retroactively treated as documentation of the original game.

## 7. R15 — modding, extensions and community creation

### 7.1 Applicability and evidence quality

**Full treatment applies.** The inspected Thunderstore catalog returned 431 entries and showed packages updated within days. It spans foundational tools, interface/convenience changes, difficulty, camera changes and new content. This is stronger evidence than a single abandoned mod. It still includes tools and dependencies, so “431 entries” must not become “431 independent playable expansions.” Multi-game mod-manager totals are not Outward audience figures. Source: [catalog snapshot](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/).

SideLoader's package page reported approximately 385,800 downloads and 364 dependents during this pass. These counters changed across retrievals and should be treated as dated, approximate package statistics. They are not unique people, active users or a percentage of owners. Source: [SideLoader](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/sinai-dev/SideLoader/).

The appropriate conclusion is that Outward supports a sustained community ecosystem with visible experimentation and shared infrastructure. It is not evidence that every foundation is recently maintained, every listed package still works or the developer provides an official supported SDK.

### 7.2 Roles and responsibilities

| Role                         | Contribution                                                           | Boundary that matters                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Game developer and publisher | Game binaries, editions, patches and underlying assets                 | Public mod availability does not establish an official support commitment. |
| Runtime/tool maintainer      | Loading hooks, reusable content support and authoring aids             | A tool's compatibility becomes a dependency for many downstream packages.  |
| Mod creator                  | Changed rules, interface features or authored content                  | The creator must describe requirements, limitations and relevant rights.   |
| Distribution platform        | Package pages, version/dependency metadata and discovery               | Hosting is not proof of security, quality or universal compatibility.      |
| Player or co-op group        | Selects versions, configures profiles and accepts a changed experience | A local change can affect saves, multiplayer agreement or reproducibility. |

This role map is an analytical model derived from the visible toolchain, not a claim about formal contractual obligations.

### 7.3 Representative modification classes

The catalog included FirstPersonCamera, IngredientTracker, JournalAventure, configurable MoreEnemies, AuraiCheatMenu, ArenaRush, AssetBrowser and beta new-region content. These illustrate camera/UI changes, information aids, difficulty changes, convenience, repeatable encounters and world authoring. [MoreEnemies](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Catalysten/MoreEnemies/) requires matching versions for all participants and uses host settings; its detailed limitations call co-op beta and untested, qualifying the stronger headline claim. The presence of these options is evidence of what creators offer, not a representative vote on what players dislike. Sources: [catalog entries](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/); individual packages [Ingredient Tracker](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Tchernobill/Ingredient_Tracker/), [FirstPersonCamera](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Tchernobill/FirstPersonCamera/), [Asset Browser](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Wrango/Asset_Browser/), [Arena Rush](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Rox/Arena_Rush/) and [Journal Aventure](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/CgEido/Journal_Aventure/). These are examples, not tested endorsements.

**Interpretation:** Modification often reveals alternative preferences that one default ruleset cannot satisfy simultaneously. Faster access to an arena, more information or fewer resource constraints may help one player and undermine another's preferred sense of expedition. A dossier should connect each change to the experience it alters, instead of labeling every convenience feature an obvious repair. Likewise, a difficulty-increasing package can coexist with complaints that the original is too punishing; different audiences can want opposite changes.

### Three deeper examples: a changed opening, an overhaul and a repair

- **[AlternateStart, by IggyTheMad](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/IggyTheMad/AlternateStart/)** changes starting situations, introductory quests and character tradeoffs. For example, its Conflux Watcher pairs passive mana recovery with reduced stamina recovery. The host chooses the multiplayer scenario while guests can select their own starting perks. Its changelog records added scenarios and balance/bug revisions, and credits Sinai, Emo and community feedback. These are mod rules, not native character-creation options.
- **[OutHard Gameplay Overhaul, by Dubdubaba](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Dubdubaba/OutHard_Gameplay_Overhaul/)** rewrites training, adds combat-earned Expertise and changes survival, encounters and cooperation. The inspected version was 7.13.30. It combines changed dependencies with additional content; the author warns against updating some pinned dependencies and supports only the intended configuration. Credits distinguish coding guidance, testing and upstream creators, including IggyTheMad. This is a substantive alternate ruleset with a maintenance contract, not a cosmetic patch. Its claimed larger-party behavior was not tested here.
- **[SideLoader Building Patch, by ModderJohn](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/ModderJohn/SideLoader_Building_Patch/)** addresses documented building templates whose construction cost/time fields were ignored. It adds no buildings; it makes other authors' intended values take effect. The maintainer distinguishes modifications of existing buildings from more general removal safety and recommends backup. This is an example of a compatibility repair enabling other content, with claims attributed to its author.

A curated modlist chooses and configures several packages; an installer or manager delivers that selection. Neither is the same artifact as a single authored mod or the repair on which another mod depends. The examples establish different creator roles and documented limits; their development budgets, total labor and creator income were not disclosed.

### 7.4 Toolchain and authoring surfaces

The documented stack separates several jobs. A manager such as r2modman can organize a game profile and its packages; BepInEx supplies runtime plugin loading; SideLoader offers reusable content support; and a region-authoring project such as Outward Construction Kit serves a different creation problem. These layers should not be conflated into a single official editor. Sources: [installation guide, indexed access only](https://outward.wiki.gg/wiki/Installing_Mods), [SideLoader](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/sinai-dev/SideLoader/), [Construction Kit](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Blue_Chamber_Collective/Outward_Construction_Kit/).

SideLoader's inspected release was 3.8.4. Its documentation and changelog expose content templates and support developed across quests, skills, containers, drops and animations. XML templates and the in-game editing interface offer data-authoring paths; full custom quest logic still requires C# code. A manual DLL location is documented under BepInEx's plugins directory. Its changelog also records compatibility and loading fixes, showing that content tooling has its own version history. Source: [SideLoader package and changelog](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/sinai-dev/SideLoader/); [historical source-repository link](https://github.com/sinai-dev/Outward-SideLoader), which returned 404 during the final access check. The package remained accessible; this does not establish why the repository link failed.

Construction Kit describes itself as beta tooling for Definitive Edition, using Unity 2020.3.26f1 and a starter project for regions connected by doors/loading. Its documentation specifies Windows, says co-op is untested and describes a first-launch asset cache. It distinguishes decorative objects from functional item placements, notes limited defeat handling, and warns that updates can break references. Its normal asset-reference approach is not an unrestricted redistribution license; the concrete workflow below identifies an export exception for altered props. Sources: [Construction Kit package](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Blue_Chamber_Collective/Outward_Construction_Kit/), [source repository](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit).

### 7.5 Constructed creation-to-play workflow — not executed

This example describes a creator-authored clearing containing one hyena, a pickup and a return door. It names the documented controls and artifacts; no scene, package, installation or test was produced for this research. It is a bounded example of the documented toolchain rather than a promise that arbitrary quests, regions or multiplayer combinations work.

1. **Prepare the authoring environment.** The maintainer's setup uses Windows, Definitive Edition, Unity **2020.3.26f1**, and an r2modman profile containing Construction Kit. Open the downloaded starter project and select the game/profile paths. Extract Monsoon for the tutorial's terrain assets and Chersonese for its hyena. This reads the creator's installed game. [Maintainer setup guide](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/getting-started.md).

2. **Create the artifact.** Use `Construction Kit > New Region Scene`. In the Prop Browser, assign a unique Region ID—our proposed example is `dossier_test_clearing`—and a displayed name. Add ground underneath the supplied spawn point, add a hyena from the enemy tab, place one ordinary game item through the item tab, and add a return door. This produces an editable Unity scene representing an encounter and its exit. Decorative chest props do not become working containers. [Maintainer first-region tutorial](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md).

3. **Export and establish the entrance.** The export operation writes a region package to the selected profile's `BepInEx/plugins/OCK-Region-<id>` folder. In the modded game, choose the region with Ctrl+F8 and position its entrance with Ctrl+F10; a subsequent export incorporates that placement. The intended player result is a usable entrance, a loading transition, the authored clearing and an exit back. This is the documented intended behavior, not an observed result. [Authoring/export guide](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/making-a-region.md).

4. **Produce and install a distributable.** `Construction Kit > Package Region` accepts team, package name, version and description; `Make Upload Zip` writes a versioned ZIP without uploading it. Test that ZIP through r2modman's local-mod import after removing the duplicate exported copy. A published package declares the kit dependency, allowing the manager to install it. Distribution would be a separate creator action; none occurred here. [Packaging instructions](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/making-a-region.md).

5. **Validate persistence and revise.** The tutorial's in-game test control runs normal gameplay, including damage and saves, so use a disposable character. Check entrance, pickup, enemy behavior, exit, reload and removal before calling the package compatible. Keep the region ID stable because saves depend on it. These checks are proposed, not passed. [Tutorial](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/tutorial.md).

**Constraints:** The repository describes beta, Windows-only tooling, untested co-op, no working dialogue/quest system and game-update sensitivity. These limit the example to a modest encounter space. The repository identifies its code as MIT-licensed; that does not license Outward's art. [Repository README](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit). The detailed authoring guide also warns that altering/stretching props can embed art rather than references, so review exported contents and redistribution rights; do not generalize the references-only default to every possible export. [Authoring guide](https://github.com/Robin-Rounthwaite/Outward-Construction-Kit/blob/main/docs/making-a-region.md).

**Release and support interpretation:** a complete extension needs more than an impressive scene. Its author should state the exact game/tool versions, dependencies, save and multiplayer constraints, known failures and removal procedure. A useful issue report supplies those versions and a minimal reproduction. An isolated profile helps reproduce a configuration; it does not itself prove compatibility or security.

### 7.6 Version, platform and installation constraints

The indexed installation guide distinguishes original Outward from Definitive Edition, identifies a Mono configuration and the Definitive Edition executable path, and cautions about older tooling or uploads. Direct access to that wiki page was blocked, so those details have a narrower access basis than the package pages read in full. A Nexus page's availability alone should not override more recent maintainer instructions. Sources: [wiki, indexed excerpt only](https://outward.wiki.gg/wiki/Installing_Mods), [SideLoader Nexus page](https://www.nexusmods.com/outward/mods/96?tab=description).

A useful compatibility record would include game edition and build, runtime loader, shared framework versions, every direct dependency, operating system and the exact failing operation. Labels such as “PC compatible” or “works with Outward” are too broad to settle those questions. None of the inspected Windows/PC documentation establishes mod support on PS4, PS5, Xbox or Switch. It also does not establish that a particular package works through a compatibility layer or on handheld PC hardware.

### 7.7 Multiplayer, saves and progression

There is no single multiplayer rule for the entire ecosystem. A mod can be local presentation, a synchronized rule change, new content that other clients must know, or a change to persistent state. Whether host-only installation suffices depends on implementation. The documented all-participant requirement for one package cannot be generalized to every package, and Construction Kit's untested co-op status must remain visible.

For research and release notes, the critical questions are: who owns the changed state; which clients need its definitions; what happens when a client joins late; whether reconnecting reconstructs the same state; and whether the save remains usable if the package is removed. These are proposed tests, not verified failures. They matter especially where the base game already distinguishes personal character advancement from a hosted world or quest state; a mod can add another layer to that ownership model.

Construction Kit's player instructions describe retaining the kit when removing a region so that a character inside can be returned to its entrance. That is a package-specific recovery provision, not a guarantee that arbitrary content removal is safe. Source: [Construction Kit instructions](https://thunderstore.io/c/outward/p/Blue_Chamber_Collective/Outward_Construction_Kit/).

### 7.8 Trust and security

Runtime plugins are software that executes on a player's machine. A popular page, open repository or large download counter does not itself constitute a security audit. This research did not inspect package binaries, perform static analysis, compare release artifacts to source, assess dependency vulnerabilities or run a sandboxed installation.

A proportionate trust practice would examine the maintainer, release history, source when available, exact download location and requested configuration changes. Version pinning and a separate profile improve reproducibility and recovery; they do not make malicious code safe. Logs and support requests should avoid exposing unrelated local information. These are general software-risk observations, not an allegation about any named Outward package.

### 7.9 Rights, attribution and creator incentives

The availability of source code does not establish its license, and a code license does not automatically grant redistribution rights to the game's models, music, textures, writing or trademarks. The Construction Kit repository declares its code MIT-licensed, but this research did not audit all package licenses or the rights to every included asset. No claim is made that every referenced package permits commercial reuse, redistribution or incorporation into Open Legend. Runtime access to installed assets and bundling those assets for third parties are different operations with potentially different rights questions.

The evidence establishes public distribution and community authorship. It does not establish a paid mod marketplace, creator earnings, typical donation income or whether a given maintainer is compensated. Plausible incentives include personal customization, learning, portfolio work, reputation and helping a community, but those remain hypotheses until individual creators state them. Maintenance burden is similarly unquantified: dependency changes can create work for downstream creators even when the original mod's design has not changed.

For Open Legend, the useful research implication is to make extension rights, attribution expectations and support boundaries explicit if an ecosystem is planned. It would be inappropriate to copy a community project's code or content based only on the fact that its package can be downloaded freely.

### 7.10 Discovery, maintenance and the long tail

Catalogs make extensions discoverable and shared frameworks let creators build on common work. Dependency relationships can also concentrate risk: a widely used framework's compatibility issue may affect many otherwise unrelated packages. Conversely, a stable foundational package may remain useful without frequent updates. “Recently updated” and “well maintained” overlap but are not identical judgments.

Download counts are particularly easy to overinterpret. Reinstallations, updates, automated dependency downloads, multiple profiles and inactive users can all separate package downloads from unique active players. A framework's dependents indicate how many packages declare a relationship, not how many creators remain active or how many installations work. Without game-level mod telemetry or a representative survey, the share of Outward players using mods remains unknown.

The safest long-tail conclusion is qualitative: extensions provide additional ways for some players to revisit, adapt and create around the game. Attributing a specific amount of retention, play time, sentiment improvement or sales to that ecosystem would require more evidence.

### 7.11 Open Legend transfer hypotheses and boundaries

The positive opportunity is expressive variation: the documented alternate openings, rule overhauls and region tools let creators pursue different experiences with shared foundations. For Open Legend, the useful comparison is whether a creator can make one meaningful change, understand its consequences and reach ordinary play that honors it. The building-template repair illustrates why retaining a declared value is insufficient when the running game ignores it. The [existing world-creation and invention-reuse journeys](../../../../../docs/projects/world-creation-feature-spec.md#151-the-first-complete-round-trip) own the corresponding design; the [applications map](04-openlegend-lessons.md#10-use-existing-owners-and-observations) identifies its current task routes.

These examples also raise four practical questions within those owners:

- **Supported contribution and dependencies:** can the creator understand what the chosen world can run, including a useful explanation of missing or incompatible content? Current-format definitions and admission retain their existing authority. Published-version continuity and removal promises belong to the selected sharing product; this research introduces no legacy support during development.
- **Authoring capability and trust:** bounded content authoring and unrestricted runtime code offer different powers and risks. A general mod loader is not a prerequisite for useful authored variation.
- **Complete use:** can a small example cover entry, interaction, persistence and exit? Multiplayer installation and state requirements need their own evidence; solo success does not establish them.
- **Actual adoption:** successful installation, useful play and retained creators answer different questions from raw downloads. These are interpretation distinctions, not a new instrumentation requirement.

These are research hypotheses, not a stack recommendation or additional delivery gate. Existing architecture, creator audience and support burden determine product scope. [Open Legend's root policies](../../../../../AGENTS.md) retain authority over compatibility, engine/world separation and generated-content boundaries.

### 7.12 Explicit unverified areas

No mods were installed, launched, authored or published. No modded saves were loaded; no multiplayer sessions, uninstall recovery, performance measurements or platform compatibility tests were run. Package-wide license compliance, binary safety, source-to-release reproducibility, maintainer compensation and the developer's formal modding support policy were not established. The dossier can fully describe the observed ecosystem while preserving these boundaries.

## 9. Remaining information that public evidence did not resolve

The unavailable private data are original all-in budget, actual publisher contract, recoupment and funding repayment details, platform/territory/price mix, game-level net receipts and profit, marketing spend, channel attribution, creator compensation and expansion/port profitability. Public-facing uncertainty also remains around precise sales-count methodology, unique mod adoption, package-by-package compatibility and the formal rights/support policy for extensions.

These gaps should remain visible. They do not prevent a substantive dossier: the available record supports the production constraints, financing sequence, commercial scale, long-lived product strategy, meaningful soundtrack contribution and a full description of the community extension ecosystem. It supports qualified hypotheses for Open Legend, while keeping those hypotheses separate from measured results.
