## Central contrast with the preceding games

The third game shifts from gathering specialists to obtaining the support of societies already burdened by old conflicts. Shepard's personal history makes the war intelligible through people, not merely fleet totals. The central design question is how those particular relationships coexist with an aggregate readiness system and a necessarily bounded ending. This is our interpretation of the reviews and documented systems, not a claim of complete autonomous diplomatic simulation. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2)

## Reputation is not the second game's morality check

Patrick Weekes's developer account explains a deliberate change: Paragon, Renegade and general reputation contribute to overall persuasive standing. Mixing moral approaches no longer imposes the same penalty as in Mass Effect 2. General reputation can reward an action without giving it an artificial moral label. This preserves expression while still rewarding involvement in the world. Shepard remains a protagonist trying to save the galaxy, not a player who can switch to the Reapers' victory campaign. [S6](#s6)

**Interpretation:** an expressive distinction and a capability threshold need not be the same numerical axis. The system can record how someone acts without requiring moral consistency merely to retain access to competent responses. That does not imply reputation alone overrides every story prerequisite.

## Combat depth through loadout cost

Classes no longer prohibit whole conventional weapon categories in the same way. A weapon's weight instead affects power recharge, making a heavy arsenal compete with frequent power use. This is a soft constraint rather than a free combination of every advantage. Mods and weapon upgrades offer additional choices; exact weight is not simply synonymous with damage. [S3](#s3) [S7](#s7)

The action vocabulary adds more movement and melee flexibility while retaining squad powers, cover, weapon handling and defense types. Enemies can force relocation rather than permit indefinite firing from one safe barrier. The new flexibility is undermined when one contextual button must ambiguously choose among rolling, taking cover and interacting: both PC Gamer and Destructoid report this frustration. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3)

Nova illustrates a more unusual resource relationship. It consumes a Vanguard's barrier energy for an area attack instead of operating only through an ordinary cooldown. Charge can replenish that defensive resource, creating an aggressive cycle, but proximity to enemies with lethal grabs remains dangerous. A build guide's “risk-free” description should not erase those exceptions. [S8](#s8)

## Launch-review evidence collected

**Kevin VanOrd, GameSpot, March 6, 2012:** the full review values the emotional use of prior relationships and improved combat spaces. Its praise coexists with criticism of scanning, camera/scripting issues and residual squad/cover problems. It is a launch review, not an assessment of Extended Cut or Citadel. [S1](#s1)

**Tom Francis, PC Gamer, March 6, 2012:** the complete substantive body connects imported survivors and replacement characters to a personally owned campaign, and welcomes the weapon/power tradeoff. It criticizes the overloaded interaction key, scripted antagonist escapes and an ending both moving and overly simple. Multiplayer servers were not live for the review; the article must not be represented as a full live-mode test. [S2](#s2)

**Conrad Zimmerman, Destructoid, March 6, 2012:** the complete body appreciates customization, character continuity and optional voice commands while criticizing the journal and contextual controls. His uncertainty about readiness effects is reception evidence, not a reliable replacement for documented war-asset rules: the review appears to conflate persistent readiness and campaign-specific assets. [S3](#s3)

**John McCarroll, RPGFan, March 6, 2012:** the substantive review praises character resolution and richer build choices but finds a mismatch between the urgent fiction and unhurried Citadel errands. Its editor explicitly excludes multiplayer from the evaluated experience because servers were not yet live. [S4](#s4)

**Hollander Cooper, GamesRadar:** substantial first-page text praises personalized narrative and dramatic set pieces; the verdict qualifies the comparatively impersonal final ending. The remaining second page and displayed publication context are still to inspect before finalizing this evidence entry. [S5](#s5)

## Commercial and version facts already verified

EA's March 9, 2012 announcement reports **over 3.5 million units shipped to retailers worldwide** in the first week. This is not a consumer sell-through total, a revenue figure or an active-player count. The final pass will retain that terminology and distinguish it from later analyst estimates. [S9](#s9)

Extended Cut is an additional ending treatment, not the original March launch presentation. The Xbox publisher listing records June 26, 2012 and describes additional scenes and epilogue material. BioWare's contemporary announcement presents it as a response to demands for greater context while retaining the underlying artistic conclusion. Neither a launch review nor a later remaster review should silently stand in for both versions. [S10](#s10)

Original multiplayer contains character progression, randomized reinforcement packs and cooperative objectives. Free expansion maps and newly available kits do not mean every kit is immediately unlocked. The Earth announcement explicitly distinguishes available maps from weapons and characters obtained through reinforcement packs. The mode is not a cooperative version of Shepard's campaign. [S11](#s11)
