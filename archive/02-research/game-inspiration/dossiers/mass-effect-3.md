# G103 — Mass Effect 3

[Research roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Mass Effect 2](mass-effect-2.md) · [Library](../README.md)

**Research date:** September 26, 2026. **Pass:** R01–R14 complete, with evidence-access limitations recorded below and the requested second-pass audit recorded after the requirement map. Documentary research, not a gameplay test. **Spoilers:** sections 5–7 discuss Tuchanka, Rannoch and the ending; DLC sections disclose premises and modes. No video, benchmark or personal play observation is claimed.

**Central finding — interpretation:** the strongest consequences preserve the identity of the people involved, not merely the quantity of support earned. A returning friend, a substitute with different convictions and a species whose future depends on old decisions can make the same war feel personally specific. The aggregate military-strength system connects many activities, but also exposes the risk of compressing distinctive commitments into interchangeable points. The reception dispute over the finale makes that tension unusually visible.

## 1. Identity, release and the player's role

BioWare and Electronic Arts released Mass Effect 3 for Windows, Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 in March 2012, with the North American launch on March 6. The Wii U **Special Edition**, developed with Straight Right, followed later in 2012. The 2021 **Legendary Edition** remasters the Shepard trilogy's single-player campaigns. These releases differ in controls, included content and readiness rules; a launch review is not an assessment of every later edition. [S9](#s9) [S26](#s26) [S14](#s14)

The campaign is a **single-player third-person action RPG**. Shepard is directly controlled and normally brings two AI squadmates. A separate original-release multiplayer mode supports cooperative missions with up to four players using different characters and species. It is not a cooperative playthrough of Shepard's story. Legendary Edition omits that multiplayer mode; no claim about present server availability or population is made here. [S12](#s12) [S14](#s14)

The opening places Shepard in the Reaper attack on Earth, then turns escape into a search for allies and a possible means of ending the invasion. A returning player can import a surviving Mass Effect 2 career; a newcomer can create a Shepard with an established background state. Importing provides more than cosmetic continuity, but does not import every preceding game's equipment rules unchanged. The Wii U interactive comic reconstructs selected earlier decisions rather than recovering a console save from the prior games. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2) [S26](#s26)

Established play alternates among authored missions, conversations on the Normandy and Citadel, equipment decisions, galaxy-map searches and the accumulation of war support. The player recruits help but does not directly administer all allied nations, command a strategic fleet battle or run a civilian economy. The urgency is primarily narrative, with particular mission-state deadlines and exclusions rather than one universally advancing invasion clock. [S3](#s3) [S16](#s16)

## 2. Action vocabulary and combat relationships

### Classes remain identities, but weapons become a soft constraint

Soldier, Adept, Engineer, Infiltrator, Vanguard and Sentinel remain the six protagonist classes. Their powers encourage different rhythms: Adrenaline Rush emphasizes a shooting opportunity; biotics manipulate and damage targets; technology attacks defenses or creates distractions; Tactical Cloak supports an opening; Charge and Nova encourage aggressive proximity; Tech Armor changes defensive tradeoffs. Powers now develop to six ranks, with alternatives at the higher ranks rather than one final binary evolution alone. [S7](#s7) [S8](#s8) [S18](#s18)

The crucial loadout change is **weight**. Conventional weapon categories are not simply barred by class as before; carrying a heavier arsenal instead competes with power recharge. This does not make every heavy weapon superior or every light loadout optimal. It makes the desired frequency of powers part of an equipment decision. Modifications and character development can alter the comparison. Recharge-speed percentages must not be read as a literal negative cooldown duration. [S7](#s7)

| Activity | Available actions and conditions | Cost, failure or feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct combat | Aim, fire, reload, take cover, move, dodge, use melee and activate powers. | Ammunition, exposure, cooldowns and enemy positioning matter. A contextual input can perform the wrong intended action. |
| Squad tactics | Choose companions, allocate powers, position them and select targets or abilities. | A chosen companion must be present and able to act. Command access does not guarantee flawless allied movement. |
| Defense and recovery | Read shields, armor, barriers and health; seek safety, revive allies or use the appropriate recovery resource. | Temporary incapacitation differs from authored death. Recovery does not make close-range lethal enemy attacks harmless. |
| Investigation | Inspect authored objects, read messages, overhear conversations, select journal destinations and choose dialogue. | Not every object is usable; an overheard request does not guarantee the journal clearly reports its subsequent state. |
| Expedition | Travel on the galaxy map, scan for useful targets and extract people or resources. | Scanning attracts Reaper attention; remaining too long creates an escape problem rather than a ship-to-ship combat encounter. |
| Preparation | Buy and upgrade weapons, configure mods and armor, adjust powers and inspect war assets. | Credits and development points compete among uses; equipment improvement is not general-purpose crafting. |

The inventory above summarizes the manual, combat references and review observations, not an assertion that every location accepts every action. [S12](#s12) [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3) [S19](#s19)

### Powers interact through target state

The game distinguishes biotic, electrical, fire and cryogenic combination families. An appropriate primer establishes a state that a compatible detonator exploits. For example, electrical priming and Concussive Shot can support a different interaction from lifting a target and applying Warp. These are defined combinations, not a guarantee that any two powers create a bonus. Enemy protection, power availability and the particular game's patch/mode rules remain relevant. Early manual descriptions should not override later balancing changes. [S12](#s12) [S13](#s13)

**Interpretation:** a useful tactical interface exposes more than damage numbers. It should help the player see a target's current condition and which ally can exploit it. Otherwise the cooperative vocabulary exists only in a guide. Conversely, unrestricted automation of all combinations would remove the timing and selection decisions that make the relationship interesting.

Nova uses a particularly clear resource exchange: a Vanguard spends barrier energy on an area attack. Charge can restore barrier energy, creating an aggressive sequence, but the player still needs an available target, appropriate timing and a safe exit. The animation's protection is not universal immunity to lethal grabs. Defense becomes a resource that can be deliberately converted into offense, not a passive statistic that always improves independently of damage. [S8](#s8)

**Enemy roles and temporary machinery.** Grissom Academy mixes infantry, shield-support devices, riot-shield Guardians and Atlas mechs. A shield-removing power and a way around a physical riot shield answer different obstacles. Near the mission's end, Shepard can enter an empty Atlas and use its gun and missiles, then abandon it if its protection becomes inadequate. This is a bounded pilotable weapon platform, not an exploration vehicle retained for later planets. Smoke, shield support and different engagement ranges make target selection matter beyond recognizing a faction label. [S37](#s37)

## 3. Equipment, progression, resources and recovery

The conventional weapon families are pistols, submachine guns, assault rifles, shotguns and sniper rifles. The M-3 Predator, M-4 Shuriken and M-8 Avenger are concrete examples of recognizable equipment rather than anonymous loot tiers. Weapons can be upgraded through the Normandy's armory; a first playthrough's ordinary upgrade ceiling differs from New Game Plus, which permits rank X under its acquisition conditions. Reacquiring a found weapon can matter as well as paying for its improvement. [S20](#s20)

Mods change a weapon's practical role, and armor bonuses offer another layer of configuration. A weapon can be found during a mission or bought from a vendor; the Normandy's procurement interface provides another route to purchases. This is not the first game's constant management of randomly generated tiers for every companion, nor a workshop economy that manufactures arbitrary designs. The finite campaign's vendors and rewards support prepared combat styles. [S26](#s26)

A conventional weapon has **two modification slots**, with compatibility restrictions between some modifications. A discovered mod is reusable across applicable weapons and squad loadouts, not consumed to improve only one physical gun. Additional instances raise that mod's level, up to V; this differs from the weapon's own upgrade rank. Thus finding a better barrel and paying to upgrade a rifle are separate progression routes. [S35](#s35)

The Normandy procurement interface exposes visited shops at a **10% markup**. Buying there trades credits for less travel; it does not create a second autonomous supply market. Its neighboring weapon-upgrade, workbench and armor interfaces make the shuttle bay a preparation hub. A player can rationally pay for convenience or return to a store, depending on the value of credits and interruption. [S43](#s43)

Experience develops Shepard up to level 60. An imported Mass Effect 2 character begins with carried development rather than necessarily returning to level one. Powers require increasing point investment and branch at ranks four through six. Retraining allows a player to reconsider allocation; it is not permission to turn one class into every class simultaneously. The separate multiplayer progression has its own level-20 milestone and unlock structure, not a second Shepard leveling track inside the campaign. [S18](#s18) [S21](#s21) [S22](#s22)

**Interpretation:** respecialization and a soft equipment constraint encourage experimentation because a mistaken build is not automatically a ruined career. The tradeoff still needs to be visible: changing from an elaborate arsenal to a lighter setup should alter how the player fights, not merely move a hidden efficiency value.

Ordinary defeat is a checkpoint/save-recovery problem. Scripted deaths, failed rescues and imported absences are separate kinds of state. New Game Plus restarts the campaign with retained character/equipment development, not an already won war or every prior war asset. A post-ending return is not an unrestricted playable reconstruction of the galaxy after the chosen ending. The campaign's closure and replay systems therefore differ from a persistent society simulator. [S20](#s20) [S27](#s27)

**First Aid is not unchanged Unity.** The single-player ability spends medi-gel to heal/revive the squad and restore Shepard's health, with a health-restoration exception on Insanity while taking damage. A living squadmate's shields are not restored by it. Shepard can instead revive an ordinarily fallen squadmate manually at their body without spending gel, exchanging movement and exposure for supply preservation. First Aid has its own activation behavior rather than the universal power cooldown. None of this reverses an authored permanent death. [S36](#s36)

In multiplayer, named supplies make preparation concrete: **Cobra Missile Launcher**, **Thermal Clip Pack**, **Medi-Gel** and **Ops Survival Pack** answer different emergencies. A single-mission equipment boost is distinct from a capacity upgrade or a persistent gear slot. A player can spend a scarce emergency item to save an objective rather than hoard it for a hypothetical perfect match. This vocabulary belongs to the cooperative mode and should not be copied wholesale into Shepard's single-player inventory. [S12](#s12) [S23](#s23)

## 4. Reputation, people and relationships

Patrick Weekes's developer explanation describes a deliberate revision from Mass Effect 2: Paragon, Renegade and general reputation contribute to overall persuasive standing. Mixed moral choices no longer impose the same consistency penalty. General reputation can recognize meaningful involvement without declaring every helpful act morally opposite to an aggressive one. This does not mean a high bar bypasses every mission prerequisite or makes Shepard able to join the Reapers' campaign. [S6](#s6)

**Interpretation:** expression and capability do not have to use one axis. A game can remember how a person behaves while allowing a broadly respected person to choose either a compassionate or forceful argument. The remaining design obligation is to communicate when a refusal comes from reputation, a missing relationship, unavailable evidence or another party's actual priorities.

The field team combines returning survivors with newcomers: Liara, Garrus, Tali, James and EDI, with Ashley or Kaidan depending on the established history and current events; Javik is added by From Ashes. Availability must not be inferred from a franchise character list alone. People who do not become regular squadmates can still have consequential roles, and an authored replacement can have a different attitude rather than merely a different portrait. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2) [S24](#s24)

Romance is a set of written routes and continuation conditions, not a universal affinity-and-gift system. New possibilities include Steve Cortez for male Shepard and Samantha Traynor for female Shepard. Kaidan can be romanced by either male or female Shepard in this game; the first game's restriction must not be carried forward. Returning relationships, brief encounters and a full continuing route are distinct. A player cannot assume every former partner is available for a fresh courtship or that every flirt produces the same commitment. [S25](#s25)

Hospital visits, messages, Citadel meetings and Normandy conversations make attention to people part of the return loop. Those are authored scenes and state changes. They do not establish autonomous daily schedules, unrestricted off-screen romance, emergent family formation or independent faction government. The game's ability to make a person feel responsive should not be confused with a claim about an underlying general social simulation. [S4](#s4) [S25](#s25)

## 5. The war and the difference between specific and aggregate consequences

The Reaper assault changes the meaning of familiar places. Earth is no longer simply humanity's distant home; the Citadel becomes a place where refugees and officials interpret the war; the missions around Palaven, Tuchanka and Rannoch connect military help to older political conflicts. Cerberus is an opposing force with a different agenda, not merely another interchangeable enemy faction. The Crucible supplies a strategic goal while individual relationships give that goal a human scale. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4)

War assets represent people, fleets, equipment and other contributions through recorded military values. In the original game, **Effective Military Strength** combines total assets with Galactic Readiness. Multiplayer and associated original Galaxy at War routes can affect readiness. Legendary Edition removes that active readiness layer and multiplayer dependency, rebalancing the single-player asset requirements. Original EMS thresholds are therefore not safe instructions for the remaster's visible total. [S14](#s14)

**Interpretation:** aggregation solves a practical problem: many different activities can visibly contribute to one shared effort. It also creates a narrative hazard. A diplomatic compromise and an unrelated recovered resource may both increase a total, even though the player does not regard them as substitutes. The dossier's central question is how much of that difference survives when the campaign resolves.

**Different deadline types.** Once acquired, **Tuchanka: Bomb** fails after completing three other missions. **Grissom Academy: Emergency Evacuation** instead expires at **Priority: The Citadel II**. That distinction corrects the tempting but unsupported generalization that every urgent rescue has the same three-mission timer. The guide also warns that returning to the Citadel after the Tuchanka story transition triggers an attack rather than an ordinary shopping visit. These are authored mission-state changes, not a sixty-minute rescue clock or a universal simulation of everyone waiting off-screen. [S37](#s37)

**Tuchanka spoilers.** The cure negotiation involves krogan leadership, salarian demands and the scientist's convictions. Earlier preservation of Maelon's research affects Eve's survival. Sabotaging the cure is not automatically an undetectable way to obtain everyone's support: Wrex can discover the deception later, unlike Wreav. The situation links previous judgment, present trust and future political consequences, rather than presenting a universally superior moral answer. [S16](#s16)

**Rannoch spoilers.** Peace depends on more than an impressive conversation statistic. Tali and Legion's survival, prior decisions and relevant preparatory missions constrain the available route. A substitute geth unit is not mechanically identical to Legion. Even the peaceful outcome does not preserve every person unchanged: Legion's sacrifice remains part of that resolution. [S15](#s15)

**Ending spoilers.** The finale offers Destroy, Control and Synthesis subject to the applicable game-state conditions; Extended Cut adds a Refusal route and substantial contextual presentation. Military strength influences available outcomes and collateral consequences. These are bounded authored resolutions, not a simulation calculating every civilization's future independently. Extended Cut and Legendary Edition must be distinguished from the original March presentation. [S10](#s10) [S27](#s27)

## 6. Six worked interactions

The following are **constructed, rules-based illustrations**, not observed sessions. They assume the stated abilities, equipment and mission history. Their purpose is to expose dependencies and limits rather than supply a universal optimal walkthrough.

### A. Carry less to do something more often

**Intention:** use powers frequently while retaining a reliable firearm. **Conditions:** a class with useful powers and several available weapons. **Actions:** compare a heavy multi-weapon loadout with a lighter selection, inspect the recharge effect and enter an encounter with the intended rhythm in mind. **Interaction:** equipment breadth competes with ability frequency. **Result:** a smaller arsenal can be more capable for this plan. **Next decision:** retain it or add a specialist weapon for the next encounter. **Limit:** lightness does not solve every defense matchup or ammunition shortage; the soft constraint creates a tradeoff, not a universally correct answer. [S7](#s7)

### B. Spend protection, then recover it deliberately

**Intention:** break a clustered attack as a Vanguard. **Conditions:** Charge, Nova, sufficient barrier energy and a valid approach target. **Actions:** close distance, use Nova to convert barrier energy into an attack, then reposition or recover through the next valid ability opportunity. **Interaction:** defense funds offense, so the sequence exposes the player after the expenditure. **Result:** an aggressive loop that rewards timing. **Next decision:** remain in close range or disengage. **Limit:** a lethal-grab enemy, unavailable target or poor cooldown timing can turn the plan into a fatal commitment; the combo is not permanent invulnerability. [S8](#s8)

### C. Make a persuasive request the history can support

**Rannoch spoilers. Intention:** preserve both quarians and geth. **Conditions:** the required surviving characters, adequate reputation, preparatory mission completion and a sufficient history of relevant decisions. **Actions:** finish the available supporting work, then take the appropriate conversation route when it is offered. **Interaction:** present persuasion operates on an accumulated relationship history. **Result:** reconciliation becomes possible under qualified conditions. **Next decision:** interpret the loss that still accompanies the agreement. **Limit:** raising reputation at the last moment cannot resurrect Legion, undo every earlier event or make his replacement equivalent; “peace” is not “everyone survives.” [S15](#s15)

### D. A past research decision changes who reaches the negotiation

**Tuchanka spoilers. Intention:** secure a cure and a durable alliance. **Conditions:** the imported decision about Maelon's data and the current krogan leader. **Actions:** confront the offered sabotage rather than evaluate only the immediate asset reward. **Interaction:** Eve's survival and the leader's ability to discover deceit alter both the available social situation and later consequences. **Result:** the same superficial offer can have a different meaning in another career. **Next decision:** accept a sacrifice or another political cost. **Limit:** preserving the data does not make every person immortal; this authored benefit also does not establish a general ethical defense of the experiments that produced it. [S16](#s16)

### E. Distinguish a search from an unlimited collection screen

**Intention:** retrieve a useful target from a Reaper-controlled region. **Conditions:** the Normandy's scanner and a visible attention gauge. **Actions:** scan, locate a target, decide whether to make another pass and escape when the pursuers arrive. **Interaction:** exploration raises a local threat rather than directly producing a space battle. **Result:** an additional item can carry a movement risk. **Next decision:** leave with the current find or attempt another acquisition. **Limit:** this is a bounded map interaction, not evidence that every planet runs an autonomous occupation model. The review criticism also matters: a threat can be legible and still feel like irritating interruption. [S19](#s19) [S26](#s26)

### F. Win the objective rather than the personal damage contest

**Original multiplayer. Intention:** complete a timed package-retrieval objective. **Conditions:** a cooperative match with the Rebellion objective type and teammates able to cover the route. **Actions:** assign someone to carry the package while others suppress threats or revive a fallen player; spend an emergency supply when it protects the mission. **Interaction:** individual combat output becomes subordinate to a shared task. **Result:** coordinated roles can finish an objective that isolated shooting does not. **Next decision:** regroup for the next wave and assess remaining supplies. **Limit:** unlocked characters, consumables and permanent gear are distinct resources; a free map expansion does not instantly grant every desirable kit. [S23](#s23) [S12](#s12)

## 7. DLC and multiplayer additions change different parts of the experience

**From Ashes, March 2012:** the return to Eden Prime adds Javik, a surviving Prothean whose presence complicates the crew's inherited assumptions about that civilization. This is a new recurring perspective and squad capability, not only a weapon reward. Its original paid/edition-dependent distribution is separate from its inclusion in later complete packages. [S24](#s24) [S28](#s28)

**Extended Cut, June 2012:** additional scenes, dialogue and epilogue material expand the finale's context. BioWare's announcement presents this as maintaining the underlying artistic conclusion while addressing requests for clarity. Added explanation should not be equated with universal audience satisfaction or with a completely new campaign ending system. The player accounts below distinguish those reactions. [S10](#s10) [S27](#s27)

**Leviathan, August 2012:** an investigation links laboratory clues, disturbed people and an underwater encounter to the Reapers' origins. VanOrd values its atmosphere and several emotional moments but criticizes repeated clue-clicking that supplies little genuine deduction. This is a useful distinction between performing the gestures of an investigation and making consequential inferences. Its major revelation is largely delivered through dialogue and scenes, rather than through a new repeatable simulation. [S29](#s29)

**Omega, November 2012:** Aria's attempt to retake the station supplies temporary companions, including Nyreen, aggressive enemies and larger-feeling combat spaces. It does not restore Omega as a freely revisitable hub or add those companions permanently to the Normandy. VanOrd praises the fighting while arguing that thin characterization fails to make the mission's stakes matter. Winning a location in fiction is therefore not the same as acquiring an ongoing management system. [S30](#s30)

**Citadel, March 2013:** an assassination conspiracy leads to a deliberately comic reunion and time with the crew. The apartment party, Silversun Strip activities and Armax Arena create a social and mechanical return loop. Arena modifiers and eligible former squadmates provide replay opportunities without rewriting the regular campaign roster; the enemies are simulated, while returning companions require survival and access conditions. McGee enjoys the reunion but sees limited narrative substance beyond fan-oriented humor. The tonal shift is a design choice with an audience boundary, not automatically a better version of the war story. [S31](#s31) [S38](#s38)

**Armax's repeatable rules.** Configure the map, enemy set, challenge tier and zero to two allies; earn score through the fights, streaks and bonus objectives. Optional handicaps such as removing placed ammunition increase score, whereas a player-damage advantage reduces it. Bronze, silver and gold rewards can buy further options or credits. Former squadmates such as Wrex and Miranda require ally licences and suitable living/story states; the simulator does not resurrect dead friends. These choices turn repeated combat into constrained experimentation, but they are not the separate four-player mode. [S38](#s38)

**The arcade is actual play, not just scenery.** Shattered Eezo uses mech-boxing jabs, blocks and charged attacks; the Claw Game rewards selecting moving/color-changing prizes; Relay Defense combines shooting threats with routing allied shuttles. Credits fund attempts, and rewards include mods, apartment items and money. By contrast, the visible Kepesh-Yakshi and Towers of Hanoi installations are not playable by Shepard. This matters when cataloguing environmental affordances: a named fictional game or a companion's scene does not establish a player-controlled minigame. [S39](#s39)

**Original multiplayer support:** Resurgence, Rebellion, Earth, Retaliation and Reckoning expanded maps, objectives, kits, enemies and equipment across the first year. Earth adds urban battlefields and a higher difficulty; its announced N7 kits include roles such as Fury, Demolisher and Paladin. The Acolyte, Piranha and Typhoon are named weapon additions, not proof of automatic ownership. Maps can be immediately available while characters and weapons still require reinforcement-pack unlocks. [S11](#s11) [S24](#s24) [S32](#s32)

Randomized packs and earned currency provide a longer-term multiplayer acquisition loop; the original mode also offered paid pack purchases. Single-match boosts, expendable supplies, permanent gear and character unlocks should not be grouped under one generic “upgrade” label. Multiplayer balance and available kits changed with support updates, so launch reviews describe an earlier roster than the first-anniversary announcement. [S12](#s12) [S23](#s23) [S33](#s33)

## 8. Presentation, interface and experience

The campaign alternates spectacle with quiet scenes. Refugees, memorials and a hospital visit can give the war a scale that a larger explosion cannot. Dark environments and contrasting colors reinforce danger, while performances carry uncertainty and grief. These are authored presentation techniques, not measured proof that every player develops the same attachment. Reviewers also observed camera errors, awkward animations and allied AI behavior that could interrupt the effect. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4)

Sam Hulick describes increasing piano's emotional role while revisiting the first game's electronic sound. Cris Velasco and Sascha Dikiciyan describe a hybrid orchestral/electronic score that introduces material without abandoning the series' identity; their earlier Arrival theme informs “The Scientists.” Hulick identifies the romance cue's piano/orchestra/synth combination and layered Mars combat music as different uses of that vocabulary. These are composers' stated choices, not a soundtrack listening test performed for this dossier. [S40](#s40)

The contextual movement/interact button illustrates a practical conflict: minimizing controls can make intentions ambiguous. PC Gamer and Destructoid describe unwanted cover or movement actions. Destructoid also criticizes a journal that inadequately records changing side-task status. These are not cosmetic objections. When the game asks players to care about consequences, poor action and information feedback can make a failure feel accidental rather than owned. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3)

The Wii U edition uses the tablet for mapped powers and squad positioning. VanOrd finds direct power access helpful to combat flow but describes setup and positioning friction. Its comic helps newcomers establish context, and its incorporated Extended Cut differs from the original launch ending. Leviathan and Omega were absent from the reviewed package. These observations concern that port, not a feature set automatically present in Legendary Edition. [S26](#s26)

Xbox 360 Kinect voice support is another original-platform interface option, not a universal remaster capability. The selectable Story, Action and Role Playing styles vary the balance of combat difficulty and conversation control, rather than creating different autonomous worlds. Specifically, Action automates dialogue choices into scenes, while Story retains conversation choices with greatly reduced combat difficulty; Role Playing keeps both normal choice and combat control. These presets change participation, not the world's simulated breadth. No formal accessibility audit, subtitle legibility measurement, soundtrack analysis or input-latency test was performed for this dossier. [S3](#s3) [S12](#s12)

## 9. Production, promotion and the maintenance of a shared story

The developer's reputation explanation provides direct evidence of a specific design revision: separating moral expression from the penalty of inconsistent point collection. That is a stronger production claim than inferring internal intentions from a score or a character's popularity. The original game also had to reconcile imported survivors and absences with a finite authored campaign; the differing replacement characters visible in reviews show the result, not the exact proprietary implementation or staffing cost. [S6](#s6) [S2](#s2)

**A concrete production collaboration.** Audio lead Rob Blake describes planning ME3's musical narrative with Casey Hudson after ME2, then coordinating Clint Mansell with Christopher Lennertz, Sam Hulick, Sascha Dikiciyan and Cris Velasco. The aim was to keep individual relationships emotionally intelligible amid a galactic war. This corrects the shorthand that Mansell alone scored the whole game. Blake's retrospective about layered combat music between ME1 and ME2 is historical background, not proof that a particular new middleware system was invented for ME3. The account provides team roles and an iteration goal without inventing budget, staffing totals or proprietary architecture. [S41](#s41)

Extended Cut documents post-release work responding to audience feedback. BioWare explicitly framed the revision around greater context while preserving its intended conclusion. That is neither proof that all criticism was a misunderstanding nor evidence that the entire ending was replaced. The first-anniversary post subsequently treats substantial single-player DLC, multiplayer additions and weekly challenges as parallel forms of support. [S10](#s10) [S24](#s24)

The original promotion benefited from being the conclusion to an established trilogy; imported history supplied a personal reason to return. Continued multiplayer operations offered recurring community goals and rewards. BioWare's anniversary **Operation: LODESTAR** banner is a concrete example of celebrating participation through a shared challenge rather than merely announcing another sale. No independently audited influencer attribution, acquisition budget or causal viral-growth estimate was established. [S9](#s9) [S24](#s24)

**Marketing interpretation:** a game can be highly discussable because players disagree about what it owes their chosen history. That discussion is not automatically positive advocacy. The useful research question is how promotion, local responsiveness and final closure establish an expectation together; a promise of personal consequence can become a liability when the audience interprets the conclusion as interchangeable.

## 10. Commercial evidence and limits

EA's March 9, 2012 announcement reports **more than 3.5 million units shipped to retailers worldwide** in the first week. This is an attributed shipment figure, not a verified count of purchases by consumers, unique players, completed stories or concurrent users. No title-level development budget, profit, lifetime sell-through or current multiplayer population was established in this pass. [S9](#s9)

The original business model combined a premium campaign, paid story/equipment content, free multiplayer expansions and optional paid reinforcement packs. A free map did not guarantee possession of every associated character or gun. Later collections changed packaging and, in Legendary Edition, removed the multiplayer loop entirely. In-game credits, war-asset values and commercial revenue remain separate measures; none is a substitute for another. [S11](#s11) [S14](#s14) [S33](#s33)

## 12. Conditional research lessons for OpenLegend

**Keep the identity of a contribution visible.** An aggregate may help players understand overall readiness, but relationships should not disappear into a total. A useful experiment would show both the shared resource and the particular person, obligation or risk behind it. The failure mode is pretending every contribution is narratively interchangeable because it is numerically additive.

**Let expression differ from competence.** Reputation can support access to an argument without forcing the player to repeat one tone indefinitely. This requires clear distinctions among respect, trust, fear, evidence and willingness. A single universal reputation currency would lose the situational specificity that makes the better conversations credible.

**Preserve counterexamples to easy mastery.** A high skill, a loyal friend or excellent equipment should not automatically erase every historical fact. Rannoch is useful because a current dialogue choice has prerequisites that belong to earlier relationships. The danger is concealing those dependencies so thoroughly that the player experiences them as arbitrary guide requirements.

**Use opportunity costs to broaden access.** Weapon weight demonstrates an alternative to forbidding a tool outright. A system can allow a choice while making its cost meaningful. Such a cost must remain legible and connected to the intended play style; an obscure penalty creates frustration rather than experimentation.

**A resource can serve more than one purpose.** Nova suggests studying understandable exchanges between protection and exertion. The player should see the expenditure, know the recovery route and understand its exceptions. An aggressive loop with an invisible lethal counter is not automatically deep merely because it is risky.

**Closure needs a declared scope.** A world can support personal stories without promising an independently resolved ending for every possible state. What matters is whether the finale acknowledges the commitments the experience taught the player to value. Adding exposition and adding recognition are different interventions.

**Social downtime can be substantive content.** Citadel demonstrates the appeal of spending time with familiar people after repeated crisis. The prerequisite is earned familiarity; copying in-jokes or a party scene without that history is unlikely to produce the same response. A quieter local gathering may serve the function without copying the space-opera format.

These are research hypotheses, not accepted product requirements, implementation plans or permission to reproduce BioWare's characters and fiction.
