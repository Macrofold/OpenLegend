## Core distinction emerging from the research

Fallout 4 makes scavenging feed several different intentions. A found object can be sale value, ammunition, wearable equipment, a weapon modification ingredient or a piece of a future settlement. Phil Savage's launch review describes the change in attention this creates: household junk becomes worth examining because of its components. That does not imply unrestricted crafting; workshop boundaries, material requirements and perk gates still determine what can be made. [F1]

There is a separate distinction between equipment that persists and equipment that needs maintenance. Ordinary guns should not be assigned a weapon-durability system merely because power armor has repairable pieces and consumes fusion-core energy. Nor should VATS be called a full pause: it slows time in this game. Both distinctions matter when comparing the game with earlier Fallout entries and with Breath of the Wild. [F2]

## Review evidence already captured

**Phil Savage, PC Gamer, November 9, 2015.** Praised the dense Commonwealth, flexible character builds, improved shooting and crafting's ability to give junk a purpose. His main-story assessment was relatively favorable for a Bethesda game, although he wanted more moral latitude and unusual discoveries. Workshop interfaces, defensive interruptions and occasional script failures limited the freedom. [F1]

**Leon Hurley, GamesRadar, 2015 launch review.** Described growing attached to a personal garage and power armor after initially finding maintenance burdensome. This is a useful account of repeated care producing ownership rather than only friction. He praised the discovery loop and brighter visual palette while recording cumbersome inventory/crafting work and PS4 technical problems. Those are his tested-version observations, not a 2026 platform benchmark. [F2]

**Chris Carter, Destructoid, November 9, 2015.** Preferred many side stories to the principal family-search plot and found the voiced presentation, inventory and technical roughness uneven. Fusion-core scarcity led him to avoid power armor rather than develop Hurley's attachment. His VATS preference also differs from Savage's enthusiasm for manual shooting. The comparison preserves incompatible preferences instead of manufacturing a single consensus. [F3]

RPG Site and Push Square review bodies are being completed before they are counted toward the five-review gate. A store description, score roundup or review excerpt does not substitute for those readings.

## Steam player evidence

The accessible Steam Community surface was **Most Helpful (Week), English**, not an all-time top-review census. SilverSie's recommended account (808.0 displayed hours, September 22) praised combat, exploration, settlements and Nick Valentine while criticizing main-story depth. EmilyShoots120's negative account (23.9 displayed hours, September 19) objected to update/mod compatibility and paid Creations, but still valued side content and companions. Other sampled recommended accounts also complained about updates, demonstrating that the binary rating alone does not capture the reason for approval. Displayed hours are not assumed to be hours at original publication. These qualitative accounts do not measure how prevalent an opinion is. The attempted all-time-filter route did not load. [F4]

## Edition and commercial boundaries already verified

Bethesda's May 5, 2016 version 1.5 announcement describes the redesigned Survival difficulty: sleep-based saving, no fast travel, greater lethality, disease and fatigue. This must be separated from the launch review experience and from the earlier difficulty with the same name. The article's then-planned future mod dates are not proof that those launches happened on schedule. [F5]

Bethesda announced the Anniversary Edition on October 23, 2025 for November 10, 2025. It combines the base game, six official add-ons and over 150 Creation Club items, alongside a new Creations menu. This is not the date Fallout 4 first supported mods. Current platform additions and patch details still need verification before the final dossier. [F6]

Bethesda's November 13, 2015 launch communication reports approximately 12 million units worldwide and a represented retail value above $750 million. The final dossier will retain the announcement's distribution/accounting context rather than converting this into verified consumer sell-through, recognized net revenue or profit. It is not a current lifetime sales total. [F7]
