## 1. Identity and the useful question

EVE's useful design question is how a fitted ship, a physical shipment and a relationship with other players can acquire purpose together. A pilot can pursue exploration, industry, combat or support without treating the initial career choice as an irrevocable class. The AIR Career Program presents four paths—Explorer, Industrialist, Enforcer and Soldier of Fortune—with suggested goals, skill plans and rewards. Tracking a different goal does not erase earlier progress; valid untracked actions also count. [S01]

Current onboarding has a meaningful exception to EVE's dangerous-space reputation. After the introductory experience, new characters enter Exordium, a protected starter region where hostile player combat is mechanically disabled. Players can leave and return, while lower-yield activities and the absence of player structures give reasons to move outward. Its June 2026 rules must not be projected backward onto older reviews of the opening. [S05]

## 2. What the player does

A ship is a set of constrained capabilities. High, medium and low slots accept different modules; weapons can additionally need the correct turret or launcher hardpoint. Rigs consume calibration. Online modules must fit within the hull's CPU and powergrid, while cargo and drone capacity limit what can travel with it. A shared fit is therefore a recipe for a particular job, not a free hull or a bundle of automatically granted equipment. [S02]

The fitting panel exposes consequences before undocking. Capacitor powers active systems and recharges over time; a fit can exhaust it despite looking strong on paper. Shield, armor and structure provide successive defensive layers with distinct resistances. Damage per second and volley damage describe different offensive properties. Mass, inertia and speed affect maneuvering and alignment, while sensors affect locking and electronic warfare. Displayed effective hit points and price estimates depend on assumptions rather than guaranteeing performance or purchase cost. [S03]

Training skills uses real time rather than requiring repeated attacks to advance every prerequisite. Books unlock trainable skills; trained requirements determine access to many hulls, modules and ammunition. That persistent preparation is separate from learning when to engage, whom to trust or how to escape. [S06]

Industrial production connects acquisition to transformation. An original blueprint can support repeated manufacturing, research or copying; a blueprint copy has limited runs. The industry interface shows material and time requirements. Manufacturing consumes inputs to produce items, while research can improve material or time efficiency. Concurrent job limits make the order of work relevant. The publisher's introductory account is dated February 2022; it establishes this loop, not a current best-profit recipe. [S07]

## 3. Native creation and useful authorship

Fitting Simulation separates an experiment from its physical cost. A player chooses a hull, places equipment and charges, then changes module activation or overheating to inspect the resulting statistics. The holographic trial can be saved as a personal fit or, with the necessary role, a corporation fit. Applying it to an owned hull still requires the actual equipment; fitting another hull can lead into purchasing and assembling ships. Removing a simulated rig changes the draft without destroying a real one. This workflow was introduced in 2016 and remains corroborated by the current fitting support page. [S04, S02]

A recipient can open a fit linked in chat or import its text from the clipboard, inspect the recipe and adapt it before committing resources. The documented import/export loop makes an experienced pilot's design usable by another person. It does not by itself establish automatic synchronization when its author revises it. [S02]

## 4. Constructed play situations

These are **constructed situations**, not reports of our play.

1. **A fit can succeed at the wrong thing.** A pilot wants sustained fighting and combines powerful weapons with an active defense. Simulation reveals that their chosen active modules drain capacitor faster than it recovers. The pilot reduces consumption or changes the intended engagement, then asks what the revised ship can safely do. The point of the preview is to make this trade visible before loss makes it expensive. [S03–04]
2. **Choose what the factory is for.** An industrialist wants a batch of replacement equipment. They inspect a blueprint's ingredients and duration, compare available job capacity and consider research before manufacturing. Producing sooner and producing more efficiently compete; the next choice depends on when someone needs the output. [S07]
