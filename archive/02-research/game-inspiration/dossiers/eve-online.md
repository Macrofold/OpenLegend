# EVE Online

> **G197 · research inspected October 27, 2026 · researched and reviewed.** Original PC/Mac service, with explicit regional and encounter boundaries. No client, editor, executable, transaction or linked video was run. Lore spoilers in §6. Source IDs refer to the annotated register in §12.

## 1. Identity and the useful question

EVE Online began development at CCP in Reykjavík in the late 1990s and launched on May 6, 2003. [S33] Its useful design question is how a fitted ship, a physical shipment and a relationship with other players can acquire purpose together. The current service combines authored encounters with player production, political organization and consequential loss. Cradle of War launched on June 9, 2026; its new campaigns connect combat, mining, hauling and hacking to empire objectives. The publisher promises lasting outcomes, which should be distinguished from independently measured player influence. [S31]

A pilot can pursue exploration, industry, combat or support without treating the initial career choice as an irrevocable class. The AIR Career Program presents Explorer, Industrialist, Enforcer and Soldier of Fortune paths with suggested goals, skill plans and rewards. Tracking a different goal does not erase earlier progress; valid untracked actions also count. [S01]

A character’s name, race and bloodline remain fixed. Hair, clothing and portrait can change without a resculpt; changes to body shape, facial structure, complexion or gender require a purchased resculpt. [S43]

After the introduction, new characters enter Exordium, a protected starter region where hostile player combat is mechanically disabled. Players can leave and return, while lower-yield activities and the absence of player structures give reasons to move outward. Its June 2026 rules must not be projected backward onto older reviews of the opening. [S05] The Agency supplies activity discovery, and corporation recruitment can filter for interests, language and time zone. These offer routes toward a purpose; they cannot guarantee that the available routine will be enjoyable. [S28]

## 2. Actions, equipment and progression

The player commands a ship in three-dimensional space: approach, orbit or keep range relative to an object, or choose a direction manually. Double-clicking empty space turns and moves the ship along a camera-dependent vector; the tactical overlay makes that direction legible. Maneuvering can change the opponent's ability to maintain range, rather than merely shorten travel. [S24]

Select a target through space, the Overview or a shortcut, wait for the lock, and activate the appropriate weapons or other modules. Multiple locked targets are distinct from the currently selected recipient of a new action. The targeting interface shows distance, defensive layers and active effects; its lock countdown also beeps. These are documented interface behaviors, not observations from a new play session. [S25]

A ship is a set of constrained capabilities. High, medium and low slots accept different modules; weapons can additionally need the correct turret or launcher hardpoint. Rigs consume calibration. Online equipment must fit the hull's CPU and powergrid, while cargo and drone capacity limit what travels with it. [S02] Capacitor powers active systems and recharges over time. Shield, armor and structure have distinct resistances; DPS and volley damage describe different offense. Mass, inertia and speed affect maneuvering and alignment, while sensors affect locking and electronic warfare. Displayed effective hit points and price estimates depend on assumptions. [S03]

The publisher's Incursus example makes a role concrete: blasters favor closing in, railguns support distance, an armor repairer sustains the hull, and web/scrambler tackle helps hold an opponent. A drone adds another capability. This is an illustrative published fit, not a current competitive recommendation. [S18] Fleets combine damage, tackle/scouting, remote repair and electronic warfare. EVE University's own fleet guide asks a recruit to agree a role, inspect a doctrine, check ammunition/drones/repairs and coordinate with the commander. These are organization-specific practices. [S27]

Skill books unlock trainable skills; real-time training supplies prerequisites for many hulls, modules and ammunition. Persistent preparation is separate from learning when to engage or how to escape. [S06] Implants can improve attributes or specialist performance, but their body-bound loss matters (§4). [S14] Progress can mean a reliable inexpensive role, better fitting options, a business, a trusted reputation or territory, rather than one final equipment ladder.

## 3. Persistent world and payment contracts

Global PC/Mac EVE centers on **Tranquility**, one shared universe implemented across multiple simulation nodes and services. The Chinese service is separate: publisher documentation names Serenity and Infinity, while the FY2025 filing lists NetEase's EVE China independently of the global service and mobile EVE Echoes. A current independent monitor reports Serenity operating; exact October 2026 Chinese rule parity and Infinity status were not established. Do not combine these into one shared character economy. [E02/E03/E14]

**Alpha** provides free access with a restricted skill/ship set; ordinary training pauses at five million skill points, although permitted skills can receive additional points through injections. **Omega** unlocks the broader skill set and its associated ships/activities, doubles training speed, and permits multiple simultaneously logged-in Omega accounts. Payment changes industrial and organizational capacity as well as character options. [E04]

**PLEX** can be purchased for money, sold to other players for ISK, or bought with earned ISK and used for Omega and other services. ISK buys ships, modules, materials and blueprints: the cash-to-resource advantage is concrete. Buying PLEX from another player transfers existing ISK rather than creating it. PLEX kept in its account vault has different custody from physical cargo. This is not an unrestricted real-money cash-out route; the prior thematic study owns that policy. [E06]

**Skill extractors/injectors** turn trained skill points into tradeable items; injection accelerates another character's capability growth, with diminishing yield at higher skill totals. Extraction removes selected trained skill points and is irreversible. **Multiple Character Training** adds parallel queues for different characters rather than accelerating one queue or extending subscription time. [E07/E08]

The sanctioned **Character Bazaar** also permits purchasing an existing character for ISK, with disclosure and transfer rules. The seller pays the account-transfer service fee. The dedicated current transfer article specifies card/PayPal payment, despite a broader currency guide listing transfers among PLEX uses; this pass does not resolve that documentation conflict or promise a PLEX payment route. **Interpretation:** money can buy resources, preparation and broader options; it does not establish tactical judgment, trusted allies or guaranteed victory. [E09]

## 4. Loss, recovery and return

Ordinary ship destruction leaves the pilot in a capsule; a brief session transition can restrict immediate actions such as jumping a nearby gate. [S15] Capsule loss returns the character to a home station through the clone system; escaping the wreck and getting home with the ship intact are different outcomes. [S12] Implants normally die with the capsule or when unplugged. [S14] Destroyed cargo can be unrecoverable or remain in the wreck; the loss report distinguishes them. A mission-critical item can be stolen before recovery, blocking that objective. [S26]

Insurance supplies an ISK payment based mainly on estimated hull construction materials. It does not replace the lost modules, rigs or cargo, nor promise the hull's market purchase price. CONCORD destruction invalidates coverage. [S16] High-security policing is retaliatory: enough attacking firepower can destroy a victim before intervention. [S08] Replacing equipment therefore involves money, availability, transport and a decision about the next risk—not merely a resurrection click.

Killmails preserve the fitted ship, cargo, participants, location and estimated loss; a pilot can reopen, share or simulate the lost fit. External killboards contain voluntarily exported records and are not a complete event census. [S29] This provides material for learning and shared history, while leaving room for a player to decide that rebuilding is no longer worthwhile.

## 5. Constructed play situations

These are **constructed situations**, not reports of our play.

1. **A fit can succeed at the wrong thing.** A pilot wants sustained fighting but combines hungry weapons and an active defense. Simulation reveals capacitor exhaustion. They reduce consumption or change the intended engagement, then ask what the revised ship can safely do. Preview makes the trade visible before loss makes it expensive. [S03–04]
2. **A small ship supplies the decisive role.** A novice agrees to tackle for a group. They close, lock and disrupt the target's escape; spending capacitor on extra weapon damage could undermine that assignment. Holding long enough for allies to act is useful even without winning the damage comparison. The next choice is whether to maintain range, disengage or accept the hull's loss. [S27]
3. **Exploration must include the way home.** An explorer probes a route, locates a relic site and cracks its containers. Valuable cargo makes continuing attractive, but lost probes can leave them dependent on another person's help or destructive return. They preserve the means of exit and decide whether another site justifies the additional exposure. [S12–13]
4. **Choose what the factory is for.** A manufacturer wants replacement equipment for a fleet. Blueprint research would save inputs but delay output. They compare job capacity and the delivery deadline, produce a smaller immediate batch, and decide whether later demand warrants further research. The economic output has a recipient and a time of use. [S07]
5. **A visible pipe need not carry anything.** A planetary producer places an extractor, processor and storage, yet the expected output does not arrive. A physical link exists without the required input route. They correct the routing and observe whether storage and processing now balance, then choose between a longer extraction program and more frequent collection. [S36]
6. **A lower purchase price is not a completed delivery.** A hauler sees cheaper goods away from their customer's base. They compare route exposure, cargo capacity and replacement cost before loading. Even high security permits lethal attacks. A smaller shipment may reduce the consequence of interception while still serving demand; the next trip depends on the actual margin. [S34, S08, S16]
7. **A shared home needs a custody decision.** A corporation authorizes a structure and grants selected service access. Members decide what to store there after checking its location and operating state: wormhole/Pochven recovery can eject possessions, and abandoned destruction lacks automatic protection. Funding maintenance or relocating valuables becomes part of using the home. [S10–11, E10–11]

## 6. World, history and motives

**Lore spoilers.** The EVE Gate's collapse isolated human settlements, causing starvation, technological decline and the disappearance of societies whose relics later peoples cannot fully explain. New Eden's empires arose from that broken continuity. Exploration thus encounters both profitable objects and gaps in collective knowledge. The official archive contains different historical viewpoints and eras; its undated faction profiles are not a reliable roster of October 2026 officeholders. [S19]

The Amarr rebuilt an authoritarian religious empire whose Reclaiming treats conquest and enslavement as spiritual obligations. Holders' material interests frequently contradict the supposed salvation of those they dominate. Jamyl Sarum's historical return from required ritual death illustrates another contradiction: the ruler acclaimed as divinely reborn secretly used prohibited royal cloning, with an alien intelligence entering her mind. This is a past political and theological crisis, not a claim that she still rules. The culture makes authority, technology and coercion inseparable. [S20]

The Minmatar were a technologically capable tribal civilization before Amarr raids and occupation. Rebellion freed many while leaving enslaved kin and a dispersed diaspora. Their later rejection of imported Gallente governmental forms reflects a desire to recover tribal autonomy. Karin Midular pursued reconciliation; Maleatu Shakor combined a confrontational reputation with careful tribal rebuilding. Ana Utulf concealed surviving Starkmanir, then aided their rescue and the Nefantar return. These competing responses to the same trauma make liberation more than a simple faction color. [S21]

The Caldari grew from neighbors and partners of the Gallente into a secessionist corporate state. Megacorporations organize employment, property and public authority around duty and collective survival, while minority cultures such as the Achura retain distinct traditions. Tibus Heth turned popular anger and anti-Gallente nationalism toward recapturing Caldari Prime; his later coercion and failures undermined him. Admiral Visera Yanala refused repeated orders to bombard the homeworld, prioritizing its people over the leader's command. Loyalty to a polity need not mean obedience to its ruler. [S22]

The Gallente Federation offers pluralism, democratic participation and refuge, yet its cultural dominance and security institutions can undermine those ideals. Its early attempts to unify disparate member nations helped provoke Caldari resistance. Jacus Roden opposed nationalizing military industry before becoming president, while Mentas Blaque's Black Eagles claimed security powers at the expense of privacy and freedom. Their conflict joins profit, fear and political legitimacy rather than assigning every citizen one moral position. [S23]

Capsuleers occupy a privileged position within this history. Jovian capsule designs given to Ishukone eventually combined neural ship control with clone transfer, creating a small class able to survive bodily destruction. The fictional continuity of consciousness explains repeated return while ships and other people's work remain expendable. It also makes the player an unusually powerful actor among far more vulnerable inhabitants. [S30] The June launch announcement describes Visions of Greatness, scheduled for June 10, as simulations of four historical battles that connect empire motives with flying and fitting; campaigns invite participation through several professions. This connects lore to activity without requiring every player to consume the entire archive. [S31]

## 7. Presentation, motion and information

Fortier's 2006 review found bright space backgrounds could hide active modules' faint green glow, forcing camera rotation to check state. He appreciated ambient electronic music and the heavier guitar cue on entering mission space. These are dated written observations, not newly watched or auditioned evidence. [R02](#r02)

The camera is also an input frame: the documented manual-flight vector changes with the view, and ships turn and accelerate toward chosen directions. The tactical overlay externalizes that spatial instruction. [S24] Target-lock beeps and countdowns make an otherwise invisible acquisition process perceptible. [S25] Scenery, movement and readable state must work together.

The current Overview is configurable across the account. Tabs and filters decide which objects and in-space brackets appear; hierarchical exceptions and color/background priorities encode relationships and danger. A player can export settings, share a link or restore a previous shared layout. This is powerful player-authored information design, with the corresponding possibility that a filter hides a relevant object. [S32] Fitting simulation and loss reports complement it by exposing plans and consequences. These interfaces turn complexity into inspectable decisions, but their availability does not prove a newcomer knows which question to ask.

## 8. Native creation and external-tool boundary

### Fits, doctrines and infrastructure

Fitting Simulation separates experiment from physical cost. Choose a hull, place equipment and charges, change activation or overheating, then inspect the result. Save a personal fit or, with the required role, a corporation fit. Applying it still requires a real hull and equipment. Removing a simulated rig edits the draft without destroying a physical one. This workflow originated in 2016 and is corroborated by current fitting support. [S04]

A recipient can open a chat-linked fit or import its text, inspect the requirements and adapt it before buying and fitting. An author can revise and share a replacement recipe; the inspected documentation does not establish automatic synchronization of recipients' copies or safe reversal of committed physical changes. A fit is useful because someone can understand and assemble it, not simply because it has been exported. [S02]

An authorized corporation member transports a structure, places and rotates its preview, names it and chooses its profile before anchoring. Vulnerable setup, installation of its quantum core and fueled services turn placement into a defended operating commitment. Decommissioning remains vulnerable; the hull must be collected in space, attached equipment can be exposed, and rigs are destroyed. The dated deployment guide's obsolete Infrastructure Hub references are not used as current sovereignty rules. [S10]

Access lists and profiles determine which people, corporations or alliances may use services. Managers can revise the list or explicitly block a character; more specific membership settings take precedence. A recipient's ability to dock or use a service is therefore a permission checked against the live structure, not ownership conveyed by receiving a link. [S11] Planetary layouts have their own commit boundary: submitted command-center placement cannot simply be moved or reclaimed, and destroying it removes the colony investment. [S36] The primary support guide confirms remote decommissioning and the need to export and collect output. [S09]

The [October 14 creator comparison](../essays/player-created-content-modding-and-marketplaces.md#4-eve-online-creators-can-design-appearances-markets-and-collective-work) remains the detailed owner of SKINR/Paragon Hub and corporation-project design, production, distribution, attribution and reward workflows. Those forms of authorship complement fits and infrastructure; their earlier dated evidence is preserved rather than copied into another tutorial.

### External ecosystem

**R15 result: uncertain; a popular external client-mod community was not established.** The policy still prohibits client modification, cache scraping and unfair automation. A supported data interface or a tolerable external overlay is a different permission category. This repeats the boundary checked in the [October 14 creator comparison](../essays/player-created-content-modding-and-marketplaces.md#41-not-an-unrestricted-client-mod-platform), whose SKINR, corporation-project and API walkthrough remains the detailed owner. [M01]

The developer-hosted community directory provides concrete adjacent examples: EVE Workbench for fittings and haul routes, Alliance Auth for organizational access, and zKillboard for combat records. It explicitly disclaims endorsement or support of listed projects. This is a substantial external application ecosystem, not an installable quest/world-editing SDK; the listing supplies neither adoption totals nor creator earnings. Tools can help people coordinate without gaining authority to rewrite the service's rules. No client, application, account authorization or package installation was performed. [M02]

## 9. Production, maintenance and actual promotion

In a September 2005 interview, senior producer Nathan Richardsson traced EVE's combination to Elite's space fantasy and Ultima Online's social conflict. The roughly fifty-person studio had prioritized tools for player activity over authored PvE, but he acknowledged that even committed PvP players wanted better PvE. Development followed observed behavior and repeated feedback; accommodating emergent uses still required unpopular balance changes. He credited Simon & Schuster's initial support while describing greater freedom after self-publishing. That staffing choice does not establish that authored activities are dispensable. [P01]

The August 2025 ESI maintenance report offers a smaller, concrete example: request tracing exposed incorrect error translation and a one-second connection timeout that disagreed with the load balancer. The team fixed those faults and introduced richer corporation-project listings to reduce follow-up requests. Supporting the surrounding creator ecosystem has continuing reliability and operating costs. This does not imply a development-save compatibility requirement for OpenLegend. [P02]

**Actual marketing:** the November 2014 _This is EVE_ trailer solicited players' voice communications, edited them into a production premiered at EVE Down Under, and offered censored and uncensored versions. It promoted people reacting to shared stakes. The announcement was read; the video was not watched, and no conversion or retention effect is inferred. [P03]

**Current studio boundary:** the May 6, 2026 CEO letter describes CCP's return to independence as Fenris Creations with teams and direction retained. Announced DeepMind research initially uses controlled offline versions disconnected from Tranquility; live-economy agents are not established. [P04]

## 11. Transfer, critique and existing routes

**The positive opportunity is useful interdependence.** A created object, learned role or ordinary journey can matter because another participant actually needs its result. EVE also shows why waiting, uncertainty and organizational obligation cannot be assumed to make that usefulness enjoyable. Test a satisfying action, visible beneficiary and chosen next step in OpenLegend's playable world.
