# Fire Emblem Fates — full research dossier

**G63 · Complete research pass, September 26, 2026.** This dossier treats **Birthright, Conquest, and Revelation as three materially distinct campaigns built on one mechanical platform**. It also distinguishes the rare physical Special Edition from the original retail/download model and the much harsher 2026 preservation reality: Nintendo 3DS eShop purchases ended March 27, 2023 and 3DS online services ended April 8, 2024, so Revelation and other digital-only content are no longer newly purchasable through their original storefront. [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md).

Fates is particularly relevant to OpenLegend for three reasons:

1. **One world state can branch into genuinely different campaigns**, not merely different endings.
2. It deliberately repairs Awakening's overpowered Pair Up system by separating **offensive cooperation** from **defensive pairing**.
3. **My Castle** turns between-battle social/economic preparation into a spatially authored home that also functioned as a shareable multiplayer artifact.

Its failure modes are equally useful: splitting narrative truth across separately sold routes creates context debt; its child mechanic feels less fictionally grounded than Awakening's; and Revelation's original digital dependence is now a preservation problem.

## 1. Identity, releases, and the route contract

**Developer:** Intelligent Systems. **Publisher:** Nintendo.  
**Japanese release:** June 25, 2015 as *Fire Emblem if*.  
**North American release:** February 19, 2016.  
**European release:** May 20, 2016. [FA01](#fa01) [FA02](#fa02)

All routes share the opening chapters. Corrin—a customizable royal raised in Nohr—learns they were born into Hoshido and must choose at the Chapter 6 split:

- **Birthright:** side with Hoshido.
- **Conquest:** remain with adoptive Nohr.
- **Revelation:** refuse both kingdoms and investigate the force behind the war.

The campaigns diverge substantially in:
- allies;
- maps;
- objectives;
- resource availability;
- grinding;
- narrative events;
- route-specific classes/items;
- difficulty emphasis.

Nintendo's own developer interview says the team wanted all three to be substantially different and resisted economizing by simply flipping/reusing maps. [FA03](#fa03)

### Original commercial packaging

In North America:
- Birthright and Conquest were separate full-price retail/digital entry packages;
- after owning one route, another route could originally be bought at a lower digital add-on price;
- Revelation was primarily a digital third route;
- the limited Special Edition placed all three on one physical game card. [FA04](#fa04)

The route split therefore was both a **design choice and a commercial/distribution boundary**.

## 2. Corrin: avatar, body, and route choice

Corrin can be customized for:
- name;
- gender;
- appearance;
- voice;
- boon/bane;
- secondary talent/class.

Corrin is also a **dragon-blooded royal**, which matters mechanically:
- Dragonstone use;
- Dragon Vein activation;
- route-specific promotions;
- family/lineage story.

Unlike an invisible commander, Corrin is the emotional object of the central family choice.

The route decision is a rare RPG example where a single early choice changes:
- who is ally;
- who is enemy;
- what resources exist;
- what maps/objectives follow;
- which relationship graph is available.

### OpenLegend lesson

A consequential choice is more meaningful when it changes **simulation structure**, not only dialogue flags.

## 3. Three campaigns as different difficulty/economy contracts

### Birthright

Birthright is the more accessible/open route:
- repeatable Challenge/scouting battles permit grinding;
- resources are more generous;
- many main objectives are straightforward rout/boss fights;
- Hoshidan classes/weapons dominate the native roster.

GameSpot and RPG Site both describe it as the more forgiving entry path. [FA01](#fa01) [FA05](#fa05)

### Conquest

Conquest deliberately removes ordinary repeatable grinding:
- experience/resources are more bounded;
- map objectives are more varied;
- defensive, escape, limited-turn and gimmick-driven scenarios matter more;
- the player must extract more value from a finite roster/economy.

Reviews disagree on whether this scarcity improves focus or feels restrictive. GameSpot's Peter Brown admired the challenge but disliked losing the downtime/training freedom available in the other routes. [FA06](#fa06)

### Revelation

Revelation combines most Hoshidan and Nohrian characters/classes/resources:
- grinding returns;
- roster breadth is largest;
- map gimmicks/interactive terrain are frequent;
- My Castle can use both factions' facilities.

GameSpot and Nintendo Life praised the route's roster/build breadth and some experimental maps, though later fan discussion is much more divided over pacing, balance and gimmick-heavy design. [FA07](#fa07) [FA08](#fa08)

### Transferable principle

Difficulty can be authored through **economy and opportunity**, not just enemy stats.

## 4. Attack Stance and Guard Stance: repairing Pair Up

Awakening's Pair Up bundled offense, defense, stats and mobility with probabilistic Dual actions. Fates intentionally rebalanced it because developers considered the old implementation too powerful. [FA09](#fa09)

### Attack Stance

When two unpaired allies are positioned adjacent and one initiates combat:
- the adjacent ally can perform a **Dual Strike**;
- support bonuses apply;
- the system emphasizes spreading units across multiple map tiles.

Offensive cooperation is predictable enough to plan around.

### Guard Stance / Pair Up

Two units can occupy one position:
- Lead gains partner stat bonuses;
- a **shield gauge** builds through combat;
- when full, the support unit blocks an incoming attack;
- enemy Dual Strikes are prevented against the paired unit.

The Lead/Support roles can be switched.

### The interesting tension

**Attack Stance rewards more bodies on the map.**  
**Guard Stance rewards concentration into fewer, tougher bodies.**

That creates a genuine positional decision instead of “always pair everyone.”

### OpenLegend transfer

When two agents cooperate, distinguish:
- help that requires **independent presence**;
- help that requires **tight formation / shared action**.

Do not make cooperation a single universal buff.

## 5. Weapons, no durability, and contextual drawbacks

Fates removes ordinary weapon durability (staves/limited consumables remain exceptions). Instead, weapons differentiate through:
- might;
- hit;
- critical;
- follow-up effects;
- stat bonuses/penalties;
- post-combat debuffs;
- special restrictions.

The developers explicitly replaced durability pressure with **choice-at-use pressure**: a stronger weapon may reduce speed, defenses or follow-up capability. [FA10](#fa10)

The weapon triangle also expands:
- swords/tomes > axes/bows;
- axes/bows > lances/hidden weapons;
- lances/hidden weapons > swords/tomes,
with Hoshidan equivalents such as katanas, naginatas, clubs, yumi, scrolls and shuriken. [FA11](#fa11)

### Hidden weapons

Daggers/shuriken:
- attack at close or ranged distance;
- often have lower raw power;
- inflict temporary stat debuffs.

They demonstrate a useful item principle:
> a low-damage tool can be tactically strong because it changes the next interaction.

## 6. Forging and material loops

My Castle's **Smithy** combines:
- two identical weapons;
- a matching mineral

into an improved +1 weapon, with later tiers requiring recursive combinations. [FA12](#fa12)

Route matters:
- Birthright initially focuses Hoshidan weapon/mineral ecology;
- Conquest focuses Nohrian;
- Revelation can access both.

This is more systemic than Path of Radiance/Awakening forging because repeated object copies and materials are part of the upgrade economy.

### OpenLegend lesson

Upgrading an object can consume:
- another instance of the same object;
- a material whose origin ties the item to place/faction.

That can make trade/travel matter without requiring hundreds of bespoke recipes.

## 7. Classes, seals, and relationships as training access

Fates refines Awakening's reclassing.

Major seals include:
- **Master Seal** — promote;
- **Heart Seal** — access a character's personal alternate class;
- **Partner Seal** — access spouse's class line;
- **Friendship Seal** — access an A+ partner's class line;
- **Offspring Seal** — helps later-recruited children catch up.

[FA13](#fa13)

This creates an unusually social progression graph:

> relationship → access to another person's discipline → learn class skills → carry knowledge onward.

That is one of the clearest OpenLegend precedents in the entire Fire Emblem series.

### Relationship-based teaching

The abstraction is gamey—the spouse does not literally teach lessons—but the semantic model is strong:
- knowing a person can open a path unavailable alone.

OpenLegend can implement this more literally:
- observation;
- mentorship;
- prerequisites;
- practice;
- trust.

## 8. Supports, S/A+ relationships, and same-sex exception

Compatible units can build supports through joint battle.

Fates uses:
- C/B/A;
- **S** for one eligible marriage;
- **A+** for one same-gender friendship class-link relationship.

The Avatar can form same-gender S supports with **Niles** or **Rhajat**; those pairings do not generate Kana through the child system. [FA14](#fa14)

Support rank affects:
- Attack/Guard stance bonuses;
- dialogue/characterization;
- marriage;
- access to Partner/Friendship Seal class lines.

### Limitation

The support graph is still a whitelist authored in advance. Many plausible relationships simply cannot develop mechanically.

OpenLegend's relationship substrate should be open-ended while allowing authored arcs to sit on top.

## 9. Children and Deeprealms

Fates retains recruitable children even though its story is not fundamentally about time-travel descendants.

After marriage:
- a route-dependent child Paralogue can appear;
- the child has one fixed father in most cases plus a variable mother (with exceptions);
- classes/stat modifiers/skills depend partly on parents.

To explain rapid aging, children are raised in **Deeprealms**, alternate spaces where time flows differently.

Contemporary reviewers frequently identified this as less convincing than Awakening's fictionally central future-children premise. GamesRadar and RPG Site specifically call the explanation contrived. [FA15](#fa15) [FA05](#fa05)

### OpenLegend warning

Do not preserve a successful mechanic merely because players liked it if the world must contort implausibly to support it.

Mechanics should arise from world law whenever possible.

## 10. Personal skills and character individuality

Each major unit has a **personal skill** separate from class-learned abilities.

This creates a durable identity layer:
- class can change;
- learned skills can change;
- the person retains one distinctive rule.

Examples span:
- conditional damage;
- support;
- economy;
- healing;
- proximity;
- terrain/HP conditions.

### OpenLegend transfer

An actor can have:
- transferable learned skills;
- role/profession skills;
- **personal traits** that remain tied to identity.

Avoid reducing personality traits to combat bonuses only; they should influence behavior/perception/social state too.

## 11. Dragon Veins: authored environmental control

Royal-blood units can activate **Dragon Veins** placed on maps.

Effects are map-specific and can:
- create/remove bridges;
- drain water;
- change terrain;
- destroy/raise obstacles;
- alter hazards;
- move platforms;
- heal/damage or otherwise transform battle state.

Destructoid specifically praised how these environmental switches add tactical options. [FA16](#fa16)

### Why this matters

A Dragon Vein is:
- a visible environmental affordance;
- with an eligibility rule;
- whose effect changes the shared map.

OpenLegend should make comparable world actions describable as:
> actor capability + world affordance + explicit state transition.

## 12. Capture and recruited enemies

Certain units such as Niles/Orochi can use capture-related abilities on eligible enemies.

Captured enemies can be:
- held in the Prison in My Castle;
- persuaded/recruited;
- used as generic units;
- reclass only within constrained class ancestry. [FA17](#fa17)

This is mechanically unusual because an enemy unit can become:
> opponent → prisoner → recruited actor.

### OpenLegend opportunity

Surrender/capture should be stateful:
- captivity;
- negotiation;
- ransom;
- conversion;
- parole;
- escape;
- grievance.

Do not treat “defeated enemy” as only dead/loot.

## 13. My Castle: spatial intermission and player-authored base

The development team called **My Castle** a major feature and said its purpose was letting players feel as though they lived with the characters. They considered it large enough to be “a game in and of itself.” [FA18](#fa18)

Players can place/upgrade facilities such as:
- armories;
- item shops;
- Smithy;
- Arena;
- mess hall;
- prison;
- resource points;
- statues;
- defensive buildings;
- character facilities.

[FA19](#fa19)

The castle supports:
- preparation;
- small conversations;
- resource collection;
- meals/buffs;
- forging;
- relationship flavor;
- invasion defense;
- visiting other castles historically.

### Design importance

Unlike Path of Radiance's menu Base, My Castle gives the intermission layer:
- **space**;
- **layout**;
- **ownership**;
- **shared presentation**.

OpenLegend can use home/camp/workshop spaces as persistent social memory rather than abstract menus.

## 14. My Castle invasions and multiplayer

Historically, players could:
- visit shared castles;
- exchange data;
- battle defensive layouts;
- use local/online functions;
- receive resources/items under applicable rules.

The developers expanded My Castle beyond StreetPass specifically so players in low-StreetPass areas could connect over the Internet. [FA18](#fa18)

### 2026 boundary

Nintendo 3DS online communication ended April 8, 2024. Original Internet castle visiting/battles therefore are historical features in ordinary official service.

This is a strong preservation warning for OpenLegend:
> if a created place has meaning, its basic existence should not depend on a matchmaking server surviving.

## 15. Economy and route identity

Economy differs by route.

### Birthright
- repeatable encounters;
- more opportunities for gold/experience/resources;
- easier recovery from weak builds.

### Conquest
- bounded ordinary experience;
- tighter gold/items;
- greater value in optimization and map rewards.

### Revelation
- broadest access;
- both factional shop/ecology families;
- grindable challenge encounters.

The same combat engine therefore supports different campaign experiences largely through **resource rules**.

## 16. Failure modes: Classic, Casual, Phoenix

Fates separates tactical difficulty from defeat permanence.

- **Classic:** most defeated units are permanently unavailable.
- **Casual:** defeated units return after battle.
- **Phoenix:** defeated units can return during battle on the next player turn.

Destructoid notes Phoenix as effectively story-first accessibility. [FA16](#fa16)

This creates three consequence contracts on top of difficulty settings.

### OpenLegend lesson

Death/failure policy should be configurable independently of:
- enemy intelligence;
- damage;
- scarcity.

## 17. Story and route morality

**Spoiler-light:** Corrin is caught between Hoshido—the family of birth—and Nohr—the family that raised them.

The premise is unusually strong:
> loyalty is not aligned cleanly with biology, affection, nation or morality.

Developer interviews say the team wanted the choice to produce regret/curiosity about the road not taken. [FA20](#fa20)

### Execution criticism

The story was also Fates' most contested element.

Reviews and later criticism frequently argue:
- Conquest bends character logic to keep Corrin on a morally compromised path;
- Revelation withholds/explains central truths unavailable to the other routes;
- family framing can be undercut by romance availability and hidden lineage facts;
- child Deeprealms feel mechanically motivated.

This is a critical OpenLegend warning:
> branching worlds need each branch to be internally truthful, not deliberately incomplete merely to upsell another branch.

## 18. Production: why three games existed

After Awakening's success, Nintendo asked for another Fire Emblem. Producer Yamagami recalled that the team had exhausted its “put everything in” ideas on Awakening, then returned to an older ambition: let the player choose between two sides of a war. Director Maeda added the natural third option—refuse both—and insisted the three paths should materially diverge. [FA03](#fa03)

### Shin Kibayashi and scenario scale

The team sought outside story help because it wanted a deeper narrative response to criticism of Awakening's story. Manga/story writer **Shin Kibayashi** was initially asked for roughly ten pages per route, but says he produced around **500 pages**. [FA21](#fa21)

### Not just recycled maps

Yamagami explicitly notes that the team could have economized by mirroring armies on the same map, but in practice did not recycle much; Maeda says maps and scenarios were built separately. [FA03](#fa03)

### My Castle scope expansion

My Castle began as a StreetPass-oriented idea and grew into Internet-connected visits/battles and a broad character/home system partly because the team wanted more ways to deepen character attachment. [FA18](#fa18)

## 19. Art, music, UI, and cultural identity

Fates gives the two kingdoms distinct visual/mechanical languages.

### Hoshido
- Japanese-inspired architecture;
- katanas/naginatas/clubs/yumi/scrolls;
- shrine/ninja/samurai class language;
- brighter natural aesthetics.

### Nohr
- European dark-fantasy castle imagery;
- swords/lances/axes/bows/tomes;
- knights/cavaliers/dark mages;
- colder/gothic military palette.

This faction styling is not merely decorative: it maps onto different:
- item families;
- classes;
- resources;
- castle buildings;
- rosters.

The soundtrack likewise uses factional instrumentation/motifs while recurring music such as “Lost in Thoughts All Alone” binds routes together.

The dual-screen 3DS UI keeps:
- map state above;
- unit/combat detail readily available below.

### Localization boundary

Western Fates removed Japanese dual-audio availability present in Awakening and altered/removed some interaction content. Destructoid confirmed the localized release was English-audio-only. [FA22](#fa22)

This dossier does not assume every Japanese interaction/localization feature exists identically in Western copies.

## 20. Worked interactions

### A. Attack Stance versus Guard Stance

**Intent:** clear two adjacent enemies this turn without exposing a frail ally.

**Attack Stance line:** keep two units separate and adjacent so one contributes a Dual Strike to the other's attack.

**Guard Stance line:** Pair Up, sacrificing an independent map body/action for stat bonuses and shield-gauge protection.

**Result:** offense and defense compete for formation. [FA09](#fa09)

**Limit:** optimal solutions can still lean heavily on Pair Up/stat stacking depending on units/map.

### B. Learn a spouse's discipline

**Intent:** give a unit access to a class family they do not naturally possess.

**Conditions:** reach S support with a compatible spouse.

**Action:** use Partner Seal.

**Result:** relationship changes the character's training graph. [FA13](#fa13)

**Next:** learn class skills, then return to another class.

**Limit:** the mechanic abstracts teaching entirely; relationship itself behaves like a key.

### C. Dragon Vein changes battle topology

**Intent:** cross or deny a route.

**Condition:** deploy a royal-blood character near a relevant Vein.

**Action:** activate it.

**Result:** terrain changes for everyone—bridge, hazard, platform or other state.

**Next:** exploit the newly changed movement/position relationships.

**Limit:** effects are authored per node rather than generally simulated elemental physics.

### D. Capture rather than kill

**Intent:** acquire an enemy unit.

**Condition:** eligible capturer and target.

**Action:** defeat using Capture/Kidnap.

**Result:** target becomes a prisoner instead of ordinary defeated enemy.

**Next:** persuade/recruit in My Castle. [FA17](#fa17)

**Limit:** generic captured units have much less social simulation than authored cast members.

### E. Route choice changes economy, not only story

**Intent:** side with Nohr.

**Result:** Conquest's campaign removes ordinary grinding and tightens resources while adding more constrained objectives.

**Consequence:** the same class/combat mechanics demand different planning.

**OpenLegend lesson:** authored reality variants can modify *laws/opportunities* while reusing engine primitives.

## 21. Marketing, distribution, participation, and preservation

Fates' pitch centered on a marketable binary:
- **birth family** versus **adoptive family**;
- beginner-friendly route versus veteran route.

The Special Edition bundled all three and became especially desirable because it was the only common official physical route to Revelation. [FA23](#fa23)

### Commercial milestone

CESA shipment data summarized by Perfectly Nintendo reports **3.09 million** units for Fire Emblem Fates in its later 3DS dataset. That aggregates the product family rather than proving route-by-route sales or revenue. [FA24](#fa24)

### Store shutdown

On March 27, 2023 Nintendo ended new 3DS eShop software/DLC purchases. [FA25](#fa25)

Consequences:
- a Birthright owner can no longer newly purchase Conquest/Revelation through the original in-game/eShop route;
- a Conquest owner cannot newly acquire Revelation;
- the Special Edition cartridge retains all three routes physically;
- previously purchased DLC may still be redownloaded under Nintendo's current support policy.

This makes Fates a direct case study in **content architecture outliving commerce infrastructure**.

## 22. Five substantive written-review perspectives

### 1. GameSpot — route review set

GameSpot reviewed all three.

**Birthright:** praised accessibility, character intimacy and resource freedom; criticized melodrama/awkward writing.  
**Conquest:** praised demanding combat and relationship integration; criticized the lack of grind/downtime as overly restrictive for the reviewer.  
**Revelation:** praised roster combination and interactive maps. [FA01](#fa01) [FA06](#fa06) [FA07](#fa07)

### 2. Nintendo Life

Praised:
- meaningful mechanical difference between Birthright/Conquest;
- core battle refinement;
- route identity;
- cast/My Castle.

Its Revelation follow-up praised closure and map experimentation, while noting the campaign should be played after the other routes. [FA02](#fa02) [FA08](#fa08)

### 3. Destructoid — Chris Carter

Praised:
- Conquest's veteran challenge;
- My Castle;
- Dragon Veins;
- no weapon durability;
- character/social systems.

The reviewer thought the multi-route content was substantial enough to justify the structure more than expected, while still calling the packaging unusual. [FA16](#fa16)

### 4. GamesRadar+

Praised:
- refined battle system;
- emotional impact of seeing both sides;
- character interaction.

Criticized:
- route/DLC paywall needed to see full perspective;
- lack of an easier traditional difficulty target for some newcomers;
- children as fictionally contrived. [FA15](#fa15)

### 5. RPG Site — Alex Donaldson

**Birthright:** praised combat/UI/music but found its simplification and grinding made it too easy/shallow for experienced players.  
**Conquest:** judged the route substantially stronger because objective diversity and constrained economy made tactics more demanding; still criticized children/story elements. [FA05](#fa05) [FA26](#fa26)

### Reception synthesis

Strongest repeated praise:
- core tactical refinement over Awakening;
- Attack/Guard Stance;
- Conquest map design;
- cast/social systems;
- route mechanical identity.

Strongest repeated criticism:
- story execution;
- contrived child system;
- confusing/expensive route packaging;
- imbalance between route quality;
- Revelation's role as digital “truth/closure.”

## 23. Player/community evidence

No Steam release exists; Steam sampling is not applicable.

Community discussion remains unusually split by **what players want Fire Emblem to optimize**.

A recent GameFAQs discussion among returning players describes:
- Conquest as the most mechanically intricate;
- Birthright as simpler but sometimes preferable precisely because Conquest can feel gimmicky;
- the faction aesthetics/rosters as meaningful reasons to replay. [FA27](#fa27)

Older community discussion around play order commonly recommends **Birthright → Conquest → Revelation**, partly because Birthright teaches the mechanics more gently and Revelation assumes context from both. [FA28](#fa28)

These are qualitative accounts, not population estimates.

### Long-running tension

Fates is evidence that one engine can support multiple audiences if campaign rules change meaningfully.

But product fragmentation has costs:
- no route becomes the obvious canonical whole;
- story criticism spills across packages;
- preservation becomes worse when one route is digital-only.

## 24. Transferable inspiration for OpenLegend

### A. World forks should change laws/opportunities, not just cutscenes

The route choice changes:
- people;
- maps;
- economy;
- classes;
- goals.

That is a strong template for authored alternate realities.

### B. Relationship → apprenticeship is powerful

Partner/Friendship Seals encode:
> knowing someone changes what you can learn.

Implement that semantically in OpenLegend with:
- teaching;
- observation;
- trust;
- time/practice.

### C. Give cooperation mutually exclusive forms

Attack versus Guard Stance creates a real formation choice.

Agents might choose:
- spread out and cover more ground;
- cluster for defense;
- synchronize for precision;
- act independently for throughput.

### D. Environmental control should be visible world state

Dragon Veins are legible switches with shared consequences.

OpenLegend can generalize to:
- floodgate;
- power grid;
- ritual seal;
- bridge mechanism;
- weather machine;
- social alarm.

### E. Home spaces can be social/economic state

My Castle makes preparation a place:
- facilities;
- inhabitants;
- layout;
- visitors;
- defenses.

A settlement/home should remember who built and uses it.

### F. Capture creates richer state transitions than binary combat

Opponent → captive → negotiated ally is a better systemic chain than alive/dead only.

### G. Faction identity should cross art and mechanics

Hoshido/Nohr differ in:
- visuals;
- classes;
- weapons;
- resources;
- map style.

This is stronger worldbuilding than lore text alone.

## 25. Limits / do not copy automatically

### Do not sell essential narrative truth as a fragile add-on

Revelation's 2026 availability problem is the clearest warning.

### Do not preserve a popular mechanic when fiction rejects it

Deeprealm children show the danger.

### Do not equate “three routes” with meaningful choice by itself

Distinct consequences require substantial content/state divergence.

### Do not make every relationship a class-access token

Relationships need value independent of optimization.

### Do not let a hub become compulsory chores

My Castle is charming partly because many interactions are lightweight. A living-world hub can become exhausting if every visit demands collection clicks.
