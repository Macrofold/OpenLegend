# Umamusume: Pretty Derby — full-game dossier (G168)

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md#october-17-mobile-successes-and-unsuccessful-services) · [Research status](../research-progress-mobile-2026-10-17.md) · [Research requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research date: October 17, 2026.** This is an additive reference study of the selected mobile game and its explicitly identified platform/edition counterparts. It combines documented mechanics, concrete constructed situations, original commercial evidence, production context and independent reception. It grants no OpenLegend implementation or monetization decision. Detailed access and version limitations remain beside the claims.

## Game, mechanics and player-created content

### What the player actually does

The player is a trainer, choosing an established character and assembling the means to help her achieve a racing career. Recruiting a trainee unlocks repeat access to that athlete; it does not immediately produce a competitive racer. A Career produces a distinct **Veteran**, preserving that attempt's attributes and learned skills for subsequent races and as inspiration for another trainee. Several builds of one character can coexist. The initial setup comprises the trainee, two legacy Veterans and six support cards—five owned and one borrowed. This makes the central creation activity a repeatable build-and-test process. [GameTora's English quickstart, June 26, 2025](https://gametora.com/umamusume/guides/global-quickstart-guide).

In the original URA career, roughly three school years are divided into turns. The trainer chooses training, rest, recreation, treatment or a race while managing the next deadline. Training improves attributes; rest restores energy; outings help mood; treatment addresses adverse conditions. Energy governs training risk, while mood affects performance and training returns. Events interrupt this schedule with authored dialogue and sometimes choices. A required finish or fan target determines whether a manual career continues. Skills may be bought without spending a turn. [GameTora beginner guide, updated June 26, 2025](https://gametora.com/umamusume/beginners-guide).

The original opening therefore teaches preparation before execution: choosing a goal, arranging support, preserving energy, then watching an uncertain result. Later play asks for a roster covering different race conditions, useful legacy lineages and more exact event builds. A first successful career is a milestone; it is not the point at which the whole game ends. That interpretation follows the documented separation of reusable recruits, completed Veterans and external race modes rather than assuming a conventional RPG campaign.

The original URA mode centers on configuring athletes, schedules and races within supplied rules, without an action-combat or freely traversable exploration world. Later Japanese scenarios add bounded cooking, construction and development systems, inventoried below. Those systems do not establish a player-scripted quest editor or open settlement simulation. Relations with characters are expressed through authored events and sporting roles. An affection or bond value is not evidence of open-ended conversation, autonomous everyday schedules, freeform romance or generative cognition.

### The economy inside the game

This is chiefly a controlled acquisition-and-upgrade economy. Its meaningful scarcity is attempts, usable support strength, compatible legacies, upgrade materials and calendar opportunity. The documented exchanges use prescribed prices rather than player-run auctions.

**Monies** and **Support Points** enhance the roster; **shoes**, **Winner's Sashes** and **Dream Glimmer** support trainee enhancement. [Enhancement materials](https://www.gacha.gg/umamusume/progression).

**Toughness** refills training entry energy, **Carrot Jelly** refills race entries, and **Alarm Clocks** enable applicable retries. Daily races, careers, missions and events replenish resources. [English items overview](https://umamusu.wiki/Game:New_Player_Guide).

The shops create conversions with different opportunity costs. Unneeded support duplicates can become Cleats; duplicate trainees yield Goddess Statues exchangeable for pieces of owned trainees; spare banner points become Clovers. Friend/Club currencies reward participation and sharing, while rare uncap crystals provide another route to stronger cards. These are different exchanges, not interchangeable balances. [Shop mechanics](https://uma.guide/guides/beginners-shop).

#### Paid acquisition, precisely bounded

The English launch-era standard tables show 150 Carats per scout and 1,500 per ten, with ordinary per-result rates of 79% low rarity, 18% middle and 3% top rarity. A standard ten-scout's final result has a middle-rarity floor; it does not make an SSR or three-star certain. The featured top-rarity target commonly has a 0.75% chance, not the full 3%. Every scout earns a point on its eligible banner; 200 points allow selection of a featured reward. That is 30,000 Carats before free tickets or promotions. Points are banner-specific and expire to Clovers, rather than accumulating toward the next target. Special paid guaranteed products have separate rules. [Destructoid's July 7, 2025 rule explanation](https://www.destructoid.com/umamusume-pretty-derby-pity-system-explained/).

The commercial significance is that collecting a desired character and strengthening the reusable training apparatus are separate ambitions. A support's higher uncap level affects many future careers. This can make a card economically desirable even when the player would emotionally prefer another athlete. Exact banner pools, discounts, guarantees and promotional rates must be read for the relevant date; no live purchase screen was inspected.

### Why repeated careers can remain interesting—and when they become work

At a career boundary, temporary energy, mood, support bonds, training growth and scenario resources do not become one endlessly leveling athlete. The finished build survives as a Veteran; the owned trainee and support collection remain available. Starting again restores the sequence of consequential early decisions, with different support placements, events and inherited bonuses. Selecting or training a character does not consume that recruit. [Reusable-trainee explanation](https://uma-reference.b-cdn.net/guide/training-umas).

A successful competitive racer and a useful legacy are different accomplishments. A disappointing result can still generate helpful sparks. Inspiration happens at setup and again during April of the second and third years; completed race history and skill choices can influence the opportunities passed onward. The source expressly labels the old repetitive four-character “legacy loop” a historical strategy, not a mandatory current best practice. [GameTora legacy guide](https://gametora.com/umamusume/legacies).

**Interpretation:** a fresh run can turn expertise into a better outcome quickly enough to encourage another attempt, while the surviving Veteran makes that attempt feel productive. The same structure can become exhausting when a player repeatedly reconstructs nearly identical careers solely for one improbable inheritance result. Collection, mastery, attachment and external competition can motivate the same action for different reasons. None of this establishes measured retention or universal enjoyment; the reception section supplies direct testimony.

**The 2026 change matters.** In its July 23 interview, Cygames describes Independent Training, introduced with the July 22 English update: set a support deck, choose Balanced/Stamina/Sprint emphasis and prioritize up to ten skill hints, then receive a completed career after elapsed time—even with the game closed. It covers the released scenarios, treats some goal failures differently and permits race agendas. Cygames says manual training generally makes much stronger Veterans. This reduces obligatory manual repetition while retaining a performance reason to play actively; the latter is developer testimony, not a benchmark conducted here. [Cygames interview in AUTOMATON](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/umamusume-1-5-year-anniversary-interview-with-cygames-the-difficult-undertaking-of-bringing-the-independent-training-mode-overseas-so-early-brand-new-career-scenario-and-more/).

**Cross-scenario inference:** the recurring design pattern is to attach a secondary production cycle to familiar training turns, then make an evaluation unlock better production. The inventory establishes the major systems, not every current balance coefficient, secret event or optimal deck.

### Eight constructed interactions

These are analytical illustrations, not reported personal play sessions. Each uses the cited rules and makes the intended consequence explicit.

1. **Rescuing Rice Shower's long-race goal.** The original card's schedule requires winning the 3,200 m spring Tenno Sho after earlier medium/long tests. A trainer looks ahead, builds endurance and prepares recovery instead of maximizing one visible stat. Earlier fifth-place or third-place requirements permit survival without winning every race; the eventual first-place demand changes the margin needed. The next attempt can revise preparation rather than treating every defeat as equivalent. [Original Rice Shower goals](https://gametora.com/umamusume/characters/103001-rice-shower).

2. **Taking today's modest training for tomorrow's strong one.** Two useful supports share a facility, but neither has enough bond for friendship training. The trainer takes the smaller immediate gain to develop both relationships; later a matching-facility placement becomes unusually valuable. Energy depletion can still make the attractive later training risky, so earning the opportunity does not remove the next decision. [Support-card rules](https://uma.guide/guides/support-cards).

3. **A lost race produces an asset with a different purpose.** A run misses the intended competitive quality, but its completed record offers useful stamina or distance sparks. The player keeps it for inspiration rather than fielding it as an ace. A later trainee gets improved starting possibilities, but compatibility and random inheritance prevent a guaranteed perfect successor. [Legacy rules](https://gametora.com/umamusume/legacies).

4. **Shopping converts earlier races into one decisive training turn.** In Trackblazer, wins earn Shop Coins. A player purchases an Empowering Megaphone, Speed Ankle Weights and a Good-Luck Charm, then waits for an attractive Speed opportunity. The weights improve training but raise energy use; the charm removes that turn's failure chance. The player must fund and time the combination, while overwriting a weaker item with a stronger one can waste the former. [Trackblazer item table](https://gametora.com/umamusume/trackblazer).

5. **Helping the team improves the protagonist.** In Unity Cup a trainer strengthens lagging teammates, improves the team's training ranks and unlocks better facilities for the main runner. A stored Spirit Burst can be used at a more helpful facility instead of immediately. Focusing only on the protagonist can leave the five-category team contest vulnerable; pursuing the strongest available opposition also risks losing league position. [Unity Cup rules](https://gametora.com/umamusume/unity-cup).

6. **Taking an imperfect stat turn to complete a song.** In Grand Concert the player lacks one performance resource needed for a song before the next concert. They choose a training opportunity for that resource, purchase the song and improve the concert result, after which queued training bonuses become active. Delaying until after the concert loses some time benefiting from that investment. Resource offers remain variable; an ideal stat and song combination is not always available. [Grand Concert rules](https://gametora.com/umamusume/our-grand-concert).

7. **A strong trio still loses the tournament.** A trainer qualifies with three specialized runners, then none finishes first in the single final. Several good finishers do not substitute for the winning runner under this mode's rule. The next preparation can seek a stronger finisher or a complementary team rather than merely raising its displayed aggregate rank. The finite final creates suspense and a potentially harsh variance cost. [Champions Meeting rules](https://gametora.com/umamusume/events/champions-meeting).

8. **An extra support copy changes the next build's possibilities.** A player uncaps an SSR, raises it toward the newly available level limit and rebuilds the relevant athlete. The old Veteran does not improve automatically; the benefit enters a new career through the strengthened support. A scarce crystal can substitute for a duplicate, but consuming it on this card precludes another enhancement. [Support progression](https://www.gacha.gg/umamusume/progression); [Veteran persistence](https://uma-reference.b-cdn.net/guide/training-umas).

### Fiction, people and the game-story boundary

The setting treats Umamusume as people who inherit names and spirits from another world, have horse ears, tails and extraordinary running ability, and attend **Tracen Academy**. The **Twinkle Series** is a major sports-entertainment institution. Winners earn the central position in a celebratory **Winning Concert**, joining athletic victory to performance and recognition. These premises organize school life, competition and fandom rather than supplying a large invented cosmological history. [Official Japanese setting overview](https://umamusume.jp/about).

Individual goals prevent the roster from being only a set of stat distributions. **Rice Shower** believes nearby misfortune is her fault and wants to change it; her favorite picture book concerns a happy blue rose. **Haru Urara** comes from Kochi, repeatedly loses but keeps her spirits up, and gains academy entry through her character after poor examination performance. **Tokai Teio** admires student-council president **Symboli Rudolf** and confidently wants to become a champion. These are official character premises, not inferred psychological agents. [Rice Shower](https://umamusume.jp/character/riceshower), [Haru Urara](https://umamusume.jp/character/haruurara), [Tokai Teio](https://umamusume.jp/character/tokaiteio).

The game's main story, individual career stories and other media are distinct delivery routes. Main-story chapter **Little Big Ambition** features Rice Shower, Mihono Bourbon and Mejiro McQueen, references named historical races and uses playable race gates between episodes. One gate requires a top-two Japanese Derby finish; later practice races require first place. This connects a prepared Veteran to continued narrative access. The wiki's linked transcript is a missing page, so this mechanics account does not invent its dialogue or transfer an anime ending into the game. [Chapter record](https://umamusu.wiki/Game:Main_Story_-_Act_1/Little_Big_Ambition).

**Interpretation:** helping a particular person overcome a recognizable limitation provides an emotional reason to care about preparation. A disappointing finish can concern both the build and the person represented by it. The fictional institution gives numerical success an audience, while concerts make recognition visible. OpenLegend can study that relationship between understandable desire, preparation, consequence and acknowledgment without importing the copyrighted cast or asserting that their authored personalities demonstrate emergent AI.

### Presentation and production evidence

The core presentation alternates character-led planning screens, short dialogue scenes and spectator races. That alternation concentrates attention on expression during preparation and position during competition. Skipping repeated scenes or races trades spectacle for speed; documentation-assisted optimization similarly trades uncertainty in event choices for clearer reward planning. These observations follow the documented interfaces; this research did not conduct an accessibility or device-performance test. [GameTora quickstart and linked interface tools](https://gametora.com/umamusume/guides/global-quickstart-guide).

A directly attributable production detail comes from Cygames engineer Naoya Kadera's CEDEC 2025 session description: the team constructed shots in the Unity editor using game assets, exported with Unity Recorder and composited in After Effects. They built tools to output more varied compositing materials because device-oriented game rendering constrained video quality. This supports Unity and an asset-reuse production workflow; it does not establish the proprietary race simulation architecture or runtime costs. [Official CEDEC session, July 22, 2025](https://cedec.cesa.or.jp/2025/timetable/detail/s67aef079dfc52/).

### R15: a real unofficial creation scene, with limited scope

**Applicability: yes, for the Japanese PC translation/enhancement ecosystem, with later Android and selected global-language extensions; not evidence of a mainstream gameplay-overhaul marketplace.** On October 17, 2026, legacy UmaTL displayed 2,472 commits, 108 stars and 29 forks; its translation successor displayed 691 commits, 39 stars and 27 forks. Work is attributed back to 2021. Hachimi Edge identifies a maintained fork after a September 2025 game update and its predecessor's January 2026 discontinuation. These counts measure visible repository activity and interest, not players or installs. [Legacy UmaTL](https://github.com/noccu/umamusu-translate); [Hachimi Edge](https://hachimi.noccu.art/); [translation successor](https://github.com/UmaTL/hachimi-tl-en).

The layers differ. Ordinary players configure careers and room races. Translation contributors author reusable text and presentation assets. Tool developers extend unofficial clients/viewers. Hachimi dynamically substitutes UI text, dialogue, lyrics, images and videos; the framework indexes translation sources but does not itself write them. Its credits distinguish original author **LeadRDRK**, Edge maintainer **harveyhans**, website/English-translation maintainer **noccu**, and other language projects. [Framework description](https://hachimi.noccu.art/); [credits](https://hachimi.noccu.art/credits).

**Constructed creation-to-use route:** start with an existing permitted translation repository, open it in the documented ZokuZoku VS Code extension, connect the relevant local game metadata and UI text dump, edit a UI entry, point Hachimi at the working translation folder, inspect the complete affected menu in the game, then contribute the structured changes for review. After acceptance, other users obtain the updated translation through the project's distribution. This route was read, not executed. The current guide explicitly warns that ZokuZoku no longer supports dialogue after a game update; its older story-editor screenshots therefore do not prove current compatibility. [ZokuZoku guide](https://hachimi.noccu.art/docs/translation-guide/using-zokuzoku); [contribution and index workflow](https://hachimi.noccu.art/docs/).

Representative work extends beyond localization. **CarrotJuicer** inspects local client/server exchanges and exposes information for strategic analysis, with a separate Hakuraku viewing route. **UmaViewer**, inspected through an openly identified fork, displays character models and animations, swaps costumes, plays voices/music and exports some presentation formats. These are reverse-engineered companion capabilities, not official game editors. An AI-chat viewer fork is likewise external to the shipped game. [CarrotJuicer README](https://github.com/CNA-Bld/CarrotJuicer); [UmaViewer fork README](https://github.com/untakatan/UmaStudio).

Durability is a central cost: multiple native mods may conflict; Android game updates require repatching; translation data can lag behind changed content. UmaTL's raw-skill variant explicitly cannot be used as global skill truth because skill effects differ. Hachimi's authors say the tools violate game terms; their account of reported bans is not a publisher promise or proof of safety. There is no iOS version documented. [Compatibility FAQ](https://hachimi.noccu.art/docs/hachimi/faqs); [English translation README](https://github.com/UmaTL/hachimi-tl-en).

The work is community-maintained, with donation/support links and contributor attribution, rather than an official royalty marketplace. Source-code availability does not release Cygames' assets or every translation under an open license. UmaTL describes restrictions on its own data and requests support for ongoing labor. [Legacy maintainer account and usage terms](https://github.com/noccu/umamusu-translate).

**Transfer and limit:** OpenLegend can learn from separating translated presentation from underlying rules, previewing a complete interaction before publication, crediting distinct tool and content authors, and budgeting for update breakage. The demonstrated native injection and extracted game assets are not a security or rights model to copy. This research grants no implementation authorization and does not change the [current root policies](../../../../AGENTS.md), including the repository's generated-content and compatibility boundaries.

### How the game converts enthusiasm into repeated spending

**An established emotional audience arrived before the mobile launch.** Cygames announced the cross-media project in 2016; music, manga and anime preceded the February 24, 2021 mobile release. The premise lets players train individualized characters inspired by actual racehorses and witness races, personal stories and victory concerts. The product thus offers affection, sporting aspiration and performance spectacle in the same relationship. ([Cygames launch announcement](https://www.cygames.co.jp/en/news/id-22022))

An anonymous Cygames developer told PocketGamer.biz that dramatic racehorse histories inspired the project and that overseas anime reception encouraged localization. The team adapted Maruzensky's dated slang into older American expressions and Daitaku Helios' speech into contemporary slang while explaining unfamiliar cultural associations in dialogue. **Interpretation:** this gives an unusual premise recognizable emotional stakes without erasing its particular culture. It is developer testimony about intent, not proof that any specific adaptation generated a measured amount of spending. ([September 9, 2025 interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/umamusume-to-be-honest-we-werent-sure-how-a-game-inspired-by-japanese-horse-racing-culture-would-be-received-overseas/))

**The monetization reaches two different ambitions.** Building on the [mechanics above](#major-mechanics-inventory), our interpretation is that players may value meeting a new character and improving future training attempts for different reasons. Those ambitions can coexist in one customer; no public evidence here divides spending between these motivations.

**Purchase pressure can rise before a banner ends.** Our interpretation of the [exchange and enhancement systems](#paid-acquisition-precisely-bounded) is that an unfinished collection or approaching guarantee can make another purchase attractive. This is a mechanism-level hypothesis, not a measured spending share or a claim about every player's experience.

Reuters interviewed one player in December 2021 who reported spending over **¥2 million**, and another who described spending **¥10,000–20,000 monthly**. The first framed spending as appreciation for the characters and developer. These are unusually useful examples of actual purchase motivations, but two interviews cannot establish the typical customer, the share of revenue from heavy spenders or a diagnosis of problematic play. The business permits very different spending intensities instead of collecting one fixed purchase price from everyone. ([Reuters reporting republished by Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/reu20211213KBN2IS00Y/))

**Recurring releases renew attention and purchase opportunities.** Sensor Tower observed Japanese third-anniversary characters/supports, free draws and currency alongside a rise from tenth to first in the iOS grossing chart on February 24, 2024. Its July 2025 English-launch analysis observed daily-active-user growth when Kitasan Black and Satono Diamond supports arrived. These are timed associations; simultaneous gifts, advertising and story updates prevent attributing the whole effect to one card. ([2024 analysis](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/umamusume-3rd-anniversary); [2025 analysis](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/umamusume-en-mobile-steam-JP))

The repeatable training structure also creates a production advantage **as an inference**: an additional character, support or scenario can change many future careers without authoring an entirely new world for each session. That does not make content inexpensive. It means carefully authored additions can interact with an established set of decisions repeatedly. The limitation is repetition: more reasons to retrain can become duties rather than interesting choices.

### Production, distribution and the limits of the formula

The Japanese launch was not a tiny disposable app: it followed a project publicly announced in 2016 and arrived with cinematic races, character stories and musical performances. Cygames' 2023 production discussion, summarized by GameBusiness.jp, identifies concept work, choreography, motion capture and 3D cutscene production as separate parts of creating a victory concert. The original magazine article failed to return substantive text in this research, so this specific workflow is supported by secondary reporting, not a claim to have inspected its complete firsthand account. ([Production summary](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2023/06/06/21791.html))

Cygames' company-level account describes frequent executive/project reviews, user-observation sessions and cross-project training. It also emphasizes balancing quality against finite resources. That supports an institutional capacity to iterate, but neither the article nor CyberAgent's filings disclose an Umamusume-specific launch budget, complete team size, advertising cost or annual operating bill. The five-year announcement-to-release span does not establish five years of constant full-team expenditure. ([CyberAgent staff interview](https://www.cyberagent.co.jp/en/way/list/detail/id=31312))

Distribution widened in stages. Japanese PC access through DMM followed mobile on March 10, 2021 and linked account data. The English release arrived June 26, 2025 on iOS, Android and PC; Cygames reported over **30 million Asian downloads** by then. Downloads are neither distinct paying customers nor paid units. The English version's later release also means its launch was monetizing a mature production pipeline and an already known franchise. ([Japanese announcement](https://www.cygames.co.jp/en/news/id-22022); [English announcement](https://www.cygames.co.jp/en/news/id-24452))

Promotion connected the fictional racers to actual racing audiences. Cygames sponsored the 2025 Unbridled Sidney Stakes and offered Umamusume demos at Churchill Downs on May 2–3. Its president explicitly described the opportunity to introduce the company's content to potential fans. The partnership is verified; the number of resulting installs or purchases is not disclosed. Do not confuse the real race's purse with Cygames' marketing expenditure. ([Cygames announcement](https://www.cygames.co.jp/en/news/id-24322))

Sensor Tower's first-month English analysis estimated that the US supplied approximately **30% of mobile downloads but over 70% of mobile spending**. Steam attracted a substantial audience separately. **Interpretation:** reach and revenue are not proportional; where the customers are and what they spend matters as much as total installs. The report's “Steam sales” language must not become paid-copy sales for this free game. ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/umamusume-en-mobile-steam-JP))

The evidence does not support effortless perpetual growth. AppMagic figures reported in March 2025 fell from **$965.3m in calendar 2021 to $368.3m in 2024** for Japanese mobile. The first overseas year also concentrated spending near launch and early major banners. Cygames subsequently explained that changing lifestyles motivated earlier delivery of automatic Independent Training, reducing the need to manually repeat every career. That is a documented response to friction, not a measured retention improvement. ([Historical estimates](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/uma-musume-pretty-derby-gallops-past-25bn-in-japan-alone/); [overseas year](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/umamusume-pretty-derby-races-to-100m-on-mobile-in-one-year-overseas/); [July 2026 developer interview](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/umamusume-1-5-year-anniversary-interview-with-cygames-the-difficult-undertaking-of-bringing-the-independent-training-mode-overseas-so-early-brand-new-career-scenario-and-more/))

Long-term success remains visible without inventing current lifetime receipts: Sensor Tower ranked the game **first in Japanese mobile spending in March 2026**, **fifth for January–July**, and **third in September**. CyberAgent's May 2026 presentation attributes overseas game-business growth partly to the English version, but its **¥13.1bn** quarterly overseas figure includes other publishing and advertising income. ([March ranking](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/top-10-japan-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-march-2026-JP); [January–July ranking](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/state-of-japan-gaming-2026-jp); [September ranking](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/top-10-japan-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-september-2026-JP); [CyberAgent, page 28](https://pdf.cyberagent.co.jp/C4751/Kdx3/awpF/yc1k.pdf))

The Konami patent dispute also ended in a **November 7, 2025 settlement**. CyberAgent says terms are confidential and the consolidated financial impact is minor. This closes the historical litigation account without fabricating a settlement amount or treating settlement as a finding of infringement. ([Primary disclosure](https://www2.jpx.co.jp/disc/47510/140120251107591535.pdf))

**Judgment:** a major commercial success for the publisher at the game level, with continuing demand and a demonstrable contribution to a profitable business. Precise project profitability remains undisclosed. For OpenLegend, the transferable idea is to make a particular character's development worth caring about, then let meaningful repeated choices deepen that attachment. Character affection, legible competition and varied attempts are useful research hypotheses. Randomized paid power, expiring exchange progress and expensive multimedia production are consequential business choices with different requirements and costs; they do not become OpenLegend requirements merely because this example earned billions.

## Reception, enjoyment and criticism

### What the reception supports

The strongest explanation of the appeal is the combination of **caring about a particular competitor, taking responsibility for her preparation, and watching an uncertain result become emotionally legible**. Reviewers repeatedly describe cheering during races despite having no steering input during them. They also describe becoming confused or frustrated by the training system. These findings coexist: the presentation can make a result meaningful while the rules remain difficult to understand. The sample supports these experiences occurring; it cannot establish how common they are across the paying audience, or their contribution to revenue. [U-R1–U-R5]

The critical dividing line is often the player's ambition. Finishing character stories, enjoying races, improving a favorite's previous performance, collecting every trainee, and competing against optimized teams impose very different time and purchase demands. Consequently, “enjoyable without spending” and “expensive to compete or collect” are compatible observations. A revenue explanation should preserve this distinction rather than interpreting positive reviews as approval of every monetization mechanism. [U-R2, U-R5, U-P1, U-P2]

### Why return, why stop: researcher's synthesis

The evidence suggests several different loops rather than one universal explanation:

1. **Character story:** choose someone interesting, discover her goal, help her prepare, and care about the result. A different trainee can bring a different relationship or perspective. The finish line supplies an understandable dramatic event even when the simulation is complex. The limitation is repeated story material; attachment does not automatically make every reread worthwhile. [U-R2, U-R4, U-R5]
2. **Mastery and rebuilding:** a failed attempt leaves the player with an explanation to test, while a retained veteran can change the next preparation. Restarting can therefore renew interesting decisions. The competing interpretation is that the player repeatedly pays the same time cost for a desired result whose occurrence remains largely outside their control. Neither interpretation should be inferred solely from total hours. [U-R2, U-R6, U-P2]
3. **Collection and competition:** favorite characters and stronger supports give repeated acquisition a purpose beyond a one-off purchase. Those motivations can diverge: buying power to train an existing favorite is different from wanting a new character's story. The reviews establish perceived conflicts over a finite currency budget, not payer segmentation or revenue shares. [U-R5, U-R7]
4. **Calendar obligation:** expiring opportunities can turn “I want another attempt” into “I need to use today's allowance.” A player can like the narrative and decline this schedule, or dislike the schedule while continuing. Claims of compulsive experience deserve accurate attribution, without diagnosing individuals or treating obligation as equivalent to enjoyment. [U-R7, U-P2]

An important commercial hypothesis follows: the same freely enjoyable game can support a paying minority with very expensive collection or competitive goals. These reviews help explain why such goals might matter to people; they do not prove which mechanism generated the game's reported spending. Validating that would require payer cohorts, purchase distribution, retention curves and attribution data unavailable in this reception sample.

### OpenLegend research hypotheses, not approved requirements

**Test whether a character's goal makes a mechanical outcome matter.** A hypothetical contest involving a known resident may be more engaging than the identical contest involving a disposable competitor. Compare what players remember and why they care. A counterexample would be players finding the character's goal irrelevant while valuing the contest's decisions alone.

**Test whether a setback offers a useful next experiment.** The productive question is not simply whether loss increases session count, but whether the player can name a changed plan and reaches interesting choices again quickly. Repeated unexplained failure may extend time played while reducing satisfaction. Preserve this as a question about player experience, without importing Umamusume's economy or reset structure.

**Test presentation's contribution independently of complexity.** Race commentary and identifiable competitors are plausible ways to make an autonomous outcome understandable. More internal simulation alone may not improve the player's experience. Compare clarity and emotional response, and also ask whether players understand what their preparation actually influenced.

**Separate desired return from scheduled obligation.** Any future retention study should ask why the player returned and whether they felt comfortable stopping. A satisfying completed story is not a retention defect. These hypotheses concern research methods and possible experiential patterns, not directions to add gacha, time gates or racing to OpenLegend.
