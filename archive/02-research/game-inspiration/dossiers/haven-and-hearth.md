# Haven & Hearth — a life worth making, a life that can be lost

**Full game dossier · Evidence captured November 14, 2026 · Seatribe, Steam app 3051280.**

Haven & Hearth is a close competitor to the emotional promise of OpenLegend: start small, learn useful things, improve a home, understand a landscape and gradually become someone within it. Its depth is not confined to violence. Food, materials, tools, curiosity, neighbors and a distinctive mythic vocabulary make ordinary activities consequential. The official premise explicitly connects a wilderness beginning to a player-shaped society. [About](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/about)

**Assessment:** an unusually durable creative niche and a substantial small-team achievement, with continuing participation and strong individual attachment. Its current profitability is unverified. The same systems that make investment meaningful can produce chores, fear, opaque progression and devastating loss. OpenLegend cannot claim the basic home–craft–exploration loop as unique; its independent people and useful invention must create a preferable experience within that loop.

[Comparative findings and OpenLegend positioning](../essays/shared-world-sandbox-mmos-and-openlegend.md) · [Research record](../research-progress-shared-world-sandbox-mmos-2026-11-14.md) · [Research requirements](../research-requirements.md).

This is desk research without client play, mod execution, performance testing, footage inspection or audio audition. Modern Haven, pre-2015 Legacy and Seatribe's separate Salem are distinguished throughout. A professional-review coverage gap remains explicit below.

## Identity, versions and the promise of persistence

The current 3D version has been playable since **2015**; **November 1, 2024** is its Steam Early Access date. Steam describes continuing work by two developers, with players providing its practical testing community. This distribution milestone does not mark the project's origin. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3051280/Haven__Hearth/)

**World 16.2 launched August 14, 2026**, replacing experimental 16.1 while original **World 16 continued concurrently**. The character-creation Wizard selects the world. World-specific rules therefore matter: there was no universal August wipe of every player. [World 16.2](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=80996)

The latest retrieved live notes, **September 25's Crystal Patch**, change Thingwall exclusion claims, fix hunger inheritance and leader comparisons, allow repairing Symbel tableware, and prevent repairing something while a battering ram attacks it. August's **Sleep Study** added an early fixed Study Circle and clearer quest progress. [Crystal Patch](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=81216) · [Sleep Study](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=81081)

World 16 itself began with a **map and character reset** in November 2024. The official About page still describes an alpha whose changing foundations may require resets. Persistence is continuity inside a surviving world, not an unconditional promise of permanent characters or maps. The recurring Object-Controlled-Objects development goal is not evidence of a shipped general automation system. [World 16](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=76565) · [About](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/about) · [World 16.2](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=80996)

**Source discipline matters:** current community guides contain inherited rules. The Quest page still lists local-quality improvements as rewards, although the official 2024 prelude removed them. A recently crawled page is not necessarily an up-to-date rules reference. [Quest](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Quest) · [World 16 prelude](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=76519)

## Becoming a hearthling: early play and developing competence

Character creation is enacted in a small landscape. A spirit leaves a pool, crosses a forked stream to acquire a body, chooses its appearance, receives a name from a Wizard and enters a fire. A graveyard offers inheritance; player beacons or charters help a group begin together. These are meaningful identity and continuity interfaces, though the unusual presentation also creates opportunities for confusion. [Character Creation](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Character_Creation)

Tutorial tasks introduce branches, chipped stone, an axe, carrying and stockpiling objects, a drinking vessel, food and the return to a hearth. Labor consumes stamina and energy; eating also develops attributes. Subsequent quests come from named trees or boulders and ask for travel, gathering, making, hunting or deliveries. Chopping down a questgiving tree can invalidate its quests. The landscape is therefore both resource and relationship, even though those relationships are mechanically bounded. [Quest](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Quest)

Three progression systems overlap:

| System | Actual decisions and significance |
| --- | --- |
| Curiosities and Learning Points | Study found or crafted objects over time. Intelligence limits total mental weight, duplicate types cannot run together, and quality improves the learning return. Experience Points fund study. The player chooses what to obtain and how to occupy limited attention. Exact wiki tables acknowledge outdated values. [Curiosity](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Curiosity) |
| Food Event Points | Meals contribute toward attribute gains; food composition affects which attribute improves. Variety and quality affect efficiency. Energy, hunger and satiation have different roles, so “eat when hungry” is an inadequate explanation of progression. [Food Event Points](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Food_Event_Points) |
| Credos | Profession-themed quest paths supply successive practical perks, pursued one at a time with prerequisites. Completed Credos can be inherited. They make a forager, farmer or fisher identity more than a name without imposing one exclusive combat class. [Credos](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Credos) |

Food optimization is a live design concern. The 2024 rework explicitly sought to reduce the pressure to manufacture a narrow mandatory diet. World 16.2 changes hunger relief according to distance from the leading character's attributes, and removes travel weariness. These are attempts to change the cost of keeping up and moving around; old-world efficiency recipes should not be copied uncritically. [World 16 prelude](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=76519) · [World 16.2](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=80996)

**Analysis:** the early reward is a newly possible action; established progress is a more capable household and reliable production chain. Later purpose can shift toward quality improvement, difficult discoveries, trade, political influence or community projects. Those are different motivations. An endless numerical improvement track can sustain an expert while exhausting someone who principally wants a pleasant home and occasional adventure.

## Objects, work, combat and the fictional economy

**Quality connects the production system.** Materials, tools and the worker's capabilities affect output; a weak skill can limit excellent ingredients. Many benefits scale nonlinearly, commonly with a square-root quality multiplier, although recipes vary. Better production can improve subsequent tools, food or study objects, creating reasons to revisit familiar work. [Quality](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/quality)

| Activity family | What the player actually manages |
| --- | --- |
| Farming and food | Plant, harvest, reserve better seeds, mill and process produce. Farming ability constrains quality gains; retaining valuable seed makes tomorrow's harvest part of today's decision. [Farming](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Farming) |
| Material production | Gather wood, stone and ore; process through tools and installations. Fuel and equipment can affect product quality. Outputs include useful weapons, clothing, containers and construction materials rather than purely collectible loot. [Quality](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/quality) |
| Underground exploration | Mine cave walls for stone or ore; consider support and the environment instead of treating every tile as an equally safe resource node. The community guide describes Towercap mushrooms as both tree-like resources and supports. [Cave](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Cave) |
| Settlement work | Build, pave, dig, fish, butcher and process wood. These ordinary civil actions are significant enough that the paid convenience system explicitly accelerates them. [Convenience](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/doc-cnv) |
| Combat | Combine melee/unarmed/marksmanship abilities, attributes, equipment and a selected deck of moves. Attacks widen four colored openings; defenses close them; initiative funds stronger actions. Accumulated power and knowledge matter alongside execution. [Combat](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Combat) |
| Commerce | Stock a barter stand, specify acceptable goods, quantity and quality, and receive payment through associated storage. Coins can be minted, but exchange need not use one universal NPC currency. [Barter Stand](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Barter_Stand) · [Skills](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Skills) |

The seasons change practical possibilities. Winter interrupts ordinary planting and freezes water; forage and fruit availability vary. This creates a calendar for preparation and changing activities, but exact durations should not be transferred between historical world-speed settings. [Seasons](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Seasons)

**Analysis:** the fictional economy is also a network of reasons to care. A neighbor's better seeds, expertise or finished goods can save substantial work. Quality improvement, consumption and vulnerability create continuing demand. Conversely, abundant self-sufficient production or unequal accumulated power can reduce reciprocal exchange. The sources establish the mechanisms, not a measured share of trade conducted through barter, coins or subscription tokens.

## Claims, realms, social dependence and death

Personal claims use a deed and continuing presence upkeep associated with learning. They control decay and unauthorized access through the crime system; they are not indestructible safe bubbles. In **16.2**, the initial small claim activates immediately, superseding the older wiki delay, while expansion remains conditional. Combat also restricts passage through gates and locked houses. [Personal Claim](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Personal_Claim) · [World 16.2](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=80996)

Village membership and realm control are distinct from individual property. Realms accumulate authority, offer blessings and contest provinces through Thingwalls and war flags. Experience generated by inhabitants adds authority rather than being deducted from their rewards. Crucially, realm territory **does not itself protect every resident's possessions**. Political membership, productive cooperation and property defense are separate concerns. [Realm](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Realm)

“Permadeath” also needs qualification. The modern Murder guide, revised August 2026, distinguishes learning Rage/Murder from activating **Murderous Rage**, which temporarily sacrifices knockout protection for greater grievous damage. Ordinary knockout protection can stop continued player attacks after defeat; it is not universal immunity to lethal damage or environmental death. Avoid importing the old rule that simply buying Rage permanently removes protection. [Murder](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Murder)

An actual dead character is lost. An heir can recover the hearth, claimed land, completed Credos and part of invested development, with burial and remembrance affecting recovery. Inconsistent historical percentages in community pages are omitted here. Infrastructure, knowledge and relationships can therefore outlast one life, but not necessarily a world reset. [Character Creation](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Character_Creation)

**Analysis:** solo residence has value, but cooperation changes its cost. Another person can teach an obscure rule, supply a missing material or make a frightening encounter survivable. Other people also introduce theft, coercion and disproportionate risk. Human trust is valuable precisely because help is voluntary; guaranteed automated assistance would change this experience, not simply reproduce it more reliably.

## Four constructed situations

These examples apply documented rules. They are neither firsthand play reports nor claims about the typical outcome.

1. **A useful first evening.** A newcomer makes an axe and vessel, then studies a Cone Cow while gathering. Limited attention makes another curiosity type or a higher-quality find valuable. The next choice is whether to explore for a better study object or improve the means to produce one. Progress links noticing, making and learning; an unreadable inventory can reduce it to administrative work. [Curiosity](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Curiosity)
2. **A farm with a future.** A household reserves better seed, processes the remainder and plans around seasonal availability. Its development depends on both the inputs and the farmer's competence. Winter encourages other activities; missing an optimal planting opportunity can instead feel like falling behind. The lesson is a tradeoff between a meaningful calendar and unwanted obligation. [Farming](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Farming) · [Seasons](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Seasons)
3. **A workshop finds a purpose.** A producer offers goods through a barter stand for a specific useful payment. A neighbor becomes a customer, and production gains a social destination. A realm blessing may help both, but their goods still require separate claims and defense. The next problem is trustworthy access and replenishment, not only increasing a crafting statistic. [Barter Stand](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Barter_Stand) · [Realm](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Realm)
4. **The place survives the person.** An heir chooses a deceased ancestor in the creation graveyard and returns with partial inherited capability. Burial makes recovery and remembrance practical tasks. Rebuilding is less absolute when a place and contacts remain; a destructive encounter or later world reset can nevertheless remove the setting that made recovery attractive. [Character Creation](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Character_Creation)

## Lore, atmosphere and the limits of nonplayer life

The fiction draws loosely on Slavic and Germanic myth. Fire, ancestry and a landscape with named presences organize its meaning. Credo stories connect practical learning to old gods and natural knowledge; the Forager's fiction makes gathering a relationship with the woods. This is a framing for ordinary life rather than a single prescribed heroic campaign. [About](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/about) · [Credos](https://ringofbrodgar.com/wiki/Credos)

The Wizard, questgiving trees/boulders and animals make “no NPCs” inaccurate. However, inspected documentation does not establish a population of independently evolving domestic citizens with social memory and ongoing personal projects. That is a meaningful possible distinction for OpenLegend. It is not evidence that Haven's world is emotionally empty: authored ritual and human histories can supply meaning through different means.

A dated 2015 founder interview explains why the mundane matters: supernatural events retain mystery when ordinary life has weight. The developers also wanted attractive settlements and shaping a place to be worthwhile activities. These are historical design intentions, not evidence that every imagined feature shipped. [Seatribe interview](https://mmos.com/editorials/interview-with-seatribe-developers-of-haven-and-hearth)

## Graphics, sound, interface and felt identity

Haven's move to 3D was a deliberate production and interaction choice. In the 2015 interview, its founders discussed rotating the camera and objects while acknowledging mixed feelings about losing 2D. A separate historical interview describes preserving the old visual character through an isometric 3D presentation and reducing repetitive clicks. Its page has a 2020 timestamp but pre-relaunch contents, so it is evidence of earlier intent. [Seatribe interview](https://mmos.com/editorials/interview-with-seatribe-developers-of-haven-and-hearth) · [IGM, part two](https://www.indiegamemag.com/igm-interviews-bjorn-johannessen-and-fredrik-tolf-haven-hearth-part-2/2/)

A modern newcomer specifically praises illustrated icons/event art, animation for ordinary work, environmental character creation and background music. Those are concrete presentation strengths attributed to that player, not firsthand judgments by this research. [October 2026 diary](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=979652)

**Analysis:** modest rendering fidelity does not require weak visual identity. Coherent objects, recognizable materials, purposeful movement and a memorable entrance can make work feel authored. These qualities matter because the player spends time looking at small practical actions. Conversely, poor feedback about an invisible rule cannot be repaired by attractive scenery alone.

The cited legacy review criticized uneven audio and a cumbersome interface; current accounts criticize explanation and navigation while sometimes admiring the art. Those observations concern different builds and dimensions. They do not support “graphics do not matter.” OpenLegend should test its grounded stylized direction for recognition, atmosphere, motion and clarity, rather than treating smaller scope as permission to look generic. [Legacy review](https://www.mmorpg.com/reviews/our-official-haven-and-hearth-review-2000055098) · [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3051280/Haven__Hearth/)

## What players like, dislike and return for

**Corpus limitation:** one substantial professional review and two genuine shorter personal reviews were located, all from **2012–2013 Legacy**. Modern evaluation comes from substantial player accounts and selected Steam reviews. This is not five independent current professional reviews or a representative sentiment survey. Some current writers do not identify which concurrent world they played.

| Evaluation and scope | Specific findings |
| --- | --- |
| **MMORPG, May 7, 2012; 7.1/10** | Useful manufacturing, trade and specialization made ordinary survival compelling. A missing bone created a reason to seek another person; abandoned settlements invited exploration. Confusing entry, instability and severe loss impeded return. The reviewer recommended friends/community and did not present it as an ideal first MMO. The social subscore was 9, polish 5. These are legacy judgments. [Review](https://www.mmorpg.com/reviews/our-official-haven-and-hearth-review-2000055098) |
| **Its Dangerous To Go Alone, August 13, 2012; 4/5 hearts** | A player farming and tending livestock in a five-person group valued useful occupations, role variety and technological progress through shared labor. Starting near friends mattered; lag and empty resource nodes frustrated. This is a short personal evaluation, not another professional publication. [Review](https://itsdangeroustogoalonetakethis.wordpress.com/2012/08/13/haven-and-hearth/) |
| **mommaCarole, February 11, 2013; World 6** | Praised craft specialization, terrain change, villages and custom clients. Accepted resets as familiar from Minecraft servers, but recognized bugs, lag and the power of strong settlements. Speculative network causes and hearsay about named players are excluded. [Personal review](https://www.planetminecraft.com/blog/haven-amp-hearth----game-review/) |
| **Concierto01, October 8, 2026** | Returned after initially being frightened off by reviews because other crafting worlds felt generic. Meaningful discovery sustained play through theft, illness and winter. Opaque mining and repeated cave-ins nearly caused departure; the wiki and Ender enabled progress, at the cost of the desired blind discovery. The writer wants knowledge learned within the world and from people. [Diary](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=979652) |
| **ElGrell, Steam, July 18, 2025; negative** | Loves detailed crafting, crops, material appearance and neighbor barter but rejects constant fear of strangers and loss. A friend and safer community enabled a return after an earlier killing. Displayed 0.1 Steam hours accompany a self-report of 664 standalone hours; this is not evidence of a novice dismissing the game instantly. [Helpful review surface](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3051280/reviews?browsefilter=toprated) |

The current direct Steam capture was **72% positive from 1,038 overall reviews**, with no recent score visible. A selected roughly nine-hour newcomer's account enjoyed discovery but quit after an unexplained boar encounter. Conversely, **Ariander's October 11, 2026 recommendation** describes repeated returns despite attachment followed by death and another break. Positive recommendation does not imply uninterrupted retention. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3051280/Haven__Hearth/) · [Review metadata mirror](https://playze.io/game/haven-hearth)

Opposing social experiences appear even in the same historical discussion: one player describes avoidance and isolation, another a hermit gradually building dependable neighbor trade before joining a village. A shared map makes community possible; it does not guarantee a welcoming encounter. [May 2012 discussion](https://forums.mmorpg.com/discussion/350145/haven-hearth-our-official-haven-hearth-review)

**Synthesis, not measured causation:** useful output gives work purpose; newly understood rules expand possibility; help makes other people memorable. Severe loss can interrupt exactly the attachment that motivated investment. Community knowledge and custom clients can rescue a frustrated player while increasing dependence on external systems. These samples do not quantify each mechanism's effect on retention or establish the market size of a hypothetical peaceful server.

## Production, discovery and commercial sustainability

The official FAQ names **Fredrik Tolf, “loftar,” and Björn Johannessen, “jorb.”** Its old eight-year wording should not be treated as a current payroll audit. Current Steam text and a July 2026 joint developer-stream announcement corroborate a continuing small founder-led identity, without proving no outside contributors ever participated. [Q&A](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/paq) · [July stream](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=80925)

The historical IGM interview assigns programming to Tolf and art/models to Johannessen, with design shared. It documents a custom engine, C server, Java client, Python utilities and Blender. It also says Salem income supported the Haven rewrite. Because its 2020 stamp conflicts with the pre-2015 content, these are historical production claims, not current financing. [IGM, part one](https://www.indiegamemag.com/igm-interviews-bjorn-johannessen-and-fredrik-tolf-haven-hearth/)

Current public offers distinguish access from paid efficiency:

| Offer | Advertised consideration at capture |
| --- | --- |
| Free | Unlimited gameplay at normal development rate. |
| Verification | $15 once; inventory column, +20% development, +50% convenience and a hat. |
| Bronze subscription | $7/30 days or $50/365 days; inventory row, +30% development, +100% convenience. Higher tiers principally add support hats. |
| Subscription token | $10 individually; tradable month of subscription, vulnerable to loss/theft. |
| Custom imagery and hats | Sketchbook Pages from $2.50 enable uploaded imagery and can be traded/lost. Cosmetic hats can be retrieved again through the account. |

The present free model has **no old weekly playtime cap**. It also is not cosmetic-only monetization: faster development and civil actions are material benefits. Patch-linked hats connect updates and support. [Store](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/store) · [Convenience](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/doc-cnv)

A **December 28, 2016** primary post said the preceding year's income had fallen substantially short of sustaining development. It proposed stronger paid incentives and explained that other work would reduce available development time. This demonstrates a historical funding problem, not current insolvency. [Developer statement](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=52550)

**Current revenue, paying accounts, realized spend, compensation, hosting/marketing costs and game-only profit remain unestablished.** Official pages, announcements, interviews and targeted financial searches did not produce a defensible current account. Free players, multiple accounts, unknown subscription durations and token/hats spending make “players × monthly price” invalid. The sparse company site supplies no useful financial statements. [Seatribe](https://www.seatribe.se/)

**Economic assessment:** longevity demonstrates continued operation and commitment; it does not establish a comfortably funded business. The relevant small-team lesson is years of accumulated work, iteration, community knowledge and founder persistence. It is not evidence that comparable depth can be built quickly or that a low headcount implies a low total labor cost.

## R15 — an established custom-client ecosystem

**Applicability: yes.** Official source availability, endorsed interface variation, Steam integration, maintained alternatives and direct player adoption support a full investigation. Hurricane's Workshop capture shows **1,968 subscribers and 62 ratings**, evidence of distribution rather than current active use. That retrieval also exposes removal/incompatibility banners alongside the active listing and update history; their operational meaning was not verified by installation. [Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3423755273)

### What creators change

| Project or layer | Authorship, capability and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Official **hafen-client** | Tolf's public Java client source supplies the baseline. Editing client behavior is not equivalent to obtaining the authoritative server source or changing official-world rules. [Repository](https://github.com/dolda2000/hafen-client) |
| **Hurricane** | Nightdawg builds directly on the official client and merges upstream changes. Standalone and Workshop routes coexist. Its documented tools include shared map-marker presets, alarms and targeting/hitbox aids. [Repository](https://github.com/Nightdawg/Hurricane) · [Feature documentation](https://nightdawg.github.io/HurricaneDocs/) |
| **Apricot** | ntforg's Hurricane fork emphasizes convenience, suggestions, update delivery and translation. It adds optional crash-data handling and documented local language overrides. The README's claim that Hurricane lacks cookbook integration conflicts with Hurricane's current documentation. [Repository](https://github.com/ntforg/Apricot) |
| **Ender** | An established alternative with a current 2026 support thread and a modern player's reported adoption. Support posts include resource/runtime/audio problems; no comparative benchmark was performed here. [Support thread](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=49&t=40754&start=2750) |
| Mapping and cookbook services | Hurricane supports private/community mapping and optional cookbook connections. These extend shared information rather than adding authored server quests. [Repository](https://github.com/Nightdawg/Hurricane) |

Native building, signs, imagery, settlement organization and material variation remain separate from these client modifications. No inspected source establishes a general official quest editor, arbitrary server scripting SDK or a supported total conversion comparable to a separately authored game.

### A creation-to-play workflow

**Novice example: translate an interface.** Apricot documents UTF-8 JSON files under `Translations/<language>/`. Local entries override bundled ones. Enable its untranslated-text recorder, use the relevant screens, edit the generated missing strings, move them into the matching files and restart/select the language to inspect the result. Local overrides survive updater changes; removing the language folder removes the addition. Finished work can be submitted to `src/l10n/<language>/` for a subsequent release. This is a concrete edit–preview–package–update path, documented rather than executed here. [Translation guide](https://github.com/ntforg/Apricot/blob/master/Translations/README.md)

**Expert example: change the client itself.** Obtain the official source, install a JDK and Apache Ant, edit the documented client modules, build and test. The packaged `bin/hafen.jar` contains the official-server configuration/resources; bare `build/hafen.jar` does not. Distribution of a fork includes its corresponding source and appropriate notices. Upstream incorporation is a distinct process with a requested copyright assignment. [Official README](https://github.com/dolda2000/hafen-client) · [Copying rights](https://github.com/dolda2000/hafen-client/blob/master/COPYING)

For users, the 2024 official integration makes subscribed Workshop clients selectable at launch. Hurricane also offers a standalone updater. Documentation drifts: its installation guide lists Java 17–21, while the current README permits 17–25. Apricot has its own runtime packaging and login-screen updater. These are alternatives with their own dependencies, not a stack of interchangeable plug-ins with a universal load order. [Steam integration](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=76519) · [Installation guide](https://nightdawg.github.io/HurricaneDocs/guide/installation) · [Hurricane](https://github.com/Nightdawg/Hurricane) · [Apricot](https://github.com/ntforg/Apricot)

### Durability, rights, trust and incentives

Hurricane's 2026 changelog records upstream merges, broken support-coverage displays, fishing-bot fixes and removal of broken automation. Source/resource changes therefore create ongoing maintenance rather than one-time authoring. Apricot documents automatic crash reports containing stack/system information and an opt-out; this is maintainer disclosure, not a security audit. Bug ownership must distinguish the fork, its connected service and the base game. [Changelog](https://nightdawg.github.io/HurricaneDocs/changelog/) · [Apricot](https://github.com/ntforg/Apricot)

Official main client code is **LGPLv3**, with separate external-library licenses; game-content resources remain proprietary. Access to the source does not authorize extracting art for OpenLegend. The copying file explicitly separates code, resources and trademark obligations. [Copying rights](https://github.com/dolda2000/hafen-client/blob/master/COPYING)

The FAQ welcomes interface variations and treats incentives to bot partly as a game-design problem. That philosophy does not prove all automated conduct is harmless. Hitboxes, alarms, shared maps and automated work can change information and effort advantages, not only aesthetics. [Q&A](https://www.havenandhearth.com/portal/paq) · [Features](https://nightdawg.github.io/HurricaneDocs/)

The inspected projects distribute source/builds without an established paid-mod marketplace. Maintainers describe goals of convenience, responsiveness and avoiding crashes; creator earnings, commissions and full labor contributions were not established. Free access is not evidence that maintenance costs nothing. Workshop discovery, forum support and fork contributions are documented; the effectiveness of moderation and every client's present compatibility are not.

**Transfer:** extensibility can recruit useful contributions and reveal friction. It also needs an explicit authority boundary, version policy, rights clarity and decisions about automation/information advantages. OpenLegend should first make ordinary work understandable in its native experience. These observations do not change the [current project policies](../../../../AGENTS.md) or authorize a client-mod architecture.

## OpenLegend: competitive overlap and actionable differences

Haven already provides a home, useful labor, improvement through discovery, folklore, social roles and personal history in a mutable world. Removing harsh PvP or adding conversational characters does not automatically create a strong alternative. A player who loves Haven may value precisely its human uncertainty, hard-earned competence and accumulated place.

The more credible prospective difference is **independent particular people whose lives respond visibly to the player's useful interventions**. That must produce an experience worth choosing: situated help without an external wiki, a need that changes as someone lives, a practical invention that alters what a household can do, and a later encounter that makes the consequence clear. These are hypotheses to demonstrate, not features this research certifies as delivered or unique across all games.

| Lesson | Existing owner and a useful test |
| --- | --- |
| Make the first useful act intelligible | [First playable cooperation](../../../../docs/projects/first-playable-cooperation-feature-spec.md): can a newcomer understand a need, act and recognize the result without external instruction? |
| Give continuity a visible consequence | [Continuing lives](../../../../docs/projects/continuing-lives-feature-spec.md): does returning reveal meaningful changes in specific people, rather than only production meters? |
| Keep material work purposeful | [Gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md): does a new tool or construction change an achievable action, or merely accelerate the same chore? |
| Treat visuals as part of the proposition | [Art direction](../../../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-direction-at-a-glance): test recognizable silhouettes, work animation, atmosphere and feedback within the chosen direction. |
| Separate attachment from obligation | [Playtest plan](../../../../docs/projects/first-playable-playtest-plan.md): observe voluntary return, confusion, recovery cost and whether routines feel useful or compulsory. |
| Count funded effort | [Commercial offer](../../../../docs/projects/initial-commercial-offer-decision-brief.md): measure willingness to pay and operating costs instead of inferring business health from passionate play. |

A compelling OpenLegend should win a concrete comparison of experiences. “I understood this person, changed something useful, and wanted to see what happened afterward” is testable. Whether that experience is distinct enough, and whether its production cost is sustainable, remains a product question rather than a marketing slogan.
