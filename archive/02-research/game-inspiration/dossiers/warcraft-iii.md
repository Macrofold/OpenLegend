# G96 — Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos / The Frozen Throne

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Warcraft II](warcraft-ii.md)

**Full R01–R14 research pass. Capture: September 26, 2026.** The baseline is original Reign of Chaos and The Frozen Throne. Reforged, later repairs, and the newly released Forsaken Kingdom are distinguished from that baseline. Five substantive written reviews across four publications and contrasting direct player accounts are included. Worked situations are constructed from documented rules, not personally played matches. No video is represented as watched.

**Preservation:** no game-specific predecessor chapter or mechanics owner was present in this branch's original collection. Those earlier owners remain unchanged. This dossier retains and extends checkpoint `2e2683c`, including its hero, item, upkeep and September 2026 findings. The separate seven-file packet-preservation audit is not certified by completing this subject. The official legacy guide is mainly expansion-era documentation, not a universal table for every patch or custom map.

## 1. Promise and edition boundaries

The player commands a settlement and an army, but also develops a few persistent-within-the-match heroes. Fighting neutral creatures can yield experience, items and access; fighting the opponent contests those opportunities. A successful opening is therefore more than building the largest economy behind a wall. Established play asks where the hero should be, which small group can survive an encounter, and whether spending now creates an advantage before the opponent catches up. [S01](#s01) [S04](#s04)

| Release / mode | Research boundary |

| **The Frozen Throne, July 2003** | Additional faction heroes, counter-units, player-built shops, neutral recruitment and new campaigns. Rexxar's small-party adventure is a distinct mode, not a standard melee match. [S35](#s35) [S40](#s40) |

The 2026 campaign's expanded equipment and talent systems must not be retrofitted to the original six-slot hero inventory. [S02](#s02) [S13](#s13)

## 2. Actions, economy and faction institutions

### Commands are choices about attention

The player moves, attacks, focuses fire, retreats, surrounds, patrols, stops, holds positions, selects subgroups and directs individual abilities. Workers collect, construct and repair; buildings recruit and research; heroes learn, carry, buy, use and transfer items. Automated spell behavior can reduce routine work, but turning it off can itself be the right tactical choice. An order to a mixed group is not always the same as a direct order to its channeling caster. [S10](#s10)

The principal economic resources are gold, lumber and food capacity rather than Warcraft II's maritime oil economy. Technology is attached to settlement tiers and institutions. An early specialist requires buildings, time and materials before its eventual ability matters. Ordinary military research improves relevant unit families rather than equipping every soldier with a personal randomized weapon. The expansion's new units and race shops were designed to change compositions and counterplay, not merely provide universally stronger replacements. [S35](#s35)

### Four different ways to inhabit the same strategic vocabulary

| Faction | Economic / architectural difference | Consequence for play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Human** | Peasants can temporarily become Militia. Several workers can accelerate construction at added cost. Masonry and improved lumber handling support different investments. | A threatened workforce can briefly become defense, but diverted workers stop supplying the economy. Fast construction converts extra labor/material into earlier readiness rather than free production. [S06](#s06) |
| **Orc** | Burrows combine food support with worker-garrison defense. Pillage and Spiked Barricades connect attacking or defending buildings to additional effects. | Food infrastructure also shapes safe territory. Raiding can have an economic purpose even before a structure falls, while static defenses still depend on where the force can respond. [S07](#s07) |
| **Undead** | Acolytes summon buildings and can leave after initiating them; gold gathering and lumber work use different entities. Blight, unsummoning and corpse use support distinctive logistics. | A Ghoul is both combat potential and part of lumber production. Corpses can feed survival or summoned forces, so a battle site can retain value after the first fight ends. [S08](#s08) |
| **Night Elf** | Wisps collect lumber without cutting the tree. Certain Ancients can uproot, move and fight, at the expense of their rooted function. Moon Wells combine food support with limited recovery reserves. | A building can become a tactical actor, while moving it temporarily removes an economic service. The forest and the settlement do not operate like Human structures with different art. [S09](#s09) |

**Interpretation:** asymmetry is strongest when it changes what an entity is for. A worker that can become temporary militia, a lumberjack that can join the army, or a production building that can move creates decisions a renamed counterpart would not. The risk is onboarding burden: the player must learn which apparent similarities are misleading.

## 3. Heroes, units, spells and items

### Hero development and identity

Legacy melee permits up to three different heroes, gated by settlement development. Experience earns levels and ability selections; Strength, Agility and Intelligence affect different capabilities, with a primary attribute also affecting attacks. The ordinary melee level cap is ten, and ultimate abilities begin at level six. Neutral-creep experience stops for a level-five hero, preventing endless safe camp farming as the sole route to maximum power. Campaign caps and scripted progression differ. [S01](#s01)

A hero is not a player-created social biography. The player selects an archetype and build, then acquires an individual match history through close escapes, skill choices and carried items. Named campaign versions add authored identity and relationships. **Interpretation:** mechanical continuity can make the player care about an actor before any elaborate dialogue system exists, but it is not the same as autonomous personality or social simulation.

### Roster families: what compositions can express

| Faction | Hero roster and ordinary unit families | Practical role distinctions |

A Priest-supported formation is not the same plan as a mobile hero raid. [S15](#s15) [S19](#s19) |
| **Orc** | Blademaster, Far Seer, Tauren Chieftain and Shadow Hunter; Grunts, Troll ranged units, Raiders, Kodo Beasts, Shamans, Witch Doctors, Spirit Walkers, Demolishers, Wind Riders, Batriders and Tauren. | Raiding, mobility, damage distribution, support auras, heavy melee and anti-air demand different mixes. Kodo War Drums and Spirit Link affect the value of surrounding forces rather than merely adding another attacker. [S16](#s16) [S20](#s20) |
| **Undead** | Death Knight, Dreadlord, Lich and Crypt Lord; Ghouls, Crypt Fiends, Gargoyles, Abominations, Meat Wagons, Necromancers, Banshees, Frost Wyrms, Shades, Obsidian Statues and Destroyers. | Bodies, corpses, healing, mana supply, air control and spell removal form different chains. This is a functional inventory, not a patch-specific tournament tier list. The listed ordinary roster spans original and expansion play; it is not a claim that all those units shipped in July 2002. Neutral heroes add shared drafting options, but they do not make the four faction economies identical. New campaign units, scripted Naga forces and custom-map heroes are not a fifth standard legacy melee faction. [S35](#s35)

### Abilities change relationships, not only damage

The Human combination guide illustrates healing and protection around a line of conventional troops, Archmage mana support, Water Elementals as bodies and targeted Mountain King disruption. These interact with retreat paths and vulnerable casters. Human magic is therefore not one undifferentiated healing pool, and a researched support ability is useless when its user cannot survive in range. [S19](#s19)

The **Blood Mage** makes particularly clear distinctions. Flame Strike threatens an area; Banish makes a target ethereal, restricting ordinary physical interaction while increasing magical vulnerability; Siphon Mana requires maintaining a channel; Phoenix supplies a temporary combatant with an egg-based return cycle. Banish can help a friendly caster escape physical focus or make an enemy more vulnerable to another spell. It is not generic invulnerability, and it does not necessarily silence its target. [S23](#s23)

The **Shadow Hunter** supplies bouncing Healing Wave, temporary Hex, stationary Serpent Wards and Big Bad Voodoo. His ultimate protects allies while leaving its channeling caster vulnerable. Protecting the source of protection is therefore part of the ability's cost. Hex is not permanent removal of the target, and hero effects need not share ordinary-unit durations. [S24](#s24)

The **Death Knight** can use Death Coil to heal friendly Undead or harm living enemies, but cannot simply cast it on himself. Death Pact offers another recovery route through sacrifice; Unholy Aura changes mobility and regeneration; Animate Dead makes corpses useful again. The apparent simplicity of a health bar conceals different ways an army can or cannot repair damage. [S25](#s25)

The **Warden** has a different geometry: Blink crosses terrain but is constrained by immobilization, Fan of Knives attacks nearby targets, Shadow Strike creates continuing pressure, and Vengeance makes another actor whose survival matters. She can threaten exposed units and leave, but a successful teleport does not prove that every remaining harmful effect has disappeared. [S26](#s26)

Orc and Undead compositions also show that secondary effects can multiply value: Spirit Link distributes incoming focus; an aura supports nearby allies; statues sustain a force; summons screen expensive units. Enemy dispels, disabling effects or attacks on the support actor are therefore alternatives to racing the main army's health bars. Official legacy tactical recommendations are possibilities to examine, not timeless guarantees that a particular build always wins. [S20](#s20) [S21](#s21)

### Damage type, defense and timing are different predicates

The armor reference distinguishes attack types from armor classes. A unit's appearance is not sufficient to determine every multiplier, and a magical attack is not identical to spell damage. Heavy armor can be a disadvantage against a relevant attacker even when its name sounds universally superior. This gives force composition a counter-system rather than a single ladder of bigger numbers. [S11](#s11)

Spell rules add further distinctions: physical disabling effects are not necessarily removed by a conventional dispel; channeling can be interrupted; beneficial and harmful effects have different removal conditions. “Immune,” “invisible” and “invulnerable” should therefore be read with the specific rule they govern. **Interpretation:** the depth comes from combinations with comprehensible limits. The failure mode is an interface that invites a universal interpretation and then punishes the player for believing it. [S10](#s10)

### Item economy: acquisition, carrying, use and death

The legacy guide separates permanent bonuses, charged abilities, consumed items and instant power-ups. Camps, shops and authored placements supply them. Six slots create a composition constraint, not six anatomical equipment positions; repeated effects do not all stack. A hero retains carried items through revival, while an ordinary carrier drops its cargo on death. Useful items can be transferred or sold, so the best owner can change as a match develops. [S02](#s02)

Examples include recovery consumables, Boots of Speed, defensive rings and scrolls that change an engagement or travel decision. A Goblin Merchant sells capabilities, not a full persistent crafting profession. Limited stock and replenishment make access and timing relevant. Neutral recruitment and services are accessible through proximity rather than permanent ownership; a nearby opponent may use the same site. [S27](#s27)

The expansion's Backpack research lets suitable ordinary units transport items without turning them into full heroes. Delivering equipment on a disposable carrier can save hero travel, but its different death rule makes that convenience risky. In standard melee there is no open-ended profession crafting, weapon durability economy, player auction house or automatic recipe-combination system. Such systems in custom RPGs or arena maps belong to those authored games. [S20](#s20) [S02](#s02)

## 4. Progression, map time and loss

### A map is an opportunity field

Neutral camps are simultaneous sources of experience, bounty, items and access. Fighting them also spends health, mana and attention. A force committed to a camp can be ambushed; a cleared site can reveal where someone has recently been. Hero progression therefore links apparently noncompetitive exploration back to competition. The player should evaluate both the camp's rewards and the next position it enables. [S04](#s04)

Day and night alter visibility and some creature behavior. Certain Night Elf units can hide at night, and different unit families have different regeneration rules. A Moonstone makes the cycle temporarily manipulable. These are not proofs that every creep sleeps, every Elf becomes invisible or all injured units recover in the same circumstances. [S05](#s05)

### Army size changes future income

The expansion-era upkeep guide specifies full newly mined gold at 0–50 food, 70% retained at 51–80, and 40% retained at 81–100. This is not periodic confiscation of an existing bank. Blizzard connects the system to smaller-army tactics and preserving hero relevance. Original-release thresholds and custom rules must be checked separately. [S03](#s03)

**Interpretation:** waiting below a threshold can make a future commitment more affordable, but only while the opponent does not take every valuable location. Crossing the threshold can purchase an immediate attack window. Sitting in the expensive band without using the additional force accepts a cost without realizing its intended advantage. This is a tension to evaluate, not a universal instruction to remain at one food count.

### Recovery preserves identity, not the whole situation

A fallen hero can normally return through an Altar with its level and items, at the cost of resources and time. A Tavern can offer faster recovery with different costs and an initially weakened hero. Neither restores the ordinary troops, lost information, missed opportunities or destroyed economy around that hero. A campaign's explicit failure conditions and saved-game rules are separate from normal melee revival. [S01](#s01) [S27](#s27)

Early play develops a first hero, a working settlement and manageable targets. Midgame adds supporting heroes, technology and counters. Later play increases the importance of combinations, information and resource denial, rather than merely filling every slot with the most expensive unit. A new melee match resets that material history; player knowledge, replay study and social familiarity can persist outside it. [S34](#s34)

### Rexxar and the later RPG branch

The Frozen Throne's bonus adventure reduces the army to a small entourage and emphasizes quests, equipment, travel and encounters. Greg Kasavin's launch review finds that experiment interesting but also describes repetitive respawns and travel. Its first act should not be mistaken for the entirety of the later patched adventure. The December continuation explicitly supported carrying a prior party forward or entering with a default party. [S35](#s35) [S41](#s41)

Forsaken Kingdom makes that contrast more explicit: a four-mission Lordaeron prologue precedes a semi-open RPG campaign with new leads, expanded inventory and talent development. Blizzard's roughly thirty-hour claim is promotional, not measured here. Its community-and-home framing follows newly free Undead rather than simply replacing their names in an old Orc story. Garek Bandarion and Anya belong to this new material, not original 2002 characterization. [S13](#s13)

The September 25 PTR shows continuing changes to that new campaign's equipment, talents, encounters and racing activity. Those notes are evidence of testing and maintenance, not permission to describe their adjusted item values as already live. The warning that old saves will become invalid also demonstrates a different persistence risk from losing a fight inside the fiction. [S14](#s14)

## 5. Eight constructed interactions

These examples follow documented rules but are not claimed match recordings, solved campaign walkthroughs or personally tested balance recommendations.

### A. A camp can make the army stronger and the position weaker

**Intention:** gain a level and a useful item before attacking. **Conditions:** a manageable camp, sufficient recovery and uncertain enemy location. **Actions:** send a summoned body first, concentrate attacks and preserve the valuable hero. **Interaction:** the army earns resources while spending health, mana and time in a predictable place. **Result:** winning the neutral fight can still leave it vulnerable to an enemy arriving afterward. **Next decision:** attack, heal or retreat according to what remains—not merely the loot received. **Limit:** a camp's apparent value does not include the whole risk of being intercepted. [S04](#s04)

### B. The fifty-first point of food is a timing commitment

**Intention:** use a larger army before the opponent can answer. **Conditions:** the legacy expansion rules, a force below the first upkeep threshold and saved resources. **Actions:** complete additional units, cross the threshold and move toward a consequential engagement. **Interaction:** extra power reduces the share of future gold retained. **Result:** waiting indefinitely now costs more than it did before recruitment. **Next decision:** exploit the force, accept a trade or revise the plan after new information. **Limit:** this does not mean intentionally throwing away soldiers is always economical; replacing them can exceed any mining benefit. [S03](#s03)

### C. A building can join a fight by abandoning another job

**Intention:** stop pressure against an Elf settlement. **Conditions:** a suitable Ancient can uproot while the attacking force approaches. **Actions:** turn it into a moving combat actor and use its body to contest the approach. **Interaction:** an economic structure becomes military presence, but its rooted service is unavailable. **Result:** the defense gains strength without immediately recruiting a conventional unit. **Next decision:** pursue, recover or root again to resume production. **Limit:** moving it at the wrong time can solve the visible skirmish while delaying the reinforcements or income needed afterward. [S09](#s09)

### D. Cheap delivery can make an expensive item less safe

**Intention:** keep the hero active while bringing it a purchase. **Conditions:** appropriate transport capability from Backpack research and a traversable route. **Actions:** send an ordinary carrier to collect the item and meet the hero. **Interaction:** the player exchanges hero travel time for a carrier's vulnerability and different death rule. **Result:** the hero can continue fighting or scouting while the item moves separately. **Next decision:** complete the transfer, divert to safety or abandon the delivery when the route changes. **Limit:** unlike an item retained through hero revival, cargo dropped by the carrier can become an opportunity for someone else. [S02](#s02) [S20](#s20)

### E. One protection can expose another weakness

**Intention:** save a caster from physical focus. **Conditions:** Blood Mage support can apply Banish and the enemy's current pressure is mostly physical. **Actions:** make the threatened target ethereal and create distance. **Interaction:** the target's relationship to attacks changes, but magical danger increases rather than disappearing. **Result:** a good response to one composition can be a poor response to another. **Next decision:** escape before a different threat arrives or deliberately combine Banish with friendly offensive magic against an enemy instead. **Limit:** the ability is not a universal shield, and a banished opponent may still cast. [S23](#s23)

### F. Night offers concealment, not permission to ignore the enemy

**Intention:** preserve a vulnerable Night Elf hero near dangerous terrain. **Conditions:** the relevant night capability is available and the enemy has no effective detection at the intended refuge. **Actions:** use terrain and, for a Warden, Blink where its restrictions permit; stop to exploit eligible concealment. **Interaction:** time of day, movement, spell constraints and observation all matter. **Result:** the hero can escape a chase without defeating the pursuing force. **Next decision:** recover or rejoin elsewhere. **Limit:** immobilization can prevent Blink, and concealment is not a cure for every continuing harmful effect. [S05](#s05) [S26](#s26)

### G. Automation can become the opponent's resource

**Intention:** punish an enemy caster formation. **Conditions:** Faerie Dragons can use Mana Flare while Dryads remove relevant magical effects. **Actions:** position the anti-casting threat and strip effects the enemy would normally restore. **Interaction:** automatic recasting can expose those casters to repeated punishment. **Result:** a convenience setting that was efficient in an ordinary fight becomes dangerous here. **Next decision for the defender:** disable autocasting, change spell timing, remove the threatening unit or disengage. **Limit:** this is conditional counterplay, not proof that every cast should remain manual or that the combination cannot be answered. [S22](#s22)

### H. Returning now and returning ready are different choices

**Intention:** recover a dead hero before losing an important opportunity. **Conditions:** Tavern access, adequate resources and a choice between immediate purchase and slower Altar revival. **Actions:** pay for the quick return and reposition support around the weakened hero. **Interaction:** the recovery preserves identity but initially lacks the health and mana readiness of a fully prepared force. **Result:** being present may save the objective, yet immediately charging into battle can lose the hero again. **Next decision:** heal, defend cautiously or concede the objective to preserve resources. **Limit:** instant return does not rewind the fight or recreate the lost army. [S27](#s27)

## 6. People, multiplayer and player-authored worlds

Native play supports competition and team coordination around shared battlefield information and separate forces. A support ability, scouting report or saved ally can matter beyond the caster's own army. There is no native romance meter, household schedule, freely authored personal class or automatically persistent guild settlement in the standard rules. Campaign loyalties are authored relationships, not a general diplomacy simulation. [S34](#s34) [S42](#s42)

The World Editor is a more expressive creation boundary than Warcraft II's map placement: it provides triggers, scripts, campaign construction and control over presentation. A creator can change objectives and event sequences, not merely place a portal-shaped object and hope that its appearance supplies behavior. The official FAQ also distinguishes customization from making arbitrary claims about every engine capability. [S28](#s28)

Roper describes the editor as a learning surface where creators can inspect and reuse map elements. That is evidence of an intended sharing mechanism, not a promise that every modern protected map can be opened or that every copied asset has unrestricted legal rights. The expansion's own RPG experiment demonstrated that the same toolset could support a different play structure. [S29](#s29) [S40](#s40)

**Interpretation:** the important transferable pattern is a game becoming material for other games. A repeatable map, a recognizable rule format and an inspectable example are much easier to share than an abstract engine capability. Their limits also need to be explicit. Custom arena, survival, tower-defense and role-playing rules are authored experiences, not proof that every such mechanic exists in standard Warcraft III melee. User creations can keep a tool socially valuable while increasing compatibility and maintenance obligations.

The 2026 patch introduces further equipment, talent and editor capabilities. These are additions with compatibility consequences, not evidence that the original release already possessed an equivalent authoring surface. PTR requests for broken-map reports make the distinction between an advertised capability and preservation of existing content especially concrete. [S12](#s12) [S14](#s14)

## 7. Story, perspective and objectives — spoilers

**Major campaign spoilers.** Reign of Chaos moves through different factions within a continuous narrative rather than giving both sides incompatible victorious histories. Arthas's response to the plague leads to the Stratholme purge, rupture with Uther and Jaina, and pursuit of power through Frostmourne. His return to Lordaeron turns a protective prince into a threat to his own kingdom. Thrall's westward migration and the conflict around the Burning Legion eventually converge with the Night Elves' defense of their world. The final coalition does not simply destroy another symmetric enemy base: the defense of Hyjal creates time for Archimonde's defeat at the World Tree. [S30](#s30)

The Frozen Throne follows Maiev's pursuit of Illidan, Kael'thas's break with Garithos and alliance with Vashj, and the struggle around the threatened Lich King. Sylvanas establishes the Forsaken as a power distinct from the Scourge, while Arthas's weakening and later recovery make progression itself express the story. The finale's contest over access to Icecrown ends with Arthas taking the helm and merging with the Lich King. A transcriber's speculation that Illidan died is not adopted as canon. [S31](#s31)

**Interpretation:** controlling an actor, opposing that actor later and then seeing events from another faction's position creates continuity without requiring freeform branching dialogue. The player's tactical competence does not necessarily make the protagonist morally right. Authored objectives can make the player participate in a character's destructive logic, although this is not the same as permitting an alternative political choice.

Rexxar's smaller-party framing and the 2026 Forsaken campaign shift emphasis toward traveling with recognizable individuals and finding a place for them. The latter's prologue and newly free Undead community belong to a later expansion of this history; they are not evidence that the original campaigns depicted every subsequent relationship. The publisher's new story framing is distinguished from a personally completed playthrough. [S13](#s13) [S35](#s35)

## 8. Art, audio, interface and production

The original's readable silhouettes, stylized models, voiced acknowledgments, music and dramatic cinematics contribute to different scales of attachment: a recognizable combat role at a distance and a named personality during an authored scene. Kasavin praises that combination while still finding some portrait and presentation details uneven. A strong historical presentation judgment is not an assertion that it satisfies every later accessibility or display expectation. [S34](#s34)

Roper's interview explains a production correction: the earlier concept emphasized a closer camera, fewer units and more role-playing, before the team re-centered the strategy game. In-engine scenes required artists, designers and cinematic specialists to collaborate under performance constraints. He also describes events and side objectives as ways to add emotional and narrative context without simply reproducing an RPG grind. These are participant accounts of intent and iteration, not reverse-engineering of proprietary code. The page's displayed 2006 date conflicts with its explicitly pre-release interview context, so it is not relabeled as a reliably dated 2006 design decision. [S29](#s29)

The five reviews expose different expectations for the same visual change. PC Gamer finds Reforged's new detail at odds with some old animation and environmental presentation. TheSixthAxis is more receptive to the update while criticizing execution and missing features. CriticalHit reports interruptions and pathing frustrations alongside the underlying game's appeal. These are dated critical experiences, not independent proof of an undisclosed asset pipeline. [S36](#s36) [S37](#s37) [S38](#s38)

The 2026 presentation promise emphasizes preservation of classic readability alongside newer graphics. That intention does not certify every model's silhouette or performance in this research. Likewise, expanding selection from the original twelve-unit groups changes how much intent can be expressed at once, but it does not automatically fix every pathing, collision or command-feedback problem. [S12](#s12) [S13](#s13)

**Interpretation:** visual renovation and usability renovation are related but not equivalent. A clearer health bar or control group can improve decisions; a more detailed model can make a screenshot richer while making a moving battle harder to parse. A preservation project needs to decide which experience it preserves—not assume that more polygons and fewer restrictions invariably answer that question.

## 9. Release economics, distribution and ongoing support

Blizzard's July 22, 2002 press announcement distinguishes **more than one million copies sold worldwide** from **more than 4.4 million copies shipped to retailers**. The sales measure combined tracking data and company estimates from retail accounts; the publisher's fastest-selling claim was a historical assertion, not a present ranking. These figures are not concurrent players, profit or later Reforged purchases. [S39](#s39)

For The Frozen Throne, the July 2003 launch announcement identifies **2.5 million initial orders**, which must not be relabeled as consumers who had already played. The August 14 announcement reports **more than one million sold worldwide**, again with the company's measurement qualifications. These dated measures demonstrate scale without establishing lifetime retention or a causal explanation of success. [S40](#s40) [S44](#s44)

The business model moved through premium base game, expansion and remaster, with later subscription distribution and new paid campaign content. Blizzard announced Warcraft III Reforged's inclusion in PC Game Pass in June 2025. That is an entry date, not a promise of indefinite inclusion or automatic ownership of every future expansion. Fictional gold remains distinct from those real-world purchases. [S45](#s45) [S12](#s12)

The publisher's 2020 developer update acknowledges a difficult launch and describes repair work and planned features. The subsequent 2022 release of ranked systems supplies a concrete example of a later delivery, rather than permission to present every 2020 plan as shipped immediately. Maintenance can change technical access and save compatibility as well as balance. [S42](#s42) [S43](#s43)

**Interpretation:** multiplayer, editor-created experiences and recognizably shared characters provide plausible routes to repeated use and word of mouth. Public betas, expansion campaigns and anniversary announcements are documented promotion/support events. No channel-attribution percentages, current population, Forsaken Kingdom revenue or remaster profitability were established. Their absence is not evidence of commercial failure.

**Synthesis, not a prevalence claim:** the original game's lasting strengths and a remaster's shortcomings can coexist. These are different dimensions of reception, not an inconsistency that needs to be averaged away.

## 11. Transferable patterns and limits

**Make exploration compete with other valuable uses of time.** Camps provide rewards outside direct player conflict but expose the traveler to opportunity cost and ambush. A peaceful-world analogue would need its own credible scarcity and danger, not simply copied combat encounters.

**Preserve an actor without erasing consequences.** Hero revival keeps identity and a developing build while losing time and surrounding resources. It avoids permanent loss of the central actor, but can still make a defeat meaningful. The system needs clear limits so recovery does not become either trivial or a hopeless economic spiral.

**Use asymmetry to change decisions about familiar objects.** Militia workers, corpse logistics and walking production buildings are powerful because they recombine understandable roles. More faction-specific exceptions are not automatically better; they must create choices worth the learning burden.

**Make power relational.** An aura, a channeling protector, a dispel and a moving summon matter through other actors. Those relationships can create richer situations than isolated damage upgrades, provided the player can identify the source and counterplay.

**Keep automation inspectable and interruptible.** Autocasting reduces tedious work but can be exploited by an opponent. Players need understandable controls and feedback, not a choice between constant micromanagement and an opaque policy that cannot be changed.

**Treat authoring and preservation as product responsibilities.** Accessible example maps can teach an expressive system; compatibility failures can strand the resulting community work. A new capability is not a successful upgrade if the old creations can no longer be understood or used.

These are research interpretations, not accepted OpenLegend implementation tasks. Warcraft names, dialogue, characters and art are reference examples, not proposed reusable assets.
