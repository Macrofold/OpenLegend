# RuneScape: Dragonwilds — full research dossier

**G50 · Complete research pass, September 26, 2026.** Current scope is the released 1.0 game, with Early Access observations preserved as historical evidence. Inspiration below is not an accepted OpenLegend feature specification.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [RuneScape](runescape.md) · [Old School RuneScape](old-school-runescape.md)

## 1. Identity and release boundary

Dragonwilds is a survival-crafting adventure in RuneScape's universe, not a client or rules mode for either MMO. It asks a player to establish a home, acquire capabilities and confront dragons on Ashenfall. The recurring promise is that magic eventually transforms ordinary survival work, while recognizable materials, characters and rewards connect that work to RuneScape. [S3](#s3)

**Full release occurred September 15, 2026**, on PC, PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X|S and Nintendo Switch 2. Scorned Wilderness concludes the initial Kuldra Saga, not all planned development. Luminance is scheduled for December 2026 and remains future content. Ordinary play is solo or up to four players; dedicated servers separately support six. Crossplay is released, but crossplay is not evidence of unrestricted cross-save between console accounts. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2) [S5](#s5)

The April 2025 launch had nine skills; Farming, Fishing and Agility were subsequently added. Consequently the documented released progression contains twelve skill families. Roadmap references to Prayer must not be turned into a thirteenth currently playable skill. The March 2026 update increased skill caps to 99; reaching a cap does not imply every level contains an equally substantial unlock. [S2](#s2) [S4](#s4) [S9](#s9)

## 2. First session, established play and character identity

The first session teaches a practical cycle: gather basic materials, make tools, construct useful stations and shelter, and investigate increasingly dangerous places. An established player repeats the cycle at a different scale, provisioning expeditions and choosing upgrades rather than merely trying to survive the next encounter. Equipment and learned capabilities matter together; the game is neither a fixed-class RPG nor an MMO with the original RuneScape skill list transplanted intact. [R5](#r5)

The twelve families are Attack, Ranged, Magic, Woodcutting, Mining, Runecrafting, Artisan, Construction, Cooking, Farming, Fishing and Agility. Practice supplies experience; progression adds passive efficiencies and active Skill Spells. Appearance expresses identity, while tools, equipment and chosen activity determine practical roles. The producer describes a character as able to develop multiple competencies and carry progress across compatible worlds. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

**Interpretation:** one player can supply a group through gathering and production without being permanently assigned a crafter class. The design risk is that specialization becomes temporary busywork if every member rapidly learns everything and resources no longer create interesting cooperation.

## 3. Objects, materials and recipes form a dependency network

Wood, ore, hides, fibres, food, rune inputs and enemy drops have different origins. Tools and equipment then gate access to tougher materials or encounters. Processing stations turn those inputs into components, consumables and finished goods. Progression is therefore a network of prerequisites rather than a single experience bar. A discovered recipe is not necessarily immediately craftable: an ingredient, tool tier or station can remain missing. [R2](#r2) [R5](#r5)

The June 2026 crafting overhaul made workstation roles more explicit. Blacksmithing handles tools and melee/shield families; Fletching handles ranged equipment and ammunition; the Mystic Forge supports magical equipment and upgrades; the Armour Bench handles worn protection. The update returned resources from replaced stations rather than simply destroying prior investment. That is a concrete example of improving an economy's interface while preserving the player's material history. [S10](#s10)

Weapon and armour families support close combat, ranged attacks and magic. Named examples across later progression include Steel equipment, Green Dragonhide and Splitbark in Fellhollow, followed by higher-tier regional rewards. Repair, ammunition and provisions preserve some ongoing demand after initial crafting. Masterwork and Vestige equipment can be raised through Ascension rather than every distinctive old weapon becoming permanently obsolete. [S7](#s7) [S8](#s8)

**Interpretation:** a recipe chain only becomes a satisfying discovery when its prerequisites are legible. Hiding the source of an ordinary fibre is not equivalent to designing a mystery.

## 4. Magic changes work as well as damage

Skill Spells can alter gathering geometry, processing speed, mobility or survival. Axtral Projection is a useful early example: arranging oneself relative to several trees changes the value of one cast. Magic is therefore an additional action grammar, not merely a colourful combat projectile. It still requires the appropriate capability and inputs; the existence of a spell does not authorize arbitrary effects described in prose. [R1](#r1)

Runecrafting has an active-versus-automatic production distinction. Attuning provides a more involved route with additional returns, while automatic production allows attention to move elsewhere. The choice makes participation intensity a design variable. Rune availability, spell cooldowns and the work required to replenish inputs determine whether a convenience genuinely saves effort. [S4](#s4)

Farming supplies another example. Humidify creates a moving watering effect, while Uproot changes harvesting at area scale. Such spells are meaningful because crops, water, disease and tools already have ordinary rules. Without those underlying systems, the spell would be little more than a menu shortcut. [S6](#s6)

**Interpretation:** for OpenLegend, the transferable pattern is a power acting on shared world properties. The caution is that eliminating every logistical constraint can erase the very situations that make the power interesting.

## 5. Survival, rest and regional hazards

Food, water, fatigue and combat resources shape an expedition. Consuming supplies or seeking shelter interrupts the main activity; reviews disagree about whether that produces welcome tension or repetitive maintenance. Equipment durability adds another reason to plan a return. This dossier does not treat every Early Access drain rate or inventory complaint as a current 1.0 rule. [R2](#r2) [R5](#r5)

Housing gained a Coziness system around a claimed bed, protecting time spent at home from ordinary survival pressure. Palisade pieces and material-dependent structural health made defence more explicit. Visible wear on a damaged building should not be confused with proof that all structures passively decay. [S17](#s17)

Regional hazards ask for different preparations. Fellhollow's Withering and cleansing locations make exposure spatially meaningful. Umbral Sands introduces Sunscorch: sunlight accelerates water pressure, so shade, shelter and time of travel affect the route. A region is more distinct when it changes how players act, not only which texture covers the ground. [S7](#s7) [S11](#s11)

**Interpretation:** a need becomes a decision when several viable responses exist. A meter that simply forces the same click every few minutes is much less generative.

## 6. Farming and fishing: production with a return rhythm

Farming uses plots, seeds, water, crop development, disease and harvesting. Better plots and tools change reliability and productivity. Compost reclaims otherwise low-value organic material; clean and dirty water are not identical inputs. Crop development follows game time, including sleep advancement, rather than OSRS's real-world offline growing contract. [S6](#s6)

Fishing arrived April 21, 2026. The introductory net, later rods and appropriate locations create a progression route. Fishing Frenzy increases a node's supply, Infernal Rod cooks catches, and Fishnado gathers in a burst. Pristine fish can become decorative trophies rather than food; a catch can therefore have display value as well as survival value. [S18](#s18)

**Interpretation:** these systems provide quieter activities between encounters and let a supplier contribute to a group. Their limits are repetition and surplus: once everyone has more food than they need, another harvest needs a purpose beyond keeping an experience number moving.

## 7. Building, storage and persistent ownership

Snapping pieces, planning with blueprints and a free-camera construction ability support both practical shelter and ambitious structures. The attractive part is not merely a large menu of pieces: the camera and preview systems let the player reason about a build before spending materials. Reviewers nevertheless report confusion around structural support and roof coverage. [R3](#r3) [R5](#r5)

**Crafting and building from nearby chests shipped in August 2025.** A later review reporting storage friction must not be paraphrased as proof that the feature does not exist. The 1.0 Storage Ledger further changes unloading by filing matching inventory stacks into linked storage. Different interactions—supplying a recipe, depositing expedition loot and organizing a storeroom—need separate evaluation. [S15](#s15) [S2](#s2)

Creative and Custom worlds change the ownership contract. Creative play removes ordinary survival/resource barriers; Custom worlds expose adjustable rules. Characters and compatible world types are intentionally separated, so unrestricted creative acquisition does not simply become standard progression. Private chests, protected graves and building-ownership tools address griefing without assuming all co-op participants are equally trusted. [S14](#s14)

**Interpretation:** a shared house is simultaneously a creative artifact, a production interface and a permissions problem. OpenLegend's equivalent should distinguish permission to visit, consume, alter and retrieve rather than hide all of them behind one 'friend' flag.

## 8. Combat and the 1.0 control changes

Melee, ranged and magic support different distances, resources and equipment. Ordinary fighting uses attacks, movement, dodging and timing; gear suitability can matter as much as execution when entering a harder region. Enemy encounters and status threats give value to preparation, but this is not evidence of a general simulated predator ecology or tactical NPC memory. [R2](#r2) [R3](#r3)

Jagex's combat-development posts identify specific friction: unclear blocking, stamina costs, rooted casting, weak special attacks and controller selection. The released 1.0 changes allow dodge/block cancellation, introduce a dedicated special-attack resource, and make successful parries useful openings. Wands favour mobility over a staff's heavier damage. These changes materially qualify launch-era descriptions of rigid combat. They do not establish that every player or platform now finds combat responsive. [S13](#s13) [S2](#s2)

Ranged development includes crossbows, special ammunition, status effects and control spells. The September 2025 release is evidence of actual delivered tools, whereas announcements promising further Ranged attention remain plans. Spell choice, ammunition and equipment are a compositional loadout, not a rigid import of the MMO combat triangle. [S14](#s14) [S3](#s3)

## 9. Travel, exploration and the shape of the map

The world is authored rather than a newly generated map for every session. Landmarks, vaults, ruins and enemy-controlled places create repeatable geographical knowledge. Dungeon interactions include keys, mechanisms, hazards and encounters; finding a location is not the same as having the capability to finish it. [R5](#r5)

Lodestones and Law Runes turn earned resources into a player-shaped travel network. Vault rewards are therefore useful beyond the room in which they are found. The power to shorten a familiar journey can be a substantial reward, but travel conveniences still need an understandable discovery path. [R2](#r2)

Agility's July update adds courses, medals, movement abilities and shortcuts. Windstep moved into that skill rather than remaining under its previous owner; Recall provides a placed return point. These are version-sensitive details, not timeless launch rules. Terrorbird and carpet mounts offer other traversal styles, with the latter especially relevant to desert terrain. [S8](#s8) [S7](#s7) [S12](#s12)

**Interpretation:** movement progression is strongest when it changes which route the player chooses. Merely making a mandatory commute slightly faster is a weaker version of the same idea.

## 10. Death, recovery and configurable consequence

Failure can leave possessions at a gravestone and turn the next session segment into a retrieval expedition. A route that was manageable with equipment may be dangerous during recovery. The value lies in a new situation created by failure, not in erasing all learned progress. Historical reviewers also describe intentionally dying to escape irritating interruptions; that is a warning that a penalty can become the least troublesome option. [R1](#r1) [R2](#r2)

Protected graves and configurable friend access distinguish recovery from theft. Custom settings allow a different risk contract, while separate character/world compatibility protects standard progression from unannounced rule changes. Exact loss and difficulty settings should be read from the chosen world, not assumed from another person's review. [S14](#s14) [S16](#s16)

**Interpretation:** OpenLegend can use recovery to generate help, debt and companionship. It should not assume that punishment itself creates an interesting story. A corpse run with no meaningful choice is only a delay.

## 11. Story, NPCs and region progression

**Light-to-moderate campaign spoilers.** The early Dragon Slayer goal expands into a sequence of regional threats and the confrontation with Kuldra. The Wise Old Man helps orient progression; Fellhollow adds Death and Postie Pete, and later regions bring Black Knight and desert conflicts into the survival world. These are authored encounters and quest states, not evidence that the NPCs autonomously run a changing society. [S7](#s7) [S4](#s4) [S20](#s20)

Smaller quests connect familiar RuneScape humour to mechanical rewards. Cook's Assistant involves Slopfinger's cake ambitions; Animal Magnetism leads to an ammunition-recovery item; A Room With Garou asks the player to build for an elderly resident. Construction and provisioning can therefore participate in a story rather than only exist between combat quests. [S18](#s18)

The final Scorned Wilderness distinguishes a buildable hub from a raid area that forbids building. That is an important boundary: the game does not promise unrestricted construction as a solution to every authored boss encounter. [S2](#s2)

There is no verified generalized companion-party control, romance approval matrix, faction diplomacy simulator, full NPC work schedule or open-ended dialogue engine. Temporary helpers and particular NPC services should not be inflated into those systems. This absence is central to the OpenLegend comparison: a world populated by quest-givers is not yet an agent society.

## 12. Endgame, activity variety and economy

Later purpose comes from higher equipment tiers, special weapons, skill capes, building projects, repeatable encounters and collections. The Fight Caves and Nightmare Crucible provide different combat structures and rewards, while Ascension helps retain chosen equipment's relevance. Agility medals and trophy fish make achievement visible at home rather than only in a menu. [S12](#s12) [S8](#s8) [S18](#s18)

The in-world economy is primarily gathering, processing, consumption and group exchange, supplemented by specific NPC trading. Death's Soul Fragment shop is a bounded service, not a Grand Exchange or a universal simulated market. Material sinks include construction, upgrades and provisions; the same rare input may force a choice between personal advancement and shared infrastructure. [S7](#s7) [S10](#s10)

**Interpretation:** a cooperative game can have meaningful economic decisions without an auction house. Scarcity only remains useful if sharing is comprehensible and players can see what the group is saving for.

## 13. Multiplayer and persistence are separate contracts

Standard sessions support up to four players. Dedicated servers support six and can be self-hosted or rented. The official guide documents importing world saves and emphasizes backups for self-managed hosting. A dedicated world can remain available without the friend who normally hosts a session being present. That is a social-continuity capability, not merely a larger player count. [S1](#s1) [S5](#s5)

Character-carried progression and world-owned structures create different persistence scopes. Bringing an experienced character into a friend's world does not mean the friend's house is stored in that character. Likewise, released crossplay and invite codes establish group access across supported platforms, not independently verified transfer of every save between platform accounts. [S3](#s3) [S2](#s2)

Players can divide gathering, building, farming and expedition work, but the system does not require one exclusive role per person. Permissions and grave access become more important as a group expands beyond close friends. Public experiments mentioned in earlier roadmaps must not be represented as a released MMO-scale service. [S14](#s14) [S19](#s19)

## 14. Art, audio, interface and feel

The presentation retains recognizable RuneScape objects and humour in a more colourful, spatially detailed survival world. Firsthand criticism values the light, landmarks and construction views but also reports repetitive audio, unclear building feedback and terrain/interface defects. These are aesthetic and usability observations, not evidence about undisclosed engine internals. [R3](#r3)

Audio changed during Early Access. The September 2025 report describes new music by Mod Debris and Peter Michael Davidson, with audio work involving Mod Dodds and collaborators, covering different activity and time-of-day contexts. The team also adjusted the gaps between tracks. This is documented production iteration; no fresh listening session or video viewing is claimed here. [S16](#s16)

The crafting-station redesign and later radial controls illustrate progressive organization of growing complexity. Controller access should not be judged only by whether a controller is technically supported: switching ammunition, reading feedback and arranging a structure are distinct tasks. One player's comfortable building setup does not resolve another's inventory-navigation complaint. [S10](#s10) [S8](#s8) [P1](#p1)

## 15. Production, feedback and distribution

An April 2025 interview with executive producer Jesse America dates prototyping to 2021. It describes choosing an evolving survival game rather than a one-off AAA production and allowing players to develop several competencies. This is evidence of production intent, not proof that every later system was planned from the beginning. [S3](#s3)

The June roadmap acknowledges that growth outpaced parts of communication and support, and discusses expanding the team and co-development capacity. Later patches provide tangible evidence of iteration: workstation ownership changed, nearby-storage use shipped, new skills arrived and combat controls were revised. These are more informative than simply calling the game 'community driven'. [S19](#s19) [S10](#s10) [S15](#s15)

A community preference for the crossbow was followed by implementation, but that does not establish OSRS's formal content-poll constitution in Dragonwilds. Community streams, creator activity, public patch communication and player-build sharing are documented promotional surfaces. Their presence does not reveal what percentage of sales each produced. [S16](#s16)

## 16. Commercial context and sustainability

Jagex reports **more than 1.5 million Early Access copies sold** by the September 15, 2026 full launch. This is a publisher-reported cumulative unit figure, not active players, subscribers, concurrent users or profit. The press release's rounded '18 months' wording should not replace the actual April 2025 and September 2026 dates. [S1](#s1)

The business model is paid game access, with editions and platform-subscription distribution, rather than either MMO's membership economy. Launch distribution includes PC storefronts and console services; future physical availability and future updates are not current shipped entitlements by implication. No independently audited development budget, retention curve or profitability figure was established in this pass. [S1](#s1)

**Interpretation:** a successful paid launch creates an audience to support, not proof that indefinite service expansion finances itself. World continuity, platform certification and hosting expectations create obligations beyond adding another biome.

## 19. Worked interactions

The following are **constructed rules-based examples**, not observed footage or personal gameplay accounts.

### A. Turn gathering into a positioning problem

A builder needs wood for a planned structure. With the required spell and runes available, they look for a useful alignment of trees rather than selecting the nearest trunk, cast, collect and compare the yield with their remaining needs. The next choice is another cast, ordinary gathering or returning to build. **Limit:** input replenishment and follow-up processing can consume the convenience. The interesting transfer is spatial planning, not an automatic promise of lower total labour. [R1](#r1)

### B. Make a farm's waste part of its reliability

A group wants more predictable provisions. One player handles plots and water while another supplies compostable leftovers. Better care changes the expected harvest, freeing an expedition from some uncertainty. They then decide whether more land is useful or merely creates surplus. **Limit:** a productive farm can become an attendance obligation if recurring maintenance is not bounded. [S6](#s6)

### C. Use a quiet activity to create public identity

A fisher catches an exceptional specimen. Rather than consume every result, they preserve the suitable catch as a trophy and display it in the shared base. The activity produces both provisions and a visible account of what someone enjoys doing. **Limit:** the documented trophy records a collectible, not a complete simulated biography of the fish or fisher. [S18](#s18)

### D. A desert changes the departure decision

Before crossing exposed terrain, a player compares daylight, shelter opportunities and water reserves. They postpone, choose a shaded route or spend supplies to cross now. The destination can remain identical while the decision changes. **Limit:** if only one route and one departure time are viable, the hazard becomes a waiting tax rather than useful planning. [S11](#s11)

### E. Preserve a favourite weapon without freezing progression

A player prefers an earlier distinctive weapon but faces higher-tier encounters. They pursue the appropriate repeatable challenge for upgrade inputs, then use the supported Ascension route. The next choice is to improve that weapon or invest in a different capability. **Limit:** maintaining relevance should not flatten every item into the same final statistics and erase meaningful differences. [S8](#s8) [S12](#s12)

### F. Make rescue an explicit permission

A friend dies during a shared expedition. The group consults the world's grave-access rules rather than assuming anyone may take the contents. With permission, recovery can become cooperative; without it, the survivor can still help secure the route. **Limit:** trust in one context should not silently grant access to all storage and buildings. [S14](#s14)

### G. Turn an NPC request into a place

A player responds to the elderly Garou's request for a home. They gather, plan and build toward a named character's need rather than a generic resource quota. The resulting place gives the construction activity narrative context. **Limit:** a scripted housing quest does not establish ongoing autonomous occupancy, preferences or household simulation. Those would be additional OpenLegend capabilities. [S18](#s18)

## 20. Transferable inspiration and cautions

**Let powers operate on shared systems.** Gathering, crop care and traversal provide meaningful targets for magic. OpenLegend can generalize the interaction principle while keeping effects within inspectable world laws.

**Separate convenience from new agency.** A spell that creates another viable route or production plan offers more than a spell that removes a click. Some friction should simply be fixed rather than sold back as progression.

**Make progress discoverable.** Coherent material dependencies are insufficient when the next missing prerequisite has no sensible trail. Knowledge, tool access and recipe visibility should be distinguished.

**Treat co-op as ownership and availability.** More player slots do not solve permissions, absent hosts, shared projects or rescue rights. Explicit persistence scopes matter as much as combat synchronization.

**Preserve material investment during redesign.** Workstation migration and resource returns are a useful precedent for evolving a living game's rules without casually destroying prior work.

**Use local stories to motivate ordinary activities.** Building for a particular resident or supplying a named cook can make everyday mechanics memorable. Do not mistake a quest-giver for a fully simulated person.

**Do not copy every survival meter.** Autonomous agents change the cost of gathering and maintenance. Systems balanced around human click time need reassessment when labour can be delegated.
