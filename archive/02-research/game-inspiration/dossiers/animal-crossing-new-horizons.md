# Animal Crossing: New Horizons — full research dossier

**G03 · Full research pass completed September 25, 2026.** This covers the major systems and all R01–R14 research categories, with a coverage and preservation review below. It is not an exhaustive catalog of every furnishing, a source-code audit, or a claim of personally playing the game.

Read the [preserved earlier chapter](../games/animal-crossing-new-horizons.md), [assignment](../research-requirements.md) and [resume ledger](../research-progress.md). This is gameplay inspiration, not an OpenLegend specification or a request to reproduce Nintendo's assets. Version boundaries distinguish the 2020 release, subsequent free updates, Happy Home Paradise and the paid Switch 2 Edition. Progression rewards and later activities are discussed; this is not a spoiler-free purchasing guide.

## 1. A place worth developing, not a combat ladder

In Andrew Webster's March 2020 interview, director **Aya Kyogoku** explains that removing the established village made nature more central. **Nook Miles** also gave ordinary activities value beyond their sale price and helped newcomers discover goals. Producer **Hisashi Nogami** connects expanded fashion to seeing other players. These are the creators' intentions, not measured causes of success. [AC01](#ac01)

**Interpretation:** a self-chosen project can coexist with small supplied objectives. A player needs something approachable to do without the game deciding every long-term ambition. If selling the most valuable catch were the only recognized activity, many otherwise interesting discoveries would become economically interchangeable.

The launch-era Nintendo Wire review describes building up an island, placing buildings and outdoor furniture, and discovering a personal relationship with its initially unfamiliar geography. It also objects to limits on moving major fixed landmarks. [AC02](#ac02) **Interpretation:** authorship is not identical to unrestricted editing. A stable landmark can provide continuity or become an unwanted permanent decision; how clearly the game communicates that commitment matters.

The basic action families are gathering and catching, making and purchasing, placing and rearranging, investigating and collecting, talking and helping, and visiting or hosting. Their value depends on the player's purpose. A fish can be a sale, a missing museum specimen or an object to display; an empty area can be a route, a garden or a setting for meeting friends. Those are different uses of shared systems rather than separate campaigns requiring a new control language.

## 2. The current game is not the launch feature list

The official support history records **v3.0.0 in January 2026** and **v3.0.3 on April 29, 2026** in its US dating. In v3.0, players can craft up to ten items at once and use home-storage materials for DIY. That makes the old absence of batch crafting a historical criticism, not a current universal rule. The free v2.0 update had already introduced cooking, Brewster's café, Kapp'n tours and other systems in 2021. [AC03](#ac03)

The January 2026 announcement separates the free update for both Switch generations from a paid **Switch 2 Edition**. The latter includes display/control improvements, a microphone-using megaphone, and twelve-player online sessions when all participants have the required edition. Free Slumber Islands and the hotel must not be misrepresented as exclusive to purchasing that upgrade. [AC04](#ac04)

**Interpretation:** a reference library needs both historical criticism and the actual subsequent response. Silently deleting an old complaint loses useful production history; repeating it as a present defect misrepresents the game. A convenience change can remove repetitive execution while preserving decisions about what to make and why.

## 3. Crafting, handling and items that serve several projects

DIY records learned recipes in the NookPhone app and converts supported materials into objects. Cooking uses kitchenware rather than an ordinary DIY workbench, even when the recipes appear in the upgraded app. A recipe and a work location are distinct prerequisites. [AC06](#ac06)

**Worked interaction:** acquiring a recipe creates a reason to retain an otherwise saleable material. Building a workbench or using an available one lets the player turn that stored possibility into a usable furnishing. The January 2026 storage change removes a particular handling step; it does not eliminate the recipe or material requirement. [AC03](#ac03) [AC06](#ac06)

**Interpretation:** an inventory item can participate in several plans: immediate sale, a personal decoration, a gift, or another transformation. This is more useful design information than the number of item names alone. Conversely, gathering duplicates with no understood purpose can turn breadth into sorting work.

Tool wear is another constraint. The launch critic found repeated replacement disruptive even while enjoying personally crafting furnishings. [AC02](#ac02) This is an attributed experience, not a claim that every present tool has the same durability or that all readers should prefer unbreakable equipment.

### Food can change what a familiar tool does

A shovel digs and plants, but eating food also permits uprooting a grown tree or breaking a rock. When visiting another island, the documented shovel permission requires Best Friend status. [AC16](#ac16)

**Worked choice:** a player planning to move an established tree can prepare by eating first, then relocate it rather than waiting for a replacement to grow. The same strength can destroy a rock instead of merely harvesting it. The action's prerequisites therefore affect its consequence; a tool name alone does not fully specify the player's intention.

**Interpretation:** everyday preparation can support a meaningful new verb without becoming a severe survival meter. The corresponding danger is an unclear mode change: when the same input now destroys instead of harvests, feedback must make that state understandable.

### A catalog remembers acquisition without creating unlimited access

Nook Shopping records obtained items and lets the player reorder eligible ones. The catalog is not a guarantee that every recorded special or crafted object can simply be purchased again. [AC17](#ac17) **Interpretation:** knowing an object exists, having possessed it and being able to obtain another copy are distinct states. A useful creation library likewise needs to distinguish a reference, a reusable recipe and an available physical instance.

Custom-design tools add another expressive layer. **Custom Design Pro Editor+** extends the kinds of objects that can carry designs, including umbrellas, fans, flags and face-cutout standees. [AC18](#ac18) A pattern's meaning can change with its display surface. That is not arbitrary generation of new physical behavior; it is expressive variation inside supported forms.

## 4. Time, Nook Miles and a perishable object

Nook Miles recognize varied activities and can purchase conveniences, recipes, expressive options and travel opportunities. **Nook Miles+** supplies repeated smaller goals after the early progression requirement. This is distinct from Bells and the shop/loan economy. [AC19](#ac19)

**Interpretation:** a second reward channel can make a common action worthwhile when its sale price is low. It can also become a checklist that competes with self-chosen play. The design question is whether supplied goals reveal interesting possibilities or make an unoptimized afternoon feel irresponsible.

### Turnips connect the calendar to social access

**Daisy Mae** sells turnips on Sunday mornings. Their sale price varies afterward, and unsold turnips spoil at the following Sunday rollover. Players can sell on another person's island. Changing the clock backward can also spoil them; these are game rules, not a real-world investment model. [AC07](#ac07)

**Worked choice:** a player can accept a known return, wait for a better local price, or arrange a visit to another island. Waiting risks losing the opportunity entirely, and visiting adds a social/access requirement. A decision about an ordinary object becomes a decision about timing and participation.

**Counterexample:** spoilage does not make the item absolutely meaningless. Rotten turnips can attract ants. [AC07](#ac07) **Interpretation:** a failed economic plan can incidentally serve a collection goal. That does not make the financial loss desirable, but it demonstrates why one value measure should not automatically define every object's worth.

The broader calendar and actual clock also change the experience of returning, including the game's hourly and weather-sensitive music. [AC24](#ac24) A lived-in place can feel different at night without becoming a completely different map. For a player with a rigid real-world schedule, however, time-linked opportunities can constrain access rather than supply a pleasant rhythm. That is a design tension, not a finding that one pace suits everyone.

## 5. Social history, promises and bounded character routines

At sufficiently high friendship, villagers can give their photograph as a chance reward for appropriate interactions. In New Horizons these photos have individualized presentation; their rules differ from older games' goodbye-letter rewards. [AC08](#ac08)

**Interpretation:** a small object can make a relationship displayable in a personally arranged home. Its value need not be combat strength or sale price. The opportunity also exposes a tension: the player may cherish a particular friend or treat interactions as an efficient route to completing a photo collection. The same mechanic can support both readings.

### Friendship has specific inputs and consequences

The documented New Horizons friendship system responds to first daily conversation, suitable gifts, assistance and certain intrusive actions. Helping with a delivery, medicine or a flea problem is distinct from merely selecting the same conversation repeatedly. Clothing preferences can matter to gifting; stronger relationships permit further interactions such as changing greetings or obtaining a photograph. [AC09](#ac09)

**Worked interaction:** a villager asks for help delivering a package. Delivering it intact, opening it first and failing to deliver it are not necessarily equivalent social acts. The relationship rules distinguish that handling, and more than one person can be involved. [AC09](#ac09) **Interpretation:** an ordinary item can carry a social obligation. A simulated relationship is more interesting when it refers to what the actor actually did, not just a generic reward for any clicked interaction.

**Counterexample:** offering medicine without having it can disappoint a sick villager. [AC09](#ac09) The point is not to make every promise punitive. It is that the meaning of an offer depends on whether the player can carry it through. A helpful-sounding line and successful assistance are different events.

### Hobbies are not interchangeable with personality labels

New Horizons uses six hobbies—education, fashion, fitness, music, nature and play—alongside personality categories. A villager keeps its hobby rather than changing it as in some earlier games. Education can lead to examining a displayed fossil, flower or fish with a magnifying glass; fitness changes ordinary exercise behavior; music supplies another way of occupying a place. Special characters' hobby effects in Happy Home Paradise have a narrower scope. [AC10](#ac10)

**Interpretation:** a person can feel distinct because of what they do while the player is occupied elsewhere. The reference establishes bounded routines, not open-ended cognition or a real private life. It also warns against counting a familiar personality label as complete characterization: appearance, voice, preferences, activities and remembered events do different jobs.

## 6. Later creative modes separate experimentation from changing home

The free 2026 update adds **Kapp'n's family hotel**, with themed guest-room decoration and DIY requests earning **Hotel Tickets** for a separate souvenir shop. **Slumber Islands** let eligible online members create up to three islands separate from their main home and collaborate on their design. **Resetti's cleanup service** changes the cost of clearing an existing space. [AC04](#ac04)

**Interpretation:** these are three different solutions to renewed purpose. A requested room supplies a bounded creative problem; a separate dream island protects the existing home while experimenting; cleanup reduces the manual cost of changing one's mind. None is equivalent to automatically resetting the player's world.

The earlier **Happy Home Paradise** expansion supplies vacation-home design work and **Poki**, and lets players bring learned techniques and purchased items back home. It is a separate paid expansion or membership entitlement, not the free update. Losing subscription access without purchasing the expansion prevents further resort play, but the save remains available for later resumed access. [AC05](#ac05)

**Interpretation:** construction can be satisfying without requiring that every chair first be gathered through the same scarcity loop. A game can support both accumulated ownership and a less resource-constrained creative assignment, provided it explains which mode the player is in and what crosses the boundary.

## 7. Guidance, terraforming and a house that grows

Isabelle's evaluation combines **development** and **scenery** requirements; achieving a target requires meeting both. A three-star evaluation brings **K.K. Slider's** first performance and access to **Island Designer** progression. Furniture placed for display is treated differently from objects merely dropped on the ground. Bridges, inclines, vegetation and resident-related development participate in the evaluation. [AC11](#ac11)

**Worked choice:** a player can pursue the rating as a near-term goal, then decide whether to keep optimizing the metric or reshape the island to personal taste. A rating can expose useful activities without becoming the player's only standard of success. A deliberately rustic area can matter to its maker even when another arrangement would satisfy a hidden scoring condition more efficiently.

**Interpretation:** the important distinction is between guidance and authorship. A system can reveal possibilities and still leave room to stop optimizing. If every personal decision is interpreted as a numerical mistake, the decorating fantasy may collapse into threshold management.

### Editing terrain has prerequisites and exclusions

Island Designer initially provides basic paths; cliff construction and waterscaping require separate permits. Objects and water can prevent removing a cliff. Beaches, river mouths, the airport dock and Resident Services plaza are not freely reshaped by these tools. Hard and soft path types also interact differently with digging and plant spread. [AC20](#ac20)

**Worked interaction:** before removing a waterfall's supporting cliff, fill in the water, then alter the terrain. A layout is not merely a picture: the parts constrain one another. A path can also be a plant-management decision rather than decoration alone. These relationships reward understanding, but unexplained refusal can feel like input failure rather than a world rule.

The home has a separate expansion sequence. Paying the current loan permits another enlargement; expanding the house also expands storage. Once the house is fully enlarged, later storage upgrades use upfront payments. [AC13](#ac13) **Interpretation:** a bigger home is simultaneously more expressive space and a practical way to keep more potential projects. It is not merely a higher-level character portrait.

## 8. Collections give different objects different destinations

The museum turns donation into an accumulated public place. In New Horizons, the early donation sequence establishes Blathers's presence and the museum; later updates added art and the Roost. Fossils can be assessed in batches and donations submitted together, while Blathers's explanatory presentation provides a separate reason to inspect a single discovery. Filled exhibits change the museum's appearance. [AC12](#ac12)

**Interpretation:** selling an item, donating the first specimen and keeping it for display are different possible rewards. A collection is more than a count when the player can walk through its accumulated result. The institution connects otherwise unrelated activities—catching insects, fishing and finding fossils—without requiring every session to pursue the same one.

### Weather can create an invitation rather than only an obstacle

In New Horizons, the **Coelacanth** is an ocean catch associated with rain, not snowfall. [AC14](#ac14) **Worked situation:** a rainy day changes the value of going to the coast, even if the player had planned to decorate. Weather alters atmosphere and supplies a practical opportunity. Knowing where and under what conditions to look is progress that lives partly in the player's understanding.

The July 2020 update added wet-suit swimming and diving for sea creatures that can join the museum collection. [AC03](#ac03) **Interpretation:** a previously enclosing boundary becomes a place for another gathering activity. The change adds a new relation to the existing home rather than requiring a new combat tier.

### A flower is a longer experiment

The **blue-rose plant** is obtained through breeding rather than ordinary purchase. It can be relocated with a shovel, picked for blossoms, or damaged by running through it; v3.0 also permits storing the plant. [AC15](#ac15)

**Interpretation:** propagation, placement, harvesting and care do different jobs. A rare bloom can record patience or help from another player, while the living plant continues to occupy expressive space. An interesting collection system need not make every reward a stronger tool. The limit is discoverability: a complex breeding project may appeal to a specialist while being unnecessary for an ordinary satisfying garden.

### Art collection adds a different kind of inspection

**Redd's** New Horizons offerings can include multiple genuine works or none. Forgeries contain visible differences; the player can inspect before committing, but a counterfeit cannot be donated to the museum or sold normally to Timmy and Tommy. The game does not guarantee one correct answer in every visit's selection. [AC21](#ac21)

**Worked decision:** spending more time comparing an object can avoid a mistaken purchase, but deciding not to buy is also valid. This makes restraint and recognition part of collecting. It is a bounded authored authenticity puzzle, not a general simulation of art expertise or arbitrary visual inference.

## 9. Shared homes, live visits and protected copies

Nintendo documents one main island per console, even with multiple copies, and up to eight resident users. Four residents can play together on one system. Standard online/local visits support eight players; twelve-player online play requires all participants to use the Switch 2 Edition. The first player becomes **Resident Representative**, with specific early progression responsibilities that cannot simply be reassigned. Other residents can still contribute—for example, donate after the representative establishes Blathers. [AC22](#ac22)

**Interpretation:** sharing a world is not the same as equal authority over every milestone. This can support a common household project or frustrate someone who expected an independent town. A plain description of the permission model is more useful than either calling every resident powerless or attributing an unverified commercial motive to Nintendo.

Live visits are arranged through **Orville** at the airport, with host entry controls and ways to close the gate or end a session. Same-console play uses a leader/follower arrangement. [AC23](#ac23) The earlier shovel example shows that admission and permission to alter a place are distinct. A visitor can be welcome without receiving every destructive tool.

### Dreams and Slumber Islands are different promises

**Luna's Dream Addresses** permit asynchronous visits that do not alter the original island. They require the documented network/account access. [AC25](#ac25) Slumber Islands, described in §6, instead provide separate editable creative spaces with collaboration. Calling both “dreams” should not conceal the difference between viewing a protected copy and working on another persistent design.

**Interpretation:** sharing can preserve either experience, editability or continuity. A player who wants to show a treasured place may not want visitors changing it. A creator seeking collaborators needs a different permission model. One universal share button would hide that important distinction.

Nintendo also documents special island backup and transfer mechanisms rather than ordinary Save Data Cloud support. [AC22](#ac22) **Interpretation:** a promise of long-term attachment creates expectations about retaining a place through hardware change or loss. Persistence is part of the product experience, not just a storage implementation detail.

## 10. Art and audio make ordinary moments worth noticing

A CEDEC 2020 report of the art team's presentation explains selecting detail according to an object's gameplay role. Experiments with denser grass made removable weeds harder to see; the team restrained it. Villagers share a bone structure and much animation, while gaze and a brief hesitation before changing activity help suggest attention. Furniture research sought recognizable properties rather than indiscriminate realism. [AC26](#ac26)

**Interpretation:** fidelity and legibility are not a single slider. A background can become prettier and make play worse if actionable objects disappear into it. Likewise, instantly switching to every new stimulus can make an inhabitant seem mechanical even when its decision is correct. A small transition can preserve the impression of an ongoing intention.

The report also describes retaining room for player interpretation: excessive specification can crowd out projection, while too little information makes that projection tiring. [AC26](#ac26) This is an attributed design principle, not evidence that all players imagine identical personalities. A generated world could fail in either direction—overexplaining every state, or requiring the player to invent the significance of everything.

### Music leaves space for action and tells the time

In the inspected Gameplay podcast transcript, composer and educator **Scruffy** explains the music's restrained density, short looping hourly pieces and rain/snow variations. He discusses the unusual late-night instrumentation, suspended harmonies and the space left for informative effects such as rustling and falling objects. This is an independent musician's analysis, not a Nintendo developer describing the implementation. [AC24](#ac24)

**Interpretation:** ambient music need not compete with the player's small actions. A recurring theme can make a place recognizable while a different hour changes its emotional character. Leaving the harmony less conclusively resolved can suit an activity without one mandatory ending. That does not mean music can never become repetitive or that quietness is universally preferable.

The 2026 paid-edition critic praises clearer presentation but criticizes the limited upgrade and continued 30fps target. [AC27](#ac27) Visual quality, interface improvement and value for a paid upgrade are therefore separate judgments. Do not infer the rendering implementation from a reviewer's speculation about how an image looks.

## 11. Narrative lives in the place and its recurring people

**Interpretation of the documented systems:** Tom Nook's development requests, Isabelle's evaluation, Blathers's collection and K.K. Slider's performance give the early island a recognizable sequence. They do not need to dictate what every later day is about. The player can then build meaning around a villager, a room, a rare catch, a future letter or a regular visit.

This is different from a large branching campaign with a fixed protagonist arc. The same mechanics can become a story about collecting, hospitality, family compromise or patient experimentation. A relationship photograph or a displayed gift can serve as a visible reminder without the game generating a long biography for the object.

The direct player account from **Totalmiracle** describes writing letters to their future self and revisiting them. [AC28](#ac28) That is an attributed personal practice, not an official story quest. **Interpretation:** a simple persistent communication tool can connect in-game ritual with the player's own changing life. It gives time meaning without requiring the game to predict that life or fabricate a psychological model of the player.

## 12. Development and the work behind apparent simplicity

The developers traced the concept to work after New Leaf in 2012, before they knew the Switch hardware. [AC29](#ac29)

The CEDEC account identifies separate art-direction, character and furnishing work by **Koji Takahashi, Asako Shibata and Hiromi Sugimoto**. Its examples show iteration on representational detail and reusable character assets, not simply choosing a cute style once. [AC26](#ac26)

**Interpretation:** reuse and specificity can coexist. Shared movement infrastructure can support a large cast, while carefully selected visual and behavioral differences make individuals memorable. A pipeline that creates thousands of objects still requires judgment about which properties communicate their intended use. Neither a small-looking avatar nor a repeated animation proves cheap overall production.

The release history provides another production lesson: new activities, paid creative work, platform improvements and convenience changes arrived at different times. The 2021 statement that v2.0 was the final major free update is historical; the later delivered v3.0 supersedes it. [AC05](#ac05) This is a reason to preserve dated promises and actual subsequent delivery, not a reason to pretend the older statement never existed.

Public material inspected here explains design intent, art-production decisions and a substantial update history. It does not disclose a verified project-wide labor budget, proprietary architecture or the exact cost of any feature. Those unknowns should not be converted into unsupported claims that a similar game would be easy to build.

## 13. Marketing, distribution and reusable culture

The observed model combines a premium Nintendo-platform game, optional paid expansion/edition upgrades and membership-dependent online features. Free updates can supply new activity for existing owners while a hardware edition provides a separate buying occasion. The official documents distinguish those entitlements rather than presenting every improvement as a new purchase. [AC04](#ac04) [AC05](#ac05) [AC22](#ac22)

### A concrete external creation loop

Getty's **Animal Crossing Art Generator** supplied a way to crop an open-access artwork, turn it into a QR-compatible design and bring it into the game through NookLink. Contemporary reporting describes support for other institutions' compatible IIIF images and inviting players to share their results. The generator's JavaScript interface did not expose usable text in this retrieval, so the workflow is attributed to that reporting rather than claimed as an independently tested export. [AC30](#ac30)

**Interpretation:** this is a specific creator-consumer loop. An existing collection gains another use; a player gains a recognizable personal artifact; the result fits a place people already want to decorate and show. Lowering creation friction works here because there is an existing meaningful destination for the artifact. A generalized editor with no reason to display its output would not reproduce that loop.

### Promotion can connect a game activity with a real setting

Nintendo's 2026 aquarium-tour announcement describes Blathers-themed creature signage, photo opportunities, stamp activities, selected character appearances and chances to try the updated game. It connects that event with the current free update and Switch 2 Edition. [AC31](#ac31)

**Interpretation:** an aquarium is a relevant setting because collecting and encountering aquatic creatures already belong to the game's vocabulary. The promotion invites participation through a recognizable interest rather than only exposing a logo. Its existence does not establish how many attendees bought the game or whether the tour caused a particular sales increase.

The launch-era review's sense of an absorbing personal place, and the later players' return stories, help explain why screenshots, designs and island visits could be shareable. Those are plausible mechanisms, not measured virality coefficients. Pandemic timing is not by itself a controlled explanation of commercial success, and this dossier does not reduce the game's appeal to that circumstance.

### Commercial context with the right denominator

Nintendo's Switch software table reports **50.29 million life-to-date units as of June 30, 2026**, with its stated treatment of packaged, downloadable and bundled software. This confirms the earlier chapter's dated scale comparison; it is not active users, concurrency or title profit. [AC32](#ac32)

A player returning occasionally to renovate can be receiving continuing value from a premium purchase without appearing every day. Conversely, a long playtime does not establish satisfaction: someone may be enjoying a ritual or enduring a repetitive prerequisite. These interpretations require the person's account, not conversion of sales into assumed engagement.

## 15. Transferable mechanics and limits

**Several meanings for one thing.** A turnip can be a timed sale opportunity or attract an insect after spoilage. A photo can be a relationship keepsake; an acquired furnishing can be a reusable catalog option or a one-off object. Keep these roles separate instead of ranking everything by one number.

**Guidance that eventually releases control.** Miles, evaluations and the initial island sequence expose activities. The desired transition is toward a purpose the player wants to choose—not toward an empty screen with no reason to act. Some players still prefer ongoing supplied goals, as the reception demonstrates.

**A lived-in home is more than a storage hub.** Music, weather, routines, displays and correspondence can make quiet return meaningful. A world need not manufacture constant danger to justify persistence. It also should not assume that furniture and a friendship meter alone deliver this experience.

**Protect the version of the place that matters.** Live visits, protected dreams and separate collaborative spaces represent different permissions and consequences. Experimentation does not have to erase the home. Clear shared ownership matters especially when several people use one device or world.

**Remove manipulation, not authorship.** Batch crafting, storage-fed materials and cleanup can reduce repeated expression of an already chosen plan. The player still decides what to build, retain, display or give. An assistant that chooses all those purposes would remove a different kind of activity.

**Do not copy the whole contract.** Real-time calendars, constrained household ownership, fixed landmarks and probabilistic relationship rewards have costs as well as value. OpenLegend's inhabitants need not inherit them. The transferable standard is that a small action changes a place, relationship or future opportunity in a way a player can understand and care about.
