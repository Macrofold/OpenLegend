## Identity and central question

BioWare/EA's 2011 sequel follows **Hawke**, a voiced human protagonist whose family escapes Lothering and settles in Kirkwall. Race and social origin are more constrained than in Origins, while class, appearance, conversational tone and later decisions remain variable. Varric's retrospective narration frames the story. This is not a continuation in which the player directly controls the imported Grey Warden. [R1](#r1) [R5](#r5)

The design question is whether staying in one place across years can make relationships and consequences more meaningful than visiting a larger succession of places. Reviews strongly disagree: some value familiarity and politics; others find a static city, repetitive dungeons and a weak overarching goal. Both reactions need to remain visible.

## Friendship/rivalry is not merely a renamed approval score

The published guide describes a single axis with friendship and rivalry at opposite ends. Both can produce developed relationships, special abilities and loyalty; rivalry need not mean dislike. Thresholds interact with personal quests and explicit plot exceptions, so neither a high friendship score nor a high rivalry score guarantees every possible decision will be accepted. Romance likewise has eligibility, dialogue and quest requirements rather than being purchased by filling one bar. [M1](#m1)

Writer Jennifer Hepler explicitly connects this redesign to dissatisfaction with Origins' gift economy: strongly held convictions could be softened too easily by repeated gifts. Her production account also distinguishes character authorship, review of other writers' lines, voice sessions and shared discussion of inter-companion relationships. This is evidence of deliberate authored consistency, not a generative-memory system. [S1](#s1)

## Five independent written assessments

### Rich McCormick — PC Gamer, March 8, 2011

McCormick is exceptionally enthusiastic about the faster combat, distinctive ability animations, companion builds and the long-term emotional implications of choices. Familiarity with Kirkwall becomes an advantage for understanding its politics. He acknowledges initial disappointment with the limited geographical scope and is less interested in the surviving sibling than the other companions. His account is a personal evaluation; assertions about freely buying companion armour are not used instead of the actual equipment rules. [R1](#r1)

### Joseph Leray — Destructoid, March 22, 2011

Leray values flawed companions, political moments and the tactical potential of ability trees. He criticizes limited agency, uneven act pacing, recycled spaces and a city that changes too little across the time jumps. Camera and position-holding problems, plus reinforcement waves, undermine some tactical planning. This is an Xbox 360 review and its control experience is not silently generalized to PC. [R2](#r2)

### Kyle E. Miller — RPGFan, April 8, 2011

Miller recognizes customization, companion quests, voices and an unconventional premise, but finds an awkward compromise between action immediacy and tactical control. Repetitive environments, weak visual change across years and difficulty/interface frustrations reduce his enjoyment. His speculation about which audience BioWare wanted is not treated as verified production intent. [R3](#r3)

### Jason Schreier — WIRED, March 28, 2011

Schreier's Xbox 360 assessment is substantially more negative. Menial tasks, repetitive combat and backtracking dominate his experience, despite effective banter, voice work and occasional moving scenes. The critique is about his encounter with the launch game, not an independently tested current compatibility report. [R4](#r4)

### Kevin VanOrd — GameSpot, March 8, 2011

VanOrd values moral ambiguity, expressive characters, important decisions and energetic combat, but considers Origins the stronger game. He questions the loss or simplification of non-combat skills, companion equipment control and the PC tactical camera, and finds the main story insufficiently focused. His review carefully distinguishes controller and mouse/keyboard experiences. [R5](#r5)

## DLC reception already read

**Legacy:** GameSpot finds some improved arenas and useful environmental traps, but criticizes routine corridors, overt enemy spawning, command problems and bugs during the final fight. The story's link to Hawke's father does not by itself deliver the emotional weight the reviewer wanted. The page has migrated October 2013 metadata; it is a historical review of 2011 DLC, not a 2013 release claim. [D1](#d1)

**Mark of the Assassin:** GameSpot finds Tallis and the lighter heist tone more successful, with varied locations and social scenes. The stealth section adds vision cones, distraction and knockouts, but reset bugs and overlapping dialogue hurt execution. Tallis is a temporary guest, not a new permanent member of Hawke's Kirkwall party. [D2](#d2)
