# Dragon Age II — full research dossier

**G52 · Research pass completed September 26, 2026, with a documented Steam-sampling access gap (§17).** Scope: BioWare/EA's 2011 game, Legacy, Mark of the Assassin, The Exiled Prince and the smaller equipment/service additions. Interpretations are research proposals, not accepted OpenLegend requirements.

[Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Origins](dragon-age-origins.md)

## 1. Identity: a particular person in a particular city

The sequel follows **Hawke**, a voiced human whose family escapes Lothering and settles in Kirkwall. Class, appearance and choices remain variable, but race and starting circumstances are more constrained than Origins' six backgrounds. Varric's retrospective account frames the story. Importing an Origins save changes selected history; it does not make the Grey Warden the playable protagonist. [R1](#r1) [M3](#m3)

The central experiment is temporal rather than geographical breadth: revisit a city and its people across years. Familiarity can make political and personal changes meaningful. It can also make repeated caves, streets and encounters feel like missing content. The critics' disagreement is partly about whether the city changes enough to justify that structure, not merely whether a smaller map is inherently worse. [R2](#r2)

## 2. Creation, voice and remembered conversational style

A warrior, rogue or mage Hawke enters the same family story, with class affecting which sibling survives the opening. The creator also supports appearance choices; family appearance is adjusted to the protagonist. Imported decision states or supplied historical presets establish parts of the wider world. They are selected continuity flags, not a complete transferred world simulation. [M3](#m3)

The dialogue wheel distinguishes diplomatic/helpful, humorous/charming and aggressive/direct tendencies. Repeated choices influence Hawke's automatic delivery; act boundaries permit that tendency to evolve rather than permanently fixing a personality at creation. Investigation, lying, bribery, flirting and calling on a companion serve different purposes. A tone icon is not a universal good/evil score. [M2](#m2)

**Interpretation:** repeated expression can make a protagonist feel consistent without removing individual choices. The danger is surprising the player with automatic lines that imply stronger commitments than the earlier tone selections warranted. A generative version would need a transparent distinction between style, belief and authorization.

## 3. Attributes, abilities and progression

Experience yields level-up choices: attribute allocation and an ability point. Strength, Dexterity, Magic, Cunning, Willpower and Constitution remain distinct, with class-appropriate damage, defence, critical, resource and survivability roles. A bigger health pool, better avoidance and stronger mitigation are not the same improvement. [M4](#m4) [M5](#m5)

Abilities are organized into trees containing active powers, sustained modes, passives and upgrades. An upgrade can make an existing action interact differently with a status rather than simply add another hotbar button. Active powers consume resources and have cooldowns; sustained modes reserve part of the available pool. Prerequisites prevent immediately purchasing every desirable effect. [M6](#m6)

Hawke can specialize further. Warriors choose among Berserker, Reaver and Templar; rogues among Assassin, Duelist and Shadow; mages among Blood Mage, Force Mage and Spirit Healer. Companions possess distinctive personal trees rather than being interchangeable copies of Hawke. The class still sets real boundaries: this is not RuneScape-style universal skill acquisition. [M7](#m7) [M8](#m8) [M9](#m9)

## 4. Equipment, loot and a different crafting economy

Weapons, armour, accessories, runes, potions and poisons support different builds. Hawke has broad outfit selection, but companions retain characteristic armour that improves and accepts specific upgrades. Weapons and accessories allow some customization, with exceptions such as Varric's Bianca. Preserving a companion's silhouette reduces dress-up freedom while making the person easier to recognize. Replacing an inserted rune destroys the previous one rather than returning an intact reusable object. [M15](#m15)

Crafting is notably different from Origins. Discovering a resource establishes a **source**, not a stack that must be recollected for each order. Recipes, the required variety/number of sources and a crafter's fee determine what can be ordered. Exploration thus expands a production catalogue while money remains a repeat cost. It is not a detailed simulated supply chain whose source can be exhausted by NPC consumption. [M14](#m14)

Loot, merchants and quest rewards still drive repeated inventory comparison and selling. Quest objects and resource records are treated separately from ordinary backpack clutter. The useful decision is whether an item's effect supports the party; the less useful cost is repeatedly sorting interchangeable low-value drops. The manual's equipment rules take precedence over a review's loose description of buying new armour for every companion. [M15](#m15)

## 5. Combat: faster presentation, retained party decisions

Combat remains real-time with pause and direct control of party members. It is not a turn-based action-point system. Attacks are more immediate and visually forceful than Origins, but positioning, target selection, ability timing and preparation still matter. Controller and mouse/keyboard workflows differ, as does the absence of Origins' PC overhead camera. [R5](#r5)

Enemies have different tactical functions. A dangerous caster, an assassin and a supporting commander need not be handled in the same order. Crowd control, focused damage and threat management can make a composition effective without every group using one mandatory tank/healer formula. Strategy-guide recommendations are examples of viable planning, not engine-enforced party slots. [M10](#m10) [M13](#m13)

Reinforcement waves complicate that planning. A formation that accounts for visible enemies can be disrupted by later arrivals. Critics describe cases where this feels less like reacting to a plausible battlefield and more like being punished for the encounter's hidden script. That is a design warning, not evidence that every reinforcement encounter fails. [R2](#r2)

## 6. Tactics and cross-class combinations

The tactics editor exposes ordered conditions and actions. An actor can react to their own state, an ally or an enemy; priorities and suitable targets determine which action is attempted. Direct intervention remains available. This is explicit rule-based delegation, not a companion deriving combat policy from their dialogue personality. [M11](#m11)

Cross-class combinations give team composition a concrete grammar. Warrior abilities can create **Staggered**, rogues **Disoriented**, and mages **Brittle** states; qualifying upgraded abilities from other classes exploit them. The required upgrade, target susceptibility and actual state matter. A frozen animation is not a guarantee that every possible shatter interaction is available. Precise multipliers vary across reference versions and are not copied here. [M12](#m12)

**Interpretation:** a good delegation interface should expose the difference between a rule that did not match, a matched rule lacking resources, and a failed execution. Simply adding more conditions cannot repair navigation or targeting defects. The strongest transfer is readable cooperative preparation and exploitation, not the exact three status names.

## 7. Magic and personal combat identities

Mage schools include elemental damage, control, entropy, spirit, creation and protective effects. Force Mage emphasizes moving or disabling groups; Spirit Healer emphasizes recovery; Blood Mage changes the resource bargain by drawing on health. Choosing one mode can restrict other actions rather than only add benefits. [M9](#m9)

Companions make these boundaries personal. Anders' Vengeance tree links powerful aggression and supportive Panacea behavior to mutually constraining modes. Merrill's Dalish Pariah abilities connect her unusual magic to a different health/resource contract. They are distinctive authored kits, not evidence that the simulation invents new spells from a character's life history. [M9](#m9)

Warriors and rogues likewise have more than one role. A Reaver's damage-and-health tradeoffs differ from defensive sword-and-shield play; rogues can use archery, ambushes, debilitation and threat reduction. Stealth is a bounded combat/exploration ability, not proof of a general citywide awareness model. [M7](#m7) [M8](#m8)

## 8. Travel, day/night and non-combat interaction

Kirkwall's map offers day and night versions of locations, with different encounters and quest access. The surrounding Free Marches supply further destinations. These explicit selections are not a continuous simulation of all citizens' schedules. Returning to a familiar district can reveal another authored scene without implying that its unseen inhabitants have been living autonomous lives. [M16](#m16)

Rogues use Cunning thresholds for locks and traps. The system no longer presents the same broad standalone non-combat skill catalogue as Origins. Investigation and persuasion occur through authored dialogue opportunities; calling on a companion can provide a different solution. A restricted but specific alternative can be more expressive than a universal 'persuade' button, provided the player understands why that person helps. [M16](#m16) [M2](#m2)

**Interpretation:** explicit time slices are a production-efficient way to broaden a place. OpenLegend's stronger persistence ambitions would require reconciling what changes in the interval instead of assuming a menu transition constitutes lived time.

## 9. Companions, homes and ideological disagreement

The cast includes Varric, Aveline, Anders, Fenris, Merrill and Isabela, with Bethany or Carver and the DLC companion Sebastian under their own availability rules. Personal spaces and occupations give them anchors in Kirkwall: a guard, a healer and a tavern regular should not feel like identical recruits waiting in one camp. Their conversations and quests remain authored, however; residence is not a general household simulation. [M18](#m18)

**Friendship and rivalry are opposite ends of one axis.** Both can represent an important relationship, support personal developments and unlock distinct abilities. Rivalry is not simply 'this companion dislikes Hawke'. Thresholds, personal quests and decisive plot conditions all matter; reaching an extreme does not grant permission to override every conviction. [M1](#m1)

Anders, Fenris, Isabela and Merrill support romances under specific conditions. Friendship and rivalry can produce different routes; Sebastian's more restricted courtship is another exception. Flirting, intimacy and long-term commitment are not identical state changes. Varric and Aveline are not automatically romanceable merely because they are important companions. [M18](#m18)

**Interpretation:** respect, affection, agreement and dependency need not move together. A single axis is still a simplification, but allowing meaningful disagreement is a useful corrective to relationships that demand constant affirmation.

## 10. Story structure: fixed crises and branching participation

**Major spoilers.** The first act centers on establishing Hawke's position and funding a Deep Roads expedition. The stated investment gives money an early narrative purpose; alternative financing has later implications. Family and companion selection matter to the expedition's consequences. [J1](#j1)

A surviving sibling taken underground can contract the Blight. Anders' presence enables a route toward the Grey Wardens; without that intervention, survival is not assured. Leaving the sibling behind produces a different institutional future: Bethany enters the Circle, while Carver joins the templars. These routes all remove the sibling from the ordinary continuing party, but they do not mean the same thing or imply the person can never reappear. [J2](#j2)

Later acts escalate the Qunari crisis and the mage–templar conflict. Hawke can influence people and choose sides without preventing every catastrophe. Anders' decisive action against the Chantry is not simply toggled off by winning enough approval; the player's judgment afterward has its own consequences. Sebastian's refusal to accept sparing Anders is an explicit boundary rather than a generic relationship-score check. [M18](#m18) [J3](#j3)

**Interpretation:** agency need not mean control over every event. It does require distinguishing what the player can change from what the story will impose. A strong tragedy lets choices define loyalties and costs; an opaque one can make many hours of preparation feel irrelevant.

## 11. Failure, recovery, replay and absent systems

Combat knockouts leave injuries that reduce maximum health. Injury kits or returning home remove that penalty; restoring ordinary health is a different operation. A full-party defeat generally returns the player to a save rather than generating a continuing captured-party narrative. Difficulty can be changed, with Nightmare introducing friendly-fire considerations. [M17](#m17) [M3](#m3)

Replay comes from classes, dialogue tendencies, companions, relationship routes and decisions, not an endless procedural endgame. There is no cooperative base campaign, general farming/survival economy, freely constructed player city, or autonomous faction simulation. Hawke's improved home expresses social ascent but is not a survival game's building editor. Imports preserve selected continuity, not the entire state of Origins. These limits matter when comparing the game's strong character writing to OpenLegend's broader simulation goals.

## 12. Legacy: a weapon that is also a key to family history

Legacy takes Hawke outside the repeatedly visited city to investigate attacks tied to the family's blood. The prison and Corypheus connect personal history to a much older threat. Hawke's Key is a class-appropriate weapon and a mechanism for opening seals; progression through the module adds selected properties. The upgrade choice is constrained—one intended selection at a seal—not an invitation to treat exploit-based combinations as ordinary design. [D3](#d3)

The Larius/Janeka conflict changes the route through the prison and its interpretation. Companions bring contextual reactions; Anders' connection to the Wardens gives some events additional meaning. The module is not a separate canonical replacement for the main ending. [D3](#d3)

GameSpot praised some improved arenas and environmental traps but criticized routine corridors, overt spawning, command problems and final-battle bugs. A link to Hawke's father did not, for that reviewer, automatically create emotional weight. The review's migrated October 2013 date is not the DLC's release date. [D1](#d1)

**Transfer:** an item can carry both mechanical progression and narrative function. **Limit:** a supposedly personal excursion still needs meaningful character interaction, not only a bloodline explanation attached to a dungeon.

## 13. Mark of the Assassin: temporarily change the action vocabulary

Tallis joins Hawke for a heist around Château Haine. She is a guest for this module, not a permanent replacement in the Kirkwall roster. A wyvern hunt, social encounters and infiltration produce a lighter rhythm than the main campaign's political collapse. [D2](#d2)

The stealth section uses vision cones, distraction and knockouts, with a different set of available actions from ordinary party combat. The player can encounter alternative approaches rather than solve every room through their usual build. GameSpot found Tallis and the variation enjoyable but identified reset bugs and overlapping dialogue that undermined the intended scenes. [D2](#d2)

**Transfer:** a temporary constraint can make familiar characters reveal another side. **Limit:** removing well-developed abilities needs a clear fictional and mechanical reason; otherwise the expansion feels as though it invalidates the player's investment to stage its preferred solution.

## 14. The Exiled Prince, the Emporium and smaller additions

The Exiled Prince introduces Sebastian's revenge and religious/political conflict across several acts. Duty appears before the later recruitment and personal quests; completing the initial task does not immediately supply an unrestricted permanent companion. His distinctive archer role adds combat value, while his obligations create a consequential disagreement late in the main story. [D4](#d4) [M18](#m18)

The Black Emporium supplies unusual shopping and the Mirror of Transformation for revising Hawke's appearance. Its mabari summon is not simply another ordinary four-person-party recruit. The original new-copy/promotional delivery arrangements are historical distribution facts, not proof of today's availability on every platform. Item packs and promotional equipment provide build options but are not additional campaigns. [D5](#d5)

**Interpretation:** a character added across an existing campaign creates more integration obligations than a standalone challenge room. Scenes, late-game convictions and dialogue presence are part of the feature, not optional polish.

## 15. Production, art, audio and distribution

Writer Jennifer Hepler explicitly related friendship/rivalry to a weakness in Origins' gift economy: repeated gifts could undermine strongly held convictions. The developer interview describes character authorship, review of other writers' lines, discussions of relationships and voice sessions. That is evidence of deliberate consistency work, not a generative-memory mechanism. [S1](#s1)

The art and animation emphasize readable, exaggerated character action. Critics disagree about the result: energetic fighting and distinctive people coexist with repeated interiors, static-looking years and technical roughness. The relevant production lesson is not simply 'reuse is bad'; reuse needs sufficient contextual, visual or systemic change to support the time and consequence the story claims. [R3](#r3)

Composer Inon Zur's interview describes a more personal musical approach, including smaller string textures and regional influences. EA's contemporaneous announcement documents the reworking of Florence + the Machine's existing vocal material with a Dragon Age II arrangement. This was a soundtrack/marketing collaboration, not proof that the band created the game's entire score. No fresh audio audition is claimed in this research. [S2](#s2) [S3](#s3)

Launch reception should also be separated from later fixes. Accessible official patch notes describe restored Legacy banter and corrected save/relationship behaviors. Those notes demonstrate specific repairs, not that every complaint in a 2011 review remains current or that the modern game is universally bug-free. [S6](#s6)

In April 2011 BioWare reported over **one million units** since the March 8 launch, reached faster than Origins. The contemporary report does not settle a sell-in versus end-customer definition, so the figure is not relabelled as active users or audited sell-through. A cross-promotion offered eligible owners Mass Effect 2 for PC; that historical offer is not represented as available today. Current profit, budget and active-user numbers were not established. [S4](#s4)

## 18. Worked cross-system situations

These are constructed examples using documented rules, not personal gameplay or watched footage.

### A. Party selection changes a relative's future

Before the Deep Roads expedition, Hawke decides whether to bring the surviving sibling and Anders. A combat-composition choice changes the available response to the sibling's illness and therefore their later institutional identity. The next decision is not merely how to replace a lost damage dealer. **Limit:** the regular-party departure is largely fixed; the branching concerns survival and affiliation, so the interface should not imply unlimited control. [J2](#j2)

### B. A tactician turns two powers into a routine

A player assigns a mage to apply a qualifying Brittle effect and another class to exploit it. They check upgrades, resources and tactic priority, then retain manual intervention for resistant enemies. **Limit:** state detection and execution reliability matter more than the sophistication of the character's written personality. [M11](#m11) [M12](#m12)

### C. Exploration establishes production capacity

Hawke discovers another needed resource source, obtains a recipe and pays a supplier for a preparation before a difficult quest. Future orders reuse the discovered source rather than consume a newly gathered stack each time. **Limit:** this is an abstraction of access, not a fully simulated economic supply chain. [M14](#m14)

### D. Disagreement strengthens a relationship without erasing a boundary

Hawke consistently challenges a companion's outlook and develops a rivalry route. Personal quests and conversations make the connection consequential. A later act can still cross a non-negotiable line. **Limit:** rewarding both extremes may encourage players to optimize a meter instead of respond honestly; intermediate relationships need intelligible treatment too. [M1](#m1)

### E. Repeated humour becomes an expectation

The player repeatedly chooses humorous responses and later hears Hawke use that style automatically. A new act allows a change in tendency. **Limit:** a conversational habit should not silently become permission for a major insult, romance or political commitment. [M2](#m2)

### F. Upgrade a weapon by opening a historical prison

Legacy's weapon-key gains a selected property as Hawke advances through a seal. A build choice now records progress through a place connected to family history. **Limit:** the connection remains authored; it does not establish arbitrary object provenance or open-ended enchanting. [D3](#d3)

### G. A heist suspends the usual solution

Tallis and Hawke enter a section whose visibility and distraction rules differ from ordinary combat. The player learns which familiar abilities are unavailable and chooses a permitted route. **Limit:** a temporary mode needs clear entry/exit rules and reliable resets, or its novelty becomes frustration. [D2](#d2)

## 19. OpenLegend inspiration and counterexamples

**Disagreement need not be relationship failure.** Distinguish affection, agreement, respect and non-negotiable commitments. Rivalry is an instructive approximation, not the final ontology for agents.

**A place should earn its elapsed years.** Reusing geography can deepen memory if institutions, relationships and visible conditions change. A date jump without meaningful change undercuts the promise of a living world.

**Separate voice from belief and authority.** Remembering a style is different from deciding what the person believes or what they consent to. Automatic dialogue must not silently enlarge the player's commitments.

**Distinguish supply access from resource consumption.** Dragon Age II's crafting abstraction reduces repeated gathering. A simulation can intentionally choose a similar contract for background procurement, but should not pretend that it models physical inventory depletion.

**Make constrained agency honest.** A world may contain events no player can prevent. It should still make the consequences they can influence comprehensible and substantial.

**Keep social cognition and operational execution distinct.** Strong character writing, relationship state, combat policy and movement are separate owners. Failure in one should not be hidden behind the word 'AI'.
