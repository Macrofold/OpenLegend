# Star Wars: The Old Republic

**G194 · Windows PC · researched 2026-10-27.** This is a desk-research dossier, not a client test. **Checkpoint: incomplete** — production, commercial evidence, reception, external tools and final integration are still being researched; this file does not yet claim R01–15 completion.

## 1. Identity, playable promise and persistence

SWTOR combines an authored Star Wars role with a continuing online character: choose a side and personal history, speak in cinematic conversations, develop combat competence, gather companions, and enter shared activities. The eight origins are the Republic's Jedi Knight, Jedi Consular, Trooper and Smuggler, and the Empire's Sith Warrior, Sith Inquisitor, Bounty Hunter and Imperial Agent. Each supplies its own early story and companion ensemble. Legacy achievements and unlocks connect characters on a server; an alternate character can explore another perspective within the same wider investment. [S02]

Origin and fighting style are now separate decisions. BioWare explained the separation in 2021; the delivered 7.0 update made the former class the **Origin Story** and the former advanced class the **Combat Style**. Tech origins can choose among tech styles; Force origins begin with faction-aligned choices, with opposite-alignment access subject to unlocks. Loadouts preserve equipped items and ability arrangements. This supports more expressive casting without making every origin's story or weapon freely interchangeable. [S01, S03]

## 2. Actions, equipment and progression

Combat styles supply different weapons, abilities and group roles. The 7.0 ability tree adds selectable branches as levels rise; defaults let a character function before the player changes these choices. An ability preset is therefore distinct from the narrative identity and from the equipment supporting it. Later sections will distinguish current item acquisition, group roles and entitlement limits from this dated architectural change. [S03]

Companions add a chosen relationship and practical assistance. The 4.0 overhaul separated their combat effectiveness from worn equipment, made standard companions capable of switching tank/damage/healing roles outside combat, and replaced Affection with Influence. Both approval and disapproval can build Influence: the stated intention was to reward time together without forcing agreement for mechanical benefit. Story progression, rather than an affection threshold, opens the relevant conversations. These are authored characters and responses, not independent residents improvising their own goals. [S07]

Crew Skills turn companions into a remote production workforce. Gathering finds archaeological objects, biological materials, salvage or sliced data; crafting converts inputs into equipment, modifications and supplies; missions send a selected companion away for a stated reward. Orders can continue while the player adventures. The system permits up to three crew skills with at most one crafting profession, subject to account access. Influence improves crew performance. Selling a product is an option, but the existence of a recipe does not establish profitable demand. [S04]

## 3. Story, shared activity and time

Original class missions use voiced cinematic conversations and persistent choices. The official story guide distinguishes these nonrepeatable missions from later Knights of the Fallen Empire/Eternal Throne chapter replays: replaying another decision does not replace the character's original story state. A player can revisit a performance without rewriting every historical commitment. This describes the documented narrative structure, not a claim that every later mission uses identical presentation. [S09]

Flashpoints provide bounded group stories and combat situations. Selected story-mode Flashpoints can be played with a companion and combat-support droid; higher difficulties create a different social and tactical contract. Operations support groups of eight or sixteen, while compact Uprisings offer another repeatable group activity. Availability, difficulty and role requirements must be checked for the particular activity rather than inferred from one mode name. [S10, S02]

## 4. Native creation and social places

Strongholds are personal or guild spaces, not a freeform world editor. The official workflow starts at a directory: acquire a property for credits or Cartel Coins, open rooms, collect decoration unlocks, then enter Edit Mode. Select a compatible floor/wall/ceiling hook, choose an owned furnishing, nudge or rotate it, and swap eligible hook layouts to alter placement choices. Pickup permits revision. Decorations come from multiple game activities and purchases; showing an earned trophy can connect the room to a personal history. Invite a visitor, issue a key for later access, or publish the property on a public listing. [S06]

Jesse Sky's 2014 design account framed the house as both exhibition and useful legacy space. Its collection and prestige incentives also expose a design question: a score based on collection or occupancy need not identify a memorable place to visit. That is an inference about the stated design, not a current audit of the ranking formula. [S05]

## 5. Constructed situations

These are causal examples assembled from documented systems, not observed play sessions. Additional cases will complete the variation across combat, competition, production and return.

| Intent and conditions | Action, interaction, result and next decision |
| --- | --- |
| **Keep a character's conviction** | A player chooses a response a companion dislikes. Disapproval can still develop Influence under the documented companion system; the player need not answer only for approval. The authored response remains a story consequence, and the next decision is whether to continue this relationship or travel with another companion. [S07] |
| **Make a usable supply** | A player assigns a companion to obtain materials, then queues a suitable recipe while completing another activity. Elapsed production and consumed inputs produce an item, if the documented task succeeds. The player decides whether personal use or an eligible market sale justifies another cycle; the market price, not the recipe alone, determines the commercial result. [S04] |
| **Give a room an audience** | A player chooses a trophy and a compatible hook, revises the arrangement, then invites a friend. The visitor sees the owner's accumulated identity in a social setting. The next furnishing can follow the intended activity or atmosphere rather than the completion meter. [S06] |
