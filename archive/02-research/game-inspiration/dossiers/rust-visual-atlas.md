# Rust — inspected visual evidence atlas

[Canonical dossier](rust.md) · [Topic map](../synthesis/README.md)

**Inspection date: October 14, 2026. Scope: PC Rust, with historical images explicitly retained as historical.** This is an observation register, not a gallery of newly captured playtests. Every numbered asset below was opened and visually inspected. No video was watched, no Rust client was operated, and no screenshot was used to infer a measured completion time, accessibility result or server setting that it cannot show.

## What was actually inspected

**48 distinct visual assets:** 24 interface captures/crops or interface presentations, 14 construction/world frames, four annotated building diagrams, three contextual illustrations, one isolated item icon, one publisher statistics graphic, and one external building-planner view. These are **not 48 ordinary gameplay screenshots**. Angled publisher UI presentations, historical wiki crops and promotional world frames have different evidentiary value. Failed image requests are excluded from these counts.

Images remain with their publishers. Links are research/viewing references, not permission to reuse proprietary artwork in OpenLegend. Several pages reuse images from elsewhere; that does not provide independent corroboration. Publication dates below identify the containing page, not a verified executable build number. Each source link supplies context; each asset link opens the actual inspected image.

## Cross-screen UI/UX findings

**Expose the next valid action, not merely the object name.** V01, V02, V05 and V47 demonstrate a consistent contextual-command vocabulary. This can reduce the need to remember commands, but hold gestures and radial alternatives remain discoverability burdens. Distinguish ordinary actions from permission-changing ones and show consequences clearly.

**Translate resources into a decision horizon.** V03 and V09 are more useful together than either alone: a player can connect inventory stock, per-day consumption and remaining time. A further design question is whether the player can predict the new horizon *before* expanding. These images do not establish that Rust provides such a forecast.

**Separate geometry assistance from strategic difficulty.** V12–V16 show a small construction grammar supporting door behavior, protective layering and dense storage. Snapping can remove repeated alignment work without deciding what should be protected, where an entrance belongs or how much space to sacrifice. Ease of placement is not ease of security reasoning.

**Make ruleset selection part of onboarding.** V27–V28 and V39–V40 show that server identity, map choice and current raid permission can be surfaced at different stages. A good entry contract would also explain death loss, blueprint persistence, offline risk and team limits; the screenshots do not prove all of that is currently communicated well.

**Keep three inventories mentally separate.** The survivor's carried goods, a physical container's contents and the account's cosmetic entitlement catalog are different systems. V43 is the last of these. Confusing them produces false conclusions about what death erases or what a purchase grants.

**An evocative object model is not a usable production interface.** V20 and V30–V36 show readable machine/domestic forms. They cannot answer whether filtering, queuing, connection diagnosis, missing-material explanations or full-inventory recovery are smooth. The visual gap must stay explicit.

**Creator and commercial UI deserve separate evaluation.** V37 reduces cinematic authoring to familiar editing primitives; V41–V45 make purchases and expression discoverable. Neither demonstrates that ordinary survival play is fair, that the store is free of pressure, or that this UI caused revenue growth.

## Failed access and remaining capture gaps

Attempts to open the wiki's blocked-placement ghost, several Corrosion Hour server-detail/connection images, the older death/bag-limit panel, several 2019 chat/report captures, the gamever inventory/hover-loot captures, one post-processing comparison, the Harder Core weapon-looting image and two Gamers.Wiki placement/decay images returned timeout, cache-miss or internal-error responses. These were **not inspected** and are not counted.

The largest remaining gap is a complete, current, normal-PC **carried inventory → recipe detail → crafting queue → loot transfer → full inventory → armor swap** visual sequence. A complete contemporary **building-plan wheel → valid/blocked preview → hammer upgrade → correction window** sequence is also missing. We have a historical rotation crop and construction results, not an end-to-end capture. Current group-upkeep calculation, livestock needs, industrial filters, death/respawn selection, iron-sight combat and accessibility interaction also need direct current-client captures for high-confidence micro-UX conclusions.

Next evidence should be a small, version-recorded capture session on a disposable vanilla server, plus clearly labeled comparison captures from Softcore/Hardcore or a named modded server. Record the controls, server configuration and failure states; do not substitute another collection of attractive promotional frames. This is a research gap, not an assertion that Rust lacks those screens or that any playtest was performed here.
