Its older FAQ still says severed limbs were not added; that is obsolete relative to the current product description and the launch-era review's explicit lost-arm account. ## Five substantive written review bodies read

Four editorial reviews and one substantial independent player retrospective have been read through their arguments and conclusions. This is not described as five professional publications. Steam sampling is additional and separately identified.

- **Robert Zak, PC Gamer, January 8, 2019:** 84/100; the performed rescue of an enslaved companion gives meaningful continuity, while slow training/travel/food and multi-character inventory management burden play. Preserve the rescue's aftermath, including recognition and hiding, as the critic's account rather than our reproduction. [R1]
- **Simone Brown, GameGrin:** 7.5/10; values broad self-chosen roles and a distinctive world, but emphasizes onboarding and loading friction. The accessible article did not expose a reliable publication date, so none is invented. Its tested hardware/drive observations are historical, not a current benchmark. [R2]
- **Tim White, GameSkinny, December 11, 2018:** 9/10; values emergent purpose, squad delegation, lore and sound communicating danger. Notes crude visuals, rough movement and loading. The article's broad claims that everything works as intended are the reviewer's experience, not a reliability audit. [R3]
- **Emil Gunnarsson, Cultured Vultures, December 6, 2018:** 8/10; finds failures and gradually competent people unusually rewarding, and enjoys automated settlement work. Roughly forty hours included extensive mining downtime; wall placement, pathfinding and camera issues impose further friction. His preference for base-building is not a rule that everyone must build two settlements. His statement about gaining more health after healing is not adopted as a verified progression formula. [R4]
- **Zehnpae, patientgamers, June 2024:** a substantial mod-influenced retrospective values early vulnerability and base-building but dislikes micromanagement, pathfinding and diminishing late-game purpose. Replies dispute the perception of sparse lore and point to books, places and travel dialogue; the author acknowledges a construction-centered lens. Mod features and proposed Reactive World changes are not vanilla guarantees. [R5]

**Interpretation:** the shared tension is not simply difficulty. Being unable to win a fight can create a rescue, investment or travel decision. Being unable to tell why a worker stopped is a different kind of cost. The completed dossier should keep meaningful aftermath, repetitive training and interface/automation friction separate.

## Player and mechanics evidence acquired

The English helpful-all-time Steam positive surface was read. A detailed account by **Spyder, September 19, 2022**, makes a prosthetic procurement problem memorable: two right legs do not supply a needed left leg. This is attributed player testimony, not an independently tested whole scenario. Other positive anecdotes sometimes include mod-dependent activities; they cannot establish vanilla fishing or arbitrary world behavior. Multiple negative-only Steam routes failed; no negative-rated body has yet been read. [S1]

Tim White's three December 2018 guides on money, squads and combat were read. They support concrete production and job examples, but also contain broad or inaccurate prescriptions: mandatory sleep, universal paid recruitment, fixed investment thresholds and market claims must not be treated as authoritative rules. The intended full pass will cross-check the useful chains and explicitly qualify unsupported advice. [G1] [G2] [G3]

Chris Hunt's August 2015 interview was read in full. It documents early solo work supported by an overnight security job, direct alpha sales, Steam Early Access enabling a small team, injury-design intent and fan fiction/art. Its roster and planned features are historical, not current staffing or delivery claims. [P5]
