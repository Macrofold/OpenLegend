# Kenshi — survival gives people a shared history

**G36 · Full research pass · September 26, 2026.** Original Kenshi, not its unreleased prequel. Four editorial reviews and one substantial independent player retrospective were read in full; Steam sampling is additional. Negative-rated Steam retrieval failed and is not claimed complete. No gameplay, code experiment or footage playback performed.

## 1. Identity, scope and first-session promise

Kenshi is Lo-Fi Games' single-player, squad-controlled open-world RPG and settlement game. Its full release was December 6, 2018. The player is not a chosen hero in a level-scaled world: trading, fighting, scavenging, recruiting and establishing a home are possible projects rather than steps in one required campaign. The real-time world can be paused to issue orders. [P1] [R4] [P2]

Starting scenarios supply different initial predicaments. **Wanderer**, **The Slaves**, **The Holy Sword**, **The Wandering Trader**, **The Hive Exile**, **Rock Bottom**, **Guy with a Dog**, **Freedom Seekers**, **Cannibal Hunters** and **Nobodies** are not interchangeable difficulty labels. They vary the initial people, resources and situation. Almar's guide follows a recruiting/base-building path; its description of that path as inevitable is not a rule of the game. [A1]

Character creation establishes appearance and the bodies permitted by the selected start, while use develops competence. The human, Shek, Hive and robotic peoples are not a conventional mage/warrior/rogue class selection: occupation emerges from skills, equipment and player decisions. This pass does not reproduce a racial-multiplier table after dedicated wiki retrieval failed. The important supported consequences are differing bodily/equipment requirements, robotic repair, and the relationship between identity and the world encountered. The official description establishes customization and prostheses; the guides demonstrate particular recruits and equipment restrictions rather than a complete physiology reference. [P1] [G4] [A2]

There is no native multiplayer, spell system, standard romance progression or mandatory main-quest ending in this scope. A player's account of fishing, recruiting arbitrary prisoners or a dramatically expanded squad may involve mods. Those features must not be inferred from an entertaining story alone. [P2] [M1] [S1]

### Important corrections to older official material

Lo-Fi's old FAQ says severed limbs were not added. The current product page explicitly describes replacement prostheses, and the launch review recounts losing an arm. Likewise, the FAQ's unbounded-squad and prospective-editor wording is historical, not a reliable current specification. The **256 Recruitment Limit** creator documents a base recruit cap of 30 and explains the mod's larger limits and compatibility risks. The official patch archive includes the shipped Forgotten Construction Set, ending in the retrieved archive with Kenshi 1.0.55/FCS 2.12; that archive endpoint is not proof of the version installed on every distribution. [P1] [P2] [R4] [M1] [P6]

The official Kenshi 2 landing page still offers only a signup and says Lo-Fi will share more “when the time comes”; no release date is announced. Lo-Fi's September 2026 hiring activity confirms ongoing studio work but is not a release schedule. No prequel mechanics, engine promises or staffing implications are attributed to original Kenshi. [P4]

## 2. The action, body and equipment inventory

### Individual capability and group control

The principal attributes are **Strength, Dexterity, Toughness and Perception**. Skills cover weapon families, melee attack/defense, ranged operation, stealth, lockpicking, thievery, assassination, athletics, swimming, medicine, robotics, engineering, science, farming, cooking, labor and smithing. These are developed through relevant activity rather than a universal experience-level purchase screen. A fighter, runner, medic, thief and smith can consequently be the same person's history or different squad specializations. [G4] [P1]

Combat involves selecting targets, positioning and choosing behavioral settings rather than executing a manual sword combo. **Block, Passive, Ranged and Taunt** influence participation; a passive medic is not invulnerable. Training devices supply bounded practice, while surviving actual danger can develop Toughness. Treat guide recommendations about ideal training victims as strategies, not assurances that a beating is safe. [G3]

The physical condition display separates the head, chest, stomach, four limbs and blood. Unconsciousness, bleeding and impaired movement are not the same state as death. A living casualty can be carried, treated or protected; irreversible loss is also possible. Hunt's design explanation emphasizes that winning a fight need not end the danger when wounds continue to matter. [G4] [P5]

Food, medical kits, repair kits and crossbow ammunition produce different supply requirements. Nearby members can share food from an appropriate squad backpack when the relevant behavior is enabled; that is not teleportation of supplies across the map. First aid and robotic repair are distinct provisions. Beds assist recovery, but an old guide's prescribed eight-hour sleep routine is not adopted as a native fatigue requirement. [A3] [G2]

### Weapons, armor and carrying choices

The melee families include **katanas, sabres, hackers, heavy weapons, blunt weapons and polearms**. Named contrasts include the light Guardless Katana, defensive Foreign Sabre, robot-oriented Paladin's Cross, heavy Falling Sun and long Fragment Axe. Quality ranges from scavenged/rusted grades through named manufactured grades to **Meitou** examples. Weapon weight, reach, damage profile and situational modifiers matter alongside the user's capability; a guide's preferred weapon is not universally optimal. [A4]

Armor is similarly more than a single rating. Light, medium and heavy clothing trade protection, coverage, skill effects and environmental resistance. **Dustcoats**, **Crab Armour**, **Samurai Legplates**, **Leather Turtlenecks**, **Iron Hats** and **Wooden Sandals** illustrate different purposes. A high-quality item does not erase a movement or combat penalty. The cited equipment guide mixes useful item comparisons with debatable rankings; its assertion that full leg coverage makes all boots useless is not adopted. [A5]

Inventory has both spatial and weight constraints. A backpack expands carrying possibilities but changes burden; giving every character separate supplies increases management work. Carrying a casualty makes an existing carrier more important and may force a different load or route. The earlier granular study preserves that group-level implication without inventing a precise carrying formula. [G4] [R1]

**Interpretation:** the body model earns its complexity when a local injury changes several choices—who fights, who carries, what gets left behind and where the group can safely rest. Merely adding more wound labels would not guarantee that effect.

## 3. Crime, trade and the cost of moving value

Ownership matters when looting or taking an item from a town. Stealth, theft, lockpicking, disguise and escape make different routes through an encounter possible. A successful theft is not the same as clearing the item's history or making every buyer accept it. Current patch history includes disguise fixes; old stealth generalizations should therefore retain their version context. [A3] [P6]

Currency is **Cats**. Mining and selling ore supplies a relatively legible early livelihood, while scavenging depends on surviving or reaching someone else's fight. Production can turn raw resources into more useful goods, but time spent repeating an efficient routine may dominate the fiction of becoming a trader. The money guide's income targets are advice, not universal price guarantees. [G1]

Regional trade adds a second layer: a profitable item at the destination is cargo and exposure during the journey. Almar describes buying **Sake and Hashish around Shark in the Swamp**, carrying goods toward **Flats Lagoon**, and choosing return cargo such as repair supplies according to local prices. Pack capacity, guards, hostile animals and legal context complicate the margin. These are fictional goods and a player-authored route, not a universal fixed-price arbitrage contract. [A6]

Purchased town buildings offer shelter and room for research without establishing a remote outpost. Specialized destinations can then give trade and exploration purpose: **Armor King**, the **Scraphouse** and **Black Scratch's Great Library** are different sources of equipment or knowledge. The guide's reports about stock and long waits are observations, not a guarantee of identical stock in every save. [A2]

**Interpretation:** earned money is useful because it can buy a prosthesis, medicine, help or the next productive capability. When it becomes only a larger balance, the player may need a new project rather than another repetitive income loop.

## 4. Research, farming, industry and delegation

Research benches, books and prerequisite technologies connect a town workshop to a larger production system. Ordinary books do not replace **Ancient Science Books, Engineering Research or AI Cores**. In an April 2024 discussion, experienced mod author Shidan distinguishes labs, tech labs, post-ancient workshops, libraries and armories: visiting many ruins is not equivalent to searching the category that contains the missing resource. [G5]

Production spans building materials, metal processing, textiles, food, weapons, armor, electrical components and robotics. A concrete food chain is **wheatstraw → flour → bread**, then combining bread and cactus into **Dustwiches**. A grain silo, water, oven, cooking station, crop suitability and labor connect the chain. A powered automatic silo changes the labor requirement rather than eliminating all inputs. Stone processing supplies construction materials; iron refining supplies plates; more advanced work requires additional research and resources. [A7]

A separate textile example is **cotton → fabric → clothing**, with stations and skill affecting what can be made. Production for sale competes with food, defense and the limited labor of the squad. It is not an abstract crafting button that ignores where inputs and workers are located. [G1]

Persistent jobs can be assigned with shift-click interactions and ordered priorities; one-shot commands and continuing work are different. Mining, hauling, research, crafting, farming and medical duties can support an expedition elsewhere. However, a valid purpose still requires reachable inputs, appropriate storage, inventory room and a workable job order. The older granular study's January 2026 player thread preserves a particularly useful failure: general storage did not substitute for the ore-specific storage the requester needed. Conflicting replies are retained as advice, not reverse-engineered scheduler documentation. [G2] [Earlier automation case](../mechanics/kenshi-injury-rescue-and-the-cost-of-delegation.md)

Site choice links fertility, water, power, resource deposits, access and hostile neighbors. Fences, walls, gates, turrets, storage and buildings are not purely decorative. A defended productive outpost can enable travel; it can also become a responsibility that absorbs the player's attention. Brown's review specifically connects prospecting and territory with this planning burden. [R2]

**Interpretation:** home, workshop and expedition are complementary only when delegation works well enough. A worker stopping because there is nowhere valid to put ore is an execution problem. Deciding whether ore is still worth producing is a different, strategic problem. A good interface should not force the player to confuse them.

## 5. People, factions, places and continuing consequences

Recruitment adds both capability and overhead. **Beep, Hobbs, Ruka, Kang, Rane the Giant, Griffin, Bard, Crumblejon and Shryke** are examples of named people encountered through distinct places and accounts, not identical generated job tokens. Some have distinctive dialogue; their history also comes from what the player does with them. A hireable mercenary group is a time-limited protective contract, not automatically a permanent member of the player's squad. [A7] [A8] [A9] [R4]

Pack bulls and garru create transport roles; other animals can become companions or threats. Animal age can change capability, so an adult and elder of a familiar creature are not necessarily equivalent encounters. Beak Things can hit nearby participants with sweeping attacks, making position matter even when several characters are not the current target. This does not make a hired escort a fail-safe against every creature. [A7]

The world includes the **Holy Nation, United Cities, Shek Kingdom, Hives, Flotsam Ninjas, Tech Hunters, slavers and anti-slavers**, alongside bandits and dangerous wildlife. The earlier chapter's point is that establishing a home places the group among other powers. Territorial safety is not established merely by finding fertile ground. The Cannibal Hunters account demonstrates a concrete interaction: another roaming group or a Flotsam outpost can alter an escape, without having been created as the player's quest helper. [P1] [A10] [R5]

**World-state changes are bounded, authored systems.** In a September 2025 discussion, Shidan explains that key people being alive, dead or imprisoned can select town ownership, population and related states. The resulting change need not mean an autonomous army physically marched across the map to conquer the town. A mod that recruits an important NPC can leave different conditions than killing or imprisoning them. This matters when interpreting “living world” language: dynamic encounters, conditional town changes and a fully simulated strategic war are different claims. [M2]

There is substantial environmental and historical discovery without a mandatory plot itinerary. Readers encounter fragments of earlier civilizations, books, ruins, regional cultures and travel dialogue. The 2024 retrospective's replies specifically challenge a base-focused reading that found little lore. That disagreement is preserved: the available story can be deep in an activity a particular player rarely pursues. [R5]

**Optional late-game destination spoilers:** the **Bugmaster**, **Cat-Lon in the Ashlands**, major faction leaders, unique weapons and the **Crab Tournament** can supply ambitious goals. These are possible projects, not a required final-boss checklist or proof that defeating one leader resolves every institution or injustice in the fiction. One consulted endgame guide misplaces the Anti-Slavers' home; that geographic claim is excluded. [A11]

## 6. Eight worked situations

The following are **constructed illustrations of documented rules**, except where a review or player account is explicitly attributed. They preserve earlier examples rather than claiming new play tests.

### A. Winning the escape still leaves a rescue

**Attributed account:** Robert Zak describes escaping after a companion is captured, earning money, hiring mercenaries and returning for the captive. Recognition and the freed person's status then force the pair to hide. **Next decision:** where can they safely go now? **Limit:** this is the critic's particular chain of events, not a guaranteed scripted sequence. The rescue matters because its consequences survive the immediate fight. [R1]

### B. The correct item category is not enough

**Attributed account:** Spyder's September 2022 Steam review describes acquiring two right robotic legs when a left one is needed. Later injuries complicate the group further. **Interaction:** equipment compatibility creates a specific procurement problem rather than generic healing. **Next decision:** seek the matching part while transporting the casualty. **Limit:** the narrated sequence is not independently reproduced, and displayed playtime is not assumed to be time at publication. [S1]

### C. A weak fighter trains without making death useful

**Intent:** develop a novice. **Conditions:** a recoverable encounter, medical supplies and somebody able to intervene. **Actions:** participate in combat, monitor injuries and withdraw or recover the fallen. **Result:** surviving activity can improve competence. **Next decision:** increase risk or recover. **Limit:** “losing teaches” is not “every loss is beneficial”; bleeding, capture or a predator can terminate the opportunity. Efficient training can also become repetitive labor. [G3]

### D. Turn an idle worker into a legible dependency problem

**Intent:** keep mining while explorers travel. **Conditions:** a continuing mining job but no suitable output destination. **Actions:** inspect the current task, provide the correct researched storage and ensure it is reachable. **Result:** hauling can release the blocked output. **Next decision:** whether the stock target still justifies labor. **Limit:** the Holy Nation guide's simple example does not exclude other failures from distance, inventory or priority; the earlier thread explicitly raises these alternatives. [A9]

### E. Make research an expedition motive

**Intent:** unlock a technology requiring an unusual research item. **Conditions:** ordinary books are insufficient. **Actions:** identify the relevant ruin category, prepare for its guards and locks, then retrieve and deliver the material. **Result:** exploration changes the productive possibilities at home. **Next decision:** build the new capability or pursue another bottleneck. **Limit:** counting generic ruins visited does not guarantee finding an AI Core or engineering document. [G5]

### F. Earn a trade margin without forgetting the journey

**Intent:** sell regional goods where they are more valuable. **Conditions:** capital, carrying capacity and a destination buyer. **Actions:** buy in the Swamp, plan the route and move a guarded load toward Flats Lagoon. **Result:** an economic opportunity becomes a navigation and protection problem. **Next decision:** reinvest, choose return goods or reduce exposure. **Limit:** local demand, vendor funds and survival can defeat the apparent margin; no fixed profit is promised. [A6]

### G. Alter a town without inventing a simulated war

**Intent:** change a faction's control. **Conditions:** a relevant authored world-state condition. **Actions:** kill or capture the key person through the available systems and later observe the resulting state. **Result:** local ownership or population can change. **Next decision:** deal with the new situation. **Limit:** the rule does not establish an off-screen strategic campaign; recruiting the person through a mod can also leave different trigger conditions. [M2]

### H. Escape by recognizing that other people have enemies

**Intent:** survive the Cannibal Hunters opening. **Conditions:** immediate pursuit and nearby hostile groups or a defensible outpost. **Actions:** inspect encumbrance, choose an escape direction and exploit an encounter between pursuers and another group. **Result:** conflict not centered on the player can create a window. **Next decision:** hide, treat wounds or rescue the second survivor. **Limit:** the guide's optimistic guard-rescue language is not a guarantee of safety, and capture may have a short fatal aftermath. [A10]

## 7. Presentation, control and accessibility

Hunt's 2015 explanation combines wandering-ronin and wasteland influences with a world of its own rather than literal historical Japan. Ramshackle equipment, large empty distances and vulnerable bodies establish a particular social imagination before the player understands the numbers. That is a documented influence; the conclusion that it helps make survival feel lonely is an interpretation, not measured audience response. [P5]

Tim White praises the sound of wind and combat as information about an otherwise austere landscape. A distant fight can be noticed through sound before the camera centers it. The same review acknowledges primitive-looking movement and terrain. No composer attribution, music implementation detail or accessibility equivalence is invented from that account. [R3]

Brown and Gunnarsson describe loading pauses, camera obstruction, wall placement and pathfinding friction. These are historical experiences on their reviewed systems, not fresh performance measurements. The official archive contains later fixes to font scaling, crafting inputs, save locations, disguise and display options; that prevents treating every old defect as unchanged, without claiming the updates solved all friction. [R2] [R4] [P6]

Pause and speed controls give time to inspect a complicated situation. They do not by themselves make dense inventories, small labels, distant actors or color-coded states accessible. No screen reader, assistive input, controller setup or hardware benchmark was tested. The important tradeoff is between a view that communicates a large squad and one that keeps a particular person's needs legible. [G4]

## 8. Production, distribution and commercial evidence

Chris Hunt's August 2015 interview describes early solo development supported by overnight security work, direct alpha sales, then Steam Early Access enabling a small team. Public availability brought both financial runway and pressure to keep the game usable while adding systems. The named team and planned work are historical, not a current staffing audit. He also reports fan fiction and artwork, showing that players were already supplying cultural meaning beyond the executable. [P5]

Lo-Fi's **May 26, 2026 “3 Million Copies Sold”** announcement is a primary cumulative-sales milestone. It thanks players, creators and modders and points to community events and a Beep plush campaign. The referenced preorder ended June 25; its continued appearance on the page does not make it an active offer in September. The announcement is not an audited profit statement or an estimate of current active players. [P3]

The product is a paid standalone game rather than a required subscription service. Lo-Fi's page links Steam, Humble and a demo route; the demo was not downloaded or verified as running. Steam's age gate prevented a reliable current price/review-total capture in this pass. No current dollar amount, conversion rate, development budget or revenue estimate is fabricated. [P1]

**Interpretation:** the distinctive pitch is a recognizable predicament—be nobody, survive, become capable—not merely a list of engine systems. Videos, stories, memorable companions and mods are plausible ways to communicate it, while the developer's milestone thanks do not quantify which channel caused purchases. The long development story is evidence of persistence and financing constraints, not a repeatable recipe for every ambitious sandbox.

Modding is part of continued participation, but mod support does not turn Kenshi into a natively multiplayer or unlimited-population product. The recruitment-limit author explicitly warns about performance and overlapping changes. The patch archive's FCS work establishes maintained authoring tools, not compatibility of every workshop combination. [M1] [P6]

## 9. Five written reviews and player disagreement

These are **four editorial reviews plus one substantial independent player retrospective**, not five professional publications. Their substantive bodies and conclusions were read. Scores are attributed historical judgments, not this dossier's verdict or a survey.

**Robert Zak — PC Gamer, January 8, 2019, 84/100.** His rescue account gives personal stakes to performed actions. Freedom and eventual achievement coexist with slow training, travel, food production and cumbersome inventories. The positive story does not cancel the routine burden; both help explain whom this game suits. [R1]

**Simone Brown — GameGrin, 7.5/10; publication date not reliably exposed in the retrieved page.** Brown values self-chosen roles, prospecting and an unusual world but finds entry demanding and loading disruptive. Moving between storage configurations did not erase every pause in that reviewed experience. Those hardware observations are historical rather than a present benchmark. [R2]

**Tim White — GameSkinny, December 11, 2018, 9/10.** White values emergent goals, delegation, lore and audio communicating danger. Crude visuals and loading are part of the reservation. His claim that the systems worked as intended reflects his session, not an exhaustive reliability audit or proof that another player's automation problem is imagined. [R3]

**Emil Gunnarsson — Cultured Vultures, December 6, 2018, 8/10.** Gunnarsson enjoys watching initially weak people become competent and an automated settlement come together. In approximately forty hours, a substantial portion was mining downtime. Placement, camera and pathfinding problems add friction. His base-building preference is not a universal progression requirement; his casual health-growth description is not adopted as a numerical rule. [R4]

**Zehnpae — patientgamers, June 2024.** This long, mod-influenced retrospective praises early vulnerability and construction but dislikes micromanagement and fading purpose as power grows. Replies dispute its sense of thin lore, pointing to books, places and dialogue; the author acknowledges a base-focused approach. This is useful disagreement about activities pursued, not a settled diagnosis of the entire vanilla endgame. [R5]

### Steam sampling and access limits

The English **Most Helpful (All Time)** positive surface was read. Alongside Spyder's prosthesis account, **Deargbleidd, December 9, 2021**, recounts enslavement, learning and escape as the source of enjoyment. These selected stories suggest what individuals remember, not how often a mechanic produces satisfaction. Reviews containing mod-dependent occupations are not used as vanilla inventories. [S1]

Multiple negative-only and filtered Steam routes returned retrieval errors. **No negative-rated Steam body is claimed read.** The player retrospective and editorial reservations provide contrasting evidence without pretending to fill that missing sample. The additional Grimdark review was blocked, and RPS was inaccessible; neither is counted among the five. Search snippets or promotional blurbs were not substituted for full criticism.

**Synthesis:** distinguish consequential adversity, repetitive training, unclear feedback, technical interruption and chosen-playstyle fit. A player can love the first and reject the next three. Someone bored with a secure base may need visible alternatives; someone whose worker cannot find storage needs an explanation, not another enemy raid.

## 10. Transferable patterns and limits — interpretation

**Aftermath can create agency.** A rescue, rehabilitation or equipment mismatch creates another decision after the initial loss. Borrow the continuing consequence, not the assumption that more suffering is automatically interesting.

**People become specific through shared dependence.** A medic or carrier matters because a plan depends on them. Dialogue can acknowledge that history, but merely generating grateful prose does not create equivalent consequences.

**Home should enable more than maintenance.** Production and research can fund and motivate expeditions. Automation must make accepted routines dependable without removing the strategic choice of what is worth doing.

**Separate conditional change from comprehensive simulation.** World-state rules can generate significant consequences without simulating every army, institution and motive. Honest boundaries are more useful than an expansive “living world” promise.

**Preserve several kinds of purpose.** Trading, exploration, rescue, collecting, construction and faction conflict need not become one optimization path. Do not automatically copy the pacing, bleakness, protected fiction or technical friction along with the useful relationships.

## 11. Coverage, preservation and viewing

### September 26 requirements-and-corrections audit

A fresh pass against the assignment's explicit mechanics inventory confirms coverage and records the meaningful absences:

- **Identity / classes / attributes / skills / leveling / trees:** starts, appearance, peoples, Strength/Dexterity/Toughness/Perception and use-grown combat/civil skills are covered in §§1–2. There is no fixed class picker or universal character-level/perk tree.
- **Items / inventory / weapons / armor / equipment / crafting / upgrading:** spatial/weight inventory, melee/ranged families, armor, prosthetics, food/medical/repair/ammo supplies, crafted equipment and quality grades are covered in §§2–4.
- **Magic / spells / powers:** absent natively; extraordinary capability comes from training, gear, prosthetics, squad composition and world knowledge rather than spellcasting.
- **Traversal / environmental and object interaction:** walking/running/swimming, carrying casualties, stealth/crime interactions, doors/locks, town buildings, caravans and large-map travel are covered across §§2–5.
- **Activities / minigames:** trading, mining, crafting, research, farming, base planning, scavenging, exploration, crime, bounty-like goals and combat are integrated activities rather than separate arcade minigames.
- **Combat / stealth / loot / rewards:** squad combat, Block/Passive/Ranged/Taunt, stealth/theft/disguise/assassination, scavenging, unique equipment and research loot are covered in §§2–5.
- **Death / failure / recovery / economy / trading:** body-part injuries, blood loss, unconsciousness/death, capture, first aid/robot repair, beds/prosthetics, Cats and regional trade are covered in §§2–4.
- **Story / relationships / romance / reputation / party / companions:** named recruits, squad dependence, hireable mercenaries, faction relations and emergent shared histories are covered in §§5–6. Native romance/marriage and a mandatory protagonist quest arc are absent.
- **NPC/AI schedules / factions:** factions, town states, roaming squads, jobs/automation and authored conditional world-state changes are substantial; the dossier explicitly distinguishes them from a fully simulated strategic war or unrestricted autonomous society (§§4–5).
- **World map / environment / quests / events:** the seamless world, ruins, factions, raids/encounters and optional destinations supply projects without a conventional required main-quest itinerary (§5).
- **Building / settlements / management:** research, farms, industry, storage, walls, gates, turrets, housing and job priorities make outpost management a major pillar (§4).
- **Multiplayer / social / endgame / return loops:** original Kenshi is **single-player**; mods/community stories are external social layers. Optional faction conflict, unique enemies, exploration, production and player-defined settlement goals substitute for a mandatory authored endgame (§§1, 5, 8–9).

The release/commercial audit also rechecks three easily conflated boundaries: Lo-Fi's patch archive currently ends at **Kenshi 1.0.55 / FCS 2.12**; the May 26, 2026 primary milestone says **more than three million copies**; and Kenshi 2 remains an unreleased future title with no date on its official signup page. None of those facts is used to imply current active-user counts, Kenshi 2 mechanics or continued Kenshi 1 feature development. [P3][P4][P6]
