## Reception evidence already read

### Fraser Brown — PC Gamer, September 21, 2017

[Full review](https://www.pcgamer.com/divinity-original-sin-2-review/). Brown values experiments that the game accommodates rather than treats as invalid inputs: teleportation, unusual builds and quest consequences. He describes conversations that retrospectively give a killed NPC a social identity. His caveats are the hard-to-parse journal, intimidating encounters, and anxiety that one quest decision might close another. His enthusiasm for origin stories and companions is not a consensus claim. His reported cooperative mischief also demonstrates that one participant's freedom can disrupt another's conversation.

### Patrick Hancock — Destructoid, October 16, 2017

[Full review](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-divinity-original-sin-2/). Hancock praises branching side quests, earlier engagement with the main story, spoken performances and unrestricted cooperative movement. He criticizes repetitive armor-removal followed by crowd control, encounter foreknowledge, inventory transfers for trading, and conversations selecting an unsuitable party member. His suggestion that balanced damage is necessary is a reviewer interpretation, not a universal build restriction. His launch-version bug and balance observations must not be presented as a September 2026 technical audit.

### Jerry Williams — RPGFan, November 7, 2017

[Full review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/divinity-original-sin-ii/). Williams finds investigation and tactical customization rewarding, but objects to obscure quest progression, awkward clicking and early technical/AI problems. He is less emotionally connected to the companions than Brown, despite appreciating their writing and performances. His discussion distinguishes enjoyable moment-to-moment play from the emotional effect of the larger story. Suspected causes of slowdown in his review remain his interpretation; this research did not inspect Larian's implementation.

### Daniel Cunningham — Geek to Geek Media, February 17, 2020

[Full review](https://geektogeekmedia.com/geekery/divinity-original-sin-2-review/). Cunningham emphasizes alternative escape routes, environment-sensitive combat, race reactions and companion backstories. His criticisms concern the effort of locating inventory objects and insufficiently informative quest descriptions. This later assessment is useful alongside launch criticism: improved or enduring reception cannot be inferred solely from release reviews. He also warns readers about the game's body-horror imagery. The article's then-current platform and price information is historical, not a present availability check.

## Initial interpretation

The clearest research question is not whether more freedom is always better. It is how alternative actions retain understandable consequences when party members, quest actors, objects and combat states interact. The reviews identify both successful improvisation and information-management costs. A proposed adaptation would therefore need to preserve readable goals, explain consequential state changes, and support different group pacing; copying the number of available spells would not address those dependencies.
