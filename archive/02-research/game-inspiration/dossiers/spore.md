# Spore — full research dossier

**G16 · In progress, September 26, 2026.** This substantive checkpoint covers the five-stage structure and selected embodiment, social, city and terraforming interactions. Remaining: complete editor/ability constraints, vehicle/city and Space progression breadth, Galactic Adventures/pack distinctions, sharing and preservation, fuller art/audio/production/promotion, contrasting direct reception and R01–R14 review. [Preserved chapter](../games/spore.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md).

This is Maxis/EA's 2008 Spore, not an unrelated earlier game with the same name, a scientific evolution simulator or an automatically generated general-intelligence world. Early demonstrations, unused editors and mods do not establish released behavior. No personal playthrough or complete video inspection is claimed.

## 1. Five activities share the history of one created species

EA describes **Cell, Creature, Tribe, Civilization and Space** as five stages, with editors for creatures, buildings, vehicles and spacecraft also usable as creative activities. It explicitly calls the game **single-player**: shared creations can appear in separate players' galaxies without those players jointly controlling one authoritative world. [SP01](#sp01)

**Interpretation:** the continuity is partly the recognizable species and the consequences of how it developed. It is not an unchanging action system scaled up five times. A body that was the centre of direct control becomes a unit in a group, then the inhabitants of a city and finally an empire's identity. The changing scale can create awe while leaving a player who preferred an earlier activity behind.

This distinction is essential for studying success or disappointment. A highly enjoyable creature editor does not prove that its later strategy game is deep. Conversely, a deliberately simple battle system does not erase the pleasure of seeing a self-authored species inhabit the result.

## 2. Cell: body parts change which interactions are possible

The starting **Jaw** supports meat and biting, while **Filter Mouth** supports plant consumption. A **Proboscis** adds another feeding method. Food contributes DNA for editing and growth; larger predators can become manageable as the player's scale changes. Parts can be acquired through supported encounters and meteor fragments. The actual diet history contributes to the stage's consequence, rather than only the last visible mouth determining every future outcome. [SP02](#sp02)

**Constructed interaction:** add defensive spikes near the directions from which predators approach, but retain a practical feeding arrangement. More protection can make food harder to reach when the geometry obstructs it. The design is meaningful because contact and placement participate in the activity, not only because the editor produces a distinctive silhouette.

**Proboscis** is not simply every mouth at once: its ability to feed from a living cell does not mean it can eat the resulting loose meat after that cell dies. [SP03](#sp03)

**Constructed counterexample:** acquire a seemingly broader eating capability, kill prey using another part, then discover that the remains require a different mouth. The object's exact action matters more than the broad label omnivore. A help system should clarify that distinction instead of treating a plausible player inference as an impossible request.

**Interpretation:** a small set of anatomical affordances can generate useful variation. This does not make the game a literal account of Darwinian evolution: the human deliberately selects parts and goals. The creature's development is an expressive progression system with biological imagery.

## 3. Creature: anatomy supplies a vocabulary, but not arbitrary biomechanics

On land, the player can pursue combat or social approaches, collect parts, return to reproduce/edit and form a pack. Social actions include **Sing, Dance, Charm and Pose**; supported mouths, feet, accessories and hands supply those capabilities. Attacks and movement capabilities have their own part relationships. [SP04](#sp04)

**Constructed situation:** meet a species whose social display includes a gesture the current body performs poorly. Return with a suitable part, bring helpful pack members or pursue another encounter instead. A mouth can participate in both survival and communication, while a hand can support expressive behavior beyond combat.

**Interpretation:** the appearance, animation and rated strength of an action are related but not identical. A creature with many hands need not receive unlimited independent actions. Likewise, a component positioned as decoration does not necessarily create a newly simulated attack direction. The final full pass checks those limits rather than accepting the advertising phrase anything you can imagine as a literal rule.

The body also carries voice. Mouth families use different sound sets; the **Creepy & Cute** pack adds further mouth styles rather than a wholly new evolutionary stage. [SP03](#sp03) A recognizably different creature can therefore be distinguished through sound and movement as well as its static shape.

### Affiliation can survive as a later capability

A **Social** Creature-stage result supplies later benefits such as **Fireworks** in Tribal play, a diplomatic effect during Civilization and **Pleasing Performance** for colonies in Space. More generally, consequence abilities depend on earlier play and carry into subsequent stages; starting directly at a later stage does not automatically award the missing history. [SP05](#sp05) [SP06](#sp06)

**Interpretation:** history can be compressed into a usable rule. The game does not need to replay every encounter for a later society to retain a trace of its earlier orientation. The limitation is that a categorical reward is not equivalent to remembering a particular ally, promise or individual relationship.

## 4. Tribal: the body stops changing while tools and responsibilities expand

The Tribal stage shifts to controlling a group. Biological editing ends, while an outfitter changes accessories and their bonuses. Food becomes a shared resource for sustenance, reproduction, tool buildings and gifts. Each member holds at most one tool, and tool-hut availability/space limits prevent installing every possible role at once. Other tribes can be allied with or destroyed. [SP07](#sp07)

**Constructed choice:** send a band to establish an alliance while retaining enough workers and defenders to sustain the home. Equipping every member for the same task can simplify one encounter but leave another need unmet. The useful constraint is not a larger inventory spreadsheet; it is a reason for different group members to contribute differently.

### Musical cooperation is an actual action sequence

**Didgeridoos, Wooden Horns and Maracas** answer different musical requests during a social encounter. A tribe member needs the corresponding tool to perform that role. **Stone Axes** favor attacking creatures, **Flaming Torches** structures, **Healing Rods** recovery and **Gathering Canes** appropriate food collection. [SP08](#sp08)

**Constructed interaction:** assemble the instruments a negotiation may require instead of sending an undifferentiated crowd. A visually large band missing the requested capability is not equivalent to a smaller suitable one. A healing tool also changes what a group can recover from without becoming the best attack tool.

**Interpretation:** a peaceful approach still has prerequisites, preparation and execution. That supplies more agency than a dialogue option that automatically succeeds because it is labelled friendly. However, repeating the same call-and-response pattern can expose its simplicity; the player may experience either a charming ritual or a mechanical gate.

The historical Tribal reference describes mourning and idle activity, but its broader claim that use teaches general autonomous temperament is not adopted as a demonstrated learning system. Bounded routines and expressive animation should not be mistaken for open-ended cognition.

## 5. Civilization: acquisition method and political relationship diverge

The goal is to control the planet's cities through **Military, Religious or Economic** approaches. Tribal history influences the first city's specialization, but a starting specialization and the eventual consequence result are distinct. Relationships respond to trade, compliments, threats and incursions. [SP09](#sp09)

Economic acquisition needs a compatible route, sufficiently good relations, trade progress and an acceptable buyout offer. It is not an instantaneous purchase of any city on the map. [SP10](#sp10)

**Constructed choice:** invest in a trade relationship while another civilization threatens the target, or redirect resources to protect the route. A peaceful plan still competes over space and time. The existence of a buyout route does not imply that every non-player civilization performs the same economic strategy as the human.

**Interpretation:** several methods can reach a common objective without being morally or mechanically identical. Yet if the ultimate activity always collapses into filling one control bar over every city, the different names may promise more variation than the player experiences. The full dossier retains that reception tension rather than declaring all approaches equally deep.

## 6. Space: changing a planet requires more than changing its appearance

Terraforming uses atmosphere and temperature to change **T-score**, affecting habitability and colony capacity. Plants and creatures stabilize supported ecosystem levels. Painting a planet or changing its visible geography does not by itself supply that habitability. [SP11](#sp11)

**Constructed sequence:** bring a hostile planet into a usable climatic range, then establish the needed living layers instead of assuming the climate tool completed the whole project. Introducing a predator before its supported food structure exists is a different action from building a stable biosphere.

The **Grox** create a useful exception: they require conditions ordinary species cannot inhabit. Improving habitability for the usual life forms can therefore harm Grox settlements. [SP11](#sp11)

**Interpretation:** one operation's benefit depends on whom it affects. A tool called an improvement is not universally benevolent. This is a strong inspiration for differently embodied inhabitants, provided the difference actually changes their survival and choices rather than only their portrait.

It remains a deliberately bounded game model. A few food-web slots and a two-axis climate display are not a predictive ecological science system. Their value is the readable connection among an intervention, who can inhabit the place and what the player can do next.
