## Written criticism now read

Five independently authored review bodies have been read, plus a sixth substantial hands-on article. These are not score-aggregator excerpts.

- **Josh Harmon, EGM, Wii U:** enjoys populated locales and multistage problems but observes that the obvious answer often arrives before an imaginative one. His orphan/parents and adjective-based removal of an obstacle are actual reported play examples; the hypothetical tiny jetpack should not be relabeled a verified solution. Publication date was not exposed in the retrieved body. [R1]
- **James Archer, PC Gamer, March 4, 2013:** finds easy small tasks and hidden acceptance rules frustrating, while larger missions allow meaningful alternatives. His glacier/hammer failure and parallel-Maxwell incident are attributed accounts, not reproduced tests. [R2]
- **Tom Mc Shea, GameSpot, November 29, 2012:** values absurd experiments and bonus riddles, but criticizes simple tasks and plausible solutions that are rejected. Unlimited's loose story progression need not require visiting every area. [R3]
- **Ron DelVillano, Nintendo Life, November 28, 2012:** praises Wii U controls, playful art/music and editing, while finding the supporting multiplayer limited. This does not establish PC multiplayer or an equal notebook for every Wii U participant. [R4]
- **Bryan Rose, Nintendo World Report, October 8, 2018:** enjoys Mega Pack's relaxed play while criticizing its console text entry. His procedurally generated mission complaints concern Unmasked, not Unlimited. [R5]
- **Alex Cocilova, PCWorld, December 6, 2012:** a substantial first-person article, labeled News rather than a scored review. Describes a confetti-throwing edited character and a Santa-like pie, appreciates editor usability and finds the main objectives simple. The article's loose sibling count is not used over the primary product description. [R6]

## Main distinction to retain

**Interpretation:** expressive play can be enjoyable without demanding an optimal solution. That does not excuse a mismatch between the apparent goal and the recognized result. The two issues—too little challenge and opaque acceptance—need separate treatment. The existing toxic-waste account remains a valuable example but does not reveal the game's exact internal predicate.

The current PC store explicitly lists object creation, reusable library and Steam Workshop; user tags do not establish platform mode support. Its captured release date is November 19, 2012.
