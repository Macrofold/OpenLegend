# G91 — Final Fantasy XIV

**Status: substantive research checkpoint, not a completed R01–R14 pass.** Research date: September 26, 2026. Resume owner: [research progress](../research-progress.md); scope: [roster](../research-roster.md); completion standard: [requirements](../research-requirements.md).

## Scope and evidence already established

This dossier must distinguish the failed original 2010 service from the rebuilt **A Realm Reborn** service launched in 2013, and both from individual expansions and current patch rules. The original game is not a usable rules reference for modern XIV. Kevin VanOrd's review explicitly contrasts the replacement world's layout, movement, grouping and economy with the original release; its description of historical cross-class abilities must not be silently imported into the current job system. [S1]

The current official Job Guide separates tank, healer, melee, physical ranged and magical ranged roles. It lists **Beastmaster and Blue Mage as limited jobs**, and describes Evercold's additional battle mode as a future expansion feature, not something to assign to the shipped Dawntrail combat system. The official patch index lists the 7.5, 7.51, 7.55 and 7.56 releases. Current source pages are live documents, so the final dossier needs a dated current-state section, not an undated claim that Dawntrail launch describes the service forever. [S5][S6]

## Reception research captured so far

These are separate written critical accounts, not a vote count or an inference about the whole population. Their publication/version boundaries matter more than a combined score.

**Kevin VanOrd, GameSpot, A Realm Reborn; page currently dated May 5, 2014.** The body praises beautiful and varied regions, witty localization, flexible jobs on one character, and dungeons whose encounter rules make group combat interesting. It also finds familiar quest formulas, slow outdoor combat, needless travel and repetitive leveling on additional classes. Its strongest design distinction is between mundane overworld fighting and the more demanding coordination of dungeon encounters. This is a historical account; it is not evidence that the same early dungeon layouts, role prerequisites or cross-class abilities remain unchanged in 2026. [S1]

**Brittany Vincent, GameSpot, separate A Realm Reborn review.** The article describes attractive customization and an initially confusing class/job vocabulary. It appreciates demanding Primal encounters and the range of crafting, gathering and housing activities beyond fighting, while observing grind and time costs. Its low-level and level-50 account belongs to the early ARR/PS4 period. Its simplified story description is not being used as a canonical lore source. [S2]

**Kazuma Hashimoto, GamesRadar+, Dawntrail.** This critic welcomes the new jobs and especially encounters that require a healer to use more of the available kit. The review describes vibrant zones and distinct dungeon themes, but is sharply dissatisfied with the central narrative, flat characterization, aspects of English voice work and increasingly similar job structures. This is not a claim that attractive environments or challenging duties compensate for weak story for every player. [S3]

**Rhys Wood, TechRadar, Dawntrail.** This contrasting review regards Dawntrail as a promising new beginning and values its later narrative surprises. Its stated testing includes the main scenario, several jobs, Normal Arcadion raids, tomestone acquisition and Extreme-trial play. It therefore samples a different breadth of activities from a launch-day story-only impression. Its positive narrative judgment should remain beside Hashimoto's negative one rather than being averaged into an invented consensus. The final pass will expand this entry after rereading the complete body. [S4]

## Preliminary interpretation, not accepted OpenLegend design

XIV is already a useful counterexample to the idea that a persistent avatar requires a permanently fixed profession. Its attraction also illustrates a harder constraint: making many roles available on one identity does not automatically make learning or leveling them enjoyable. VanOrd's account distinguishes flexibility from the repeated work required to exploit it. The Dawntrail disagreement further suggests that an MMO contains several partly independent products—authored story, encounter practice, social identity, collecting and economic activity. These are research interpretations, not measured causes of commercial success or implementation requirements. [S1][S3][S4][S5]
