# G91 — Final Fantasy XIV

**Status: substantive research checkpoint, not a completed R01–R14 pass.** Research date: September 26, 2026. Resume owner: [research progress](../research-progress.md); scope: [roster](../research-roster.md); completion standard: [requirements](../research-requirements.md).

## Identity and version boundaries

The failed original **2010 service**, the rebuilt **A Realm Reborn (2013)**, and subsequent expansions are distinct research layers. The original game is not a usable rules reference for modern XIV. Kevin VanOrd's historical review explicitly contrasts the replacement world's layout, movement, grouping and economy with the original release; its cross-class abilities must not be silently imported into today's jobs. [S1](#s1)

Modern XIV combines an authored, sequential RPG campaign with a persistent multiplayer identity. A player can learn multiple combat and noncombat professions on one character rather than starting a new protagonist to change role. The official role inventory separates tanks, healers, melee, physical-ranged and magical-ranged damage dealers. Current guides distinguish ordinary jobs from **Blue Mage and Beastmaster**, which have restrictions rather than universal access to the same matchmade content. The guide's discussion of an additional battle mode for **Evercold** concerns a future expansion, not a shipped Dawntrail feature. [S5](#s5)

The official patch inventory reaches **7.56**. Its actual notes add the Beastmaster job and further level-100 main-scenario quests, including the opening quest A Winter's Dream. This dossier therefore cannot freeze the game at Dawntrail's 2024 launch. It also cannot take spoiler-concealing question marks in patch notes as missing implementation or supply invented quest names. [S6](#s6)[S7](#s7)

## Player identity, party entry and NPC help

Character creation exposes race, clan, gender, appearance, starting class and World choices. The current manual lists STR, DEX, VIT, INT and MND as starting attributes that vary with race/clan; gender does not change those initial attributes. This is character presentation plus an initial statistical distinction, not evidence of a branching racial skill tree. The class/job distinction also makes the first session less self-explanatory than simply choosing a finished endgame profession. [S8](#s8)[S2](#s2)

**Duty Finder** supplies automatic matching across Worlds for unlocked duties. It is not the same interaction as **Party Finder**, where someone posts the purpose and requirements of a group. Matching can also replace a departed/disconnected member. This lowers the practical threshold for trying group content, but does not erase role demand, queue times or incompatible expectations about learning versus speed-clearing. Those are different problems from whether a matching button exists. [S9](#s9)

**Duty Support** supplies NPC party members for supported instances. The manual explicitly distinguishes ARR dungeons with fixed NPC lineups from duties that let the player substitute available companions. It offers a way to experience selected story encounters without assembling humans; it is not a claim that every raid, trial or optional activity is now solo-compatible. The final pass will distinguish this from the separately leveled Trust-avatar system. [S10](#s10)

## Combat resources and noncombat professions

White Mage demonstrates a kit whose decisions are more than choosing a heal from a menu. It has MP-consuming spells, an instant-next-spell role action, Swiftcast, and a combat-time Lily gauge. Spending Lilies on particular healing actions develops the Blood Lily; after three Lily expenditures, Afflatus Misery becomes available. This connects recovery to a later offensive action instead of treating all healing as disconnected lost damage time. Exact acquired levels, recasts and upgraded spell forms remain patch-dependent. PvP actions are separately listed on the same guide and must not be confused with PvE potency. [S11](#s11)

Crafting is itself an action/resource system. A Carpenter must complete **progress** before **durability** is exhausted; improving quality is a competing use of steps and resources. **Basic Synthesis** advances completion, **Touch** actions develop quality, **Master's Mend** restores durability, and **Waste Not** reduces durability expenditure. Better actions, CP management, favorable material conditions and Inner Quiet accumulation make the order matter. **Byregot's Blessing** spends the accumulated Inner Quiet effect for a quality increase. **Trial synthesis** provides practice rather than requiring every experiment to risk production materials. These mechanics are not passive waiting for a progress bar after selecting a recipe. [S12](#s12)

The broad professional families are Carpenter, Blacksmith, Armorer, Goldsmith, Leatherworker, Weaver, Alchemist and Culinarian; gathering is divided into Miner, Botanist and Fisher. Their outputs include equipment, materials, medicines, food and furnishings. This allows goals to diverge: somebody can prepare consumables for difficult fights, furnish a residence, collect fishing achievements or sell production inputs rather than pursue only the strongest combat weapon. [S13](#s13)

### Current limited-job boundary: Beastmaster

The September 2026 guide describes a level-1–50 limited melee job sharing Monk/Samurai gear, not an ordinary level-100 raid role. **Gauge** checks capture eligibility; **Capture** applies a status, and defeating the target during that status may form a pact. Weakening it improves success. Familiars enter a bestiary and are assigned to battlehorns, with only one summoned at once. **Tempered Release** commands a familiar attack, **Borrow** uses a familiar-derived player action, and timed instinctual combinations coordinate both participants. Its **Crucible of the Unbroken** is a separate board-based solo challenge with attrition, temporary items and persistent familiar growth. The restrictions explicitly exclude roulettes, PvP and several other duty families. Creature collecting therefore has a deliberately bounded balance environment. [S14](#s14)

## Production, access and economic evidence captured

In PlayStation's August 26, 2013 interview, producer/director **Naoki Yoshida** frames the rebuild as restoring trust, reconstructing the play experience and delivering the promised PS3 edition. He specifically discusses a DualShock-oriented Cross Hot Bar rather than assuming a mouse interface would transfer automatically. He also describes regular content patches, housing and PvP plans, and rejects mandatory voice communication as a source of pressure on beginners. These are attributed 2013 priorities and plans, not a guarantee that the proposed three-month cadence or every interface ambition survived unchanged. [S15](#s15)

Square Enix's **August 31, 2018** announcement reports **more than 14 million registered Warriors of Light**, not 14 million simultaneous players, paying subscriptions or unit sales. It places the milestone beside an anniversary livestream, developer and actor appearances, a Producer Live Letter, the Monster Hunter collaboration, the in-game Rising celebration and a multi-city Fan Festival schedule. This is documented community/distribution activity, not a measured attribution of how many customers any channel acquired. No private retention, development budget or product-level profit figure has been established in this pass. [S16](#s16)

**Current free-trial caution:** the official trial page now advertises play to **level 80**, lists Gunbreaker and Dancer among its jobs, and retains detailed limits: a gil cap, no market-board trading or retainers, no Free Companies, restricted chat, and no PvP. Trial players can be invited to groups and use Duty Finder but cannot recruit through Party Finder. Its restriction text still names only ARR/Heavensward/Stormblood in one sentence; the final pass must reconcile that stale wording against a current expansion announcement rather than silently recycle the older level-70 trial description. A trial is not equivalent to the subscribed game's economy and social access. [S17](#s17)

## Reception: five substantive written reviews

These are separate critical accounts across four publications, not a vote count. Their publication/version boundaries matter more than a combined score. Original review bodies were read; this section does not infer rules or consensus from aggregate scores.

**Kevin VanOrd, GameSpot, A Realm Reborn; page currently dated May 5, 2014.** He praises varied regions, witty localization, flexible jobs on one identity and encounter-specific dungeon cooperation. He finds familiar quest formulas, slow outdoor combat, needless travel and repeated leveling work. His distinction between mundane overworld fights and more demanding dungeon coordination is more useful than simply calling the game traditional. Historical cross-class rules and early encounter layouts are not automatically current. [S1](#s1)

**Brittany Vincent, GameSpot, separate ARR/PS4 review.** She values the PS4 presentation, controller Cross Hot Bar, touchpad use and Vita Remote Play, alongside demanding Primal encounters and crafting/housing alternatives to fighting. The class/job terminology can initially confuse; leveling and housing require considerable time and money. This is a different platform-focused account from VanOrd's review. Its simplified lore description and historical PvP characterization are not being adopted as authoritative current rules. [S2](#s2)

**Kazuma Hashimoto, GamesRadar+, Dawntrail (2024).** New jobs and encounters that require a healer to use more of the available kit are welcome. Distinctive dungeon themes and vibrant environments do not rescue, for this critic, a thin narrative, flat characterization, disappointing aspects of English voice work or increasingly similar job structures. This preserves the possibility of liking the battle content while disliking the central reason a story-oriented player subscribes. [S3](#s3)

**Rhys Wood, TechRadar, Dawntrail (August 2, 2024).** Wood instead welcomes Wuk Lamat as the protagonist and likes a lower-stakes beginning that develops into surprising events. He particularly values Pictomancer's expressive casting and the more demanding dungeons/Arcadion encounters. Criticism concerns occasional pacing drops and largely unchanged progression structure; some jobs feel more complete than newly interesting. His declared play includes the main scenario, several jobs, Normal raids and Extreme trials. That breadth is not interchangeable with a story-only launch impression. [S4](#s4)

**Colin Buchanan, Noisy Pixel, Dawntrail (July 9, 2024).** Buchanan finds the protagonist's supporting role an effective way to rebuild stakes and let Tural's local characters lead. He likes Wuk Lamat, the expanded Krile/Erenville roles and harder baseline duties, but criticizes the abrupt middle-story slowdown that postpones a promised reveal. He also observes remaining visual problems, including inconsistent shadows and clipping despite the graphical upgrade. His disclosed White Mage playthrough, Expert dungeons and attempted Extreme fights explain the perspective without turning it into universal audience preference. [S18](#s18)

## Steam helpful player testimony

The store page encountered an age gate, and an initial language-qualified community URL failed. The accessible **Most Helpful (All Time)** community route and its **Negative Only** counterpart were then read successfully on September 26, 2026. These are selected old and updated reviews, not a random sample, a current-subscriber survey or a measurement of complaint frequency. Displayed lifetime hours may have accumulated after publication.

**Positive surface:** a May 29, 2021 account particularly values switching jobs without abandoning one character, while acknowledging separate leveling. A substantial June 5, 2017 review, updated through September 2021, values music, welcoming help and the long story, but also calls ordinary dungeons relatively conventional. A June 28, 2021 account describes the story's slow beginning and multiple endgame goals rather than equating a recommendation with unconditional praise. Old prices, trial limits, job caps and raid counts in these accounts are not current facts. [S19](#s19)

**Negative surface:** a December 22, 2017 account likes the game but objects to Steam-specific purchase/expansion constraints; a February 1, 2024 account likewise praises the MMO while reporting launcher/Steam connection friction. A June 15, 2025 long-form negative account appreciates blind or minimum-item-level group discovery but dislikes optimization pressure, Party Finder misrepresentation, loot gates and Dawntrail's pacing. Its claims about particular shared gear and writing responsibility are not independently verified rules or production facts. A review updated May 28, 2026 reports repeated installation interruptions. These describe distinct access, group-culture and design complaints; they must not be collapsed into a claim that negative reviewers uniformly dislike the combat. [S20](#s20)

## Preliminary interpretation, not accepted OpenLegend design

A persistent avatar need not imply one permanent profession, but access to many roles does not itself make learning and leveling them enjoyable. The reception also exposes partly independent attractions: authored story, encounter practice, social identity, collecting and economic production. A design can strengthen one while alienating another audience. The present limited-job boundary is particularly instructive: expressive creature powers can receive their own constraints instead of having to fit every group encounter. These are research interpretations, not measured commercial causes or engine requirements.
