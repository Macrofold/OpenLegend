# MapleStory

**G193 · original PC game · research checkpoint, 2026-10-27.** This dossier distinguishes the Korean service (KMS), Global MapleStory (GMS), and regional implementations. MapleStory M, MapleStory 2 and MapleStory Worlds are separate products. Research and review remain in progress; the commercial, reception, production and final transfer sections will be integrated before completion.

## 1. Identity and playable promise

MapleStory makes a persistent character and social identity out of a side-view action RPG. Its appeal combines an immediately legible character, a distinctive job, increasingly spectacular movement and attacks, collectible appearance, and long projects with other people. Progression eventually extends beyond the main character into an account's roster and multiple equipment systems. That accumulation can sustain affection and mastery; it can also make a return feel like an audit of unfinished work. The distinction must be tested through the activities and obligations below, rather than inferred from longevity alone.

## 2. Actions, equipment and progression

Jobs constrain identity. GMS support describes Explorer branches within five base families and separate class-specific advancement for characters such as Aran. Advancement develops the chosen identity; it is not a universal ability to turn any character into any other class. This makes choosing a character consequential and encourages trying another character when a different movement or combat style appeals. [S01]

The Japanese combat guide documents direct weapon attacks, active and supporting skills, HP/MP constraints, loot collection and disabling statuses. A stun prevents movement and attacks; a seal prevents skills; other effects can interfere with jumping or control. The player therefore manages position, resources and recovery as well as damage. These are documented regional rules, not a claim that every historical resistance multiplier remains current in GMS. [S05]

The GMS Maple Guide suggests hunting fields and quests by level, can transport the character to a recommended location, and exposes job advancement when eligible. Some destinations require earlier quests. This puts navigation, progression advice and access checks in one interface. It also means an enormous world need not be traversed entirely through remembered walking routes. [S02]

| System | Actual choices and consequences |
| --- | --- |
| **Equipment improvement** | Spell Traces spend a limited upgrade count for a selected enhancement chance; a failed attempt consumes the slot without destroying the item. Star Force spends meso for further stats and presents cost, success chance and possible failure. Eligible safeguards trade extra cost for protection from destruction. An Equipment Trace can preserve parts of a destroyed item's investment when applied to another copy. The support article's older ceiling and downgrade schedule are not treated as an October 2026 specification. [S03, S04] |
| **Roster composition** | The Japanese Union guide describes assigning same-world characters as shaped blocks on a board. Their individual effects and occupied spaces strengthen the roster; character level changes the block, and saved arrangements support switching priorities. This makes a second character useful to an existing project, although a compulsory checklist of unwanted classes would undermine that advantage. GMS calls the related system Legion; exact regional roster limits are not imported here. [S06] |
| **Production and reuse** | The Japanese profession guide documents gathering herbs and ore, then making equipment, accessories or potions in Ardentmill's equivalent. Recipes turn material stocks into useful products; extraction turns unwanted equipment into crafting crystals. Gathering/crafting consumes fatigue. The production role is a bounded recipe system, not arbitrary item invention or a universal promise of profitable player trading. [S07] |

## 3. Native creation and social places

The Japanese Home guide records a complete native creation loop. Unlock the home, acquire themes and furnishings, choose roof/wall/floor/window arrangements, move or mirror furniture on a grid, and save a named preset. Invite named characters or neighbors, permit random visits, and set access rules; blocked accounts cannot enter. This connects authorship to an audience instead of stopping at a private arrangement screen. Furniture and themes belong to the same-world collection; the guide does not describe exporting a house as a reusable game package. GMS delivery and its differences are being checked separately. [S08]

The social value does not require a room to become a production optimization. A place can provide a recognizable setting for talking, showing a collection or meeting after an activity. That is an interpretation of the documented visit and furnishing tools, not evidence of autonomous residents, player-written NPC behavior or a player-governed town.

## 4. Constructed situations

These explanatory combinations are **not observed play sessions**. They retain regional or historical boundaries and will be expanded to cover group combat, story and return decisions.

| Situation | Intention → action → consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Improve an item without losing track of the risk** | A player wants more damage but has a limited meso reserve. They inspect the next Star Force attempt, protection options and replacement availability before paying. Success improves the item; destruction creates a recovery problem requiring another copy. The next decision is whether another attempt is worth delaying a different goal. [S04] |
| **Make an alternate character part of a plan** | A player wants a different roster bonus. They develop a class they also enjoy, then fit its block into a saved board arrangement. The account gains a useful contribution without deleting the original character's identity. If the class is unpleasant, the same incentive can become an obligation; the bonus alone does not establish enjoyment. [S06] |
| **Give a decorated room an audience** | A host saves a furnished layout, sets visit permissions and invites another player. The guest can encounter a recognizable place without collecting every object personally. Success is the shared use, not a daily interaction quota or the number of furniture pieces owned. [S08] |
