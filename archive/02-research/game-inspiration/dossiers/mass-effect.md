# G101 — Mass Effect

[Research roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Library](../README.md)

**Research date:** September 26, 2026. **Pass:** R01–R14 complete, with the source-access limitations recorded below; the requested second-pass audit and corrections are recorded at the end. This is documentary research, not a gameplay test. **Spoilers:** the social-system and worked-example sections discuss Virmire, Feros and Bring Down the Sky; the story section names the central threat. Readers preserving a first playthrough should stop before those sections.

**Central finding — interpretation:** Mass Effect makes a finite, authored campaign feel personally owned by combining a customizable officer, a persistent crew, selectable expedition order and remembered decisions. Its most useful lesson is not that every object or citizen is simulated. It is that a relatively small number of understandable commitments can connect tactical preparation, personal relationships and political consequences. Its corresponding warning is that an expansive fiction does not automatically make repeated exploration tasks interesting.

## 1. Identity, releases and the player promise

BioWare's original game launched on Xbox 360 on November 20, 2007; Microsoft published that release. The 2008 Windows port, published by EA, was a separate control/interface treatment. Edge of Reality later helped bring the original to PlayStation 3: December 4, 2012 in North America, with Bring Down the Sky integrated. Legendary Edition, released May 14, 2021, remasters the three main Shepard campaigns for PC, PlayStation 4 and Xbox One, with newer-console backward compatibility. A compilation is not a fourth Shepard campaign. [S1](#s1) [S2](#s2) [S19](#s19) [S20](#s20) [S28](#s28)

The original is a **single-player, third-person action RPG**. Shepard commands AI companions but is the directly controlled person; there is no native co-op campaign, shared player inventory, competitive mode or player-controlled economy. The Normandy is a home and destination-selection hub, not a freely piloted starship combat game. Original-game multiplayer should not be inferred from Mass Effect 3's later features. [S4](#s4) [S5](#s5)

A first session constructs Shepard, introduces the Normandy and the Eden Prime crisis, and establishes shooting, squad assistance, investigation and conversation. The Citadel then expands the frame from a military assignment to the politics of a galactic community. Established play alternates between choosing an investigation, configuring a field team, resolving an authored location, examining equipment and returning to the crew. The player is choosing a version of a defined protagonist's journey, not inventing an unrelated occupation or replacing the main mission with a settlement simulation. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4)

## 2. Creation, progression and capability inventory

### The officer and the six classes

Creation distinguishes **pre-service history**—Earthborn, Spacer or Colonist—from **psychological profile**—Sole Survivor, War Hero or Ruthless. These are authored background hooks and starting moral tendencies, not a generated childhood simulation. Appearance and gender selection personalize a voiced protagonist whose rank, profession and place in the opening mission remain fixed. Later references make the selected past relevant without making every background a different campaign. [S12](#s12)

The six classes organize three disciplines. Soldier emphasizes combat, Engineer technology and Adept biotics. Infiltrator combines combat/technology, Vanguard combat/biotics and Sentinel technology/biotics. Talents unlock and strengthen specific capabilities. Equipment training matters, including armor restrictions. The player does not freely switch jobs mid-campaign, and the class names must not import sequel abilities: original Infiltrators do not receive Mass Effect 2's Tactical Cloak, and original Vanguards do not receive its Charge. [S7](#s7)

Experience comes from combat and resolved activities; level-ups grant talent investment. The Luna assignment opens a specialization within the existing class's possibilities. A character's build therefore grows along a constrained identity, rather than being an unrestricted collection of every discipline. **Analytical consequence:** a party member can be valuable because they supply a missing verb, not merely a higher damage statistic. Choosing companions is also choosing which problems the current expedition can comfortably address. [S7](#s7)

### Verbs, prerequisites, costs and feedback

| System | Player actions and conditions | Costs, failure and feedback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct combat | Move, aim, fire, take cover, change weapon, melee, throw a grenade. | Heat limits sustained gunfire; shields, health, exposure and weapon behavior constrain aggression. Cover and camera friction can frustrate intended positioning. |
| Command | Select two field companions, allocate their talents and gear, issue movement/targeting orders and select powers through a paused interface. | A trained skill on someone left aboard ship is unavailable to the field party. Cooldowns and incapacitation limit immediate options. |
| Technical interaction | Approach eligible locks or devices and pass the party's Decryption/Electronics requirement; complete the relevant interface challenge or use an available bypass. | An insufficient skill rank is a different obstacle from failing the minigame. Omni-gel should not be described as a universal substitute for every prerequisite. |
| Investigation and conversation | Talk, investigate additional topics, choose a stance and sometimes select a trained persuasive option. Read the journal and codex. | Available answers depend on authored state and skills; a short wheel label may not reveal Shepard's full spoken wording. Several options sometimes reconverge. |
| Expedition | Use the galaxy map, travel to available destinations, land where permitted, drive the Mako, inspect anomalies and enter authored interiors. | Not every displayed planet is landable. Hazard exposure, vehicle damage and traversal inconvenience can turn curiosity into a cost. |
| Inventory and exchange | Compare, equip, modify, buy, sell or convert surplus equipment. | Slot compatibility, training and foregone resale value matter. Loot containers are not general-purpose personal storage. |

The table condenses the shipped manual/tutorial vocabulary and the original/PC reviews; it is not a claim that every encounter accepts every verb. [S2](#s2) [S4](#s4) [S8](#s8) [S10](#s10) [S29](#s29)

### Powers are a vocabulary, not merely spell damage

Lift, Throw and Singularity alter an opponent's position or ability to respond. Barrier supports defense; Stasis isolates a target; Warp supplies a different offensive option. Technology includes Sabotage, Overload, Damping and AI Hacking. Weapon talents add active combat advantages, while Unity from Spectre Training supports squad recovery. These abilities require the relevant character and investment; naming a power does not establish that a new character has it. [S7](#s7)

**Interpretation:** the tactical resource is often the opportunity to act safely. Temporarily preventing retaliation can be worth more than increasing damage per shot. Conversely, combat control is not permission to manipulate arbitrary scenery: the released game's authored interaction targets and ability rules remain the boundary.

### Vehicle operation, exploration and minigame boundaries

The Mako has distinct machine-gun and cannon attacks, zoom and jump jets. A clear camera view does not guarantee the turret can reach an extremely close or steeply elevated target. Shields recover, while hull repair spends omni-gel; the original repair procedure requires stopping. Electronics investment increases repair effectiveness as well as supporting shields, Overload and technical access. These connect a specialist's combat, exploration and maintenance value rather than adding a separate vehicle-driving profession. [S32](#s32) [S34](#s34)

Driving over terrain, dismounting at an anomaly and entering a ruin are different activities from surveying a planet on the map. Surveying and collectible discoveries support assignments and rewards, not player-operated mines or industrial production. Bratcher's review describes optional discoveries ranging from a nuclear-warhead problem to searching creatures for a data module, but also notes that not every planet is landable and that ordinary civilian theft/crime is not freely simulated. These are bounded encounters, not evidence for a general pickpocketing, stealth-career or ecology system. [S5](#s5)

The PC port replaces the console's button-sequence hacking presentation with moving through rotating concentric barriers under a time limit. Watters values that variation and the port's tactical shortcuts. A failed manipulation challenge and an unmet Decryption/Electronics prerequisite are different failure conditions; changing the interface does not remove the party-skill gate. [S2](#s2) [S29](#s29)

## 3. Equipment, resources and the economy

The major equipment families are pistols, shotguns, assault rifles, sniper rifles, armor, grenades, bio-amps and omni-tools. Weapons accept compatible weapon and ammunition modifications; armor and grenades have their own upgrade categories. An ammunition modification changes the weapon's behavior: it is not a consumable stack of bullets with modification slots of its own. Ordinary original-game gunfire is constrained by heat rather than the sequel's thermal-clip reload loop. Bio-amps support biotics; omni-tools support technical performance. Credits buy equipment and upgrades; medi-gel supports healing; omni-gel can assist technical actions and Mako repair. These are fictional campaign resources, not purchasable premium currencies. [S8](#s8) [S10](#s10)

Armor separates physical damage protection, shields and technological/biotic resistance. Light, medium and heavy categories impose training constraints. Upgrades can alter defensive or cooldown behavior, so the comparison is not always reducible to one armor number. Exploits that equip otherwise invalid armor do not establish an intended universal wardrobe system. [S9](#s9)

For a concrete equipment comparison, a community Garrus guide contrasts **Colossus** protection with **Predator** shielding/resistance, and discusses **Savant** omni-tools, **Medical Exoskeleton**, **Energized Plating**, **Scram Rail** and **Frictionless Materials**. Those are examples of composing a role: protection, power availability, gun performance and heat interact. The guide covers original/Legendary play and expresses its author's build preferences; its preferred loadout is not a universally optimal solution. Exact item-tier statistics are deliberately not generalized across editions. [S26](#s26)

Selling an unwanted item and breaking it into omni-gel are mutually exclusive dispositions of that item. Equipment allocation among companions creates another opportunity cost: a surplus for Shepard may still upgrade a specialist. The result is a loot-and-replacement economy, not a deep crafting profession, resource-production chain or market with autonomous suppliers. Container rewards and shops chiefly support the mission loop. [S8](#s8) [S10](#s10)

**Merchant and crew access.** Buying a manufacturer licence expands the Normandy requisition officer's possible assortment; it does not manufacture an item or guarantee every desired tier is immediately stocked. The cargo-bay lockers open the corresponding squadmate's equipment screen, including crew not currently in the field team. Licences therefore improve access, while lockers reduce the cost of maintaining several specialists. [S32](#s32)

The PC guide distinguishes merchant inventories, level/quest conditions and successive medi-gel/grenade capacity upgrades. For example, Ledra's Feros shop depends on local quest progression and his survival; purchasing an earlier capacity tier enables the next rather than allowing arbitrary maximum capacity at the opening. This makes credits compete between immediate equipment, future inventory breadth and additional emergency supplies. It is a finite authored economy, not a player market or a generic crafting tree. [S33](#s33)

**Analytical warning:** when replacement frequency overwhelms the importance of each decision, an apparently rich equipment system becomes clerical work. The original game's inventory criticism should not be read as evidence that all equipment choice is undesirable. It points instead to a mismatch between the number of comparisons requested and the number that change the player's plan. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3)

## 4. Time, failure, replay and the end of a career

Combat operates in real time with a tactical pause; travel and conversations follow authored transitions. The game does not supply a daily hunger/sleep employment calendar, seasonal economy or settlement maintenance loop. Side assignments offer optional experience, equipment and fiction, but the urgent main plot does not imply a universally ticking simulation deadline. A local scripted hazard or countdown should not be generalized into galaxy-wide autonomous time. [S5](#s5) [S8](#s8)

Ordinary combat defeat is a reload problem rather than permanent loss of the whole career. Squad incapacitation and recovery are separate from **authored story death**. That separation is essential: Unity does not reverse a narrative sacrifice. Players can save, return to an earlier decision and replay, but doing so changes their chosen history rather than repairing the same uninterrupted world state. [S7](#s7) [S15](#s15) [S16](#s16)

The campaign ends after its finale; it is not an unrestricted post-credits sandbox. New Game Plus can reuse Shepard's level, talents, specialization and equipment while restarting the story and resetting assignments, codex and morality scales. Importing into **Mass Effect 2** is a different operation: it carries selected history and grants mapped bonuses rather than transferring the first game's complete talent/gear rules unchanged. The original and Legendary leveling/bonus details should be checked in their own editions. [S17](#s17)

A March 2014 Steam participant, **Argentino_88**, reported leaving side missions and DLC for after the story, then being disappointed that an earlier save was necessary. That is a concrete expectation failure, not a representative satisfaction measure. **Design interpretation:** a finite campaign benefits from communicating its point of no return clearly enough that curiosity does not become accidental lost access. [S24](#s24)

## 5. People, relationships and the simulation boundary

The six recruitable companions are Ashley, Kaidan, Garrus, Wrex, Tali and Liara. Their different specialties and perspectives make party composition both tactical and expressive. Conversations aboard the Normandy and at authored encounters expose disagreements about duty, institutions, species and personal history. This is **scripted character reactivity**, not evidence that every resident has an independently simulated daily agenda or develops arbitrary relationships off-screen. [S4](#s4) [S14](#s14)

Power automation is configurable: the original review distinguishes using all powers automatically, only defensive powers, or leaving power use to player commands. This is a real delegation choice, not autonomous mission planning. Bratcher separately criticizes enemies that rush directly or remain distant rather than coordinating and flanking convincingly. Those are his observed combat limitations, not an inspection of the game's AI source code. [S1](#s1) [S5](#s5)

Romance is a bounded set of written routes. In the unmodified original, Ashley is available to male Shepard, Kaidan to female Shepard, and Liara to either. Sha'ira's encounter is not equivalent to the ongoing companion romance route. Later-game romance options, unused recorded lines and save-edit/mod possibilities do not establish native original-game availability. Conversations and story progression matter; this is not a repeatable gift economy with every NPC. [S14](#s14)

Paragon and Renegade are separate accumulated measures, not a single bar where gaining one automatically subtracts the other. Original-game Charm and Intimidate are trained talents with rank availability influenced by morality and progression. Morality thresholds also have mechanical bonuses; a review's broad claim that morality has no effect on abilities is therefore too sweeping. Mixing attitudes is possible, but investment and thresholds can make a desired persuasive response unavailable. [S13](#s13)

The Citadel Council, human Alliance and other groups supply political context and authored responses. There is no general faction-management interface for founding states, issuing treaties or simulating all diplomatic actors. The game makes a person's decision politically significant by writing consequences around it, not by running a complete foreign-policy economy. **Interpretation:** this is a powerful middle ground between purely cosmetic dialogue and unconstrained social simulation.

**Virmire spoilers.** Wrex's confrontation connects his people's future to Shepard's military objective. Completing the Family Armor assignment can establish trust that substitutes for a persuasion route in resolving the confrontation. Ashley and Kaidan's later rescue choice is a different kind of commitment: the player cannot preserve both through better routine healing. The former rewards relationship preparation; the latter insists on a loss. Treating both as the same generic “relationship score check” would erase their dramatic difference. [S15](#s15) [S16](#s16)

## 6. Story and worldbuilding as playable structure

Eden Prime introduces the beacon, Saren and the mystery behind the attack. The Citadel makes the protagonist's human status politically contingent rather than universally authoritative. The chase becomes an investigation of an older threat, the Reapers. Shepard's official mission supplies a reason to encounter different social orders rather than simply tour an encyclopedia. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4)

**Interpretation:** the Normandy works as a stable social reference point against changing destinations. Repeatedly returning to a known crew makes a discovery comparable with what those people previously believed. The codex and planetary descriptions enlarge the implied setting, while a much smaller set of inhabited places carries the performed drama. That combination can make the world feel deep without every described fact needing an interactive subsystem.

Feros demonstrates a particularly important connection between story and rules. The colonists' condition changes the meaning of an apparent combat encounter: Anti-Thorian Gas grenades can incapacitate them, and melee provides another nonlethal option. Ordinary destructive habits can kill people the player intends to save. Noveria's rachni decision likewise asks the player to judge a future risk rather than merely optimize the current fight. [S30](#s30) [S31](#s31)

The narrative is authored and branching, with substantial reconvergence. Background callbacks, survival flags and different responses create ownership, but not every dialogue alternative creates an independent future. Both the small acknowledged difference and the major irreversible choice can matter; conflating them encourages an impossible promise of unlimited branching. [S4](#s4) [S17](#s17)

## 7. Worked interactions

The following are **constructed, rules-based illustrations**, not observed play sessions. Outcomes assume the stated powers, equipment and quest state. They illustrate interaction structure rather than prescribe an optimal walkthrough.

### A. Control an encounter rather than race its damage

**Intention:** cross a dangerous firing lane without losing a fragile specialist. **Conditions:** a field companion has an available control power, and another can address the target's defenses. **Actions:** pause, assess who threatens the crossing, assign the relevant power and concentrate fire rather than letting every squadmate choose an unrelated target. **Interaction:** a temporary denial of enemy action creates a movement or damage opportunity. **Result:** a tactical window, not invulnerability. **Next decision:** exploit the window or save another cooldown for the next attacker. **Limit:** an unavailable power, wrong target or obstructed position defeats the plan; party membership alone is insufficient. [S7](#s7)

The transferable question is whether the interface exposes **who can do what now**. A sophisticated capability that is difficult to command can feel absent. The PC port's shortcuts and individual orders are a useful comparison rather than merely a graphical upgrade. [S2](#s2)

### B. A locked container is a party-composition question

**Intention:** recover equipment while exploring with a combat-focused Shepard. **Conditions:** the container requires Decryption or Electronics at an adequate rank. **Actions:** bring and train an appropriate specialist, then interact. **Interaction:** the relevant check considers the present party rather than only the protagonist. **Result:** a team can access a resource that Shepard alone could not. **Next decision:** equip the find, sell it or convert surplus into omni-gel. **Limit:** insufficient party rank is not cured simply by owning gel, and an optional cache need not justify a long detour. [S29](#s29)

This is a useful form of dependency because another person expands the group's vocabulary. A weaker version would repeatedly demand the same companion for trivial locks without creating interesting expedition tradeoffs.

### C. The same “enemy” silhouette can require a different tool

**Feros spoilers. Intention:** stop attacking colonists without killing them. **Conditions:** the player has received the Anti-Thorian grenade modification and has ammunition available. **Actions:** equip the correct grenade effect, target a clustered group and avoid uncontrolled lethal attacks; use the documented melee alternative where appropriate. **Interaction:** equipment configuration changes an attack's narrative outcome. **Result:** incapacitation can preserve people whom ordinary combat would remove. **Next decision:** conserve the remaining grenades or spend one to reduce risk. **Limit:** explosive ammunition, destructive powers or allies' attacks can undermine the rescue intention. A “good” conversation choice does not automatically make every subsequent action nonlethal. [S30](#s30)

### D. A remembered favor can provide a different route through conflict

**Virmire spoilers. Intention:** keep Wrex as an ally despite a fundamental disagreement. **Conditions:** his Family Armor assignment was completed before the confrontation, or the player meets an appropriate persuasive route. **Actions:** invoke established trust or use the available trained response. **Interaction:** a past optional action changes the present conversation's possibilities. **Result:** the relationship can survive without requiring one universal social statistic. **Next decision:** proceed with the mission while accepting the unresolved moral disagreement. **Limit:** doing an unrelated task, possessing the armor after the encounter or simply having high combat power does not establish the same route. [S15](#s15)

### E. Spare an antagonist without erasing the cost

**Bring Down the Sky spoilers. Intention:** save the hostages. **Conditions:** Balak has reached the confrontation and threatens their lives. **Actions:** allow his escape rather than attack him. **Interaction:** an immediate humanitarian objective conflicts with apprehending a dangerous actor. **Result:** the hostage-saving branch sacrifices the immediate capture; the attack branch sacrifices the hostages. **Next decision:** live with the recorded result rather than expect a combat upgrade to remove the dilemma. **Limit:** this is an authored exclusive choice, not a fully simulated negotiation where any improvised plan is supported. [S27](#s27)

### F. A stronger gun can be the wrong improvement

**Intention:** make Garrus more useful through a long engagement. **Conditions:** several weapon and armor modifications are available. **Actions:** compare heat, defenses and power support, not just nominal weapon damage; choose a coherent combination and retain an alternative. **Interaction:** equipment determines how consistently the specialist can deliver the desired role. **Result:** a lower headline statistic can support a more reliable plan. **Next decision:** revise for another enemy type or mission. **Limit:** this is a comparison exercise, not a claim that one published build is optimal at every difficulty or in both original and Legendary rules. [S26](#s26)

## 8. Art, audio, interface and feel

The close-up conversation presentation asks the player to read expression and tone, while the wheel compresses a spoken intention into a short selection. This creates pace but also a potential expectation gap between the chosen phrase and the delivered line. Ship and station interiors present understandable functions alongside alien forms; the setting is not made convincing solely by adding more locations. Reviewers nevertheless identified texture pop-in, camera problems and repeated interiors that could break that impression. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

Hudson described the score's aim as a combination of cinematic orchestration and futuristic electronics, with Jack Wall and Sam Hulick contributing complementary strengths. That is a documented artistic intention, not a claim that every listener experienced the same emotion. [S18](#s18)

The reviews disagree usefully about the result. Grosnick found the menu music especially effective but questioned male Shepard's emotional range; McCarroll found the score supportive without finding it particularly memorable. Such disagreement can guide a listening study more precisely than a blanket “excellent audio” verdict. No formal accessibility audit, measured input-latency test or complete soundtrack listening session was performed for this dossier. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

## 9. DLC, ports and the remaster are not interchangeable

**Bring Down the Sky (2008)** adds asteroid X57, the threat to Terra Nova and the batarian confrontation. It is a compact story extension with exploration and a consequential ending, not a new systemic mode. The PC and PS3 packaging differs from the original paid Xbox release; the 2012 BioWare account specifically documents free PC access and PS3 integration. This is historical packaging evidence, not proof that every old installer works today. [S21](#s21) [S27](#s27) [S28](#s28)

**Pinnacle Station (2009)** instead builds around virtual combat challenges. Survival, Time Trial, Capture and Hunt ask for different performance goals, with a later challenge and apartment reward. Its fiction frames repetition, but does not add an equivalent character-driven adventure. VanOrd's review argues that emphasizing combat in isolation exposes weaknesses that the larger RPG normally contextualizes. The page now displays an October 2013 date despite discussing launch-era issues; it is not treated as a newly verified 2013 product release. [S23](#s23)

Pinnacle Station was not included in the official Legendary Edition. Reporting on the remaster attributes the omission to unusable/lost source material and the cost of rebuilding; a community restoration is a mod, not native inclusion. The relevant Game Informer page resolved but did not expose the expected full interview text, so this account relies on PC Gamer's attributed reporting and the community edition reference, not a claim that the complete original interview was read. [S21](#s21) [S22](#s22)

**Windows, 2008:** a consolidated tactical view, shortcuts, individual squad orders and an altered hacking interface change access to existing mechanics. Original console criticisms cannot simply be copied onto that port. **Legendary Edition, 2021:** BioWare documents changes to aiming, weapon usability, melee, inventory handling, experience progression and the Mako. These should not be backdated to 2007. The remaster modernizes the original campaign; it does not turn it into a universal open-world simulation. [S2](#s2) [S6](#s6)

More specifically, Legendary removes class penalties for using weapon types but retains class-specific specializations; adds out-of-combat sprint and a separate melee input; and changes several weapon-ability and inventory conveniences. Its Mako rear boosters have a cooldown independent of the jump jets, while vehicle kills lose the original experience penalty and lava changes from instant mission failure to damage over time. These can alter a player's preparation and risk calculus, not just the image quality. They are documented remaster changes, not original-console capabilities. [S6](#s6)

## 10. Production history, promotion and distribution

In an August 2007 interview, Casey Hudson dates early preproduction to 2004 after the PC version of Knights of the Old Republic. He identifies Unreal Engine 3 as the foundation and describes substantial BioWare work on digital actors, combat and exploration. He also names cinematic science-fiction influences and a planned trilogy. These are a developer's contemporary account, not an audited production budget. [S18](#s18)

The same interview illustrates why **announced** and **shipped** must remain separate: it describes direct switching into squadmates and Mako customization, neither of which should be added to the shipped game's action inventory. Its claims about future sequels are plans made before release. [S18](#s18)

Demiurge's Windows work and Edge of Reality's PS3 collaboration show that ports were production projects with interface, engine and performance consequences, not only new storefront entries. Warden's 2012 account describes beginning trilogy work after the third game and solving the original game's PS3 absence. [S4](#s4) [S28](#s28)

The documented promotional surfaces include director interviews, the Microsoft-published console launch, the later cross-platform trilogy package and EA's 2021 remaster announcement/launch campaign. The prequel novel *Revelation*, written by lead writer Drew Karpyshyn, was presented as supplementary backstory, not required mechanical instruction. [S18](#s18) [S19](#s19) [S20](#s20)

**Marketing interpretation:** the shareable unit is often “my Shepard made this decision” or “this companion changed my mind,” rather than a user-built object. Persistent identity gives people a compact way to compare playthroughs. The sources here establish those design affordances and visible promotional channels, but do **not** establish a particular influencer's causal contribution, a measured viral coefficient, paid acquisition spending or a complete history of fan-community growth.

## 11. Commercial evidence and what it does not establish

Shacknews reported on December 11, 2007 that Microsoft marketing executive Jeff Bell said the Xbox 360 game had sold **more than one million copies within its first three weeks**. This is an attributed historical unit-sales claim. It is not a count of concurrent players, completed campaigns, Steam owners, unique franchise users or lifetime revenue. No title-level budget, profit, customer-acquisition cost or independently audited lifetime sales total was established in this pass. [S19](#s19)

The business model is a premium game followed by differently packaged expansions, ports and collections. Original DLC prices and later free inclusion are edition/date facts, not live purchasing advice. EA's 2021 press releases support the remaster's release and packaging; they do not isolate the first game's share of collection revenue. Current player counts and unsynchronized review totals were deliberately not used as substitutes for commercial evidence. [S20](#s20) [S28](#s28)

## 13. OpenLegend research hypotheses — not accepted requirements

**Remembered assistance as an alternative to a universal persuasion check.** A prior favor can make a later conflict resolvable for a different reason than charisma. The promising ingredient is an intelligible connection between the favor and the present trust, not merely a hidden numerical bonus. A possible small study would compare “you helped my family” with a generic reputation threshold. It would need to show why that person remembers and why the memory matters; otherwise the callback becomes an arbitrary password.

**Companions as access to different actions.** A specialist can change the group's options without replacing the player. This only remains interesting when competence is legible, participation is reliable and the task is worth the coordination. Do not copy a pattern in which one mandatory locksmith must accompany every expedition for negligible rewards. A useful comparison would test several ways to solve the same obstacle, with different costs and different relationships involved.

**Different kinds of consequence should remain different.** Tactical incapacitation, a reversible equipment choice, a remembered dialogue stance and an authored permanent death do not share the same recovery rules. A player should be able to understand which kind occurred. Making every failure permanent would be as reductive as making every loss healable.

**Story can change an action's meaning.** The Feros example suggests studying tools whose effects distinguish stopping a threat from destroying a person. Such a system needs action-level support, allied-behavior coordination and clear feedback; a benevolent sentence alone is insufficient. The broader research question is how to make intent and consequences agree without silently overriding the player's actual actions.

**A stable social home can make a large world feel connected.** Returning to familiar people offers a place to interpret an expedition. The hypothesis does not require a Normandy-shaped vehicle, cinematic production values or a rigid command hierarchy. A village, guild house or temporary camp might supply the same continuity if return conversations genuinely reflect experience.

**Do not copy breadth without encounter density.** A large destination catalogue and repeated interiors may create an initial impression of scale but fail under repeated use. Compare a smaller set of locations with distinct purposes against a larger set of minimally differentiated errands. Likewise, more loot is not automatically more meaningful progression.

These proposals are our synthesis, not claims about OpenLegend's current implementation. They do not authorize engineering changes, reproduce BioWare's fiction or require adopting its morality dichotomy.
