## The Homestead makes people part of production

The original guide distinguishes harvesters, who supply purchasable resources, from artisans, who transform supplied inputs. Lance, Ellen, Big Dave and the other named residents gain capabilities through their sequential personal missions rather than an unrestricted skill-point menu. The Accounting Book mediates crafting and trade. Convoy profits depend on more than an item's nominal value: route duration, merchant multipliers, taxation and loss risk all matter. A land convoy can create a rescue problem; a naval loss is not necessarily recoverable by rushing to the scene. [S01](#s01)

**Interpretation:** this is unusually relevant to inhabited-world research. A person can matter as a character, a visible worker and a practical prerequisite. The danger is that all three collapse into an unlock icon if the menu makes the person disappear from the player's attention. Another risk is that production becomes a system for earning more money mainly to do more production, without helping a goal the player values.

The Encyclopedia of the Common Man asks the player to observe residents performing ordinary activities. The inspected guide distinguishes tasks such as repairing a wheel, sewing and examining herbs. These are authored routines the player can notice, not evidence that the settlement runs an unrestricted autonomous labor economy. [S02](#s02)

## Reception is divided about whether the extra systems matter

Conrad Zimmerman's October 30, 2012 Destructoid review values movement, naval play and multiplayer while arguing that many secondary systems mostly feed more secondary tasks. Kevin VanOrd's GameSpot Wii U review is more receptive to the Homestead as a place made meaningful through personal relationships, while reporting substantial technical problems. Their disagreement is not resolved by counting available activities. [S03](#s03) [S04](#s04)

Peter Chapman's November 2, 2012 TheSixthAxis review enjoys the physicality of combat and the frontier while finding the long tutorial and narrow scripted missions at odds with the wider world. Alex Culafi's November 28 Nintendo World Report review similarly values the setting but describes unreliable movement/perception and a slow opening. The final pass will preserve the different platforms and reading limits rather than manufacturing one universal launch experience. [S05](#s05) [S06](#s06)

## A remaster can change rules without inventing every advertised capability

Ubisoft released Remastered on March 29, 2019 for PS4, Xbox One and PC, with Switch following May 21. It includes original solo DLC and the separate Liberation adventure. The March update advertises interface, economy and stealth changes. Its broad language about new bush hiding, whistling and double assassinations should not be read as proof all those actions were entirely absent from 2012; the original guides and later clarification distinguish narrower previous forms. [S07](#s07) [S08](#s08)

Bryan Rose's May 30, 2019 Nintendo World Report review finds worthwhile content but objects to the Switch port's audio and visual problems. Its erroneous description of Aveline as a Templar is not adopted; Ubisoft identifies her as an Assassin. A review can be useful testimony without becoming authoritative on every fact in its introduction. [S09](#s09) [S07](#s07)

## Commercial and service boundaries

Ubisoft's February 7, 2013 financial release reports **more than twelve million sell-in and digitally distributed units**. That is not a count of concurrent users, independently confirmed retail sell-through or game-specific profit. [S10](#s10)

The August 31, 2022 service notice moved the relevant shutdown date to October 1 and distinguished original PC DLC activation from console access. A remaster's availability is not proof that original multiplayer or every old entitlement remains available. The final pass will keep original feature design separate from present access. [S11](#s11)
