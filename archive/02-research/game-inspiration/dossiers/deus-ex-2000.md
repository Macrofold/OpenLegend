# G117 — Deus Ex (2000)

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research pass and follow-up audit — September 26, 2026.** Reference research, not accepted OpenLegend requirements. Scope: original PC Deus Ex and its GOTY distribution, with PS2, community modifications and the announced remaster kept separate. Five independent PC review bodies were read in the original pass: four describe the original/GOTY game, while the fifth explicitly used the preservation-oriented Community Update. A PS2 review and a substantially modded GMDX review supply additional contrasts, not interchangeable mechanics evidence. The follow-up checked the full dossier against R01–R14 and preserved those original reviews, examples and source identities; fresh evidence is distinguished below. No gameplay, footage or source-code execution was performed.

## 1. Identity, editions and the first-session contract

Ion Storm developed Deus Ex; Eidos published it. Warren Spector's contemporary postmortem dates the Windows release June 23, 2000, while the current Steam listing uses June 22. The defensible common boundary is June 2000, not a supposed 2026 launch of the original. JC Denton is an authored nano-augmented operative whose abilities and methods the player develops. The campaign combines first-person exploration, shooting, stealth, conversations and character progression. It is not a classless life simulation in which every profession is supported. [S7, S10]

GOTY is the base-game distribution examined here, not Human Revolution or Invisible War. The 2002 PS2 adaptation, The Conspiracy, changes controls and subdivides environments; its performance and interface cannot be silently attributed to PC. The later multiplayer patch supports a separate competitive experience rather than a native co-op campaign. A 2001 review confirms its historical availability, not the health of present servers. [S4, S5]

**The remaster remains separate and undelivered in the evidence checked:** Aspyr's Steam listing displayed “To be announced” and no user reviews on September 26, 2026. The follow-up confirms that the project is announced but its **release date** is TBA and the listing says it is not yet available. Proposed visual, control, radial-menu and autosave improvements do not describe the original executable or establish delivered fixes. Community Update, GMDX and Revision are also distinct packages, with different preservation or redesign aims. [S16, S17]

The optional training mission explains core interactions, but Liberty Island then asks the newcomer to combine them in a broad, lightly directed space. Naylor's 2025 review describes this as an intimidating transition, not a gradual lesson. The opening promise is therefore unusually demanding: identify an objective, inspect the site and choose an approach before the game has proved that every initial investment will be useful. [S15]

**Interpretation:** Deus Ex's useful unit of freedom is usually the *problem*, not an entirely branching world history. A fairly stable objective can accommodate several routes, costs and ethical decisions. The player authors how events unfold within constraints, while an authored thriller supplies direction. This distinction prevents “systemic” from becoming an unsupported claim that all story and behavior are emergent.

## 2. Character, skills and augmentations

### Eleven skills, not one universal hacking statistic

The four ranks are Untrained, Trained, Advanced and Master. Five weapon skills cover Demolition, Heavy, Low-Tech, Pistol and Rifle. The remaining six are Computer, Electronics, Environmental Training, Lockpicking, Medicine and Swimming. Skill points improve capabilities rather than selecting a permanent class. Important differences include:

| Skill family | What the investment changes |
| --- | --- |
| Computer | Access to hacking, time available and higher-level security control |
| Electronics | Efficiency of multitools used on electronic devices |
| Lockpicking | Efficiency against pickable mechanical locks |
| Environmental Training | Use of powered protective equipment and rebreathers |
| Medicine | Healing obtained from medkits |
| Swimming | Underwater speed and breath capacity |
| Weapons | Handling, effectiveness and, for Demolition, mine-disarming margin |

Computer hacking, entering a discovered password and using a multitool are distinct operations. They can solve related access problems without being interchangeable skills. The historical reference contains numerical and typographic errors; its percentages are not reproduced as an authoritative executable specification. [S11]

### The augmentation vocabulary

Three starting systems are Infolink, friend/foe identification and Light. Additional augmentations occupy limited body slots, with a canister presenting alternatives. The principal pairs are:

| Body system | Canister alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| Arms | Combat Strength / Microfibral Muscle |
| Legs | Speed Enhancement / Run Silent |
| Subdermal | Ballistic Protection / EMP Shield; Cloak / Radar Transparency |
| Torso | Aqualung / Environmental Resistance; Regeneration / Energy Shield; Power Recirculator / Synthetic Heart |
| Cranium | Aggressive Defense System / Spy Drone |
| Eyes | Targeting / Vision Enhancement |

Combat Strength improves melee use; Microfibral Muscle moves heavier objects. Speed alters running, jumping and falls; Run Silent changes movement sound. Defensive systems distinguish bullets, energy attacks, EMP and environmental hazards. Vision develops toward enhanced sensing; the drone provides remote observation and EMP attack. Synthetic Heart temporarily boosts other augmentations rather than permanently purchasing their upgrades. [S12]

**The table lists canister choices, not a claim that all multi-slot body categories enforce a unique pair per slot.** Installation capacity and available canisters matter. Contemporary player accounts describe reserving both subdermal slots for the two concealment options. Installed choices are not freely respecifiable. Most active powers consume bioelectric energy; Power Recirculator itself must be switched on to reduce other drains. Cloak conceals from organic observers, whereas Radar Transparency concerns machines. Neither means universally undetectable in every circumstance. [S14]

**Acquisition, installation and improvement are separate milestones.** Finding an augmentation canister does not immediately equip its power: a medical bot installs one of its offered effects, subject to body-region capacity. An augmentation **upgrade** canister improves an already installed ability through the augmentation interface without returning to that bot. Skill points are a different progression resource; Synthetic Heart's temporary boost is not a permanent canister upgrade. This makes reaching infrastructure, choosing a permanent capability and deciding when to spend an improvement different decisions. [S25]

### Progression changes available plans

An investment is valuable when it changes a route, risk or recurring expense. For example, a rebreather and a swimming investment can both support underwater exploration, but one is an item commitment while the other changes the character. Community discussions disagree on whether specialization is worthwhile because they assume different knowledge, routes and tolerances for inconvenience. Advice that a skill is “useless” often assumes an experienced player already knows its substitutes. Exploit-based recommendations are not treated as the intended learning curve. [S22]

**Interpretation:** partial overlap is useful. It allows recovery from a build decision without making the decision meaningless, provided the substitutes have different costs. Perfectly equivalent alternatives would be cosmetic; a single mandatory skill would turn apparent freedom into an undisclosed prerequisite.

## 3. Actions, equipment and world interactions

### Combat is more than fast mouse aim

Personal aim interacts with trained accuracy, stance, target location, equipment and the time spent holding a sight picture. The early character can therefore feel inaccurate to a player expecting Quake-like handling. Ammunition scarcity and vulnerability encourage reconnaissance, positioning or disengagement instead of treating every guard as an obligatory fight. Non-lethal and lethal approaches exist, but a careless attempt can still alert others or leave an opponent able to respond. These features explain the retrospective reviewers' distinction between a deliberate hybrid and a conventional shooter. [S3, S15]

The weapon inventory includes pistols and their silenced variant, a mini-crossbow with ordinary/tranquilizer/flare ammunition, assault and sniper rifles, two shotgun families, knives, swords, throwing knives, crowbars, a riot prod, flamethrower, plasma weapon and GEP launcher. The GEP is powerful and bulky; the prod is close-range and particularly effective against an unaware target. Grenades distinguish explosive LAMs, gas, EMP and electronic scrambling. Gas interrupts exposed enemies; EMP targets electronics and can deplete JC's own energy; a scramble grenade changes robot behavior. Grenades can also be mounted as proximity devices. These are fictional game mechanics, not real weapons instructions. [S13]

**Nonlethal targeting has its own rules:** the baton and riot prod do not receive the ordinary headshot advantage, so transferring a firearm's aiming habit can produce a worse result. The mechanics reference distinguishes attacks from close behind, already-stunned targets and body location rather than reducing all circumstances to one damage statistic. A fire extinguisher can also stun human targets, making a mundane safety object useful in a confrontation. These are community-documented original-game rules; although the reference cites routines, this audit did not independently inspect or execute that code. Exact multipliers are not asserted. [S28]

### Inventory, upgrades and persistence

The grid imposes shape and capacity constraints rather than weight simulation. Large weapons compete with the small supplies needed for flexible approaches. Ammunition and nanokeys have separate storage. Most upgrades bind to the selected weapon: abandoning it abandons the investment. Paused management makes rearranging and comparing possible without simultaneous attack. Dropped objects may persist within the current mission, but the manual explicitly warns that later narrative returns do not preserve a universal personal stash. [S8]

Looting, purchases, mission rewards and exploration supply equipment; a general-purpose crafting profession, player shop, farm or settlement-production economy is not evidenced. Credits support specific purchases and transactions, not a fully simulated financial market. The completionist walkthrough makes visible how tightly knowledge and resources interlock: buying a tool, retrieving a modification and saving ammunition can be competing priorities rather than independent checklist tasks. Its preference for maximizing everything is the author's chosen challenge, not the game's required style. [S23]

### Stealth and movement

Walking, crouching, leaning, jumping, ladders, swimming and vent crawling provide a grounded movement vocabulary. Augmentations modify it instead of replacing the world with menu-based travel. Shadows and distance help concealment, but proximity, noise and electronic observation create limits. Boats, helicopters and other inter-mission transport deliver authored transitions; the player is not freely piloting every vehicle. Health damage can degrade bodily performance, so healing is not only postponing a zero-health screen. [S8]

### Information is usable equipment

Documents, conversations, credentials and maps are practical resources. Finding a login can make a security console useful without paying the same cost as forcing access. In Liberty Island, security information and a console connect doors, cameras and turrets; the site is not just a series of independent locked boxes. NPC assistance, hidden spaces and optional rescue provide reasons to examine both people and architecture. The walkthrough's specific maximum-loot route is one demonstrated arrangement, not the only successful plan. [S18]

The player can inspect a book or terminal, read email, acquire a nanokey, enter a code, use a lockpick, bypass circuitry, carry or throw suitable objects, break containers and operate switches. Some objects are movable while others are scenery; some doors are destructible while others are explicitly protected. “Everything is interactive” would be inaccurate. The interesting design question is whether the interaction distinctions are readable enough for an attempted solution to feel informed rather than arbitrary. [S8, S23]

### Social and AI scope

People have names, affiliations, information and authored responses; guards also have detection and combat behavior. These layers cooperate but should not be conflated. A character referring to a previous decision establishes a tracked consequence, not a general-purpose autobiographical memory system. Conversations and scripting support cooperation, distrust, coercion, assistance or hostility; there is no comparable simulation of marriages, children, jobs and daily household economies. There is also no full party-management or romance campaign. [S1, S4]

**Interpretation:** the design concentrates detail where the player can use it. The mission's question may be whom to believe, which access route is credible or whether an order is acceptable. The game need not simulate a city's full labor market to make those particular uncertainties meaningful.

## 4. Resources, failure and eight worked situations

The early game asks for survival with limited competence and supplies. The middle game offers stronger combinations and revisits familiar places under changed affiliations. The late game adds formidable personal tools and a concluding institutional choice rather than a permanent territory-management endgame. Skill rewards emphasize exploration and objectives; indiscriminately adding kills is not the same progression loop as repeatedly farming combat XP. Injury, spent ammunition, depleted energy and failed stealth create local costs. Ordinary death returns the player to a save rather than promoting a rival as in Shadow of War. [S3, S18, S21]

**Injury and recovery deserve a separate account.** JC tracks health in his head, torso, two arms and two legs. Losing the head or torso is fatal; disabled limbs can leave him alive with impaired accuracy or movement. With both legs disabled he is confined to crawling rather than ordinary standing, running and jumping. Medkits, Regeneration, medical bots and small food/drink/water recovery provide different responses. This player model should not be applied identically to every NPC: the community reference describes an additional aggregate-health calculation for humans and different robot/creature behavior. [S26]

The health interface lets a medkit target a specific body part. Medicine affects its usefulness, while a damaged leg can make restoring movement more urgent than topping up a healthier arm. The indexed item description supports this action; it is not an independently tested healing formula. No generic “25 HP” shorthand is adopted as a universal value. **Interpretation:** partial injury can change the next plan before demanding a reload, provided players understand both the impairment and a reachable recovery route. [S27]

**Method:** the following situations are constructed explanations based on the cited rules or walkthrough branches. They are not claimed firsthand sessions. They deliberately include imperfect information and limits.

### A. A password changes the entire room

**Intention:** enter a guarded section without exhausting tools. **Conditions:** a relevant credential has been found and its terminal is reachable. **Action:** use the login and inspect connected security options. **Interaction:** information grants control over several devices rather than only opening one door. **Result:** cameras or access can be changed before entering. **Next choice/limit:** preserve a quiet route or use security offensively; a credential does not grant global control of unrelated systems. [S18]

### B. A skill substitutes for a recurring expense

**Intention:** explore submerged routes. **Conditions:** scarce points, finite powered equipment and uncertainty about future underwater work. **Action:** compare Swimming with carrying a rebreather or choosing an augmentation. **Interaction:** permanent competence competes with consumable inventory and another installed ability. **Result:** different builds can reach similar places by paying differently. **Next choice/limit:** invest only when the intended style justifies it; an expert's route knowledge can make a specialist skill appear less useful than it is to a newcomer. [S22]

### C. Invisibility depends on who is looking

**Intention:** cross a watched corridor. **Conditions:** biological guards and cameras are both present. **Action:** distinguish organic concealment from machine concealment before activating a power. **Interaction:** observer type determines whether the ability solves the problem. **Result:** the player may still need to disable security or change routes. **Next choice/limit:** budget energy and account for uncovered observers; a power named Cloak is not a promise of universal immunity. [S14]

### D. A grenade is an emplacement, not only a thrown attack

**Intention:** prepare for pursuit. **Conditions:** an appropriate surface and a foreseeable approach route. **Action:** mount a suitable grenade rather than immediately throw it. **Interaction:** the item's spatial use changes its timing and role. **Result:** later movement can trigger the prepared effect. **Next choice/limit:** choose gas, explosive or electronic effect for the expected target; proximity, blast exposure and the possibility of an unintended encounter still matter. [S13]

### E. Freeing space can mean abandoning a strategy

**Intention:** take a promising new item. **Conditions:** the inventory contains a bulky, upgraded weapon and useful tools. **Action:** examine what must be dropped rather than regard the pickup as a free improvement. **Interaction:** capacity, acquired ammunition and past upgrades create opportunity cost. **Result:** the future range of approaches changes. **Next choice/limit:** retain specialization or buy flexibility; the dropped object's presence on a later mission cannot be assumed. [S2, S8]

### F. A physical exit is also a story decision

**Intention:** escape the raid while preserving Paul. **Conditions:** the 'Ton Hotel encounter has reached its escape stage. **Action:** remain engaged and leave through the front rather than use the apartment window. **Interaction:** the authored branch interprets the exit route as consequential. **Result:** Paul's later survival state can differ. **Next choice/limit:** the walkthrough documents a script boundary, not a universal rescue simulation; an apparently safe physical shortcut can have an insufficiently signaled narrative meaning. [S19]

### G. Refusing an order is an action, not just dialogue flavor

**Intention:** decide what to do with Lebedev aboard the aircraft. **Conditions:** Anna Navarre demands his death. **Action:** obey, continue the conversation or intervene against Anna. **Interaction:** speech, timing and combat affect who survives and what is learned. **Result:** the scene acknowledges conduct beyond selecting a tone of voice. **Next choice/limit:** accept the resulting hostility or information; the guide's maximum-XP preference does not settle the player's judgment, and its absolute claim that someone must die does not certify every exploit or alternative route. [S20]

### H. An ending is performed through the world

**Intention:** resolve the final crisis. **Conditions:** competing proposals and the relevant Area 51 systems are accessible. **Action:** carry out the required operations for a chosen conclusion. **Interaction:** an expressed position becomes a sequence of spatial and mechanical commitments. **Result:** one of the three conclusions plays. **Next choice/limit:** this is an authored terminal branch, not proof that every prior action determines a unique ending or that the world continues simulating after the credits. [S21]

## 5. World, story and presentation

### Major plot spoilers

The Gray Death and contested Ambrosia shipments initially frame JC's work for UNATCO. His brother Paul's defection and the encounter with Lebedev destabilize the official explanation. Anna Navarre, Gunther Hermann, Sam Carter, Alex Jacobson and Jaime Reyes embody different relationships to the institution rather than interchangeable quest dispensers. Returning to familiar headquarters and streets after allegiance changes makes existing geography carry new stakes. [S1, S4, S20]

The route expands through New York, Hong Kong, Paris and later research/military locations. Tracer Tong, the Triads, VersaLife, Nicolette DuClare and other contacts connect local conflicts to the larger investigation. Optional emails, books and conversations supply motives or context that are not always delivered in a compulsory scene. At Area 51, the proposals associated with Tong, the Illuminati and Helios culminate in different institutional futures. Those are the game's fictional choices; this dossier does not rank real political systems or treat its conspiracies as documented reality. [S2, S21, S24]

**Interpretation:** recurring uncertainty about an informant is productive when the player has reasons to compare claims and can still act without reading every document. Optional detail creates depth; mandatory exhaustive reading would create a different pace. The fiction also works through specificity—an office, a family relationship, a shipment or a local dispute—rather than only through vast abstract organizations.

### Art and sound are evidence channels

The low-polygon environments nonetheless distinguish workplaces, streets, laboratories and private rooms through lighting, arrangement and objects. The historical reviews show that technical spectacle ages differently from functional readability: Hamm found the graphics impressive in 2000; tankMage later found some faces and darkness distracting. These are temporal perspectives, not contradictory measurements of the same hardware. [S1, S3]

Ambient and action-responsive music helps distinguish tension and relief. Footsteps and other positional sounds can alert a listener before the source is visible. Bogdanowicz particularly values melodic regional themes and the contrast between quieter exploration and combat music, while noting some awkward accents. Naylor's later appreciation develops over time rather than arriving immediately. This suggests an attachment mechanism through repeated context, not merely a catchy isolated track. [S4, S15]

### Interface and accessibility

A quick-access belt, contextual object highlighting, notes and equipment screens externalize information that would otherwise be memory work. The manual allows player-written notes and annotations on stored images; important credentials can become persistent reference material. Menus pause the action, a deliberate tradeoff between tactical deliberation and continuous simulation. This is useful accessibility-related design, but it does not establish a comprehensive modern accessibility standard or solve small text and old display support. [S8, S7]

Dense darkness, unhelpful first impressions and uncertainty about detection remain barriers. The PS2 review adds controller aiming and frequent loading; the 2025 PC review used Community Update for practical modernization. These differences matter when comparing what a contemporary player encounters. No current operating-system compatibility, controller profile or assistive-technology test was conducted. [S5, S15]

## 6. Production, circulation and commercial limits

### What the developer actually documented

Spector's December 2000 postmortem lists approximately 20 full-time developers and six contractors, six months of preproduction and 28 months of production. It names Unreal/UnrealEd and the team's ConEdit conversation editor. These reported project figures are not a complete lifetime credits count. The article distinguishes a largely linear plot from local problem solving, describes scope cuts and changing skill design, and records difficulties with separate design groups. It also explains the choice to get the whole game playable before final refinement. [S7]

The same account rejects a lone-genius explanation and describes mixed effects from Ion Storm publicity: mainstream attention alongside recruiting and morale costs. Its deliberate menu pause favors tactical decisions over uninterrupted realism. Proposed budgets, discarded locations and unrealized ports in planning material are not shipped facts. No reliable final production budget or profitability figure was established here. [S7]

**Interpretation:** authored alternatives and reusable systems need not be enemies. A designer can prepare several credible routes while tools make unexpected combinations possible between them. The production risk is combinatorial testing: a new object capability can invalidate a carefully controlled encounter, while an overprotected encounter can invalidate the promised capability. Selective scope and consistent exceptions are more realistic lessons than “simulate everything.”

### Modding is a concrete continuation mechanism

Caustic Creative's Revision is a free community-made overhaul requiring the paid base game. Its October 13, 2015 storefront identifies redesigned environments, new music, updated rendering/assets and alternative gameplay modes including Shifter and BioMod. These are evidence of actual fan production and a distribution channel, not features that shipped in 2000. A screenshot or review using Revision can therefore misrepresent original geometry even while retaining recognizable locations. [S17]

GMDX changes gameplay more substantially; Kon explicitly used it. Naylor explicitly used Community Update and describes its purpose as quality-of-life preservation. Neither author's experience is discarded, but each is tagged so that readers can distinguish the game design from its maintenance layer. This is particularly important in a long-lived game whose modern first impression can be mediated by community work. [S6, S15]

The original game's purchased-copy model, later GOTY availability, a separately listed planned remaster and a free mod requiring ownership are distinct economic arrangements. No recurring subscription or original-game cash shop is evidenced by the reviewed material. Steam review totals are not sales totals, and a 25th-anniversary review is not a retention metric. Specific lifetime units, revenue, profit and current multiplayer concurrency remain unverified in this pass. [S10, S16, S17]

**Sharing hypothesis:** the portable story is often “I solved this obstacle another way” or “the game noticed what I did.” A screenshot alone may not explain it; a short narrated sequence or a contrasting playthrough does. Mods and challenge runs give experienced players new comparisons to make. This is an explanatory hypothesis grounded in observed community artifacts, not measured attribution of purchases or virality.

## 7. Reception — five PC reviews, plus edition/mod contrasts

These are individual written accounts whose substantive bodies were read in the original pass, not scores copied from an aggregator. Multiple authors share RPGFan as an outlet; they are independent reviews across different dates, not duplicate syndications. Historical bug reports and hardware judgments remain dated. The audit restores the original author/date/URL relationships rather than substituting a different, unverified review collection.

### Cameron Hamm — RPGFan, July 11, 2000, PC

Hamm praises the interplay of shooting, character development and multiple approaches, with conversations that respect the in-world situation rather than feeling detached from it. Responsive sound and changing music convey danger. He objects to expensive loading/saving on period hardware and reports a key-sticking bug. His enthusiastic evaluation of contemporary graphics differs sharply from later retrospectives. He supports freedom within missions rather than a claim that every plot event branches. [S1]

### metzomagic — January 2004, PC

This adventure-oriented account values observation, exploration, dialogue and bypassing a problem instead of winning a firefight. Scarce ammunition and energy make quiet routes consequential. Its criticism includes repetitive crate searching and familiar character stereotypes. Its statement about three skill levels excludes the untrained state; its generalization that augmentation upgrades all reduce energy use is not adopted as a precise rule. [S2]

### tankMage — RetroMaggedon, January 2019, PC GOTY

After an awkward opening, the reviewer finds exploration-based rewards and flexible problem solving compelling. The same account criticizes overly dark environments, angular close-up faces, inconsistent voice acting and inventory shuffling, including automatically looting unwanted weapons. Hardware and price recommendations are historical. Broad descriptions of mission rewards and characters' approval are not complete quest-script specifications. [S3]

### Robert Bogdanowicz — RPGFan, August 1, 2001, PC

This highly favorable review emphasizes distinctive characters, melodic situational music and varied mission approaches. It also notes uneven late-game difficulty and occasional visual glitches. The then-new multiplayer patch supplied a separate reason to return. The text conflates Electronics with computer hacking and briefly calls the developer a publisher; neither wording is accepted as a mechanics or company-role fact. This is a different author and review from Hamm, though from the same outlet. [S4]

### Tom Naylor — RPGFan, June 23, 2025, PC with Community Update

Naylor values experimentation and the way playable context improves a pulpy story, but nearly bounced off the overwhelming opening. He reports occasional stealth inconsistency and criticizes stereotyped depiction in Hong Kong. The music grew on him through play. He explicitly used Community Update: this fifth PC review is not mislabeled as an untouched launch binary. His broad claim of unrestricted freedom is qualified by the authored routes and ending structure, and his real-world political comparisons are not adopted as factual analysis. [S15]

### Mike Bracken — RPGFan, April 8, 2002, PS2 adaptation

Bracken likes the dense optional information and responsive world but is less convinced by hybrid combat, repetitive middle-game errands and inconsistent enemy detection. Controller aiming, darkness and smaller map sections with more loading are edition-specific concerns. This is supplemental evidence for The Conspiracy, not a PC performance review. It does not establish that every PC choice or interface survived unchanged. [S5]

### Kon — August 6, 2024, updated July 11, 2026, GMDX Community Update

Kon praises agency, music and reactivity while disliking the dated inventory. The author explicitly played with a gameplay-changing mod. This is a useful account of continuing interest and a modified experience, not evidence that every reported interaction belongs to unmodified Deus Ex. Its positive verdict does not certify the mod as a neutral restoration. [S6]

### Helpful Steam testimony

The English Most Helpful (All Time) surface was read September 26, 2026. Strunk Flugget's June 11, 2017 account describes discoveries across stealth, pacifist and combat approaches. Saleck's March 4, 2014 recommendation values tools, characters and music but criticizes empty spaces and long walks. Cxero's November 16, 2014 account praises reactivity and atmosphere while warning about the slow opening and proposing community fixes. These are qualitative accounts, not proof of every alleged GOTY bug. Current displayed hours are not publication-time hours; bodies may have been edited. The original pass's positive recommendations include meaningful criticism; its inability to retrieve the dedicated negative filter is now supplemented below. Real-world political analogies are not evidence that fictional conspiracies are true. [S9]

**Direct negative follow-up:** the English **Most Helpful (All Time), Negative Only** page was successfully read. **Jebediah Kerman** (April 27, 2020; 15.8 displayed hours, 686 helpful votes) praises the underlying game but reports lighting and save problems. **Julia-Lain** (October 29, 2021; 0.2 hours, 81 helpful votes) reports timing/speed problems that obstructed meaningful play. **Ob** (August 23, 2024; 0.1 hours, 33 helpful votes) objects to the maintenance burden and truncated dialogue. These are substantive access complaints, not three completed-campaign reviews or diagnoses independently reproduced here. Hours and votes are retrieval snapshots, not publication-time values or prevalence measures. [S29]

**Synthesis:** reviewers repeatedly value agency but disagree about how gracefully the combat, graphics, dialogue and onboarding support it. The useful criticism is not that old surfaces must be preserved unchanged. It is that modernization should distinguish needless friction from the constraints that make preparation and choice matter. The negative testimony adds a distinct access problem: appreciation of the design cannot compensate for being unable to play it reliably.

## 8. Transferable inspiration, dependencies and failure modes

The following are research interpretations, not adopted requirements.

**Make information change a plan.** A document matters more when it grants a credible route or revises trust than when it merely fills a codex. This requires readable connections between the fact and the opportunity. The failure mode is hiding mandatory credentials in arbitrary clutter; a fallback route can preserve agency without making observation pointless.

**Offer alternatives with different costs.** A tool, capability, ally and physical route can solve related problems while consuming different resources. This requires encounters designed around outcomes rather than one compulsory verb. The failure mode is a dominant option that makes every nominal alternative irrational. An alternative design is fewer routes with stronger consequences, rather than more nearly identical doors.

**Let competence change space.** Jump height, lifting capacity, breath and vision can alter which parts of a familiar environment are usable. This works when the environment communicates possible access before the upgrade arrives. It fails when visual promises and collision rules routinely disagree. Explicit affordance cues can be better than forcing every surface to be physically general.

**Track a few meaningful actions convincingly.** A named person acknowledging conduct can make the world feel responsive without an exhaustive personality model. The dependency is continuity and a plausible reason for that person to know. A brittle script that interprets an innocent exit as abandonment shows the limit: the tracked flag must fit the player's understanding of the action.

**Separate protagonist identity from method.** An authored person can support extensive variation in competence and conduct. That reduces the writing burden of a completely undefined protagonist while preserving expression. The failure mode is dialogue that insists on a personality contradicted by play; response variants or restrained characterization may work better than more branching plot.

**Support deliberation without requiring memorization.** Notes, logs, diagrams and a paused inventory can preserve complex decisions while reducing incidental burden. This depends on good organization and concise feedback. Endless text and inventory shuffling are not themselves depth; searchable records, clear comparisons and bounded storage can serve the same choices more gracefully.

**Let an ending close the story.** Local freedom need not require an endlessly simulated postgame. The dependency is honesty about where the authored experience converges and ends. Replay value can come from a different method or build instead of a compulsory perpetual progression loop.
