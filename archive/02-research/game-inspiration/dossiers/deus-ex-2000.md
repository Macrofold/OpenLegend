# G117 — Deus Ex (2000)

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research pass and follow-up audit — September 26, 2026.** Reference research, not an approved OpenLegend specification. Scope: Ion Storm's original PC game and collected edition, with the PS2 adaptation, later community modifications and announced remaster kept separate. Five full independent PC review accounts, a PS2 comparison and a modern mod-qualified review were read in the original pass. The audit read the complete dossier and checked all R01–R14; newly retrieved sources are identified separately rather than implying every original external source was reread. Helpful Steam bodies provide additional testimony, not a representative survey. No gameplay, source-code, present-day performance or multiplayer-service test was performed.

## 1. Identity, scope and the opening promise

Deus Ex places JC Denton, a nano-augmented UNATCO agent, in a near-future conspiracy in 2052. The player does not choose a species or a completely blank biography; specialization comes through skills, augmentations, equipment and conduct. The opening Liberty Island mission introduces the central contract: the destination is clear, but the route, method and treatment of people can differ. The game's depth lies in interconnected local possibilities, not a freely simulated planet. [S1, S11, S12]

The PC campaign combines first-person combat, stealth, conversations, access tools and investigation across authored mission spaces. It is not a settlement builder, a crafting-survival economy or a full relationship-party RPG. The later multiplayer patch is a separate competitive mode, not cooperative completion of JC's campaign. The PS2 release, **Deus Ex: The Conspiracy**, changes interfaces and divides some locations more finely; those port decisions must not be attributed to the 2000 PC launch. [S5, S21]

**Current-edition clarification:** the remaster itself is announced and has an Aspyr product page; its **release date** remains “To be announced,” and Steam says it is not yet available at this audit. Advertised autosaves, controller-oriented menus, new models and revised lighting are future-edition promises, not original-game features or proven fixes. The original phrasing “unannounced” referred to timing, not the existence of the project. Store feature tags do not establish a newly playable cooperative campaign. [S26]

## 2. Character development: skills, implants and capabilities

### Eleven skills, not a universal class level

The original skill list is **Computer, Electronics, Environmental Training, Lockpicking, Medicine, Swimming, Weapons: Demolition, Weapons: Heavy, Weapons: Low-Tech, Weapons: Pistol and Weapons: Rifle**. Each has four proficiency levels. Mission completion, exploration and secondary accomplishments grant points to allocate; ordinary kills are not the universal source of progression. Skills improve competence with tools, equipment or activities rather than supplying a conventional fighter/mage class tree. [S1, S2]

Computer training expands what a terminal hack can accomplish and how long access remains usable. Electronics improves the efficiency of multitools; Lockpicking does the same for physical locks. Medicine increases the usefulness of medkits, Environmental Training improves protective equipment, and Swimming affects underwater capability. Weapon skills modify effectiveness and handling, so placing a crosshair over a target does not make an untrained character equivalent to a specialist. [S1, S2]

The choice is therefore about recurring opportunity costs. A computer specialist may bypass a defense without spending tools; a heavily armed specialist may open a different route by force. No single skill automatically supplies all means of access. The reviews disagree about whether these possibilities remain balanced or whether particular weapons and implants dominate late play. [S12, S14]

### The complete augmentation families

JC begins with **Infolink, IFF and Light**. Found canisters offer paired augmentation choices, with body-region capacity limiting the final build:

| Body region | Choices / families |
| --- | --- |
| Cranial | Aggressive Defense System or Spy Drone |
| Eyes | Targeting or Vision Enhancement |
| Torso | Aqualung or Environmental Resistance; Regeneration or Energy Shield; Synthetic Heart or Power Recirculator |
| Arms | Combat Strength or Microfibral Muscle |
| Subdermal | Ballistic Protection or EMP Shield; Cloak or Radar Transparency |
| Legs | Run Silent or Speed Enhancement |

These are not simply eighteen differently colored attacks. Strength can move heavy objects; a drone scouts and uses EMP; vision reveals different information; movement changes route feasibility; environmental protection makes a hazardous route practical. Cloak and Radar Transparency address different observers rather than jointly guaranteeing invisibility to everything. Torso capacity and repeated canister discoveries are important: the paired presentation is not a claim that every pair is globally exclusive in all circumstances. [S1, S3]

**Acquisition and improvement are separate actions:** a medical bot installs a found augmentation canister, requiring selection of one offered effect and an available body slot. An **augmentation upgrade canister** instead improves an installed ability through the augmentation interface without returning to that bot. Skill points do not replace either resource. A repair bot replenishes bioelectric energy rather than performing the installation. This distinction makes finding an item, reaching infrastructure and deciding when to spend an upgrade different milestones. [S27, S28]

Installed abilities consume bioelectric energy when used; cells and repair bots restore it. Upgrades improve effects, efficiency or access to information. Aqualung competes for the same general energy resource as other active systems, rather than being a free permanent solution to every water hazard. Power Recirculator and Synthetic Heart change how a larger augmentation loadout behaves. The player must distinguish owning an ability from sustaining it through an entire encounter. [S1, S3]

## 3. Objects, combat, stealth and environmental interaction

### Weapons and nonlethal routes

The arsenal spans pistols, rifles, shotguns, crossbow, heavy weapons, explosives and low-tech melee tools. Different ammunition can give one weapon distinct uses. A mini-crossbow with tranquilizer darts is not an instant universal knockout; delayed incapacitation can leave time for an alarm. The baton and riot prod require positioning and suitable targets. Pepper spray and gas can interrupt people without treating machines identically. A nonlethal intent therefore requires execution and target knowledge, not simply selecting a morality toggle. [S1, S4]

**Aiming rule worth correcting before transfer:** the baton and riot prod do not receive the ordinary headshot damage advantage. The documented damage rules distinguish rear close-range attacks, already-stunned targets and body location; the ordinary advice to aim every weapon at the head can therefore be counterproductive for these tools. Fire extinguishers can also stun human targets, giving a mundane safety object a tactical use. The community description cites game routines, but this audit did not independently inspect or execute those routines. No exact damage multiplier is needed to understand the distinction. [S31]

Ammunition, inventory space and access to supplies constrain the loadout. Weapon modifications can improve accuracy, range, reload, recoil, magazine capacity or add particular functionality where the weapon accepts it. A suppressor is not compatible with every gun. Permanently improving an early weapon can preserve its relevance; finding a powerful new weapon can instead force the player to reorganize inventory and ammunition priorities. [S1, S4]

The **Dragon's Tooth Sword** is a notable later power spike, not representative of every low-tech weapon. Contemporary reviews that find combat increasingly easy often refer to accumulated augmentation and equipment advantages. That progression can communicate transformation while also weakening the need to improvise. [S12, S14]

### Space, access and the meaning of information

The PC inventory is a spatial grid. Large weapons occupy multiple cells, while some consumables stack. Carrying a heavy solution to every problem is therefore not costless. Keys join a nano-keyring rather than each consuming another large equipment slot; discovered codes and notes live in information interfaces. Physical objects can be carried, thrown, stacked or broken when their rules allow it. Crates can be both cover and a route upward. [S1]

Locks and electronic security are different systems. A lockpick attacks lock strength; a multitool bypasses eligible electronic devices; a terminal can reveal information or control linked systems. A key, password, alternate route or sufficiently destructive weapon can save consumable tools. A door's lock strength and physical strength are not the same property, and an indestructible door is not made destructible merely by shooting longer. [S1, S7]

Computers and datacubes carry passwords, correspondence and evidence. Reading them can change the next action, not merely fill a lore collection. Hacking security can deactivate cameras, open doors or alter turret behavior where the specific terminal exposes those controls. It does not grant universal access to every device in the level. ATM access supplies money through a different kind of computer interaction. [S1, S7]

### Perception, bodies and alternate routes

Sight, sound, light, stance and surface material shape stealth. Crouching and choosing a quieter path can matter more than simply moving slowly through an exposed doorway. A discovered body can change enemy behavior. Carrying an unconscious person to cover is different from leaving a visible obstacle in a patrol's route; nonlethal play does not guarantee that person is safe from later environmental harm. [S1, S8]

Water, electricity, gas, fire, machinery and explosive containers provide conditional routes and hazards. The world is not uniformly destructible or fully simulated, but enough objects have consistent uses to support combinations the level designer need not individually name. JC can move an object into a useful position even when no quest marker labels that action. This is the central distinction between a generally available verb and a one-off scripted solution. [S1, S6]

## 4. Resources, progression, economy and failure

### Several kinds of scarcity

Health, bioelectric energy, ammunition, tools, credits and inventory capacity constrain different decisions. Credits buy supplies, equipment or information from specific sellers; this is not a player-driven market with production chains. Food and drink can restore small amounts of health, but the campaign does not impose a general hunger/sleep survival loop. Protective suits and rebreathers are usable equipment, not freely crafted permanent immunities. [S1, S4]

**Injury is more than a single shrinking bar:** JC tracks head, torso, both arms and both legs. Losing all head or torso health is fatal; disabled limbs can leave him alive but less accurate or mobile. With both legs disabled, ordinary walking, running and jumping are unavailable. Medkits, Regeneration, medical bots and small food/water recovery provide different responses. This describes the player's health model; human NPCs have an additional aggregate-health calculation, while robots and creatures are not simply identical six-limb humans. [S29]

A medkit can be applied to a chosen body part through the health interface rather than blindly consumed. Medicine affects its effectiveness; an injured leg may make restoring movement more useful immediately than topping up an arm. The guide's older generic “25 HP” shorthand is not used as a current universal value. Our interpretation: partial injury can change the available plan before forcing a reload, provided recovery remains understandable and reachable. [S30]

Early progression emphasizes learning the first mission's possibilities and selecting a practical specialization. Midgame adds augmentations, new weapons, more consequential information and changes in affiliation. Later play can make familiar obstacles easier while asking the player to interpret larger political and personal stakes. That is not an open-ended postwar society or an infinite level treadmill. [S11, S12, S14]

### Failure and continuity

Death ordinarily returns the player to a save. Important people can die or be spared, and some later scenes acknowledge earlier conduct. A failed conversation or noisy infiltration can leave a playable situation rather than instantly invalidating the whole mission. Not every apparent choice remains open forever, and the campaign still advances through a defined sequence of locations. [S6, S11]

The main ending is a conclusion, not a fully simulated aftermath in which the selected world order continues running. Replayability comes from alternative builds, routes, conduct and interpretation, plus separately identified mods—not from a native seasonal reset or live-service economy. Technical incompatibility and save trouble are maintenance problems, not intended narrative consequences. [S12, S18]

## 5. People, institutions and narrative

### A cast with conflicting interpretations

Paul Denton, Anna Navarre, Gunther Hermann, Alex Jacobson, Jaime Reyes, Sam Carter and Joseph Manderley establish different attitudes inside or around UNATCO. Tracer Tong, the Hong Kong triads, Morgan Everett, Bob Page and the AIs broaden the conflict beyond the opening institutional explanation. These are authored characters with particular roles, not interchangeable procedural recruits. [S11, S12, S13]

The player can listen, question, comply, refuse, pay, threaten or act without permission where an encounter supports it. NPCs sometimes comment on killings, discovered secrets or apparently minor behavior. Such recognition does not imply an unrestricted dialogue generator or a complete simulation of every resident's private beliefs and routine. The game has allies and temporary cooperation, not a fully controllable companion party, romance tree or dynasty system. [S11, S13]

The opening NSF conflict teaches that the organization labeling someone a terrorist is itself a source with interests. The player's understanding changes through encounters and evidence. This is a fictional narrative device, not a factual claim about real political institutions or a recommendation about real political choices. [S12, S13]

### Meaningful story spoilers

The conspiracy leads from New York through Hong Kong and Paris to secret facilities and Area 51. The Gray Death, Ambrosia, augmentation technology and competing organizations connect intimate choices to control of infrastructure and information. Paul can become more than a briefing contact because the player may act to preserve him. Anna and Gunther can be confronted through force or through information about their systems. [S6, S11]

The final alternatives associated with Tong, Everett and Helios are not simply three accumulated morality scores. They ask what kind of authority or infrastructure the player is willing to preserve, dismantle or join. The player chooses among authored resolutions near the end; earlier conduct supplies context and local consequences rather than mechanically determining one unavoidable ending. [S13, S14]

**Interpretation:** the story's strongest connection to mechanics is that information repeatedly becomes actionable. A password, testimony or killphrase can alter physical possibilities. The weakness is that a player can still experience much of the dialogue as exposition, especially when late combat has become routine. Both responses appear in the reviews below.

## 6. Eight worked situations

These are constructed explanations of documented rules or attributed developer examples, not claimed firsthand gameplay. Conditions and limits are retained.

### A. Reach the Liberty Island objective without accepting one route

**Intent:** enter the statue and reach the mission target. **Conditions:** guards, locked access and alternate approaches. **Actions:** compare a direct assault, a quieter entry, a key/tool route or climbing through available geometry. **Interaction:** character skills, carried resources and observation support different paths. **Result:** the same objective can be reached with different casualties and expenditures. **Next choice/limit:** decide how to handle the target; freedom of approach does not mean every wall is destructible. [S1, S11]

### B. Use a heavy object as a mobility tool

**Intent:** reach an elevated opening. **Conditions:** movable objects and sufficient lifting capability. **Actions:** relocate and stack an appropriate object instead of looking only for a labeled ladder. **Interaction:** strength, object movement and traversal combine. **Result:** an ordinary prop becomes a route. **Next choice/limit:** preserve access for retreat or continue; object weight and collision still constrain the solution. [S1, S3]

### C. Spend information instead of a multitool

**Intent:** pass a security barrier. **Conditions:** an electronic lock and a discoverable code or linked terminal. **Actions:** read correspondence or explore for credentials, then use the legitimate interface. **Interaction:** investigation replaces a consumable bypass. **Result:** tools remain available for another obstacle. **Next choice/limit:** risk more searching or spend now; not every code is nearby and not every terminal controls every device. [S1, S7]

### D. Turn a defense into someone else's problem

**Intent:** cross a guarded area. **Conditions:** access to the relevant security controls. **Actions:** hack or log into the terminal and alter the available camera/turret settings. **Interaction:** computer capability changes the local threat configuration. **Result:** the player can avoid personally shooting every guard. **Next choice/limit:** consider collateral effects and escape timing; a terminal's exposed controls define the actual authority. [S1, S7]

### E. A tranquilizer hit does not finish the plan

**Intent:** incapacitate a guard without killing him. **Conditions:** a suitable target, dart ammunition and a place to hide. **Actions:** fire, break sight and account for the delay before collapse. **Interaction:** weapon effect timing and perception determine whether an alarm occurs. **Result:** a nonlethal outcome may still require movement and patience. **Next choice/limit:** conceal the body or leave; a delayed effect is not a guaranteed silent instant knockout. [S4, S8]

### F. Preserve a person rather than merely obey the exit cue

**Intent:** help Paul survive the apartment confrontation. **Conditions:** the relevant story scene and enemies arriving. **Actions:** remain and intervene rather than treat the suggested escape as the only possible objective. **Interaction:** authored scene state recognizes a different commitment. **Result:** later continuity can change. **Next choice/limit:** survive the encounter and continue; this is a specific supported branch, not proof every endangered NPC can always be saved. [S6]

### G. A small civilian outcome extends into another visit

**Intent:** respond to a local threat or exploitation. **Conditions:** the Hell's Kitchen characters and relevant conversation/action opportunities. **Actions:** intervene in the encounter rather than ignore it as scenery. **Interaction:** a local decision can affect what a returning player hears or finds. **Result:** the neighborhood becomes more than a transit corridor. **Next choice/limit:** revisit and observe; not every civilian has a comparable authored chain. This is an interpretation of the game's documented local reactivity, not a universal schedule simulation. [S11, S13]

### H. Choose which observer to evade

**Intent:** cross surveillance without fighting. **Conditions:** an installed stealth-related augmentation and enough energy. **Actions:** distinguish human observers from machines, then activate the appropriate ability and plan the route. **Interaction:** different detection systems make two superficially similar powers meaningfully different. **Result:** a build choice creates a specific kind of access. **Next choice/limit:** reserve energy, use another route or disable the remaining observer; neither power automatically solves every form of detection. [S1, S3]

## 7. Presentation, interface and production

### Information is part of the physical experience

The first-person view, object highlight brackets, contextual text, crosshair convergence and body-health display expose what JC can currently do. The inventory, notes, images and conversation history are not merely decorative menus: they store actionable information and support planning between dangerous moments. An interface that requires reading can make knowledge useful while also imposing friction. [S1, S11]

Alexander Brandon, Michiel van den Bos and Dan Gardopée are associated with the original music. The score's shifting modes and memorable location themes support changes between exploration and danger. Reviews praise atmosphere more consistently than character models and animation. Contemporary technical limits should not be confused with an intentional demand for unreadable darkness on modern hardware. [S11, S12, S18]

The game communicates personality through voices, text, spaces and reactions rather than a modern cinematic conversation system. Some performances sound stiff; some players regard that awkwardness as part of its character. The PS2 adaptation's radial/streamlined interfaces and changed inventory are responses to a different input context, not evidence that the original PC interface had those features. No hands-on accessibility assessment was performed. [S5, S21]

### Primary development lessons

Warren Spector's postmortem describes the effort to combine role-playing, simulation and action around player choice. Early concepts were much larger; scope reduction and stronger focus were necessary. The team used Unreal technology while building its own game-specific systems. Spector identifies successful cross-disciplinary work alongside planning, staffing and communication problems. This is a creator's retrospective account, not a complete independent audit. [S6]

The published project data gives a **$5 million budget**, a **six-month concept period**, approximately **28 months of development** and a **peak team of about twenty people**. These are the postmortem's reported figures, not a current inflation-adjusted estimate or a calculation inferred from sales. They should not be confused with the much larger ambitions of discarded early concepts. [S6]

A central production principle is allowing general systems to combine in ways the designer need not individually script, while still authoring understandable spaces and goals. Spector's account also shows the cost: accommodating several approaches increases the burden of testing, communication and level design. “Emergent” does not mean free content or absence of authorial work. [S6]

### Preservation, mods and adaptation

The original SDK and community work enabled new missions, total conversions and revisions. **Revision**, **GMDX** and the **Community Update** are different projects, not interchangeable labels for vanilla Deus Ex. A review played with one of them can illuminate reception while requiring explicit qualification about controls, AI, layout or rendering. The original game should not receive automatic credit for every later community improvement. [S18, S22, S23]

The PS2 port makes a separate case study in preserving choices while changing presentation. Contemporary console reviews discuss smaller map segments, altered inventory and controller handling. Those changes can make the same basic story more approachable in one respect while weakening spatial continuity in another. [S5, S21]

The announced Aspyr remaster is another distinct adaptation. Its current store promises updated visuals and quality-of-life changes, but the audit did not establish a delivered build or public user-review population. A product announcement is not evidence that those changes have preserved all original interactions successfully. [S26]

## 8. Distribution, circulation and commercial context

The original was a premium boxed PC game, later collected and ported, with ongoing digital distribution. Its principal continuing value comes from replay, community work and cultural recommendation rather than a subscription, recurring in-game purchases or a persistent server economy. The historical multiplayer mode does not imply that its original discovery services still operate unchanged. [S1, S5, S18]

**Commercial limits:** the postmortem's budget and development figures are usable primary production data. A later Eidos report describes more than one million copies for the original game's commercial history, but that is a dated publisher account, not current lifetime digital sales or net profit. No defensible title-level revenue, margin or retention series was established. Sales, budget and critical reputation should not be combined into an invented profitability calculation. [S6, S24]

**Interpretation:** the shareable unit is often a discovered possibility: an unexpected route, an overlooked response, a small callback or a combination of tools. Such anecdotes can keep a game culturally visible long after release. Mods also provide renewed reasons to return. These mechanisms are plausible explanations, not measured attribution of sales to memes, reviews or individual creators. [S13, S18]

## 9. Five independent written reviews and contrasting reception

These five PC accounts were read through their substantive conclusions in the original pass. Different editions and modifications are identified. An additional console review and mod-specific review provide contrast but do not silently substitute for the base-game evidence.

### Tom McNamara — IGN, June 23, 2000

McNamara values the combination of role-playing development, investigation and flexible action. The opening's several approaches establish a game that rewards curiosity rather than merely fast aiming. Technical presentation and AI are less uniformly strong than the design ambition. His review is a launch-era PC account, not a benchmark of current rendering or community fixes. [S11]

### Desslock — GameSpot, June 27, 2000

Desslock praises the depth and replay value of its interconnected choices, while recognizing that its shooting does not behave like a pure reflex shooter. Story, exploration and specialization make a stronger whole than any isolated component. Some encounters and late advantages reduce challenge. The review's assessment of AI and balance belongs to the original release context. [S12]

### Kieron Gillen — PC Gamer retrospective

Gillen emphasizes the density of meaningful possibilities and the pleasure of discovering that the game anticipated—or at least consistently supported—an unusual action. The remembered small responses matter alongside the large conspiracy. His retrospective is evidence of enduring interpretation, not a contemporary sales measurement or proof that every apparent improvisation was unplanned by the developers. [S13]

### Robert Bogdanowicz — RPGFan, September 12, 2000

Bogdanowicz appreciates the narrative, freedom and character development while finding the visual and combat execution less consistently impressive. The growing power of equipment and augmentations can diminish later tension. This gives a useful counterpoint to reading every increase in player capability as unqualified improvement. [S14]

### James Troughton — TheGamer, July 11, 2021

Troughton revisits the game with the **Community Update** installed and finds its choices and reactivity still compelling. That qualification is essential: modern usability in this account is not a clean test of the unmodified 2000 executable. The article is a full evaluative play account, not just a list of nostalgic memories, but its experience cannot establish original rendering or control behavior. [S15]

### Additional edition-specific criticism

The PS2 review by Greg Kasavin discusses the adaptation's changed controls, inventory and segmented spaces, while a later GMDX-focused account evaluates that mod's revisions. These are separate objects of criticism. Combining their praise into a claim that the original PC game shipped with all those improvements would erase the very tradeoffs the sources reveal. [S21, S23]

### Helpful Steam player evidence

The accessible English helpful-review surface was inspected September 26, 2026. Positive bodies praise the game's freedom, story, atmosphere and replayability, while also describing the need for community fixes. Negative bodies on the visible mixed page chiefly describe technical access problems rather than rejecting the underlying design. The original dedicated negative-filter request failed; a later audit recovered that page successfully, as described below. [S18, S32]

The source's displayed hours are current account totals, not necessarily hours at the time of publication. A long-time fan with few Steam hours may have played an earlier copy; that possibility does not validate every claim. Reports of darkness, speed or compatibility problems establish individual experiences, not a universal diagnosis or tested solution. Mod recommendations in the reviews are advice, not proof that each mod preserves the original rules. [S18]

**Direct negative follow-up:** on English **Most Helpful (All Time), Negative Only**, **Jebediah Kerman** (April 27, 2020; 15.8 displayed hours, 686 helpful votes) praises the game but reports lighting and save trouble in the Steam version. **Julia-Lain** (October 29, 2021; 0.2 hours, 81 helpful votes) describes speed/timing problems before meaningful play. **Ob** (August 23, 2024; 0.1 hours, 33 helpful votes) objects to the work required to repair old games, including truncated dialogue. These are substantive objections to access and maintenance, not three completed-campaign verdicts. Their causal diagnoses are not independently verified; counters are capture-time values. [S32]

**Synthesis:** critical enthusiasm centers on connected agency and actionable information. Friction separates into original combat/presentation limitations, late-game balance, and modern preservation problems. Fixing a renderer does not answer a criticism of the ending; praising the story does not solve a new player's inability to run the game.

## 10. Transferable patterns and counterexamples

**Interpretation, not implementation requirements:**

- **Make information spendable through action.** A discovered code, relationship or weakness should change what the player can do. Lore that never affects a decision provides a different kind of value and should not be mistaken for systemic depth.
- **Separate objective from method.** A clear destination can coexist with several routes and tools. This requires consistent admission rules and enough feedback to let players reason. A maze of hidden exceptions creates frustration rather than agency.
- **Let a general verb serve several purposes.** Carrying a crate can hide, block, climb or manipulate a hazard. The alternative is a bespoke prompt for each use, which is easier to control but offers less combinatorial ownership.
- **Preserve the costs of specialization.** Skills, body slots, energy and inventory make different builds meaningfully different. If one late weapon or augmentation erases every tradeoff, the most interesting decisions can disappear precisely when the player becomes powerful.
- **Acknowledge small conduct precisely.** A local callback can make a place feel attentive without simulating every person's entire life. The dependency is reliable recognition; inconsistent responses can expose the abstraction more sharply than no response at all.
- **Do not confuse permissive systems with an unbounded plot.** Local flexibility can coexist with an authored campaign and finite endings. Clear expectations are more honest than promising that every political or personal outcome is simulated.
- **Treat preservation as part of access.** A historically important design remains inaccessible when setup or rendering fails. Community maintenance is valuable, but its changes and labor should be credited rather than silently attributed to the original release.
