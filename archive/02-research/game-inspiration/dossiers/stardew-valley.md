# Stardew Valley — the same routine can be care or obligation

**G30 · Full research pass · September 26, 2026.** Research and interpretation, not an implementation specification. No new gameplay, benchmark, accessibility-device test or video playback was conducted. **Spoilers:** progression systems, relationship premises and late-game locations, but not full character-event resolutions.

[Original chapter](../games/stardew-valley.md) · [Preserved mechanics](../mechanics/stardew-valley-calendars-gifts-and-useful-routines.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md)

## 1. Identity, versions and the promise

Stardew Valley is a top-down country-life role-playing game about inhabiting a farm and a small community. The player leaves an unsatisfying office routine, inherits a neglected property and decides how to spend recurring days. Farming, fishing, foraging, mining, crafting, relationships and decoration supply different reasons to return. These are connected activities rather than a realistic agricultural economy or a town whose residents invent their own careers and dialogue. [P1][D1]

The 2016 launch, later multiplayer and subsequent content expansions must remain distinct. The accessed PC version history identifies **1.6.15**; console and mobile maintenance numbers differ. The February 26, 2026 anniversary confirms another update in development, not delivery of all announced future additions. The Switch 2 edition has its own controls and multiplayer limits. Neither an old feature list nor a newer platform label establishes universal feature or network parity. [V1][V2][V3]

**Interpretation:** the central promise is accumulation that belongs somewhere. A field, a friendship scene, a collection, a tool and a repaired public facility are different kinds of progress, but all attach to a recognizable life. The same structure can also turn leisure into a checklist. The dossier treats both responses as evidence rather than deciding that the word “cozy” settles the experience.

## 2. Choosing a place and learning the daily verbs

Character customization establishes appearance, name and a personal farm identity without choosing a combat class. Eight farm layouts provide different starting conditions: Standard, Riverland, Forest, Hill-top, Wilderness, Four Corners, Beach and Meadowlands. The choice persists for that save. Meadowlands begins with a coop and two chickens and supplies blue grass; it does not automatically mean special blue chickens or blue eggs. The Riverland start includes a Fish Smoker. A farm layout is therefore an initial opportunity and constraint, not only a background image. [M1][V4]

The basic actions are walking, talking, interacting, using tools, attacking, fishing, planting, watering, harvesting, placing, cooking and moving inventory. The hoe tills; the watering can supports crop growth; the axe clears wood; the pickaxe extracts minerals; the scythe cuts suitable vegetation. Weapons are distinct from tools even when both are held objects. A clicked target and the tile actually affected matter, which is why interface complaints recur in otherwise positive reviews. [M2][R3]

Copper, steel, gold and iridium upgrades require both bars and money. A stronger axe or pickaxe reduces repeated blows and opens tougher obstacles; hoe and watering-can upgrades affect the area handled by a charged action. The ordinary tool is unavailable while Clint upgrades it. The current trash-can upgrade is an exception: the old can remains usable and the upgrade recovers part of eligible discarded items' value. Upgrading is not merely a statistical level-up; it can change which projects and schedules are feasible. [M2][M3][M4]

The five skills—**Farming, Mining, Foraging, Fishing and Combat**—increase through relevant activity. Recipe access, proficiency and professions provide different benefits. Profession choices at levels five and ten specialize the resulting economy or activity, rather than making the other activities unavailable. After all five skills reach ten, Mastery creates a further progression path. There is no spell-class selection, native freeform spell-authoring interface or requirement to specialize exclusively in combat. [M5]

## 3. Crops, processing and the multiple futures of an item

A crop has a season, growth period and sometimes repeat harvests. Four seasons of twenty-eight days make timing a resource. A late planting can fail even when land, water and seed were all available. Corn's multi-season eligibility differs from an ordinary single-season plant; a greenhouse changes the seasonal constraint again. **Powdermelon is a current winter crop**, so historical statements that winter has no outdoor farming are too broad. Winter Seeds and other exceptions also matter. Missing a seasonal opportunity often means waiting rather than permanently failing the campaign. [M6][M7][P2]

The production inventory includes sprinklers, scarecrows, fertilizer, Seed Makers, Bee Houses, tappers, furnaces, kegs, Preserves Jars and animal-product processors. They do not all solve the same kind of repetition. A sprinkler replaces watering labor; a processor consumes an input and ties up equipment time; a chest preserves options. Ore and coal become bars, which can fund tools or manufacturing. A useful material can have several futures without one of those futures always being the optimal choice. [R5][M3][P2]

The earlier **keg versus preserves-jar** study remains intact: fruit, vegetables, hops and coffee beans produce particular outputs, with different processing times and value rules. Input quality should not be assumed to carry into every processed product. Value per input, value per equipment-hour and cash needed today are different measures. This dossier does not replace that study with a single allegedly universal wine recommendation. [P2]

The newer **Dehydrator** handles batches of five eligible fruit or mushrooms and completes them on its own cycle; grapes can become raisins rather than the ordinary dried-fruit output. A Fish Smoker offers a different processing route for catches. These machines broaden what a specialized farm can make, but neither eliminates the need for suitable inputs, recipes and capacity. Collection, gifting and preserving an item for a request can still be preferable to processing everything immediately. [M8][V4]

Cooking requires a learned recipe and usable ingredients, accessed through a kitchen or the appropriate portable kit. The refrigerator is connected cooking storage, not an arbitrary chest with identical behavior. One food buff and one drink buff can coexist; eating a new buffed food replaces the existing food buffs, while unbuffed healing food does not. **Cheese Cauliflower**, **Parsnip Soup** and **Seafoam Pudding** illustrate the links among relationships, farming and preparation. Recipes learned from broadcasts or friends are knowledge rewards as well as collection entries. [M9]

## 4. Animals, convenience and useful care

Coops and barns support different animal families and upgrade requirements. Chickens and ducks supply eggs, cows and goats milk, sheep and rabbits wool, and pigs find truffles under their appropriate conditions. Petting, feeding, shelter and mood/friendship affect this activity; a named animal can also become personally meaningful independently of its economic output. The system implements bounded husbandry behavior, not unrestricted animal psychology or a full simulation of domestication. The player's horse is transport rather than a produce-generating farm animal. [M10]

An **Auto-Grabber** collects supported barn and coop products into its limited storage. It does not pick up the pigs' outdoor truffles, pet animals or reproduce all experience earned by manual work. An **Auto-Petter** addresses another part of care and has acquisition routes beyond simply purchasing it on a Joja path. Automation therefore separates gathering from attention and supply. “I bought automation” does not mean every related responsibility disappeared. [M11][M12]

**Interpretation:** this is a useful place to ask what a player actually values doing. Repeated collection can be a satisfying morning ritual, a tax before adventure, or both at different stages. A convenience item can preserve the farm's identity while freeing attention, provided that it removes unwanted repetition rather than silently choosing what the farm should become.

## 5. Fishing, mines, equipment and loss

Fishing combines eligibility and execution. Season, weather, location and time determine possible fish; the catching minigame then asks the player to keep a moving fish within a controllable bar. Skill, bait and tackle affect different aspects. Merely buying a more advanced rod does not directly enlarge the bar, although it permits different equipment; the Training Rod has its own simplifying rule. Crab pots supply a separate collection route. This makes fishing more than a time-delayed gathering animation while also explaining why it can be relaxing for one player and frustrating for another. [M13]

The ordinary **Mines** contain 120 floors, with progressively different minerals, monsters and rewards. Elevator checkpoints make repeated expeditions cumulative. The bottom grants the Skull Key, which opens the Skull Cavern and also the separate Junimo Kart activity. The desert route, deeper challenges and later dangerous variants introduce different preparation problems, not simply a repeat of the same floor with a higher label. Health, available food, inventory space and the clock compete with the desire for one more reward. [M14][M15]

Swords, clubs, daggers, slingshots, boots and rings provide combat capabilities; different handling and utility remain relevant beyond nominal damage. The late **Forge** can combine two different rings or enhance eligible weapons. Galaxy weapons have a route to Infinity forms using Galaxy Souls and Cinder Shards. Combining two rings is not permission to merge arbitrarily many accessories or stack identical inputs without restriction. Existing enchantments and appearance handling have specific preservation rules, so upgrading and cosmetic replacement should not be treated as interchangeable operations. [M16]

Losing all health can cost money and carried items; Marlon's recovery service returns a selected lost item stack rather than undoing every loss indefinitely. Passing out from staying awake is a different event. Neither is ordinary permanent character death, and neither should be described with an outdated universal loss cap. The game retains the farm and larger history, giving recovery a place within continued play rather than resetting the entire life. [M14][M17]

## 6. Time, weather, routines and decisions

The usable day runs from **6am to 2am**. Sleep advances the date and normally saves progress; continuing late reduces recovery, and reaching 2am ends activity even with energy remaining. Exhaustion and health are different constraints. Outdoors, darkness changes with the season, while shops and villagers follow time-dependent availability. The game offers repeating opportunities rather than a single campaign countdown. There is no ordinary food-starvation death clock, even though eating can restore useful resources. [M17]

Weather changes practical plans. Rain can remove the day's watering work and enable a different fishing or social opportunity; certain events have their own conditions. The television, calendar and familiar routes make the world increasingly legible. A habitual schedule is not necessarily the most profitable schedule, but a missed shop opening can make the cost of an unexpected detour concrete. [M2][M13][P2]

**Interpretation:** the clock is productive precisely because the player cannot do everything. It creates chosen priorities, but a display full of unmet possibilities can make every unoptimized minute feel like failure. More available activities can therefore increase both freedom and pressure. A meaningful session should not be assessed only by how many in-game days someone completes.

## 7. People, relationships and the restoration story

Pelican Town contains connected households and recurring services rather than independent romance dispensers. Robin's household connects Demetrius, Maru and Sebastian; her work and weekly routine can affect when construction services are available. George and Evelyn's relationship with Alex supplies another family context. Pierre's shop, Willy's fishing knowledge, Clint's blacksmithing and the Saloon make particular people part of the routes a player learns. Their authored lives supply reasons to encounter them beyond delivering a preferred item. [M18][M19][P1]

Friendship can affect conversation, events, access and gifts. Daily contact, requests, appropriate gifts and event choices have bounded effects. Gift knowledge can be recorded or learned through others; it is not simply visible omniscient mind-reading. The ordinary two-gifts-per-week rule, birthdays, capped relationships and spouse-specific decay conditions mean that repeated gifting is not one identical action in every context. Telephone use is not automatically equivalent to an in-person friendship conversation. [M20]

The **Bouquet** and **Mermaid's Pendant** belong to an authored romance progression. Pendant purchase depends on relationship, housing and access to the Old Mariner under appropriate conditions; it is not a gift any stranger will accept as an immediate proposal. **Krobus** offers a roommate alternative through a Void Ghost Pendant. This is a different relationship form, not a secretly mandatory romance. Marriage adds shared domestic space and later events, but not a general-purpose commandable companion or dynamically generated family saga. [M21][M22]

The earlier observer-specific garbage-can example remains valuable: the social interpretation of the same action depends on who sees it, with Linus differing from ordinary disapproving residents. That is a small implemented exception with meaning, not evidence that all personal judgments are generated from a deep psychological model. The corresponding limitation is visible in negative reviews that experience affection as gifts exchanged for a fixed sequence. [P2][S2]

The **Community Center** links farming, fishing, foraging and other finds through bundles; the **Joja Community Development** alternative organizes improvements through payments. One route encourages breadth and seasonal collecting, while the other can support specialization in income. Both lead to concrete infrastructure changes rather than only a larger abstract completion percentage. The restoration ceremony is public acknowledgement, but does not establish that every later conversation will reflect every contribution. [P2]

Version 1.6 added more context-sensitive dialogue, custom gift reactions and alternating festival details. This directly qualifies older complaints about unchanged repeated dialogue; it does not prove that every desired form of reciprocity now exists. Its new Desert Festival, fishing events, Green Rain and raccoon-related quest line also add situated activities without converting the entire town into an autonomous narrative generator. [V4]

## 8. Later goals and continuing ownership

After initial farming and community objectives, the restored boat route leads to **Ginger Island**, with a separate farm, volcanic exploration, new people, puzzles and Golden Walnut infrastructure unlocks. Leo, Birdie and Professor Snail make different requests; the island trader uses barter rather than simply duplicating Pelican Town's gold storefront. A new place can give familiar tools different uses. The existence of more content does not imply that every player must pursue all of it to have a satisfying farm. [M23]

**Mastery** gives late skill experience a new destination: the Iridium Scythe changes harvesting, an Advanced Iridium Rod supports two tackle slots, and the Mini-Forge/Anvil and other rewards change equipment handling or preparation. These are concrete capabilities, not just another numerical badge after level ten. [M24]

**Perfection** aggregates broad achievements and late construction/collection goals. It is an optional completion contract rather than the basic condition for being allowed to continue living on the farm. Later money-based waivers provide another route, but the currency is earned within the game, not a real-money completion purchase. Multiplayer evaluation also has category-specific sharing rules; it should not be assumed that two half-completed collections simply combine into one finished individual achievement. [M25]

**Interpretation:** the early farm, a comfortable established life and full completion are different stopping points. Adding late-game objectives can address a critic who runs out of motivation, but can also extend a checklist another player was relieved to finish. A good reference study preserves that disagreement.

## 9. Eight worked situations

These are constructed consequences of documented rules and preserved studies, not new firsthand play reports.

**1 — Make a planting decision with a calendar.** You want another harvest before season change. Check the seed's valid seasons and growth time before spending money and clearing space. A crop that cannot mature in the remaining window wastes the opportunity even with daily watering. The next choice may be a quicker crop, a multi-season crop or another activity. A year can repeat, but a missed window still has a cost. [P2][M6]

**2 — Improve the tool without abandoning the field.** You want a wider watering action. Water first, verify an appropriate forecast or alternative watering route, then submit the can for its upgrade. It is unavailable during the intervening workday. The result is useful capacity only after collection; the next day's plan must account for absence. This sequence follows the documented timing, not a guarantee that the forecast or every special weather event behaves identically. [M2]

**3 — Sell, process, reserve or give away the same harvest.** You need cash but also want an artisan line and a bundle. A crop can be sold now, assigned to equipment, stored for the collection or used socially. Processing ties up time and capacity; preserving it keeps an option. The next choice depends on the immediate project, not a universal ranking of those uses. The original keg/jar comparison remains the detailed owner. [P2]

**4 — Distinguish a caught fish from one that is eligible to appear.** You want a seasonal fish and repeatedly fail. First check location, season, weather and time; then consider the catching bar and suitable equipment. Improving dexterity cannot summon a fish outside its eligibility window. Better eligibility does not guarantee landing it. The next decision can be another spot, a different date or practice with an easier catch. [M13]

**5 — Remove collection labor without removing care.** You install an Auto-Grabber and stop manually collecting supported products. It fills rather than granting infinite storage, does not gather outdoor truffles and does not automatically pet the animals. The next improvement depends on whether collection, affection, feeding or processing is the actual burden. One convenience device is not an entire farm manager. [M11][M12]

**6 — Carry healing that preserves the chosen preparation.** You have an active food buff before mining. Eating a different buffed dish replaces it, while an unbuffed restorative food can leave it intact; a drink occupies a separate category. The next choice is what food to carry and when to consume it. “All consumables stack” and “nothing stacks” are both incorrect models. [M9]

**7 — Let the observer change the social outcome.** You search a public bin while a resident can see. Their reaction can differ from Linus's, so a global rule that every witness despises the same act misses the implemented distinction. The next decision might involve timing or context. This is an authored social condition, not a claim of general moral reasoning by NPCs. [P2]

**8 — Add a friend and discover that time behaves differently.** In co-op, one player reads dialogue while another keeps working; the ordinary solo pause is not automatically applied to everyone. They may divide tasks or agree to pause rather than assuming a conversation consumes no shared day. The next group decision is about pacing, not simply how to double output. Later platform and split-screen exceptions remain relevant. [P2][M26]

## 10. Multiplayer, modification and presentation

PC supports up to eight players, while console limits differ and the Switch 2 edition has separate local/online capacities. Farm progress and infrastructure are shared; skill experience, inventory and relationships have individual aspects. The host's save anchors farmhands, and an absent host is not equivalent to an always-running server. Optional wallet arrangements change spending coordination. Mobile multiplayer is experimental rather than an unqualified universal substitute for supported desktop connections. No current universal cross-platform pool is inferred. [M26][V3]

SMAPI and compatible community mods can change content, interfaces and routines. They are separate dependencies with game-version and platform constraints, not native mechanics guaranteed in every save. The player-guide documentation distinguishes content modifications from fragile direct asset replacement. No mod was installed or compatibility-tested here. [M27]

Pixel art makes seasons, crop stages, possessions and familiar faces recognizable; portrait dialogue and short written events do work that full cinematic staging performs elsewhere. Music by Eric Barone is part of the game's integrated authorship, not proof that simple graphics or one composer automatically produce comfort. The critics' contrast between welcoming atmosphere and awkward target selection shows that aesthetic coherence and interface convenience are separate achievements. [D1][R1][R3]

Controller support and remappable controls do not alone certify accessibility. Fishing timing, small targets, reading, busy inventories and the pressure of shared time remain distinct access questions. This pass did not test screen readers, assistive devices or motion/vision accommodations. The “low pressure” label supplied by an external accessibility sidebar is not a substitute for the directly contrasting player accounts below. [D2][R4][S2]

## 11. Production, distribution and commercial evidence

ConcernedApe's official FAQ describes **4.5 years of solo work for 1.0**, with later helpers beginning with 1.3, and identifies C# as the programming language. The mixed-age press history specifies responsibility for code, art, music and design and credits Tom Coxon's multiplayer work. The original Harvest Moon inspiration and PC opportunity remain in the prior chapter's dated interview account. This exceptional origin is not a recommended staffing model, and the later product should not be described as having no collaborators. [D1][D2][P1]

The official press page contains older feature counts and a conflicting short date field; its body and the anniversary establish **February 26, 2016** as launch. Its December 2024 **41-million** milestone is superseded, not disproved, by the developer's **February 26, 2026 report of 50 million copies**. Neither milestone states title profit, current monthly participation or the acquisition channel for each buyer. The inherited Steam peak remains a dated concurrency measurement, not a sales estimate. [D1][V1][P1]

The premium game's ports, localization, multiplayer and long-running updates are observable audience-expansion routes. The official FAQ permits monetized gameplay videos and identifies authorized merchandise, a cookbook and tabletop adaptation. These demonstrate cultural participation, not measured conversion rates. **Growth hypothesis:** a recognizable underserved life fantasy, positive word of mouth, visible personal farms and reasons for friends to return reinforce each other. Early sales before multiplayer establish that the first compelling solo experience was commercially meaningful; they do not isolate one cause. Exact development budget and net revenue were not established. [D1][D2]

## 12. Reception: five full reviews and helpful Steam accounts

Five independently credited substantive written review bodies were read through their conclusions. Their historical versions and later edits are kept separate; no score average or representative-population claim is made.

**Daniella Lucas, PC Gamer, February 26, 2016.** Lucas finds the breadth, charm and recurring farm life inviting, while acknowledging practical frustrations and a large time commitment. Her account shows why a missed ingredient or seasonal opportunity can turn a broad objective into a specific day's plan. This is the launch single-player experience, not a test of later co-op or island content. [R1]

**Patrick Hancock, Destructoid, March 7, 2016.** Hancock values characters becoming more particular through scenes and the way a closed shop can redirect a day. Fishing is a favorite; slingshot handling and significant bugs are reservations. He had not tested every relationship outcome. His winter-farming shorthand and technical failures should not be transferred unchanged to the 1.6 game. [R2]

**Jesse Woo, RPGFan, July 18, 2017.** Woo values meditative task management and a comparatively manageable crafting structure, but finds targeting awkward and relationships limited by repeated dialogue and gift routines. The original article date is July 18, not an aggregator's July 19 listing. Enjoying the town does not require finding every social interaction convincing. [R3]

**Ryan Craddock, Nintendo Life, October 7, 2017.** Craddock describes initial boredom/confusion becoming attachment as the larger life opens up. Portable day-sized sessions suit the structure, but launch saving is slow in his test. He explicitly had not seen all content. The page's later multiplayer sidebar is by Felix Sanchez, not evidence that Craddock played co-op in 2017. [R4]

**Mary Kish, GameSpot, updated October 6, 2017.** The full article and editor's update were read; the current displayed date and Switch reassessment are retained rather than labeling the whole text a pristine launch review. Kish values romance and opportunities that grow from curiosity, but finds repetitive annual behavior and a lack of direction after financial/domestic success. Her account contains incidental shorthand about animal buildings and a misleading character-family transition; mechanics and family relationships here use their dedicated references instead. Later island/Mastery content changes the endgame context without invalidating her historical experience. [R5]

**Steam helpful-positive sample:** the English Most Helpful (All Time) surface includes Morgan's August 14, 2020 account of relaxing play and return, PiggyWittaBlicky's March 6, 2024 shared-play account and Serephemeral's November 14, 2021 description of personal comfort. Their self-reported circumstances are not medical efficacy evidence. Current displayed hours and later edits are not necessarily contemporaneous with initial publication. [S1]

**Steam helpful-negative sample:** the same English all-time window, filtered negative, includes **Santa, November 10, 2023**, who values surprising unlocks but finds the actions monotonous and relationships insufficiently reciprocal; **PinkFlower, August 24, 2021**, who admires the quality but experiences time management as stressful; and the preserved June 2019 recognition complaint. Vote-bait and one-line jokes are excluded from causal analysis. The 1.6 dialogue additions qualify a claim that nothing changed, but do not prove that these preferences disappeared. [S2][V4]

## 13. Interpretation, coverage and preservation

### September 26 requirements-and-corrections audit

A fresh audit against the explicit mechanics inventory found the existing research substantively complete. These category boundaries make that coverage explicit:

- **Identity / classes / attributes / skills / leveling / professions:** character/farm customization and layouts are in §§1–2; five activity skills, levels, professions and Mastery are in §2/§8. There is no combat-class picker or native spell-school progression.
- **Items / inventory / weapons / armor / equipment / crafting / upgrading:** §§2–5 cover tools, tool upgrades, chests/refrigerators, weapons/rings/boots, Forge upgrades, crafting, processors, cooking and consumables.
- **Magic / spells / powers:** absent as a general player spell system. Capability growth comes from tools, equipment, recipes, food/drink buffs, skills and infrastructure.
- **Traversal / environment / object interaction:** tools reshape the farm; mine/elevator travel, minecarts/boat routes, horse and Ginger Island expand movement and access (§§2, 5, 8). Seasonal/weather/time rules materially change what the environment permits.
- **Activities / minigames:** farming, fishing, mining/combat, foraging, cooking, decorating, festivals and the separately unlocked Junimo Kart are all represented. Fishing is an execution minigame rather than passive gathering (§5).
- **Combat / stealth / loot / rewards:** mine combat, weapons/equipment and monster/mineral rewards are in §5. There is no general stealth system; social timing/route planning should not be mislabeled stealth.
- **Death / failure / recovery / economy / trading:** §5 distinguishes health loss from passing out and item recovery; §§3, 6 and 11 cover gold, selling, processing, service costs, Joja/bundle alternatives and in-world sinks. No premium currency is involved.
- **Story / relationships / romance / reputation / party / companions:** §7 covers friendship, gifts, heart events, romance/marriage and Krobus' roommate route. Relationships are individual authored tracks rather than a single faction-reputation meter, and spouses are not commandable adventuring companions.
- **NPC/AI behavior and schedules / factions:** villagers have authored schedules, households, services and event conditions (§§6–7), but not unrestricted autonomous careers. Joja versus Community Center is a consequential development route, not a fully systemic faction-war simulation.
- **World map / environment / quests / events:** farm maps, Pelican Town, mines/desert, Ginger Island, seasons/weather, bundles, requests, festivals and special events are covered across §§1–8.
- **Building / settlements / management:** farm buildings, animal housing, machines, decoration and spatial farm planning are central; this is personal farm management, not population-level colony governance.
- **Multiplayer / social / endgame / return loops:** §10 covers host/farmhand persistence and platform differences; §8 covers Mastery, Perfection, Ginger Island and continuing ownership after formal objectives rather than a forced seasonal reset.

The current-version reread also confirms that **1.6.15 remains the PC baseline**, while ConcernedApe's February 26, 2026 primary anniversary post says a new update is in development without presenting it as shipped. The same post reports 50 million copies; no later release or sales total is invented here. [V1][V2]


**Give routine a purpose the player owns.** An improving home, a preferred craft or a relationship can motivate ordinary work. Visible progress should not make an exploratory or social day seem inherently irresponsible.

**Make care different from a delivery score.** A preference can help an object express attention, but acquiring the preference table is not the same as growing mutual understanding. A richer social game should demonstrate reciprocity in later behavior, not only generate longer greetings.

**Automate the repeated action without confiscating the project.** Watering, gathering, processing and deciding what to make are different responsibilities. An assistant can remove one while preserving meaningful choices in the others.

**Use temporal scarcity carefully.** Recurring seasons make opportunities situated and memorable. Too many simultaneous windows can turn possibility into obligation. Those are design hypotheses for research, not approved OpenLegend tasks or claims that a clock must be removed.
