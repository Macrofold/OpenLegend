## Edition distinctions already established

The original and Pixel Remaster use limited casts grouped by spell level, rather than the common MP pool used in the Game Boy Advance and later remake lineage. Pixel Remaster is not simply the NES executable with replacement art: its rebalancing, maps, auto-battle and other interface changes alter pacing. It also omits extra dungeons found in the intervening remakes. Preserve these distinctions before comparing how long a party can remain inside a dungeon. [F1][F2]

The PSP Anniversary Edition includes the four Dawn of Souls bonus dungeons and an additional dungeon. Kevin VanOrd's June 2007 review distinguishes this extra challenge from the relatively simple main campaign, but questions selling the two early games separately after earlier combined releases. That is a historical value judgment, not evidence of current pricing or a claim that the extra content appears in Pixel Remaster. [F3]

## Review disagreements preserved

Brian Mackenzie's July 2021 RPGFan review finds exploration and speaking to townspeople rewarding, even while recording occasional uncertainty about the next destination and uneven bosses. His important character-creation criticism is economic: a newcomer cannot know all the implications of choosing expensive hybrid characters before committing to the party. [F1]

Chris DeAngelus's August 2021 WorthPlaying review values the approachable combat and modern interface but finds the remaster caught between fidelity and completeness. Restoring spell charges while reducing general friction may make early mages feel less useful relative to physical attackers. His statement that promotion merely changes statistics and sprites is not accepted as an exhaustive mechanical description; equipment and spell permissions require separate verification. [F2]

Joel Couture's August 2021 Siliconera review strongly values accelerated auto-battle, maps, richer monster art and the rearranged music. He reports that speeding routine encounters revived his interest, while retaining objections to the font, character art and screen tearing. This is launch-PC evidence, not proof that every issue remains in later editions. [F4]

VanOrd's PSP review is more skeptical about frequent encounters, lack of a quest log and thin characterization. His criticism and Mackenzie's later praise can coexist: an exploration structure may reward attention yet still become tedious when encounter frequency or navigation feedback does not match the player's preferences. [F1][F3]

## Production evidence and its boundaries

Hiromichi Tanaka's official retrospective describes joining Final Fantasy I to work on game/data design and experience tables while Nasir Gebelli handled programming. Gebelli had previously collaborated with the team on WorldRunner and Rad Racer. This is named firsthand production testimony, not an inference from the visible battle interface. The interview is explicitly a 2020 article republished in 2025; its header must not be mistaken for the date of the original recollection. [F6]
