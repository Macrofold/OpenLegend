# G105 — Mass Effect Galaxy

[Research roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Mass Effect 2](mass-effect-2.md) · [Library](../README.md)

**Research date:** September 26, 2026. **Pass:** R01–R14 complete, with access limitations below; the requested second-pass audit is recorded after the requirement map. Documentary research, not a playtest. **Spoilers:** the story and investigation examples disclose the conspiracy, mission dependencies and the sequel reward. This retired mobile game is an independent subject; it is not Mass Effect 2, Mass Effect Infiltrator or an installment of Legendary Edition.

**Central finding — interpretation:** adapting a familiar world does not necessarily preserve what makes interacting with it satisfying. Galaxy retains recognizable fiction, conversation choices and a compact investigation, but delegates ordinary shooting while making movement depend on tilting the viewing surface. Its most useful lesson is the need to evaluate the remaining decisions after simplification. Reducing buttons is not enough if the player mainly waits for automatic actions or struggles to express a basic intention.

## 1. Identity, release and the first session

BioWare developed Galaxy and Electronic Arts published it for iPhone and iPod touch. Contemporary availability reporting places the release across June 22–23, 2009: TouchArcade's June 23 article describes its appearance the previous evening, while some catalogs use June 23. This is a date-boundary discrepancy, not evidence of two distinct releases. The original American price was $4.99. It is a single-player top-down action adventure with dialogue, not a multiplayer RPG or a direct port of the original game. [S7](#s7) [S10](#s10) [S11](#s11)

The player controls the predefined Jacob Taylor. There is no Shepard creation, selection among six classes, recruitable field-party configuration or full trilogy-style equipment inventory. The opening attack on the passenger ship Arcturian Jade interrupts Jacob's holiday and gives the player a simple immediate objective before a wider investigation emerges. Ordinary play alternates among small combat spaces, illustrated conversations, scenes and destination selection. [S6](#s6) [S11](#s11)

A July 2009 update is documented as version 1.1, intended to improve performance on older devices. The surviving community reference repeats an estimated percentage from a contemporary announcement, but this dossier does not adopt that figure as an independently measured benchmark. Launch criticism and the patched application are therefore not assumed identical. By March 7, 2012, Game Informer's contemporary discussion explicitly described Galaxy as no longer available. A surviving catalog's price or purchase button is not evidence of a working storefront today. [S11](#s11) [S12](#s12)

## 2. The actual action vocabulary

Jacob moves when the device tilts. He normally fires automatically, while tapping a different enemy changes the selected target. The player can also activate a special attack, pause, inspect conversation options and select an available destination after a mission. The core encounter is not a free-roaming environment: clearing enemies usually unlocks the next combat segment or triggers a scene. A whole region's fictional importance does not imply that the player can explore its every building. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3) [S13](#s13)

| Player intention | Supported operation | Conditions, costs and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move out of fire | Tilt toward a safer position, using geometry to interrupt attacks. | Movement precision and the viewing angle interact; attractive decoration may not be reliable cover. |
| Focus on a threat | Tap the desired enemy while ordinary fire continues automatically. | Selection is not a guarantee of an unobstructed shot or immediate destruction. |
| Stop or weaken a dangerous opponent | Activate an available biotic, technical or explosive attack. | Range, cooldown, target state and positioning matter; a named power is not always ready. |
| Recover | Retreat long enough for shielding, or collect a suitable pickup. | Health and shields do not share identical recovery rules. Destructible cover can disappear. |
| Obtain a local resource | Target a breakable container and collect what it releases. | Ending the encounter can remove the opportunity to inspect remaining containers. |
| Avoid a confrontation | Choose a supported conversational approach. | Only some encounters offer a peaceful route; a different phrase need not produce a different ending. |
| Choose a lead | Select among available missions on the galaxy map. | The map is a bounded mission selector, not a simulated galaxy with arbitrary destinations. |

This table joins the shipped-game references with the critics' descriptions. It is a capability inventory, not a claim that every location supports every operation. [S1](#s1) [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4) [S13](#s13) [S15](#s15)

**Interpretation:** the player still decides whom to attack, where to stand and when to use an ability. The problem is not automation by itself. It is whether those remaining choices have enough consequence and variation to sustain repeated encounters. A system can reduce repetitive input while preserving judgment; it can also remove both at once.

## 3. Powers, pickups and progression without a large inventory

The compact toolkit has three special-attack families: **Stasis**, a shield-disrupting technical attack, and an explosive projectile. The technical ability is called **Shield Overload** in the detailed mechanics guide and described as shield sabotage in other summaries. Reviews also alternate between grenade, rocket and missile terminology for the explosive attack. Those labels should not be inflated into multiple separately available weapons. [S6](#s6) [S14](#s14)

Stasis prevents a target from acting while allowing Jacob to continue attacking it. In Galaxy it can affect a shielded opponent; Mass Effect 2's defense-gated control rules must not be imported. Shields recover after safety, whereas depleted health requires a pickup. Defeat restarts the current combat segment rather than permanently ending the whole narrative. Breakable crates are not as dependable as solid walls, and opponents can make a static position unsafe. [S15](#s15)

The explosive attack has a different delivery rule from the automatically aimed rifle: it travels straight and detonates on an object or enemy. Its radius can damage Jacob as well as opponents. A close obstruction can therefore spoil an intended distant attack or make it dangerous to its user. Position and clearance matter independently of selecting the desired enemy. [S14](#s14)

Each special power can receive one mission-linked upgrade: Bekke lengthens Stasis, Tortuga improves the explosive attack, and Ahn'Kedar improves shield disruption. An upgrade marker distinguishes the improved power. Availability colors differentiate a ready, in-range attack from cooldown or an out-of-range target. The exact duration and damage values are not supplied here because the available documentation does not justify a universal numerical table. [S14](#s14)

Containers and defeated enemies supply immediate-use benefits such as health, shielding or temporary attack improvement. This is local resource collection, not a backpack of trade goods. There is no researched evidence of a deep merchant economy, recipe system, property construction, profession tree or player market. The absence matters: Galaxy asks the player to use a compact toolkit, not continually compare dozens of rifles. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

The main progression is through missions and their small capability improvements. The documented sequence begins with **Save the Arcturian Jade** and **Into the Abyss**, opens three investigation routes, then converges on **Protect the Council**. The game saves after combat segments and dialogue/scenes. Starting another game replaces the current progress; this is not a trilogy-style New Game Plus carrying a developed character into a fresh career. [S13](#s13)

**Interpretation:** a small number of upgrades can be sufficient when each changes an important decision. It becomes weaker when an upgrade only accelerates an already repetitive process. The relevant comparison is not how many inventory objects exist, but how often the player must reconsider an approach because of something newly learned or acquired.

## 4. People and an authored investigation

Jacob's former commander, **Derek Izunami**, sends him to investigate after the attack. **Miranda Lawson** supplies contacts and leads, rather than becoming a manually controlled squad member. The early confrontation with **Clint “Black Eye” Darragh** demonstrates that dialogue can be a route through an obstacle, not only decoration between gunfights. A later appearance in Mass Effect 2 does not mean all of a character's sequel abilities or relationships are present here. [S8](#s8)

The three major leads connect **Illo Nazario** on Tortuga, the element-zero stockpile on Bekke and kidnapped scientists at **Ahn'Kedar**. The asari **Batha**, protected by the krogan **Nax**, supplies scientific assistance. Illo's illness is not an unrelated biography detail: it creates a reason to obtain help before expecting his cooperation. **Ish** provides access information. The design gives several pieces of fiction different practical roles without letting the player invent an arbitrary solution outside the authored conversation. [S8](#s8) [S16](#s16)

The order changes an immediate interaction, not only the order of map icons. With Batha rescued and unrefined element zero already aboard, Jacob can offer Illo a cure at their meeting; otherwise medical care becomes an unfinished dependency. Jacob can threaten to withhold help or offer it more freely, but the investigation still leads toward Jath'Amon. Batha's research also explains the material's purpose: the scientists were deceived about the disease, and her prototype needs shipboard supplies plus the rare element to become a vaccine. This is an authored rescue-and-treatment chain, not a player-operated laboratory or general crafting recipe. [S16](#s16) [S24](#s24)

**Major spoilers.** The evidence ultimately reverses the apparent threat: ambassador **Jath'Amon** is not simply the person who must be protected from the terrorists. The plan targets the Citadel Council. The final mission resolves that conspiracy, and the story returns to Jacob and Miranda's developing partnership. This is a fixed larger conclusion with local variation and ordering, not a simulation producing alternative governments or a free-form detective deduction engine. [S17](#s17)

Social scope remains narrow. Dialogue offers aggressive or accommodating responses and, in some cases, avoids a battle. There is no playable romance-progression system, universal gift economy, family simulation or autonomous daily routine for every resident. An ending scene suggesting personal warmth is not a marriage mechanic. Nor does a persuasive sentence establish an unrestricted diplomacy system. [S1](#s1) [S4](#s4) [S8](#s8)

## 5. Five worked interactions

These are **constructed rules-based illustrations**, not observed sessions. They assume the stated target, power and mission conditions. They explain the relationship among systems rather than guarantee one optimal route.

### A. Distinguish a bad plan from a badly calibrated input

**Intention:** move behind cover without drifting into incoming fire. **Conditions:** the player has changed how the phone is held. **Actions:** pause, hold the device at a comfortable neutral angle and resume before attempting the crossing. **Interaction:** recalibration changes how the same physical tilt maps to movement. **Result:** a more controllable attempt, not stronger armor. **Next decision:** cross or wait for a better opportunity. **Limit:** this does not repair unclear collision geometry or make every decorative object solid. The control's physical reference is part of the player's action system. [S2](#s2)

### B. Use control without importing the wrong game's prerequisite

**Intention:** prevent a shielded heavy opponent from firing during an exposed movement. **Conditions:** Stasis is ready and the target is in range. **Actions:** freeze that opponent, then move or focus automatic fire while it cannot respond. **Interaction:** temporary denial of action creates a usable interval even before shielding is removed. **Result:** a tactical opportunity rather than permanent safety. **Next decision:** use shield disruption, finish the target or retreat while the power recovers. **Limit:** Stasis does not stop other enemies, and its effect is temporary. [S14](#s14) [S15](#s15)

### C. A container can matter before the encounter ends

**Intention:** recover a useful pickup without forfeiting it to a scene transition. **Conditions:** an unopened container remains near the last active enemy. **Actions:** compare the risk of targeting the container now with immediately completing the fight. **Interaction:** the encounter-completion rule competes with the resource-collection rule. **Result:** obtaining the pickup may require keeping a threat alive briefly. **Next decision:** finish the encounter or seek another safe position. **Limit:** the crate is not guaranteed to hold the needed resource, and delaying a dangerous fight may cost more health than it restores. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

This illustrates a design risk rather than an unqualified recommendation. When the player's sensible intention is “secure the room, then inspect it,” an automatic exit can make orderly play feel like a mistake.

### D. A change of tone can remove a fight without rewriting the plot

**Intention:** get past an antagonist while avoiding an unnecessary combat segment. **Conditions:** the encounter provides a persuadable route. **Actions:** select the supported conciliatory or clever response instead of provoking violence. **Interaction:** conversation changes the immediate activity. **Result:** progress with a different local experience. **Next decision:** pursue the next lead. **Limit:** an early conversation with reconverging answers does not prove this option exists everywhere, while a skipped battle does not imply a completely different finale. [S1](#s1) [S7](#s7)

### E. Make information depend on a concrete favor

**Illo spoilers. Intention:** obtain the arms dealer's cooperation. **Conditions:** he is ill and Jacob's other leads can supply help. **Actions:** investigate the scientists and relevant material before completing the exchange for information. **Interaction:** another person's need connects separately located missions. **Result:** the lead becomes more than a question selected from a menu. **Next decision:** act on the disclosed threat. **Limit:** the solution is authored; the game does not accept a player-invented medicine, arbitrary laboratory procedure or substitute resource merely because it sounds plausible. [S16](#s16)

## 6. Presentation, interface and tactile experience

Comic-book panels give Galaxy a distinct visual identity instead of attempting a miniature copy of the trilogy's cinematic realism. Faces and poses can express the tone of a response. During combat, however, the top-down symbols compress people into colored targets, and the difference between a real barrier and scenery can be unclear. **Interpretation:** abstraction should make relevant properties easier to understand, not merely fit more information on a small display. [S3](#s3) [S4](#s4)

The conversation interface displays complete selectable lines rather than relying entirely on the trilogy's short intent summaries. That changes the relationship between the player's selection and Jacob's wording. Kevin Barrett also describes a journal available during conversation, intended to keep the necessary context inside the game. This is a useful information-support feature, not proof that every dialogue decision is difficult or consequential. [S11](#s11) [S18](#s18)

Holt's review describes a more specific input failure than merely slow loading: the loading indicator sometimes remains after combat has begun, while movement and special attacks remain unavailable. That is his device-era observation, not a claim that every version or device reproduces it. It also differs from a deliberate cooldown or an out-of-range target. **Interpretation:** feedback should distinguish an unavailable capability from controls that have not become responsive; otherwise players may blame their plan for a technical failure. [S2](#s2)

Reviewers could appreciate the comic presentation while objecting to repeated loading, limited visual enemy distinction and inconsistent performance. Sound reception also varies: GamePro values the force of the audio, while jazzfan4 finds comparatively little beyond gunfire and sparse voice work. Those are dated listening/play accounts, not a formal soundtrack analysis or an accessibility audit. No complete video, device compatibility test or before/after patch benchmark was performed here. [S3](#s3) [S20](#s20)

## 7. Production and the lesson BioWare itself drew

In Jon Mundy's July 14, 2009 interview, executive producer **Kevin Barrett** describes the iPhone as an opportunity to reach new players at a manageable production cost. He argues that a large setting can be conveyed through one person's immediate experience and that the game should supply enough context for a newcomer to choose without outside reading. That is a stated design intention, not evidence that every reviewer found the resulting choices satisfying. [S18](#s18)

The credits identify Barrett as project lead and **Joy Ang** among the core graphics contributors. The illustrated style and small scenario were production choices, but no title-specific staffing budget or verified production schedule was established. A critic's suspicion that the game was rushed should not become an undocumented claim about EA's internal deadline. Engine labels in secondary catalogs are not needed to explain the research finding and are not treated as verified implementation evidence. [S21](#s21)

In James Orry's July 2010 interview report, BioWare co-founder **Greg Zeschuk** says the team had overestimated story's ability to carry the experience and learned the importance of tactile play on the platform. He still regards the experiment as worthwhile and leaves room for future mobile or cross-platform ideas. This is not an announcement that BioWare abandoned mobile forever. [S19](#s19)

**Interpretation:** the useful production response is to test what the player repeatedly does before relying on brand, worldbuilding or promised future connections. A failure of one adaptation does not prove that small narrative games are inherently unworkable; it identifies a particular mismatch between the proposed experience and its repeated actions.

## 8. Distribution, cross-title promotion and commercial limits

Galaxy was sold as a premium mobile title. A December 7, 2009 TouchArcade sale roundup records a temporary 99-cent price from $1.99, distinct from the $4.99 launch listing. These are historical American storefront prices, not current purchasing options or revenue totals. No audited units, profit, acquisition costs or retention figures were established. Zeschuk's account of disappointing success is qualitative evidence, not a numerical sales report. [S7](#s7) [S19](#s19) [S22](#s22)

The connection to Mass Effect 2 was an explicit promotional device: account-linked completion promised a later reward. The initial June 2009 report did not know the reward's contents. Later documentation identifies additional dialogue with Ish when Jacob and Miranda accompany Shepard; Legendary Edition makes that exchange available without a present-day Galaxy completion requirement. It is not transferred equipment, XP or a prerequisite for the sequel's main plot. The old account-link route has not been tested and is not advertised here as currently operational. [S9](#s9)

**Interpretation:** an optional continuity reward can encourage people to try another form of the same world, but should remain genuinely optional. Retirement makes that boundary especially important. A story can benefit from acknowledging earlier participation without permanently hiding essential understanding behind an unavailable application.

The concrete promotional record includes launch coverage, an executive interview, first-impression demonstrations, the reward announcement and later price promotions. No verified influencer-led breakout, measurable viral mechanism or causal sales attribution was found. The scale of the parent franchise is not evidence that this individual spin-off achieved the same reach.

## 10. Conditional OpenLegend research lessons

**Preserve meaningful decisions when simplifying input.** Automatic routine actions may improve accessibility or reduce busywork, but the player should retain understandable choices about goals, positioning, timing or tools. A small experiment could compare manual repetitive firing with assisted firing while keeping the same meaningful tactical decisions. The risk is mistaking inactivity for convenience.

**Make physical and informational feedback agree.** A player should know what blocks movement, which target is selected and why a capability is unavailable. Calibration is an input concern; range is a world-state concern; cooldown is a readiness concern. Collapsing those different failures into an unresponsive button would make diagnosis needlessly difficult.

**Do not end a scene before the player's sensible cleanup action.** Encounter transitions should account for recovery, inspection and conversation that naturally follow danger. An explicit exit or short grace period may preserve pace without silently discarding an intended action. This is a research comparison, not a demand that every encounter remain open indefinitely.

**Use particular needs to connect places and people.** A person needing help before sharing information can make an investigation feel socially grounded. The dependency works best when its reason is clear and several actions genuinely relate to it. A sequence of arbitrary fetch requirements would reproduce the structure without its credibility.

**Keep cross-project references optional and durable.** Acknowledging another adventure can reward attention while leaving the main experience understandable. A preservation plan should distinguish bonus recognition from indispensable state. The failure mode is an unavailable external service becoming the only way to access important history.

These are conditional research hypotheses, not accepted OpenLegend requirements or implementation tasks. BioWare's names, characters and authored situations are reference material, not proposed assets.
