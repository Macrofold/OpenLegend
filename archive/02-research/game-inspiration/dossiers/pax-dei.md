# Pax Dei — persistent places, social purpose, and sustainable scope

**G189 · Full research dossier · Evidence cutoff: November 14, 2026.**

Mainframe Industries' social sandbox MMO is an especially relevant case for Open Legend: persistent construction, human-built communities, crafting and exchange, shared geography, and an ambitious promise of a world made meaningful by its inhabitants. This study covers Early Access, the commercial release, and subsequent updates through the cutoff.

## Executive assessment

**Pax Dei is a serious warning about making a persistent world rewarding and affordable to sustain. It is not sufficient evidence that persistent social worlds cannot work.**

There is clear value in the game: a visually compelling landscape, expressive construction, recognizable neighborhoods, shared projects, and the pleasure of making somewhere that other people can visit. There is also a recurring mismatch between that destination and the everyday work required to reach and sustain it. The [reception study](pax-dei/04-reception.md) preserves both favorable and critical accounts.

The population evidence supports Mike's concern about a large decline in simultaneous Steam activity. It also shows a second expansion around 1.0, followed by another retreat, with some later recovery. Steam activity is only one part of the audience and cannot establish current paid users or profitability. The [population chapter](pax-dei/00-population-and-verdict.md) contains the complete monthly series, definitions and bounded causal assessment.

The stronger commercial conclusion comes from combining participation with staffing changes, historical company-loss indicators, and the developer's acknowledged constraints. Financing is not a disclosed development budget; modeled Steam revenue is not an audited game profit-and-loss account. The [business chapter](pax-dei/03-business-marketing.md) separates these quantities and records what remains unavailable.

### Five conclusions worth carrying forward

1. **A beautiful place needs activities and relationships that make returning worthwhile.** Construction can itself be deeply enjoyable. Additional purpose must grow from the intended audience's interests, not merely more resource requirements.
2. **Social interdependence can generate value or become a coordination burden.** A system that works with a large organized clan may fail an ordinary evening when a player is alone or a specialist is unavailable.
3. **Persistent structures do not guarantee a shared encounter.** Overflow instances preserve some common state while separating people and tactical interactions. The architecture can change the meaning of conflict, scarcity, and cooperation.
4. **Reach and acquisition are different from durable demand.** The documented campaign included paid and organic creators, organized events, audience research, expanded distribution, and trials. The public record cannot attribute sustained paid conversion to these activities.
5. **Open Legend's characters must create practical, observable value.** Independent residents may help a small world feel inhabited, but dialogue alone does not resolve weak purpose, arbitrary grind, unclear consequences, or ongoing operating cost.

These conclusions synthesize the evidence linked in the chapters. They are research judgments, not newly accepted Open Legend requirements.

## Version and evidence discipline

The game changed substantially after Early Access. Current conclusions account for later economic, combat, convenience and travel changes; a review from an older build is evidence of that experience, not a complete description of the current product.

Likewise, “persistent environmental effects” needs a narrower definition than the phrase initially suggests. The evidence establishes lasting construction, property and economic state, with specific recovery and expiration rules. It does not establish unrestricted terraforming, a fully persistent ecosystem, or a guarantee that all inhabitants occupy the same simulation.

This is desk research using retrieved written sources, developer material, financial summaries and selected player accounts. It does not claim firsthand play, a code audit, a controlled churn study, or complete video viewing. The commercial verdict remains conditional on missing private data.
