# G119 — Dishonored 2

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research pass and follow-up audit — September 26, 2026.** Reference research, not accepted OpenLegend requirements. Scope: the 2016 campaign and explicitly dated subsequent modes. Five independent written reviews were read in full in the original pass, plus direct Steam accounts, creator interviews and practical guides. The follow-up read the entire dossier against R01–R14, retained the original evidence and added separately identified sources. It does not claim every original external URL was reread. No gameplay, audio listening, footage viewing, source-code inspection or present account-entitlement test was performed. Exact chaos weights and modern hardware behavior are not certified. Death of the Outsider is a separate game.

## 1. Identity, promise and edition boundaries

Arkane's sequel follows Emily Kaldwin or Corvo Attano after a coup, with Karnaca replacing much of the earlier game's Dunwall setting. Both protagonists are voiced and have different powers. Choosing one does not permit switching freely between them each mission. The publisher dates the general PC/PS4/Xbox One release November 11, 2016, with preorder access a day earlier; November 10 listings therefore need not imply a different edition. [S7, S12]

The player's starting identity is authored, not a custom class or occupation. The campaign combines assassination objectives, investigation and infiltration with optional encounters and alternative resolutions. Its promise is freedom inside deliberately built mission spaces, not unrestricted travel through a continually simulated empire. A new player first learns movement, awareness and escape; an experienced player uses knowledge of geometry, equipment and consequences to construct a different route. [S12, S13]

Refusing the Outsider's gift supports a no-powers campaign. That is not the later Iron Mode, which adds permadeath and prevents manual saving/loading. GameSpot's original complaint that there was no New Game Plus describes its review period, not the later feature set. [S2, S7, S9]

On December 19, 2016, the first free game update added New Game Plus, including both characters' ability suites and reallocation of carried runes. January 23, 2017 brought custom difficulty and mission selection: more than twenty settings can affect details such as sleep-dart onset, leaning and active attackers. Selected missions use their original mission-start state rather than rewriting every subsequent campaign variable. [S8, S9]

**Carryover is narrower than retaining the entire inventory.** Bethesda's announcement broadly describes runes and bonecharm traits; the community mode reference specifies runes **spent on abilities** and learned trait progression. Possessing an unsacrificed charm is not equivalent to having learned its trait, nor should unspent runes be silently assumed to survive. These distinctions explain preparation advice to learn desired ordinary traits and spend runes before completing a run. They are documented carryover qualifications, not a newly executed save-migration test or a guarantee about every item and upgrade. [S8, S35]

The December 13, 2018 promotion added account-linked Mission+, a black-and-white visual option and the earlier Imperial Assassin's Pack. Mission+ opens previously completed missions with powers and weapons for experimentation. It differs from ordinary mission selection and NG+. The bonus pack includes charms, cabin objects and starting money, not a new campaign. The historical login offer was read; present entitlement behavior was not tested. [S10]

## 2. Action grammar: stealth, violence and outcomes are different axes

The meaningful choice is not simply “stealth or combat.” Concealed assassination, unseen nonlethal intervention, visible nonlethal fighting and overt lethal combat are distinguishable approaches. A player can also avoid resolving every guard encounter. The sequel expands nonlethal responses sufficiently that Savage reports accepting detection instead of treating it as an automatic reload. [S1]

Movement includes walking, sprinting, crouching, leaning, climbing, jumping, swimming and vertical traversal through balconies and service spaces. Sightlines, noise, distance and the target's awareness shape risk. Keys, combinations, open windows, breakable barriers and machinery create overlapping access methods. The presence of a collectible marker does not establish the route to it; a building can be readable as a place while concealing the entrance that a particular approach needs. [S13, S24, S29]

Sword attacks, blocks and parries coexist with ranged weapons, drop takedowns, chokes and carrying bodies. A nonlethal input is not a guarantee of a living result. Bethesda specifically attributes deaths from drowning, fire, human-shield crossfire, rewired defenses, headless Clockwork Soldiers, Domino chains and player-spawned animals to the player in relevant circumstances. Detection can also come from hounds, machinery, supernatural defenses or some civilians, not only ordinary guards. [S26]

**Interpretation:** the game's interesting unit is a causal plan: distract this observer, cross this space, disable this person, place the body somewhere safe, then recover the route. A menu label such as “sleep” describes one operation, not every later effect. That distinction increases expressive depth but requires feedback reliable enough that an accident feels understandable rather than arbitrary.

## 3. Full major ability families

Runes buy powers and their branches. The following groups upgrades by function rather than reproducing every numerical tier. These are capability families, not mutually exclusive classes. Guide recommendations about what a “stealth player should” purchase are opinions and are not adopted as rules.

### Corvo's five unique active families

| Family | Base role and consequential branches |
| --- | --- |
| Blink | Rapid relocation; longer range, time-paused aiming while stationary, and an arrival attack. |
| Bend Time | Slow events; upgrade to a stop, longer duration or faster relative movement. |
| Possession | Occupy animals; extend duration, chain hosts, inhabit humans or hide in an immobile corpse. |
| Windblast | Push targets, redirect slower projectiles and break barriers; stronger impact or surrounding shockwave. |
| Devouring Swarm | Summon attacking rats; larger/following swarms or two locations. Summoned rats can become Possession hosts. |

Possession's target type changes movement and access, not just appearance. Hiding in a corpse does not animate it. Time manipulation has a finite duration and significant resource cost; stopping time is not unlimited world editing. [S15]

### Emily's five unique active families

| Family | Base role and consequential branches |
| --- | --- |
| Far Reach | Pull toward a location; branches retrieve objects or people and improve control while falling. |
| Domino | Link targets so compatible harm or incapacitation propagates; increase the linked group. |
| Doppelganger | Create a decoy; confusion on destruction, exchange position, fighting capability or a second double. |
| Shadow Walk | Lower-profile creature form; faster movement, rat-tunnel access and additional lethal/nonlethal takedowns. |
| Mesmerize | Distract living targets; affect more people or extend the duration. |

Far Reach moves Emily through space rather than making her disappear throughout transit; observers can see her. Shadow Walk reduces visibility but is not unconditional invisibility and changes the player's viewpoint. Domino supports sleep as well as killing. [S16]

### Shared active power and seven enhancement families

**Dark Vision** reveals living targets through walls; branches expose objects/security and predicted patrol routes. Its pulsed presentation can become crowded rather than providing perfect situational understanding. Shared enhancements are **Strength** (throws and barricades), **Vitality** (health/recovery), **Reflexes** (deflection, parrying and reaction/slide assistance), **Agility** (jumping, sprinting and safer falls), **Blood Thirst** (adrenaline finishers), **Bonecharm Crafting**, and **Shadow Kill** (corpse disposal, with a bloodfly branch). [S14]

Not every mana expenditure is equivalent. Low-cost traversal and Dark Vision can recover their spent portion when the player waits before casting again; larger expenditures still make elixirs valuable. This creates a difference between deliberate intermittent use and rapid combinations. The precise power, timing and upgrades matter; it is not a wholly self-refilling mana bar. [S14–S16]

**Interpretation:** branches alter the verbs and valid plans. A decoy becomes a relocation anchor; a hostile group becomes a linked system; a small animal becomes an access route. These changes can be more strategically important than additional damage. NG+ deliberately relaxes protagonist boundaries, but its combined arsenal should not be retroactively attributed to the first campaign.

## 4. Objects, equipment, crafting and economy

### The equipment vocabulary

Both protagonists use a sword, pistol and crossbow, with different visual treatment. Ammunition includes ordinary and explosive pistol rounds; ordinary, incendiary, sleep, howling and stinging crossbow projectiles. Gadgets include grenades, sticky grenades, Springrazors, Stun Mines and Rewire Tools. Addermire Solution and S&J Health Elixir serve different recovery needs. The Heart, runes, bonecharms, blueprints, keys, currency, food and the mission-specific Timepiece are not interchangeable “loot.” [S22, S28]

A Howling Bolt disrupts a target; a Stinging Bolt induces flight; a Sleep Dart incapacitates. The pistol has no corresponding ordinary nonlethal ammunition option. Rewiring changes what security attacks rather than physically deleting the danger. A trap can therefore be useful for combat while unsuitable for a no-kill intention. [S25, S26]

Blueprint-gated upgrades affect such things as ammunition behavior, reload speed, accuracy, storage and quieter movement. **Conductive Filaments** supports additional Stun Mine functionality; **Mercury Vapor Distillation** unlocks Stinging Bolts; **Folded Galvani Resin** supports quieter movement upgrades. Masterwork choices can alter a weapon's role rather than just its statistics. The inspected upgrade table supports these examples; this dossier does not reproduce its entire price database or treat preview descriptions as a current executable specification. [S30]

### Materials and expressive loadouts

Bonecharms supply traits; sacrificing ordinary charms can teach traits and provide material. Raw whalebone and learned traits support crafted combinations, while corruption introduces drawbacks. **Black and corrupted charms yield material but do not teach their special traits when sacrificed.** Ability investment and equipped capacity constrain how much can be combined. [S7, S14, S33]

The crafting limits matter: **Trait Synergy allows four uses of a trait across crafted charms, not four new stacks in every equipped slot.** Master Crafting removes corruption from three-trait crafts but leaves a low risk for four-trait crafts. Repeatedly dismantling and recrafting a four-trait charm loses material: the guide records six whalebone to create versus five recovered. Its advice to reload until satisfied is the author's optimization preference, not a compulsory mechanic or an Iron Mode option. Concentrating a benefit therefore competes with breadth, material and risk. [S33]

Specific traits clarify what is being composed. **Swift Shadow** changes crouched movement, **Undertaker** affects carrying a body, **Strong Arms** speeds choking and **Lucky Needle** improves sleep-dart recovery. These improve different steps of a stealth plan rather than one undifferentiated stealth statistic. Prima's assertion that every build should prioritize crafting remains advice, not an objective requirement. [S38]

Coins come from money and valuables such as paintings, ingots and amber. Black markets turn those discoveries into ammunition, upgrades and access-related purchases. Blueprint discovery and the money to use it are separate requirements. Loot is not a weight-based hauling-and-selling business: valuable pickups feed a currency economy, while weapons, consumables and charms have their own capacity rules. The currency guide's “only currency” wording refers to shop money, not denial that runes are another advancement resource. [S29]

**Interpretation:** an optional apartment can pay in several ways: a new fact, a resource, an access method or a build possibility. That overlap can make exploration productive without requiring combat experience points. Conversely, searching every drawer to complete a displayed total may become a chore. More valuable collectibles are not automatically better if they make players feel that an otherwise legitimate route has forfeited necessary power.

## 5. Progression, activities, time and failure

The campaign's nine named missions run from **A Long Day in Dunwall** through **Edge of the World**, **The Good Doctor**, **The Clockwork Mansion**, **The Royal Conservatory**, **Dust District**, **A Crack in the Slab**, **The Grand Palace**, and **Death to the Empress**. Their objective sequence is authored, but approaches and local outcomes branch. The Dreadful Wale provides intermission conversations and changing reminders of earlier actions, not a free-roaming settlement economy. [S17, S25]

Advancement comes through runes, equipment purchases, charms, plans and knowledge. Early scarcity makes investments compete; later combinations can overwhelm encounter difficulty, as Butterworth argues. Replays offer the other protagonist, no-powers restrictions, different violence/detection goals, NG+, mission experimentation and custom difficulty. There is no documented native cooperative campaign or shared multiplayer economy. [S2, S8–S10, S12]

Optional activities include safes, black-market access puzzles, paintings, audiographs, journals, Outsider shrines and souvenirs displayed on the ship. These activities expose different aspects of the city rather than constituting fully separate crafting professions, farming or social minigame careers. The Jindosh Lock can be treated as a deduction problem instead of accepting a faction's proposed errand. [S17, S24, S25]

Ordinary saves support retrying an approach, while campaign state still records what was actually done. Iron Mode changes that contract explicitly. Mission selection revisits a stored starting state; Mission+ provides a more permissive experimentation mode. Neither implies that every consequence can be undone in the ongoing campaign without loading earlier state. [S9, S10]

**Interpretation:** there are several kinds of failure: death, detection, unintended killing, missing an optional person and producing an unwanted later outcome. Treating them all as the same loss erases useful distinctions. A player-defined challenge can make detection unacceptable even when the game permits a successful escape. Clear statistics help adjudicate that challenge, but prominent completion totals can also make personal goals feel imposed.

## 6. World, people, AI and narrative consequences

### A designed city, not a universal society simulator

Karnaca's history, settlement, silver extraction, topography and architecture were developed together. Verticality and windbreaks connect the city's visual identity to movement and environmental hazards. Its districts include ordinary homes and workplaces as well as fortified objectives. This production account establishes careful authored context; it does not establish independently scheduled lives for every inhabitant. [S21]

The Grand Guard, Overseers, Howlers, witches, civilians and distinctive individual targets do not all ask the same tactical question. Clockwork Soldiers have front and rear observation, blades and electrical attacks, resistance to many powers, and rewiring possibilities. The creator's account portrays them as costly prototypes within the fiction, not evidence of the real development budget. [S11, S13]

People communicate through dialogue, overheard exchanges, letters, possessions and the Heart's revelations. Meagan Foster and Anton Sokolov are recurring allies, not freely built party members with equipment trees. Temporary doubles or possessed hosts are likewise different from persistent companions. The inspected campaign supports authored relationships and choices about named lives, not a player-directed romance courtship system, faction-management strategy layer or universal reputation meter. [S5, S12, S15, S16, S25]

### Story and ending spoilers

A coup separates Emily and Corvo; the chosen protagonist follows the conspiracy through Karnaca and returns to confront Delilah. Individual targets can have specific nonlethal resolutions beyond applying an ordinary sleep dart. Hypatia's counter-serum and Ashworth's sabotaged Oraculum are examples where learning the institution's machinery changes the objective. [S12, S25]

The finale and epilogue can reflect the Duke's fate or replacement, other leaders, surviving allies and whether the petrified family member is restored. These are permutations of authored outcomes, not an unlimited political simulation. A guide's “good/bad/worst” shorthand is not a complete mechanical model of every branch; this dossier does not reproduce it as an exhaustive outcome algorithm. [S31]

Chaos, detection and kill statistics should not be collapsed into one moral score. Indirect deaths can count even when the player did not swing the final weapon. Exact weights sometimes repeated in online summaries mix the first game's rules with the sequel's; no unverified numeric threshold is certified here. What is directly established is that methods and specific choices have consequences, and that the game supports substantially different playthroughs. [S12, S26]

**Primary design clarification:** in an August 2016 interview, Harvey Smith describes assigning ordinary mission characters the categories **sympathetic, guilty or murderous**, with different contributions to chaos when killed. He links this decision to players using the Heart's secrets to judge people in the first game, where that assumed weighting had not actually existed. He also describes chaos affecting security, bloodfly infestation and dialogue, including the protagonist's tone. This is attributed prerelease design testimony, not an independently decoded final formula or a guarantee that each anecdotal secret maps transparently to a numeric value. In particular, an inherited “kill fewer than 20 percent” shortcut is not supported as a complete sequel rule. [S34]

**Interpretation:** the story tension concerns how the protagonist responds to lost authority, not merely whether a target is reachable. A technically ingenious solution may still be cruel. Conversely, refusing a proposed bargain can be a meaningful expression of agency when the environment supports an alternative rather than forcing the player to click one of two faction buttons.

## 7. Eight concrete situations

Cases 1–3 preserve **attributed observed play accounts**. Cases 4–8 are **constructed illustrations of documented mechanics or quest procedures**, not claimed personal playthroughs. Analysis of decision pressure is distinguished from evidence for the underlying behavior.

### 1. A sleep dart produces a fire

Phil Savage reports tranquilizing a guard whose dropped glass broke an ignitable bottle, causing a fire. The initiating action was nonlethal, but the surrounding objects changed its consequence. [S1]

The immediate next decision is whether the player can protect the incapacitated person or must accept/reload the outcome. The example does not prove that every glass, bottle or fall shares the same interaction. It illustrates why a safe takedown includes choosing a landing place and checking the environment, not only selecting ammunition. Bethesda's indirect-death guidance supports the broader distinction without independently reproducing Savage's incident. [S26]

### 2. A decoy becomes a shared vulnerability

Dinga Bakaba reports playtesters summoning a Doppelganger, linking it to enemies with upgraded Domino, and killing the double to affect the connected group. This is a developer-reported combination rather than a named, canned attack. [S7]

The player creates an easier target and changes the relationship among existing targets. Rune investment, mana, link capacity and valid targets bound the plan. Its next decision is where and when to prepare the connection without being interrupted. The same general linking mechanic can support incapacitation, but that does not make destroying one's own decoy automatically nonlethal to everyone linked.

### 3. A moving wall becomes an escape route

Hayden Dingman describes evading a Clockwork Soldier by slipping through a moving wall into a service corridor. The transformation exposed traversable backstage space, not merely a cinematic room swap. [S4]

The result changes how the player understands the house: its machinery is part of its route network. The next task is to regain orientation and choose an exit rather than assuming the service corridor is automatically safe. Carrier's production account explains why backstage spaces were visually connected to larger rooms; maintaining a mental map was a deliberate design problem. [S19]

### 4. A diagnosis replaces an assassination

The player learns how to make a counter-serum for the Crown Killer, obtains Vasco's notes, uses blood from a diseased corpse in the laboratory process and returns with the prepared injection. A stealth takedown then administers the cure instead of an ordinary choke. Hypatia's survival can change the following ship intermission. [S25]

The useful resource is procedural knowledge, not only stronger combat power. The next decision is whether to investigate before starting a fight and how to approach closely enough afterward. This is an authored medical objective, not evidence of a general chemistry simulator or a rule that all supernatural enemies can be cured with the same apparatus.

### 5. A faulty component defeats an institution

At the Royal Conservatory, replacing the Oraculum's lenses and activating it removes Ashworth's and her coven's powers. Ordinary incapacitation of Ashworth is not the same system-wide intervention. The December 2016 update specifically corrected an **Oraculum false-kill count**. That wording establishes an accounting defect; the original dossier's “unintended-death issue” overstated what the patch note proved. [S25, S8]

The result rewards learning a dependency rather than eliminating every subordinate individually. The next decision is whether to prioritize the apparatus or continue confronting guards and collecting evidence. A system-wide effect can be satisfying only if its targets and side effects are sufficiently understandable. Incorrectly reporting a kill can undermine a player's confidence in a nonlethal route even when that statistic does not establish an actual death.

### 6. A locked door need not mean choosing a faction

In the Dust District, factions offer paths to a solution for Jindosh's lock. The player can instead solve the name-and-heirloom deduction puzzle or obtain existing information by another route. Its solution is partly randomized, so a memorized combination is not a universal key. [S24]

The immediate result is access without fulfilling either faction's proposed bargain. The next decision is whether to spend time on deduction, investigate an office or engage with the district's conflict anyway. The guide's opening claim that the player “has to” choose a side is contradicted by its own alternative-solution instructions; this dossier preserves the alternatives rather than importing that simplification.

### 7. Preventing an event changes the present

In A Crack in the Slab, the Timepiece lets the player compare and traverse versions of Stilton's manor. Incapacitating Stilton before the ritual prevents his attendance and changes the present; leaving him alone or killing him produces different circumstances. The nonlethal intervention also changes Meagan's later injuries. Once the present changes, the originally encountered version is not simply another permanently available toggle. [S23]

The Timepiece is also a substitution of capabilities: the mission disables the usual supernatural powers and supplies observation and transitions between two periods three years apart. Looking through its lenses permits timing a crossing around people in the other period. [S34]

The next decision is whether to finish exploring before intervening and what future to preserve. The device is mission-specific, with authored causal links; it does not support arbitrary historical travel throughout Karnaca. The striking effect comes from a small action whose meaning becomes visible across both environment and a known person's body, not from the number of years on a timeline.

### 8. A crafting investment changes a resource's value

A player has learned a useful charm trait and accumulated raw whalebone. Trait Synergy permits repeated traits; crafting upgrades reduce corruption risk, and Craft Runes permits conversion between advancement resources and material. The player must decide whether to keep a flexible collection or invest in a concentrated custom loadout. [S14]

The result is not a free upgrade: learning, material, ability cost and equipped capacity compete. The next decision is whether a strong four-trait possibility justifies its remaining risk or whether a safer combination better suits the campaign. This illustrates specialization through composition, not a claim that any desirable black-charm effect can be freely duplicated.

## 8. Presentation and production

### Art supports use and identity

Sebastien Mitton explains that the Howlers' sword developed from tailoring references and the shape of a large pair of scissors. Emily's crossbow draws on musical-instrument forms; the shared folding sword exposes its mechanism, including for slowed-time viewing. Whale-oil containers are simultaneously world infrastructure and dangerous physical objects. Storyboards for Far Reach and design questions about Mesmerize show artists and gameplay designers working on effects together. These are written creator accounts; the accompanying images and videos were not independently analyzed in this pass. [S22]

**Interpretation:** an object's form can suggest who made it and how it operates. This is different from adding lore after a generic asset is finished. It also raises an expectation that visible handles, openings and moving parts matter; when they do not, presentation can misleadingly imply an unavailable action.

### Sound must inform without inventing routes

Thomas Mitton describes avoiding nearby fake sounds that would suggest accessible spaces where none exist. Fabrice Fournet explains source repositioning, obstruction/occlusion, environmental effects and audibility-based mixing; manual map tuning still mattered. Important dialogue receives priority, while combat can raise impact and power intensity. [S20]

For the Timepiece mission, past and present use different music and reverberation: a lived-in, more muffled past contrasts with an empty, echoing present. Dan Licht's exploration/suspense music is distinguished from other designed sound layers. This is production evidence, not a listening review or a verified performance benchmark. [S20]

### A difficult showcase produced reusable tools

Carrier credits Dana Nightingale and David Di Giacomo with building the Clockwork Mansion. Bob Potts's kinetic sculpture informed linked movement; room functions helped maintain intelligibility. Early prototypes used the first game's engine while new tools were developed. The ambitious announcement trailer helped revive ideas previously reduced in scope. AI had to navigate during transformations, not merely after each finished configuration; the resulting animation tooling was reused elsewhere. [S19]

The publisher's PlayStation credits identify the shipped **Void Engine powered by id Tech**, alongside Havok, Simplygon, FaceFX, Bink, Iggy, Umbra 3 and Wwise. These credits establish named technology use, not how every subsystem was implemented or why a particular frame stalled. No percentage of rewritten engine code, exact staffing count or alleged license-cost motive is inferred from forum discussion. The engine identity also should not be confused with the earlier prototype's toolchain. [S36]

**Interpretation:** a signature level can justify reusable production infrastructure, but only after confronting authoring, navigation, orientation and AI constraints. The lesson is not to build the most elaborate possible showcase first. A smaller transformation that remains readable and mechanically reliable can be more valuable than a larger one that becomes a one-off technical liability.

### Readability, accessibility and maintenance

Paired protagonists, input precision, lean behavior, collectible presentation and spatial sound affect practical access to the intended freedom. Custom difficulty allows some of these demands to be tuned independently; it is not a complete accessibility audit. Patch notes address input timing, collisions, saves and objective accounting. Launch reports remain valuable history but do not establish that a particular current installation still exhibits the same bugs. [S3, S4, S9]

## 9. Launch, distribution, business and participation

The launch campaign included character/power demonstrations, themed-level coverage, creator features and the Clockwork Mansion trailer. These are concrete publisher channels, not proof of how many purchases each generated. Its premium campaign model differs from a live-service currency economy. Subsequent free modes extended replay options without making Death of the Outsider part of this campaign. [S7–S13, S19]

In April 2017, Bethesda offered the training map and first three missions as a free trial with either protagonist, and saves could transfer to a purchased copy. The original announcement page retains an April 3 timestamp but was updated to say the trial was available; contemporaneous April 6 reporting supplies the actual launch distinction. A later visitor should not infer that an undated current promotion has the same price or entitlement conditions. [S27]

A dated Chart-Track reproduction places Dishonored 2 fourth in UK retail charts for the week ending **November 12, 2016**, with launch sales down **38%** against the original. This is physical retail in one market, not global all-format units, profit or lifetime performance. No reliable public budget, margin, lifetime unit total or retention denominator was established here. Current third-party sales estimates were not promoted to audited publisher results. [S32]

**Interpretation:** surprising combinations and room transformations are compact demonstrations of the game's value, making clips and route comparisons plausible sharing formats. Distinct protagonists and later NG+ support comparison and replay discussion. These are affordance-based hypotheses, not measured virality, causal attribution to creators, or evidence that launch competition alone explains sales.

## 10. Reception: five full written reviews and direct player accounts

All five bodies below were read in the original pass, not merely their scores or search snippets. Their disagreement is retained rather than averaged away.

### Phil Savage — PC Gamer, November 18, 2016

Savage praises connections among rules, levels and world details. Expanded nonlethal combat let him accept detection rather than immediately reload. He criticizes awkward leaning, shallow protagonist observations and the unacceptable launch-performance situation, even though his own setup fared comparatively well. His accidental fire is preserved in case 1. [S1]

### Scott Butterworth — GameSpot, November 12, 2016

Butterworth expected stealth but enjoyed elaborate combat combinations. He praises threatening enemies, mission hooks and side content, yet finds later encounters insufficiently challenging as the player becomes stronger. The rushed premise and exposition disappoint him. Hidden runes sometimes become frustrating searches. His review predates NG+. [S2]

### Chris Carter — Destructoid, November 14, 2016

Carter's Xbox One account values experimentation, distinct protagonists and Heart-led collectible puzzles. Technical choppiness particularly harms precision stealth; detection can feel unfair. Voice delivery and script issues also intrude. His use of “hardcore” for a no-powers run is descriptive, not evidence that official Iron Mode existed at launch. [S3]

### Hayden Dingman — PCWorld, November 23, 2016

Dingman praises connected routes and the mansion but reports severe PC frame-rate variation even after patch 1.2. Plot and voice acting disappoint. He argues that collectible accounting can pressure completionists to traverse every route, weakening the feeling of choosing one. Misleading voice positioning interfered with stealth awareness. His moving-wall escape appears in case 3. [S4]

### Erik Kain — Forbes, November 18, 2016

Kain finds the sequel weaker: targets, story and ship hub engage him less, while detection and repeated group fights frustrate. He dislikes the held Heart interface and restrictions on Emily's access to familiar powers. Yet he enjoys the visual style, freedom and crafted spaces. His powerful PC ran well, a counterpoint rather than a refutation of others' problems. [S5]

### Helpful Steam surface

The English **Most Helpful (Week)** surface was readable. PrexMP3's September 20 negative review likes Emily and no-powers play but criticizes the repeated premise and imprecise controls; its gun-behavior claim is an allegation, not a verified engine fact. Lokain's September 22 positive post describes replaying immersive sims. Longer positive entries on the same surface, without helpful votes at retrieval, include Rintzler's appreciation of levels and protagonist differences alongside frustration with chaos, and kencudi's fourth-mission enthusiasm. [S6]

This is not an all-time census. Rintzler's feeling that lethal powers are effectively forbidden is reception evidence, not a universal rule. kencudi had not completed the campaign. Small vote counts, changing order and cumulative hours limit generalization; brief praise-only posts do not satisfy the five-review gate.

**Follow-up all-time sample:** the English **Most Helpful (All Time)** page was successfully read. **Chupi**, June 14, 2017 (33.3 displayed hours; 3,531 helpful votes), praises varied routes and creative encounters but finds the voiced protagonists less nuanced, the narrative predictable and later difficulty too low. **Bad Whippet**, November 23, 2016 (262.4 hours; 656 helpful votes), values the enlarged spaces, crafting and alternate protagonists while qualifying the recommendation around contemporary performance and disliking the changed Outsider presentation. These are full substantive accounts, not the page's highly voted discount joke or short demo advice. Displayed hours/votes are retrieval snapshots, not values at publication, and body edits cannot be ruled out. [S37]

The dedicated all-time negative endpoint failed on both attempted URL variants in the follow-up. The original directly read weekly negative account remains valid, but is not relabeled all-time or representative. No current compatibility problem was independently reproduced.

**Interpretation:** Carter's Heart-led exploration and Kain's dislike of that interface expose a genuine design tradeoff. A contextual tool can make knowledge acquisition tangible, but repeated swapping can obstruct attention. Likewise, a no-kill target is satisfying for some players yet can become brittle when indirect causation and imperfect feedback turn experimentation into repeated checking.

## 11. Transferable patterns and constraints

These are research interpretations, not accepted OpenLegend features or requests to copy names, characters, artwork or powers.

**Separate intentions from consequences.** Case 1 shows why “use nonlethal tool” and “leave this person alive” are different specifications. Reliable attribution, status feedback and recovery opportunities are prerequisites. A simpler alternative is a narrower interaction set with explicit guarantees; pretending to simulate every collateral effect is not required.

**Let combinations change relationships.** Case 2 makes several actors share vulnerability; it does not merely add damage. Useful dependencies include clear target eligibility, capacity limits and readable propagation. An unrestricted effect graph without readable boundaries could become either an exploit factory or a source of apparently random failures.

**Make institutional knowledge actionable.** Cases 4–6 connect investigation to access or resolution. Their strength is that learning changes the plan. A lore document that merely narrates an unavoidable fight does not offer the same agency. Alternative routes must be actually supported, not only described by dialogue.

**Use local causality before promising a universal world.** Case 7 achieves a strong sense of consequence through a bounded mission and familiar people. Broad simulation would require much more state, explanation and authoring. A small number of dependable, visible consequences can outperform a larger set that players cannot trace.

**Preserve agency after detection.** Expanded nonlethal combat and configurable challenge allow a player to recover rather than erase the attempt. But persistent challenge statistics should remain legible and should not confuse optional perfection with required success. Failure recovery and difficulty selection are different concerns.

**Treat presentation as an information contract.** Backstage sightlines, distinctive props and honest sound cues help players form plans. Fidelity is not enough: if audio says “behind you” when a guard is far below, the player can make a rational but doomed decision. Creator intent and critical accounts should therefore be read together, not used to cancel each other out.
