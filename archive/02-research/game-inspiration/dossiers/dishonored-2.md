## Identity and edition boundaries

Arkane's sequel follows Emily Kaldwin or Corvo Attano after a coup, with Karnaca replacing much of the earlier game's Dunwall setting. Both protagonists are voiced and have different powers. An initial character choice is not an invitation to swap freely between them every mission. The publisher dates the general PC/PS4/Xbox One release November 11, 2016, with preorder access one day earlier; this explains November 10 listings without inventing a separate sequel. [S7, S12]

Refusing the Outsider's gift supports a no-powers campaign. It is not the same option as the later Iron Mode, which adds permadeath and prevents manual saving/loading. The initial GameSpot review's complaint that there was no New Game Plus is historically correct for its review period, not the later feature set. [S2, S7, S9]

On December 19, 2016, the first free game update added New Game Plus, including access to both characters' ability suites and reallocation of carried runes. January 23, 2017 brought custom difficulty and mission selection: more than twenty settings can affect details such as sleep-dart onset, leaning and active attackers. Selected missions retain the original mission-start state rather than retroactively changing every campaign variable. [S8, S9]

The December 13, 2018 promotion added account-linked Mission+, a black-and-white visual option and the earlier Imperial Assassin's Pack. Mission+ opens previously completed missions with powers and weapons for experimentation. It differs from ordinary mission selection and NG+. Death of the Outsider remains a separate game even though the announcement covers both products. The historical account-login offer was read; no present entitlement/login test was performed. [S10]

## Five substantive written reviews

### Phil Savage — PC Gamer, November 18, 2016

Savage praises the connection among rules, level design and world details. Expanded nonlethal combat allowed him to accept detection instead of immediately reloading. He criticizes awkward leaning, shallow protagonist observations and the unacceptable launch-performance situation, even though his own setup fared comparatively well. His environmental accident above matters because it complicates a simple lethal/nonlethal action label. [S1]

### Scott Butterworth — GameSpot, November 12, 2016

Butterworth entered expecting stealth but enjoyed elaborate combat combinations. He praises powerful enemies, inventive mission hooks and side content, yet finds later encounters insufficiently challenging as the player becomes stronger. The rushed premise and exposition-heavy narration disappoint him. Hidden runes sometimes cross from rewarding puzzles into frustrating searches. The review predates NG+. [S2]

### Chris Carter — Destructoid, November 14, 2016

Carter's Xbox One review describes strong experimentation, distinct protagonist runs and satisfying Heart-led collectible puzzles. He finds technical choppiness particularly costly for precision stealth and reports AI detection that can feel unfair. Voice delivery and script issues also intrude. His use of “hardcore” for a no-powers run is descriptive wording, not evidence that the later official Iron Mode existed at launch. [S3]

### Hayden Dingman — PCWorld, November 23, 2016

Dingman praises the many connected routes and the Clockwork Mansion, but reports severe frame-rate variation on his PC even after patch 1.2. He finds the plot familiar and voice acting uneven. More unusually, he argues that collectible accounting can push a completionist to traverse every route, undermining the feeling of choosing one. Misleading voice positioning also interfered with his stealth awareness. [S4]

### Erik Kain — Forbes, November 18, 2016

Kain considers the sequel weaker than the original: targets, story and ship hub feel less engaging, while hostile detection and repeated group fights frustrate him. He dislikes both the held Heart interface and restrictions on Emily's access to familiar powers. Yet he praises the visual style, freedom and crafted mission spaces and still reports enjoying the game. His powerful PC ran well, a counterpoint to other launch accounts rather than proof their problems were imaginary. [S5]
