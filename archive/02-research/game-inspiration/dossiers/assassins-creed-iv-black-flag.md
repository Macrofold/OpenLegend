## A ship is both a home and a capability investment

The original's naval progression depends on different kinds of cargo. Wood, metal and cloth support upgrades; rum and sugar supply sale value. The published guide distinguishes limited shared cargo capacity from the separate need for elite design plans, found through wreck exploration or buried treasure. A treasury alone does not discharge every prerequisite. [S01](#s01)

**Interpretation:** this connects apparently optional exploration to a practical desire. A player can leave a successful battle with money yet still need a particular material, or own materials yet lack the plan. The benefit is a reason to vary activity; the failure mode is a resource checklist that repeatedly postpones the activity the player actually enjoys.

The naval guide also separates defeating a ship from deciding its fate. Successful boarding grants cargo and ammunition plus a choice among repairing the Jackdaw, lowering Wanted level or adding the vessel to Kenway's Fleet. The original Fleet is an online-dependent metagame, not a second manually sailed ship or a naval multiplayer lobby. [S01](#s01) [S02](#s02)

## A new version changes the meaning of an old review

Ubisoft's current product FAQ describes Resynced as a single-player experience and permits offline main-campaign play after the required download, while Animus Hub live content needs connectivity. At a reveal event attended by GamesRadar, creative director Paul Fu explicitly distinguished the remake's focus on Edward from the omitted original multiplayer and DLC. This is a scope decision, not evidence that Freedom Cry never existed or that every original server is still accessible today. [S03](#s03) [S04](#s04)

Gareth Chadwick's July 2026 review describes changed boarding morale, early rope-dart access, freer crouching/diving and a redesigned Fleet map. He enjoys many changes but prefers the older Fleet's sense of scope and reports inconsistent prompts and Animus interruptions. Its ordinary Abstergo office sequences have been replaced rather than all modern framing simply disappearing. His observations belong to the reviewed PS5 release; exact later patch behavior still needs qualification. [S05](#s05)

## Five original reviews inspected

These are distinct written accounts, not five scores treated as consensus. Detailed comparison belongs in the final pass.

- **Andy Hartup, GamesRadar:** values the pirate fantasy, world and naval/fort transitions; dislikes repeated following/eavesdropping and uneven story delivery. His review explicitly did not yet assess multiplayer. [S06](#s06)
- **Conrad Zimmerman, Destructoid, November 1, 2013:** enjoys plunder, boarding and maritime atmosphere but finds land combat and repeated missions weaker. Reports of erratic targeting/perception are his experience, not measured defect rates. [S07](#s07)
- **Patrick Barnett, Nintendo World Report, October 29, 2013:** values tool options and underwater exploration, but finds first-time travel and naval repetition wearing. His Wii U interface observations are platform-specific. [S08](#s08)
- **Gareth Chadwick, TheSixthAxis, October 30, 2013:** praises continuity between ship and shore and the musical atmosphere; dislikes slow early unlocking and awkward boat-stealth missions. His multiplayer assessment covers only the modes he reached. [S09](#s09)
- **Claudio Prandoni, UOL, November 18, 2013:** finds naval upgrades tangible and the activity variety engaging; notes rendering/collision blemishes and less convincing Portuguese voice delivery. His favorable polish assessment differs from other writers' technical experience. [S10](#s10)

**Interpretation:** these accounts disagree about which activity becomes repetitive, not merely about whether the game is good. That matters when extracting inspiration: alternating systems works only when each remains rewarding for the particular player, and seamless transitions do not automatically make every mandatory mission enjoyable.

## Commercial measures already established

Ubisoft's May 15, 2014 full-year release reports **more than eleven million sell-in and digital units** for Black Flag. It is not a count of simultaneous players or independently measured consumer sell-through of every retail copy. Separately, the July 17, 2026 Resynced announcement reports **more than three million copies sold in a week**, following a two-million first day. These are different products, dates and measures; neither establishes a current lifetime total, profit or marketing attribution. [S11](#s11) [S12](#s12)
