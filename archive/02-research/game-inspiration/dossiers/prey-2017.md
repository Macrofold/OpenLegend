## Evidence already established

The studied game is Arkane's 2017 Prey, not Human Head's 2006 game or its canceled sequel. Bethesda's launch announcement identifies the May 5 release and a research-station setting in which Morgan uses human upgrades, acquired alien powers and improvised tools. Talos I is a bounded, interconnected place rather than an unrestricted galaxy. [S4]

The official equipment account separates a wrench, shotgun, silenced pistol, Disruptor Stun Gun, GLOO Cannon, Q-Beam, gadgets, TranScribe, Artax propulsion and Psychoscope. A nonlethal stun is particularly relevant around humans controlled by a Telepath. The engineering fantasy is therefore broader than selecting damage types: the same inventory supports movement, environmental manipulation, rescue and resource conversion. The complete item taxonomy will be added in the completion pass. [S1]

The publisher describes class-free investment in human skills and three alien trees. The useful distinction is between *learning that an ability exists*, *meeting its research requirement* and *paying to install it*. Possessing the Psychoscope does not automatically grant every Typhon power. Acquiring alien abilities can also change how the station treats Morgan, including turret hostility. The exact scope and thresholds need the forthcoming mechanics verification rather than extrapolation from the marketing summary. [S2]

Recycling is a transformation system, not a shop that buys junk for an abstract currency. Objects become materials; a fabrication plan and sufficient materials permit a different object to be made. The official account specifically includes ammunition, medical supplies, Artax propulsion and Neuromods. A Recycler Charge applies conversion to an area and can endanger its user. [S3]

## A verified player account: a toy becomes an access tool

Phil Savage's PC Gamer review describes a locked office with a breakable window but insufficient room to enter. His familiar small-object transformation route was awkward because the window offered no useful perch. He instead fabricated a toy crossbow and darts, shot the visible terminal's Utility control, then activated its door-unlock option. He subsequently found the keycard on a nearby corpse. [S5]

**Interpretation:** the interesting result is not simply that there were three routes. A traversal limitation redirected the player toward a different causal system: a projectile could operate an interface. The later keycard did not invalidate the successful solution. For an inspiration library, this is evidence for overlapping affordances with different information, spatial and resource costs—not proof that every screen, door or projectile in a game should behave identically.

## Reception evidence retained for the full pass

These are distinct written critical accounts, not an aggregate score or a claim of population consensus. Source bodies were retrieved in sections; GameSpot's later platform-update paragraphs and Destructoid's closing paragraphs remain to be read before certifying the complete review-reading gate.

### Phil Savage — PC Gamer, May 9, 2017

Savage values Talos I's believable layout, discovery and conversion economy, but finds repeated Mimic encounters irritating and early combat unrewarding. His account also records apparently inconsistent stealth detection and overlapping mission dialogue. The review is useful because it distinguishes enjoying the whole exploration experience from approving each subsystem. His crossbow incident above is an attributed play account, not an invented demonstration. [S5]

### Tamoor Hussain — GameSpot, updated May 16, 2017

Hussain finds the opening premise stronger than the subsequent character development. Resource pressure initially makes play feel constrained, while more abundant upgrades and fabrication later support satisfying combinations. He sees workplace correspondence as often mundane rather than automatically rich environmental storytelling, and argues that the ending asks for emotional investment that the preceding missions did not earn for him. This is a substantive counterpoint to accounts that praise the same station details. The final platform-update portion still needs reading. [S6]

### Dominic Preston — Tech Advisor, May 10, 2017

Preston praises the suspicion created by disguised Mimics and the breadth of exploration tools. He also regards the game as heavily indebted to earlier immersive sims, with a dramatic opening followed by a weaker main narrative. His report includes a disappearing weapon, an animation problem and mistimed sound cues; these are his launch-build observations, not verified defects in every later installation. The article's claim that all skills cannot be obtained in one playthrough is not adopted as an authoritative progression limit. [S7]

### Chad Sapieha — Common Sense Media, 2017 game review; page updated October 9, 2025

Sapieha emphasizes moral choices, representation and exploratory freedom. His criticism is specific: early aiming and enemy tracking can frustrate, and late backtracking combines poorly with loading screens. He finds combat improves with later area powers. The publication's audience makes ease of play and disturbing content especially salient; its age guidance is not a technical difficulty measurement. The review body was read separately from the site's AI-generated summary of children's reviews. [S8]

### Brett Makedonski — Destructoid, May 15, 2017

Makedonski distinguishes the crew's environmental stories from the player's own, imperfectly controlled sequence of decisions. He values the first yet feels conflicted about discovering that his ending omitted outcomes he would have preferred. His PS4 account reports sluggish aiming and repetitive, difficult combat. An illustrative encounter uses a high perch and an explosive tank against two enemies, showing why environmental methods can matter when direct gunplay disappoints. Closing paragraphs still require reading. [S9]

## Helpful Steam surface: qualitative, not representative

The English community review page was accessible with **Most Helpful (Week)** selected. On the September 26 research date, the retrieved surface included September posts. Vision821 praised foam-based traversal and converting materials; malw valued revisiting a detailed world. Cee disliked slow controls, repeated reading and Mimic encounters and alleged technical problems. Tygron described resource conservation and stronger enemies on return visits as exhausting rather than rewarding. [S10]

These are selected player accounts with small helpful-vote counts, not an all-time top-review sample, a measured prevalence estimate or independently reproduced bug reports. Displayed cumulative hours cannot establish hours played when the text was first posted. Brief praise-only entries were not counted toward the five substantive written-review requirement. The sample's disagreement usefully warns against describing scarcity as inherently satisfying.
