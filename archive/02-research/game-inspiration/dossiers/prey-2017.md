# G118 — Prey (2017)

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research pass and follow-up audit — September 26, 2026.** Reference research, not accepted OpenLegend requirements. Scope: Arkane's 2017 campaign, its free mode updates, and separately identified Mooncrash and Typhon Hunter. Five independent base-game review bodies were read in the original pass, with additional expansion criticism and a selected helpful Steam sample. The follow-up read the complete dossier against R01–R14; new sources and newly recovered all-time player testimony are identified separately, not represented as a rereading of every original source. No gameplay, source-code execution, audio listening or video viewing was performed. Launch-era guide errors, conflicting security-threshold accounts, current online population and private financial data remain explicitly bounded below.

## 1. Identity, editions and the player promise

This is Arkane's Prey, not Human Head's 2006 game or its canceled sequel. Bethesda's launch announcement identifies the May 5, 2017 release and a research-station setting in which Morgan Yu uses human upgrades, acquired alien powers and improvised tools. Talos I is a bounded, interconnected place rather than an unrestricted galaxy. The original release covers PC, PS4 and Xbox One; later storefront/subscription availability is distribution context, not a different original launch. [S4, S26, S32]

Morgan is an authored protagonist with selectable male/female presentation, not a blank-slate profession simulator. The opening disorients the player about memory and surroundings; subsequent play alternates investigation, route-finding, survival and decisions about other people. Human and alien abilities permit specialization without a permanent class selection. The first session emphasizes vulnerability and uncertainty; established play supplies more knowledge, equipment and combinations. Critics disagree about whether that transition arrives soon enough. [S2, S6, S8]

**Interpretation:** the player promise is not that every object does anything imaginable. It is that knowing an object's actual properties can offer a plan beyond the obvious mission route. The station's repeated use makes knowledge itself a durable resource, while its dangers prevent familiarity from guaranteeing safety.

## 2. Ability vocabulary and commitment

These are investment trees, not mutually exclusive classes. Human abilities become visible before the player can afford their prerequisites; alien powers additionally require research. Installation is a commitment, unlike swapping removable chipsets. [S11, S14]

### Human families

| Tree | Families and functions, with tiers grouped |
| --- | --- |
| Scientist | Physician improves medicine; Necropsy recovers organs; Metabolic Boost improves food; Hacking bypasses security; Psionic Aptitude expands Psi; Neurostimulant improves hypos; Psychotronics adds scope slots. |
| Engineer | Leverage moves heavy objects; Repair restores devices; Gunsmith and Lab Tech unlock weapon upgrades; Suit Modification expands carrying/chipset capacity; Dismantle yields parts; Materials Expert improves recycling; Impact Calibration and Pulverize strengthen wrench use. |
| Security | Firearms improves gun effectiveness; Conditioning, Toughness, Stamina and Mobility improve bodily capacity; Combat Focus slows time; Stealth reduces detection/noise; Sneak Attack rewards attacking unaware targets. |

Combat Focus consumes Psi despite being human technology. Quiet movement is not invisibility. The table identifies capabilities, not endorsement of the guide's recommended build or its erroneous self-referencing prerequisites. [S11]

### Typhon families

| Tree | Powers and effects |
| --- | --- |
| Energy | Kinetic Blast pushes/damages; Lift Field lifts; Electrostatic Burst disrupts; Superthermal creates fire traps. Thermal/Electrostatic Resistance mitigate matching hazards; corresponding Absorption converts exposure to Psi. |
| Morph | Mimic Matter copies objects, with advanced machine forms; Phantom Shift dashes/leaves a decoy; Phantom Genesis turns a corpse into an ally; Regeneration recovers recent damage; Ether Resistance/Absorption mitigate or exploit ether attacks. |
| Telepathy | Backlash repels attacks; Fear Resistance protects; Psychoshock suppresses biological powers; Mindjack controls/frees minds; Remote Manipulation operates objects remotely; Machine Mind temporarily allies machinery. |

Research, prerequisites, Psi, cooldowns, target compatibility and duration constrain powers. Alien investment can provoke station security. The original pass left the exact threshold unresolved; the follow-up distinguishes **Neuromod consumables spent** from **Typhon abilities installed**. Community documentation puts base-campaign hostility beyond two installed Typhon abilities, while Prima's launch guide says two or more. Those accounts do not justify pretending that a raw Neuromod cost, an ability family and every upgraded tier are interchangeable counters. This is a documented source disagreement, not a tested current-executable threshold; the guide's separate numerical Nightmare claim is not certified either. [S2, S14, S37, S44]

The response need not be abandoning an alien build. The Cargo Bay reference documents Hacking II to reset a found turret's allegiance and says player-fabricated turrets remain friendly despite alien investment. Those bounded access routes are more useful than an unsupported universal claim that all security is permanently lost. They do not prevent a Technopath taking control in an encounter. Mooncrash's different security descriptions must not be imported into the base campaign. [S38, S43]

**Interpretation:** a build changes how an opportunity can be used, not merely damage output. Hacking, physical access and remote interaction can converge on the same objective while consuming different investments. The resulting redundancy is meaningful when costs differ; it becomes hollow when every alternative is equally effortless.

## 3. Actions, information, equipment and composition

### Movement and deliberation

Walking, running, sprinting, crouching, sliding and jumping have different noise and stamina costs. White detection indicates investigation; red indicates recognition. The Favorites Wheel pauses action while selecting equipment or healing. TranScribe floor maps and selected objectives support navigation; security stations expose maps and crew-tracking targets. These interfaces are aids to situational knowledge, not proof of a globally simulated population schedule. [S12]

Artax supports zero-gravity movement and controlled descent in normal gravity. The Psychoscope scans living Typhon to reveal information and research opportunities; a dead specimen is not interchangeable with a live scan. The wrench, shotgun, silenced pistol, Disruptor, GLOO Cannon and Q-Beam therefore sit beside observation and movement equipment rather than forming the whole action vocabulary. [S1]

### Equipment has different jobs

Weapons occupy inventory space and use distinct ammunition. The wrench trades stamina and exposure for charged or quick strikes. Pistol and shotgun favor different ranges. Q-Beam builds an unstable-energy threshold rather than acting like an ordinary bullet. EMP Charges interfere with machinery; Nullwave Transmitters suppress Psi; Recycler Charges transform nearby matter into organic, mineral, synthetic and exotic materials. The Huntress Boltcaster is a toy with interaction uses, not a conventional damage upgrade. Weapon Upgrade Kits modify selected characteristics; advanced improvements require abilities. Duplicate equipment can become materials or parts. [S15]

GLOO restrains enemies, temporarily covers electrical hazards and creates climbable deposits. Its famous flexibility has concrete limits: **GLOO does not attach to GLOO**. The developer explains that supporting chains of attached, breakable deposits created expensive constraints on console hardware. Rejected surfaces and bouncing projectiles communicate further limits. This qualifies promotional descriptions that imply universal adhesion and player anecdotes that casually describe stacking foam on foam. [S15, S18]

### Survival and loadouts

Health, suit integrity, stamina and Psi have different roles. Friendly Medical, Engineering and Science Operators replenish health, suit condition and Psi respectively. Big Bang Candy or a Methuselah Apple provides modest recovery; Medkits, Suit Repair Kits, Psi Hypos and Anti-Rad address distinct needs. Alcohol can impair movement and vision. Reaching a service point can save consumables, but carrying a cure and safely reaching assistance are different advantages. [S12, S14]

Suit and scope chipsets are removable loadout choices. Examples include Thermal Shielding, Artax enhancements, Mimic Detection, faster or longer-distance scanning and Psi-cost optimizers. Random ordinary finds contrast with quest-specific hardware. Slot capacity creates selection pressure after discovery. Recycler Shielding is an equipment-specific exception to the usual danger of one's own recycler blast; its existence does not make every character immune. Exact edition availability should be checked before planning around unusual bonus equipment. [S13]

### Access, activities and useful objects

Keycards, discovered passwords, hacking, physical openings and powered abilities support overlapping access routes. Reading a terminal can reveal a person, route or optional objective rather than merely deliver background prose. Looking Glass displays can conceal physical spaces. Crates may obstruct, provide cover or become ammunition for Leverage; turrets can be carried and repaired. These are authored affordances with compatibility rules, not universal physics promises. [S12, S33]

The station also includes smaller activities: collecting the Starbender books, tracing a crew tabletop-game treasure hunt, recovering a distinctive golden pistol and completing a horticultural experiment. Gathering Echoes turns Danielle Sho's voice recordings—including a tabletop session—into evidence for a biometric lock. Such side material connects crew identity to usable knowledge. It does not make Prey a fully playable tabletop RPG, farming game or romance simulator. [S33]

## 4. Progression, economy, time and failure

Recycling is material conversion, not selling junk for an abstract currency. Fabrication additionally requires a plan. Ammunition, medical supplies, Artax equipment and Neuromods compete for recoverable matter. A Recycler Charge applies conversion over an area and can destroy useful objects or injure its user. [S3]

**Copy Protection adds a different production gate.** When the Neuromod fabrication license expires, more materials alone do not repair access. The documented quest route reaches Halden Graves' workstation in Neuromod Division and renews the license through its Utilities menu. That restores permission to fabricate; the recipe still consumes materials. A copied “unlimited” description refers to the license, not free matter or effortless progression. The audit does not assert an exact universal number of crafts before the quest triggers. [S39]

**Interpretation:** the core economy makes short- and long-term needs commensurable. Turning resources into ammunition solves immediate danger; using them for capacity or access may improve later scavenging. Knowledge, research, plans and permanent installations progress alongside material stocks. Killing is therefore not the only productive activity, and a familiar route can become valuable again after a new capability is acquired.

Death normally leads back to saved progress, not Mooncrash's corruption-cycle structure. Xbox's accessibility listing identifies pause and save-anytime functionality; this is a platform declaration, not a hands-on audit of every sequence. Difficulty and the later Story Mode affect the challenge contract. Optional Survival Mode adds hazards such as oxygen and injury concerns rather than establishing that all launch campaign players experienced those rules. [S20, S32]

### The optional survival rules, not merely “more damage”

The community mode reference identifies three start-of-game/NG+ choices: **Weapon Degradation, Extra Traumas and Oxygen**. Weapon wear can eventually jam affected firearms; Repair and spare parts provide recovery. Wrench, Boltcaster and thrown charges are not subject to that same degradation rule. Oxygen makes suit damage consequential during exposed travel, with refill infrastructure and supplies creating another route constraint. The reference's imprecise update chronology is not imported over Bethesda's June 2018 announcement. [S36, S20]

| Extra trauma | Distinct consequence and recovery |
| --- | --- |
| Concussion | Impairs Psi/vision and prevents Neuromod installation; Brained Pills or a Medical Operator treat it. |
| Fracture | Restricts movement and sprinting under gravity; a Skeletal Repair Kit or Medical Operator repairs it. |
| Hemorrhage | Makes exertion dangerous, including health loss while sprinting; Coagulating Gel or a Medical Operator treats it. |
| Burns | Reduce available maximum health; a Dermaweb Skin Graft or Medical Operator addresses the injury. |

Radiation sickness is a separate condition rather than a fifth newly invented trauma; Anti-Rad and medical assistance address it. The horticultural **Superfruit** can cure these injuries and radiation sickness, giving that side experiment a useful survival connection. These are documented mode-specific rules, not a current-build reproduction or medical advice. [S35]

Early play emphasizes scarce gear and incomplete spatial knowledge; middle play adds alien research, fabrication and broader station access; late play revisits locations under changed conditions while resolving major objectives and people. Further purpose comes from alternate approaches, different ability commitments and New Game Plus rather than an endlessly advancing character level. Bethesda's NG+ support requires a completed campaign save; this dossier does not attribute community mod features to that mode. [S5, S20, S34]

There is no ordinary player-owned settlement, shop-management or party-leveling loop in the documented campaign. Temporary allied machines/Phantoms and rescued crew serve different roles from a persistent, directly commanded RPG party. Named relationships, institutional conflict and individual judgments are important, but they are not a universal faction-reputation meter or a player-directed romance system. [S2, S16, S25]

## 5. Station, people and AI

The official tour contrasts an expensive, reassuring lobby and comfortable Crew Quarters with the utilitarian zero-gravity G.U.T.S. freight route. Exterior travel connects station areas as access opens. The outbreak is recent enough that ordinary workplaces still show interrupted lives. Returns may expose changed threats or consequences rather than reset the entire location. This is bounded station reactivity, not evidence that every worker follows an independently simulated daily schedule. [S28]

Mimics turn ordinary-object recognition into suspicion. Weavers connect Typhon growth to Coral and the creation of Phantoms from human corpses; Phantoms can retain traces of former human identity. Telepaths control people, whereas Technopaths threaten machines. Nightmare represents a formidable pursuer. These behavior families create different information and counterplay problems. The developer's ecological framing does **not** prove that every spawn is causally reproduced through a continuously simulated food web. [S19]

Human individuality comes through jobs, possessions, messages, voice recordings, requests and survival. Mikhaila Ilyushin and Dayo Igwe know the earlier Morgan; helping them can force the player to confront a past they did not personally choose. Sarah Elazar represents surviving security personnel. Danielle Sho's relationship and recorded voice turn apparently optional social detail into navigational and ethical context. January's advice and Alex Yu's aims are not interchangeable guarantees of truth. [S16, S25, S33]

**Interpretation:** the strongest social effect is frequently not a dialogue wheel. It is recognizing that an apparent obstacle is a person, or learning that a technical solution has consequences for someone already encountered. This demands clear identity continuity and trustworthy feedback; an unreadable or silently failed rescue weakens the intended emotional connection.

## 6. Narrative and consequences

**Opening and major ending spoilers follow.** Morgan's uncertain memory makes earlier instructions, institutional evidence and present choices potentially conflict. The campaign couples an outbreak investigation with questions about identity, experimentation and responsibility. Its major resolution distinguishes destruction of the station from a Nullwave approach intended to neutralize Typhon while preserving it. [S6, S25]

The epilogue reframes the experience as a test involving a Typhon and reconstructed Morgan memories. Alex and Operators bearing the voices of Igwe, Mikhaila, Elazar and Danielle assess consequential behavior. The important documented scene is that past actions are brought into a judgment about empathy and coexistence. A contemporary explainer mixes scene description with theories about off-screen history; those theories are not treated here as proof of every real crew member's fate. Nor does the reveal mean that every campaign choice is mechanically inconsequential. [S25]

The reception dispute matters. Some critics find the crew's details and imperfect outcomes affecting; others find the main narrative underdeveloped and the final demand for attachment unearned. A moral assessment can connect many local decisions, but it can also feel like retrospective grading if players did not understand what was being observed. [S6, S9]

## 7. Eight concrete situations

Only cases 1 and 2 are attributed observed play accounts. Cases 3–8 are **constructed, rules-based illustrations** of documented mechanics or quests, not claimed personal playthroughs. Their causal framing is analysis; cited sources establish the underlying rules.

### 1. A toy operates a locked office

Phil Savage reports finding an office whose broken window was too small to enter. His familiar small-object transformation approach lacked a useful perch. He fabricated a toy crossbow and darts, hit the visible terminal's Utility control, then selected its door-unlock function. He later found a keycard on a nearby corpse. [S5]

The result changes the next decision: the player can continue searching for credentials or spend resources to exploit a line of sight. The later key does not invalidate the improvised success. This illustrates overlapping routes, not a guarantee that every interface accepts every projectile.

### 2. An improvised falling-machine trap

Lead systems designer Seth Shain describes a playtest in which Lift Field raised a heavy machine, GLOO fixed it to a wall, and a pursuing Phantom passed beneath it. Shooting away the support dropped the machine onto the enemy. [S18]

The plan combines lift, adhesion, destruction, gravity and enemy movement. It requires appropriate geometry and timing; it is not a canned ability called “machine trap.” The player must next decide whether the setup cost and enemy-positioning risk beat a direct attack. This account remains a developer-reported incident, not a reproduced benchmark.

### 3. Research before disposal

A player wants a new alien capability but encounters a dangerous living specimen. Scanning with the Psychoscope can reveal research and weaknesses; immediately killing it removes that particular live-scanning opportunity. Installing an unlocked power still requires resources. [S1]

The decision is therefore observation versus immediate safety, followed by investment versus conservation. A safe sightline or temporary control may make research feasible. The limitation is that knowing about an ability is not the same as owning it, and a desired target may not be safely observable from the current position.

### 4. Junk competes with immediate survival

The player has a fabrication plan, unwanted objects and insufficient supplies for an upcoming area. Recycling the objects yields materials, but deciding whether to fabricate ammunition, medicine or a Neuromod changes what remains available afterward. An area Recycler Charge can recover matter from awkward objects, with collateral risk. [S3]

The immediate result is an affordable next step rather than free abundance. The next choice is whether to revisit a recycler, carry bulky loot farther or discard something. Conversion has value because plans, inventory capacity and material composition can each be the binding constraint.

### 5. The attacker is a rescue target

In Crew Quarters, mind-controlled humans can rush toward Morgan and die explosively. A player trying to preserve lives approaches cautiously and uses the Disruptor rather than treating every moving threat as a lethal target. The guide describes unconscious victims as no longer presenting that immediate danger. Mindjack offers a different ability-based intervention. [S1, S2, S33]

The rescued person's status changes the meaning of winning the encounter: surviving alone is insufficient. Range, alerting others and collateral damage still matter. The next decision is whether to neutralize the controlling threat, protect incapacitated people or retreat. A rescue-capable tool does not automatically prevent all subsequent harm.

### 6. Medicine becomes a routing deadline

Mikhaila's request creates a two-hour medicine deadline after the relevant conversation. Her supplies are in a breached office accessible from outside; nearby access depends on campaign progress. A cautious player compares the detour with available resources and other unfinished work, retrieves the medicine and returns to deliver it. [S23]

The result is not just another inventory reward: it can preserve a character with later knowledge and requests. The next decision concerns what obligations her survival creates. A launch guide's suggestion that exterior access is universally impossible before Reboot is not adopted; routes and unlocked airlocks matter.

### 7. Access and honesty are different problems

Mikhaila's father investigation leads to restricted Deep Storage records. A walkthrough describes reaching the relevant area either through high-level hacking or an overhead route using GLOO. Once there, the player can transmit or delete the recording rather than merely “complete the hack.” [S24]

The technical obstacle has substitutes; the moral choice does not disappear because the room was entered cleverly. Transmitting evidence supports disclosure, while deletion conceals it. The next decision is how to face Mikhaila with what the record reveals about Morgan's past. An alternate traversal route is not an alternate truth.

### 8. Mooncrash: one character prepares another's escape

A player begins a simulation with an engineer-capable character, repairs infrastructure and consumes some available supplies. After that character exits or dies, another unlocked character continues within the same simulation instance. Repairs and depleted resources can affect this successor. The player must budget routes, equipment and distinct escape opportunities across the roster while corruption escalates. [S21, S22]

The result rewards planning a sequence, not merely maximizing one survivor. Eventually resetting the simulation changes that local state; longer-term unlocks and purchased abilities have different persistence. The counterexample is crucial: **one character's death does not automatically reset the whole shared simulation**. These are Mooncrash rules, not the base campaign's death rules.

## 8. Art, audio, interface and production

### A plausible place with legible contrasts

Talos I's luxurious front-of-house spaces contrast with its maintenance infrastructure. The developer's Neo-Deco direction combines alternate-history corporate ambition with a working station. Comfortable materials help make the interruption of ordinary life visible; utilitarian shafts support a different movement vocabulary. These descriptions come from written creator accounts, not a claim that reference images were independently analyzed during this pass. [S16, S28]

The nearly amorphous Typhon visual direction helps sustain uncertainty rather than present a familiar army of humanoids. Mimic disguise challenges object recognition; Phantom traces connect monster and former person. At the interface level, pausing equipment selection offers room to think, whereas scanning, small moving targets and repeated loading can create friction. Reviewers' control complaints are platform- and launch-build-specific evidence, not universal accessibility conclusions. [S12, S19, S6, S8]

### Collaboration and iteration

Colantonio drafted the initial story arc during a flight. Austin's team developed it through collaboration among writers, artists and level designers; structural work preceded finished dialogue. Austin Grossman and Harvey Smith contributed early ideas. Chris Avellone helped develop characters including Igwe and Mikhaila. The Reployer joke arose from an art object whose function developers themselves could not explain. A partner-labeled Ricardo Bare interview independently discusses reciprocal influence among art, story and gameplay; its promotional status remains disclosed. [S16, S29]

GLOO's implementation evolved from less precise stream-like behavior toward projectiles. Its traversal possibilities affected level construction, and attachment restrictions were deliberate production constraints. Shain's account is evidence of iteration and tradeoffs; it is not a basis for reconstructing proprietary code. [S18]

**A second primary production account:** in Ben Barrett's March 17, 2017 interview, Ricardo Bare identifies Arx Fatalis's interconnected dungeon as a structural influence on Talos I, unlike a sequence of separately briefed missions. Austin and Lyon developed Prey and Dishonored 2 separately while sharing some people and feedback; this was not one combined all-hands team. He describes bugs whose reproduction depended on a long prior sequence of actions, making persistent systemic freedom expensive to test. His “Turret Lord” tester carried and repaired several turrets, conserved ammunition and later fabricated more—but a Technopath appropriated that strategy. The story connects a supported specialization to a concrete counter, not a guarantee that one build dominates every encounter. [S43]

### Sound and emotional pacing

The soundtrack credits extend beyond Mick Gordon to Matt Piersall, Ben Crossbones and Raphael Colantonio. Gordon's synthesizers and arpeggiated guitar followed a melancholic space-western direction. The confident helicopter opening deliberately contrasts with later vulnerability. [S17]

**Interpretation:** that transition can communicate loss of security before a player fully understands the plot. Music need not function only as a danger indicator. Likewise, workspaces can reveal institutional identity without each room explaining itself in text. The tradeoff is that atmospheric detail still needs enough interaction and pacing to reward attention.

## 9. Updates, expansions, multiplayer and distribution

### Free campaign modes and paid Mooncrash

Bethesda announced Story Mode, Survival Mode and New Game Plus as free campaign additions in June 2018. Mooncrash was a separate paid expansion, also packaged with the base game in a Deluxe offering. Its framing follows a KASMA operative examining events at the Pytheas lunar installation. This is not a continuation that gives the original Morgan campaign a randomly generated station. [S20]

Mooncrash develops five unlockable characters with different abilities and escape constraints. Sim Points purchase starting equipment; plans, upgrades and objectives support repeated attempts. A rising corruption system pressures time and strengthens danger. Shared local state spans successive characters within an instance, while resets change hazards and resources. The culminating coordinated escape goal asks the player to reason across the roster, not control five avatars simultaneously. [S21, S22]

The five bodies are not interchangeable versions of Morgan. Their documented development options include:

| Character / role | Distinct planning leverage |
| --- | --- |
| Andrius Alekna — Volunteer | Psi-focused attacks, Lift Field and Burrow; movement and exposure differ from an ordinary gun route. |
| Joan Winslow — Engineer | Repair, Dismantle, a summoned turret and Reverse Engineer; infrastructure and supplies can benefit a later survivor. |
| Riley Yu — Director | Psychoscope research, Mimic Matter and Phantom Genesis; investigation and alien-tool access shape the run. |
| Vijay Bhatia — Security Officer | Firearms, Leverage, Combat Focus and Combat Medic; physical access and sustained fighting. |
| Claire Whitten — Custodian / KASMA Spy | Hacking, stealth, the Psychostatic Cutter, Remote Manipulation and Phantom Shift; concealed or remote access. |

Reverse Engineer makes a **temporary** plan and allows only one such plan at a time. Riley's Psychotronics upgrades change scanning/marked-target/detection functions, not simply the base campaign's chipset-slot progression. These are Mooncrash-specific capabilities, not new Morgan skills. [S42]

Faulkner's GameRevolution review praises the expansion's substantial reworking of the underlying game rather than treating it as an afterthought. Bokor's Third Coast Review account values preparation and varied runs but wants more control over conditions or a broader play space and finds the story payoff less substantial. These are expansion-specific judgments, not two additional reviews of the base campaign. [S21, S22]

### Typhon Hunter and VR are separate contracts

Typhon Hunter's store listing dates its release December 11, 2018. Its multiplayer pits one Morgan against up to five player-controlled Mimics. A publisher factsheet reproduced by Gematsu describes a preparation period, disguise, a timer, Morgan's respawns and Mimics' limited lives. It also identifies maps such as Morgan's office and the Yellow Tulip. The earlier planned summer release is not the actual December launch. [S26, S27]

TranStar VR supplies separate single-player escape-room-style puzzles set before the outbreak; it is not the complete campaign converted into native VR. Typhon Hunter's ownership requirements and promotional descriptions do not establish present matchmaking health. No current-server or VR-access test was performed. The base campaign is not native cooperative multiplayer. [S26]

### Marketing and reuse

The launch campaign used weapon/power demonstrations, station tours, story and composer interviews, with a console opening-hour demo promoted before release. The 2018 announcement paired an immediately available expansion with free modes and a Deluxe package. Bethesda also promoted Mooncrash Mania, a creator-oriented charity competition with a stated matching commitment. These are documented promotional activities, not verified totals raised or measured acquisition attribution. [S4, S17, S20]

**Interpretation:** a short, explainable improvised solution is a useful sharing unit: it shows a surprising result while letting viewers reconstruct the rules. Mooncrash adds another unit, the planned multi-character run. These affordances make creator coverage plausible, but the sources do not establish what share of sales came from clips, streamers, demos or discounts.

## 10. Commercial and participation context

The documented business structure is a premium base game, paid expansion/package options and free updates, rather than a recurring paid currency economy. Storefront access and support obligations differ from the fictional recycling economy. No verified lifetime units, profit, retention or development budget were established in this pass. Their absence here is not evidence of commercial failure. [S20, S26]

A reproduction of NPD's May 2017 U.S. analysis places Prey fifth in that month's dollar-sales chart, with **digital sales excluded for Prey**. This is a dated regional ranking with an explicit coverage limitation, not a worldwide unit count or a lifetime profitability measure. The source's malformed reporting-period date is not repeated. [S30]

Current Steam review texts establish continuing player discussion, not concurrency, active population or a sales denominator. Later online modes introduce a population dependency that the solo campaign does not share, but no current service-population measurement was collected. [S10, S26]

## 11. Reception: five independent written reviews

All five complete critical bodies were read in the original pass, including GameSpot's platform update and Destructoid's closing paragraphs. These are distinct accounts, not a population consensus or five score blurbs.

### Phil Savage — PC Gamer, May 9, 2017

Savage values the believable station, discovery and conversion economy but finds repeated Mimic encounters irritating and early combat unrewarding. He reports apparently inconsistent stealth detection and overlapping dialogue. Enjoying the exploration experience does not mean endorsing every subsystem. His crossbow account is preserved in case 1. [S5]

### Tamoor Hussain — GameSpot, updated May 16, 2017

Hussain finds the opening stronger than later character development and much workplace correspondence mundane. Early scarcity feels constraining; later upgrades enable satisfying combinations. His platform update reports better controls, loading and performance on his PC than on the consoles he reviewed. The ending's emotional request did not feel sufficiently earned to him. These are specific experiences, not promises about current hardware. [S6]

### Dominic Preston — Tech Advisor, May 10, 2017

Preston praises Mimic-induced suspicion and the breadth of exploration tools, while finding the game derivative and its main story weaker after the opening. He reports a disappearing weapon, an animation problem and mistimed sound cues. These are launch observations, not verified current defects. His claim that all skills cannot be obtained in one playthrough is not adopted as a progression rule. [S7]

### Chad Sapieha — Common Sense Media, page updated October 9, 2025

Sapieha emphasizes moral choices, representation and exploratory freedom. Early aiming and enemy tracking frustrate; later area powers help. Late backtracking and loading interrupt momentum. The publication's age guidance is not a technical difficulty measurement. Its written critic section was read separately from the site's AI-generated summary of children's reviews. [S8]

### Brett Makedonski — Destructoid, May 15, 2017

Makedonski values crew stories but feels conflicted about discovering outcomes his own decisions missed. His PS4 account describes sluggish aiming and repetitive, difficult combat. He recounts using a high perch and an explosive tank against two enemies, illustrating why environmental methods can matter when gunplay disappoints. He ultimately finds the game worthwhile but less cohesive than expected. [S9]

Cee disliked controls, repeated reading and Mimics and alleged technical problems. Tygron found conservation and stronger enemies on return visits exhausting. The retrieved posts were dated September in the inspected surface. [S10]

This is a selected sample with small helpful counts, not an all-time top-review census, a prevalence estimate or reproduced bug evidence. Displayed cumulative hours do not establish hours at posting. Brief praise-only entries do not count toward the five-review minimum. Vision821's loose description of foam layering does not override the developer's explicit adhesion limit. [S10, S18]

## 12. Transferable inspiration and limits

The following are **research interpretations**, not accepted OpenLegend features or copied content.

**Overlapping access methods should preserve different costs.** Case 1 works because knowledge, fabrication and spatial fit offer distinct ways forward. A system needs legible compatibility and failure feedback; indiscriminate “anything works” removes the decision. A simpler alternative is a small set of explicit affordances whose combinations are consistently honored.

**Identity can change the meaning of combat.** Case 5 turns apparent enemies into rescue targets. This requires reliable person identity, status feedback and accounting for collateral effects. It should not be copied as an invisible morality score that punishes the player for outcomes the interface never revealed.

**Material conversion can unify otherwise isolated loops.** Case 4 connects exploration, survival and progression. The dependency is a balanced set of bottlenecks: capacity, recipes, composition, risk and travel. Excessive abundance makes recycling busywork; excessive scarcity makes experimentation feel irresponsible. The review disagreement supplies a concrete warning against one universal tuning preference.

**A past action can prepare a different actor's future.** Mooncrash's shared instance makes repair, looting and escape choices interdependent. Persistence boundaries must be explicit, especially around death and resets. The concept does not require roguelite resets in every game; a persistent expedition could instead retain consequences without repeatedly erasing local progress.

**Authored social evidence can be mechanically useful.** Danielle's recordings and Mikhaila's requests give people roles beyond decorative lore. The dependency is that discovering information changes a decision or possibility. Requiring excessive reading merely to prove attentiveness can produce the fatigue reported by critics.

**Constraint disclosure is part of expressive power.** The GLOO examples depend on bounded surface and attachment rules. Explaining a useful limit can increase trust more than adding another unpredictable effect. Named Prey characters, props and powers remain reference examples, not proposed OpenLegend assets or names.
