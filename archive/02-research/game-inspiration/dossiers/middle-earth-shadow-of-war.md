## Reception: five independent reviews, not five score blurbs

### Andy Kelly — PC Gamer, October 5, 2017

Kelly values expressive orcs, tactical weaknesses and the pleasure of an established bodyguard more than Talion's solemn plot. His named examples include frightening Lûga Skull-Cracker with flies and summoning Az-Grels Mountain-Eater. He finds the loot comparisons and map tasks excessive, and regards the bright vegetation of Núrnen as welcome relief from duller environments. His basic information box says no multiplayer; this must not erase the asynchronous online modes documented elsewhere. The launch review also predates the Market's removal. [S1]

### Robert Ramsey — Push Square, original 2017 text republished October 28, 2020

Ramsey enjoys the expanded Nemesis relationships, smooth fighting and large battles on his reviewed PS4 Pro. Unlike Kelly, he finds the wider authored cast and plot reasonably involving, while criticizing routine objectives between scenes. He regards loot as a welcome but shallow addition. The article explicitly republishes archived prose, including complaints about the Market; its 2020 header is not evidence those monetization systems still existed then. [S2]

### Chris Carter — Destructoid, displayed October 11, 2017

Carter finds movement, counter-based fighting and learning an enemy's vulnerabilities satisfying. The expanded army/fortress structure supports memorable self-directed stories despite a plot he finds convoluted and a protagonist he finds bland. He particularly dislikes stopping the action to pick up and compare many similar items. He objects to the single-player loot shop while reporting that he never needed to buy power. The current original page displays October 11; some indexes give October 5, so the discrepancy is preserved rather than silently resolved. [S3]

### Peter Taylor — Digital Chumps, October 16, 2017, Xbox One

Taylor praises consequences within orc society, battle heft and strongly performed introductions. His actual play account includes a grunt earning a title after killing him and an opponent returning repeatedly with worsening wounds. He finds the Talion/Celebrimbor conflict interesting but strongly objects to the adaptation of Shelob. Asset glitches sometimes cost him captains. He rejects the need for paid chests without endorsing their inclusion. These are launch observations, not verified current bugs or current purchasing options. [S4]

### Paul Bird — Horror Geek Life, October 21, 2017

Bird is much more enthusiastic about the expanded plot, collection rewards and size than Kelly or Carter. He specifically describes attachment to followers and distress when they die or betray him. The expanded stealth and power options support different approaches; fort assaults are impressive but sometimes hard to navigate amid the chaos. His broad claim that committed Tolkien fans will welcome the twists directly conflicts with other reviewers' reactions. His loose region/fortress wording and misspelled currency are not used as precise rules. [S5]

## Firsthand accessibility account

Dr. Josh Straub's November 7, 2017 DAGERSystem review, now archived by Can I Play That?, documents a different barrier than combat difficulty. Static controls, frequent context changes and moving quickly between face buttons and shoulder buttons prevented him progressing much beyond an hour without help. He also flags very small subtitles and potentially troublesome color coding/visual blur, while explicitly limiting conclusions about parts he could not fully test. This is not a comprehensive September 2026 accessibility audit, nor a sixth full-campaign review. [S6]

## Helpful Steam testimony

The English **Most Helpful (All Time)** surface was read September 26, 2026. Sraxton's September 16, 2022 account follows Krosh from killer to fire-scarred recurring enemy to recruited captain, then mourns his loss in a fight pit. Vvhispr's April 29, 2021 account describes repeated shaming of Dûsh, attachment, betrayal and regret after killing him. Michael {United Critics}'s February 6, 2021 recommendation praises the breadth and performance while criticizing unskippable introductions, misdirected contextual movement and badly timed ambushes; it is marked as a free copy. [S7]

These are player-reported stories, not independently reproduced outcomes or evidence that a particular sequence is guaranteed. The displayed current hours are not hours at publication. The sample's positive recommendations contain both enthusiasm and friction; it does not establish prevalence. Vvhispr's description of Iron Will as forever unrecruitable is too broad to adopt as a precise mechanics rule. No inaccessible negative body is counted.

## Primary creator perspective

In Matt Kamen's 2017 WIRED interview, studio head Kevin Stephens describes roughly three years of work, the lack of outside feedback during secrecy and the intention to preserve personality after recruitment. He distinguishes camaraderie from a direct romance system. This is an intent account, not proof that every generated relationship is convincing. The interview's planned August launch is historical; the finished game released in October. [S8]
