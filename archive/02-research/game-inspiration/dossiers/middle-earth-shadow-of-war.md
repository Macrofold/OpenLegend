# G116 — Middle-earth: Shadow of War

[Library](../README.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Progress](../research-progress.md) · [Requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research pass — September 26, 2026.** Reference research, not accepted OpenLegend design. Scope: the 2017 game, the materially revised post-July-2018 experience, its two story expansions and separate Nemesis expansions. Five full original base-game reviews, a separate accessibility account, an expansion review and helpful Steam narratives were read. No gameplay, footage, current-server test or proprietary-code inspection was performed. Contemporary guides and patch milestones do not establish the exact latest executable on every platform.

## 1. Identity and changing player promise

Monolith's action RPG, published by Warner Bros., released October 10, 2017 for the original PC/PS4/Xbox One generation. Talion and the wraith Celebrimbor fight Sauron's forces by using a new Ring to turn enemies into an army. The opening emphasizes fighting and tracking; the second act introduces recruitment and fortress command. It is a sequel to Shadow of Mordor, not an expanded edition whose rules can be freely substituted. The core campaign is single-player; online modes add asynchronous encounters rather than a jointly controlled cooperative campaign. [S9, S19, S2]

The July 17, 2018 update removed the Market, shortened the Shadow Wars epilogue, expanded progression and changed how rewards reached the Garrison. Consequently, launch criticisms of purchasing orcs remain important history but cannot describe a current purchase button. The reproduced official notes also establish that online-recruited captains can reach the Garrison and training orders can be bought with earned Mirian. The surviving Garrison is not evidence that the paid Market survived. [S10]

**Interpretation:** the distinctive promise is not merely defeating many enemies. It is meeting an identifiable opponent again under changed circumstances, remembering what happened, and deciding whether this time to kill, humiliate, recruit or protect him. Fortress warfare gives those local relationships a larger purpose, but its demands can also reduce interesting individuals to replaceable ratings.

## 2. Complete major action and progression inventory

### Talion is a defined person, not a created class

The player chooses skills and equipment rather than a custom race, profession or biography. The principal skill branches and base abilities are:

| Branch | Base action families |
| --- | --- |
| Combat | Execution, Perfect Counter, Critical Strike, Ground Finisher, Brutal Aggression, Retaliation |
| Predator | Elven Agility, Brutalize, Poison Tendril, Wraith Chain, Deadly Specter, Death Threat |
| Ranged | Detonate, Freeze Pin, Bird of Prey, Mighty Shot, Shadow Strike |
| Wraith | Brace of Daggers, Elven Light, Ice Storm, Consume, Treasure Hunter |
| Mounted | Caragor Rider, Graug Rider, Call Mount, Shadow Mount, Dragon Rider |
| Story | Drain, Spectral Glaive, Shadow Strider, Call Followers, Dominate Captain |

Skill points, level or story prerequisites unlock capabilities and modifiers. A base ability's alternative upgrades are choices, not all simultaneously active: elemental effects or teleportation variants change how an action works. Movement, mounts, followers and mind control therefore develop alongside damage. Later story changes add further powers. [S11]

### Combat and traversal

Sword strikes, counters, dodges, vaults and ground finishes operate amid many enemies. A charged glaive offers crowd control; a frontal routine that works against an ordinary swordsman may fail against a shielded enemy, large Olog or captain with a relevant counter. Alarms can bring reinforcements. Terrain, spacing and maintaining awareness matter even when the protagonist can kill ordinary enemies rapidly. [S12]

Climbing, ledge movement, running and supernatural traversal connect rooftops, towers and streets. Stealth attacks use position and detection state, not an independent turn-based phase. Suspicion and full alert differ; after breaking line of sight, enemies can search the last known position. Lures create openings, while Wraith vision helps identify targets and useful objects without making Talion invisible. An immunity can make an apparently ideal captain assassination ineffective. [S13]

Ranged combat adds two limits: elf-shot ammunition and Focus for slowed aiming. Headshots, pinning and Shadow Strike can remove a guard, restrict movement or convert a distant target into a route through space. Quick-thrown daggers interrupt rather than simply replacing the bow. The ranged slot also supports a forge hammer; its existence does not turn every ranged action into an interchangeable attack. [S14, S20]

**Might, Wrath and health are different resources.** Might supports actions such as executions, Elven Light and combat draining; spending it to recover competes with offensive use. Wrath builds toward a temporary empowered state rather than supplying every ordinary attack. Health, status effects and recovery remain relevant during spectacular attacks. These distinctions make an ability combination depend on the state of the fight, not just whether it was bought in a menu. [S15]

### Environment and mounts

Bait can draw beasts; opening a cage releases a creature that is not automatically friendly. Grog barrels can become explosions or poisoning opportunities, fires can be detonated and Morgai Fly nests can disturb enemies. These are authored interaction families, not evidence that every scenic object has a comprehensive physical simulation. Caragors, Graugs and drakes offer different movement and combat possibilities as mounting abilities develop. Trait-sensitive reactions turn the same environmental prop into either an opportunity or a poor choice. [S16, S11]

### Equipment, gems and crafting limits

Six equipment slots hold a sword, dagger, ranged weapon, armor, cloak and Ring/rune equipment. Their primary contributions differ: melee, stealth and ranged damage are not one shared weapon statistic, while armor, cloak and Ring support health, stealth and Focus. Gear arrives through encounters and rewards; rarity, conditional effects, challenges and legendary-set bonuses can matter more than the displayed level. Unwanted equipment can become Mirian. This is loot modification and build assembly, not a profession-based smithing or resource-production economy. The guide's translated 'Heroic' label is not used as an extra rarity above Epic. [S20]

Gems supply another compositional layer. Red supports damage, green survival and white wealth/progression; the effect depends on the occupied slot. A wealth gem in armor helps personal experience, while one in Ring equipment improves recruitment-related levels. Combining three matching gems produces a higher-quality gem across the documented five quality levels. The guide's green-weapon damage description is questionable and is not adopted as a precise life-steal rule or balance table. [S21]

The July 2018 update raised Talion's cap to 80 and enemies' to 85, added repeatable Prestige skills after Act III, and enabled earned-currency gear leveling and perfect-gem secondary-trait rerolls after required challenges. These are verified update changes, not a claim every item is freely rerollable at the start. [S10]

## 3. Nemesis: people, information and delegated violence

### What makes a captain more than a health bar

Each region has an army hierarchy. Captains have names, voices, appearance, level, class and trait combinations; Warchiefs have bodyguards; an Overlord anchors a fortress. Immunities, fears, vulnerabilities, enrages and special attacks change which plan works. Intelligence makes the composition visible before the encounter. A higher level does not automatically outweigh a decisive fear or immunity. Army links make bodyguard relationships legible, not just implied by dialogue. [S18]

Classes create recognizable behavior families. Tanks endure and recover; Berserkers are aggressively dangerous; Slayers specialize in fighting; Commanders strengthen or supply supporting forces; Beastmasters bring creatures into the encounter. These examples do not exhaust every class, nor validate the guide's universal recommendation to recruit everyone. The tactical question is compatibility with the opponent and assigned role, not a single best-captain ranking. [S23]

The production account adds an important limit to 'randomness': advanced classes helped constrain trait generation because unrestricted combinations could create unreasonable opponents. A memorable procedural individual is assembled within authored rules and performance material. It is not a language model improvising an unrestricted personality or a simulation of an entire independent domestic life. [S27]

### Recruiting, shaming and orders

Domination follows weakening a target and meeting recruitment conditions. A higher-level captain or an Iron Will trait can obstruct immediate recruitment. Shaming can reduce level, but should not be equated with risk-free editing of a character sheet. Recruit, release into continued fighting, withdraw, dismiss and fight again are distinct decisions. Interrogating Worms or finding intelligence reveals enemy information; the guide's assertion that a weakness cannot function before intelligence is acquired is not relied upon here. [S19]

Recruited captains can be sent to attack, support another operation, infiltrate as spies or fight in pits. These operations can proceed without Talion personally performing every action. Intervention can change the odds but does not make the subordinate invulnerable. The army interface is an assignment surface rather than unrestricted RTS control over every soldier's footsteps. [S19]

Pit fights expose an especially sharp tradeoff: choose a compatible participant, then watch the captain rather than directly fighting as him. A win can improve the follower; a loss can permanently remove someone the player values. The guide proposes sacrificing a weak follower and recruiting the winner afterward. That is a documented optimization possibility, not a morally neutral description of how attached players experience it. The qualitative Steam accounts below illustrate exactly this tension. [S22, S7]

### Memory, allegiance and continuity

Captains can return after apparent defeats with scars and references to prior encounters, rise after killing Talion, interrupt another engagement, betray a former master or intervene as a savior. Personal stories are conditional possibilities, not guaranteed sequences for every generated enemy. Names and visual changes provide recall cues; voiced interruptions make the game acknowledge the relationship. The cost is that repeated introductions can interrupt a player's immediate plan. [S4, S7]

Recruitment does not establish a conventional romance or free-form dialogue relationship. The emotional connection often comes from usefulness, recognition, survival and the player's interpretation of coercion or betrayal. There are authored companions and story encounters, but no evidenced marriage, family succession, civilian career system or comprehensive daily-life schedule. Those absences matter: the game concentrates its character simulation on conflict and loyalty rather than simulating everything a person could do. [S8, S27]

## 4. Fortresses, activities, failure and the return loop

### Attack and defense are different problems

A siege begins before the wall is reached: inspect defenders, weaken Warchiefs and choose an assault force and siege upgrades. Up to six assault captains and their forces can support the attack as capacity develops. Capturing successive points leads toward the Overlord encounter. The stronghold's final confrontation is not simply the entire open-field army following Talion into a room; a selected bodyguard can be particularly useful there. Preparation changes the fight, but an army rating is not a guaranteed result. [S24]

Defense divides attention across capture points, attackers and vulnerable followers. A bleeding captain offers a rescue opportunity, while unattended enemies can advance the assault. Siege beasts and enemy leaders can demand personal intervention. These obligations create decisions about whom to save and which position to concede rather than merely reversing the attack animation. A fortress supports appointments and selected upgrades, not a freeform city-building economy with households, farms and trade routes. [S25]

### Exploration and authored activities

Main quests coexist with Nemesis rivalries, fights, fortress events, equipment challenges and Celebrimbor's Shadows of the Past challenges. Optional performance conditions reward mastery of particular actions rather than only survival. A map icon can therefore mean authored story, a generated conflict or a specific skill test; treating all icons as the same content misses their different consequences. [S26]

Haedir towers become scouting and fast-travel infrastructure. Surveying reveals nearby collectibles: Ithildin words lead to a poem-completion door and legendary equipment; Web of Fate fragments form Shelob memories through an alignment puzzle; Gondorian artifacts add short histories. These vary the player's verbs, but several reviewers experience the overall collection layer as busywork. The five-region Ithildin guide distinguishes Minas Ithil, Cirith Ungol, Núrnen, Seregost and Gorgoroth; this is not a claim all five have identical conquest rules. [S36, S37]

### Death is a state transition, not only a reload

Outside immediate danger, limited regeneration helps, while draining an enemy restores health at the cost of exposure during the action. Fire, poison and curses impose different pressures. Last Chance recovery depends on difficulty and encounter conditions, so it is not an unlimited safety net. Death advances the Nemesis situation: killers may gain status and other conflicts can resolve before Talion returns. That makes a failed duel part of the world's history, though the protagonist's persistence does not guarantee a follower's survival. [S17]

The early loop is learning counters and gaining enough capability to survive; the middle loop is choosing and developing captains and taking strongholds; the late loop involves defending holdings, building combinations and pursuing further rivalries. The revised epilogue's shorter completion path distinguishes authored closure from continuing play. This interpretation explains why additional encounters can feel meaningful to one player and repetitive to another: the events must produce consequences the player still values, not merely replace the last opponent. [S10, S1, S5]

### Online and business-model boundaries

Online Vendettas and Conquests use other players' generated situations without putting two human Talions together in one cooperative campaign. Ranked Conquests, Garrison transfers and earned rewards have their own rules. The July 2018 notes also refer to Online Pit Fights; these should not be conflated with personally controlled PvP sword fighting. No current matchmaking population or server-availability guarantee is established by this research. [S2, S10]

The verified post-update model is a purchased game with optional expansions, not the launch-era cash-funded orc acquisition loop. Mirian is an earned game resource with uses in development and army preparation; it is not evidence of a functioning paid Market. Availability of reward chests does not by itself establish paid loot boxes. [S9, S10]

## 5. Eight concrete situations

**All eight are constructed illustrations of documented rules, not claimed personal play sessions.** The named player/reviewer narratives in section 8 are separately attributed.

### A. The convenient cage is not automatically an advantage

**Intention:** disrupt a captain's escort. **Conditions:** nearby bait or a cage and an identified creature-sensitive opponent. **Action:** release or draw the creature from a position that leaves an escape route. **Interaction:** a beast can attack the escort but also threaten Talion; its effect depends on the captain's traits. **Result:** the fight's composition changes without direct sword damage. **Next choice/limit:** exploit the distraction or disengage; a beast specialist may turn this apparent shortcut into a worse matchup. [S16, S23]

### B. Being seen can create the next hiding opportunity

**Intention:** attack from stealth after detection. **Conditions:** an alternate roof or route and enemies searching the last known position. **Action:** break sight, change position and wait for a usable opening instead of repeating the same frontal approach. **Interaction:** search state and geography create a new attack angle. **Result:** the player can regain initiative. **Next choice/limit:** target an isolated guard or leave; this does not erase a captain's stealth immunity or make Wraith vision invisibility. [S13]

### C. A distant guard is also a movement resource

**Intention:** cross a dangerous space or reach an elevated opponent. **Conditions:** Shadow Strike unlocked, a valid target and sufficient elf-shot. **Action:** use the distant target for supernatural approach rather than spend all ammunition on damage first. **Interaction:** a combat resource also purchases position. **Result:** Talion bypasses one route but arrives in a new local risk. **Next choice/limit:** attack, retreat or reserve ammunition; range and target eligibility still constrain the plan. [S14]

### D. Healing competes with a spectacular finish

**Intention:** survive a crowded fight. **Conditions:** low health and enough Might for a relevant combat ability. **Action:** choose Consume/recovery instead of immediately spending the resource on an execution. **Interaction:** the same earned combat resource supports survival or offense. **Result:** Talion may remain in the fight longer without maximizing immediate damage. **Next choice/limit:** rebuild Might or escape; recovery still needs an eligible target and cannot negate every status or incoming attack. [S15]

### E. Losing creates a more specific next objective

**Intention:** defeat an unremarkable enemy. **Conditions:** Talion fails his available recovery and dies. **Action:** return after the Nemesis update and inspect who advanced. **Interaction:** failure changes the enemy hierarchy rather than simply rewinding it. **Result:** a former nobody can become a recognizable personal adversary. **Next choice/limit:** pursue revenge, gain information or fight elsewhere; meaningful loss depends on acknowledging changes, not guaranteeing every defeat produces a unique dramatic scene. [S17, S18]

### F. A recruit must fit the fight, not just the level

**Intention:** train a valued follower in a pit. **Conditions:** the opponent's traits are available and the candidate has a distinctive class. **Action:** compare actual counters rather than choose the largest number. **Interaction:** delegated AI combat exposes matchup weaknesses without Talion's direct intervention. **Result:** victory can improve a useful captain, while defeat can end his story. **Next choice/limit:** take the risk or retain him as a bodyguard; an optimization guide's disposable-follower strategy may contradict the player's attachment. [S22]

### G. A spy changes the opening, not the entire war

**Intention:** reduce resistance before confronting a leader. **Conditions:** an eligible recruited captain and a usable infiltration assignment. **Action:** place the follower, then exploit the betrayal when it becomes available. **Interaction:** a relationship edge changes combat conditions. **Result:** preparation can replace part of the brute-force workload. **Next choice/limit:** capitalize personally or support another follower; the assignment is a bounded game operation, not unlimited diplomacy or guaranteed safety. [S19]

### H. Saving a person can cost a position

**Intention:** retain a fortress and a favorite defender. **Conditions:** one captain is bleeding while another capture point is threatened. **Action:** decide whether to reach and revive the follower or stop the enemy advance first. **Interaction:** personal attachment and territorial utility compete under time pressure. **Result:** the player may preserve one at the cost of the other. **Next choice/limit:** rebuild the defense or accept a loss; a strong roster does not let Talion personally intervene everywhere at once. [S25]

## 6. Story, presentation and expansion contrasts

### Meaningful campaign spoilers

The authored campaign connects Minas Ithil's defense, the new Ring, Shelob, Eltariel and the competing aims of Talion and Celebrimbor. Idril and Baranor provide human connections; Brûz makes the follower/fortress layer part of the authored story; Carnán broadens the supernatural setting. This is an adaptation with deliberate departures, not a neutral guide to Tolkien canon. Reviews disagree about whether that freedom produces an engaging plot or distracting inconsistency. [S4, S5, S27]

Later, the separation from Celebrimbor and Talion's use of Isildur's Ring change his power set toward necromancy. In the creator postmortem, de Plater acknowledges that the interface did not adequately communicate these new capabilities to everyone. The team considered more branching endings but prioritized a defined central arc and the stories generated through Nemesis. This exposes a concrete production tradeoff: local personal histories do not imply an endlessly branching main plot. [S28]

### Art, sound, camera and accessibility

Captain introductions combine a face, title, silhouette, voice and callback before returning control. A scar is valuable because it makes a prior encounter recognizable. Monstrous humor contrasts with the protagonist's grave presentation; regional changes such as green Núrnen help prevent every conflict feeling visually identical. The same interruption that establishes personality can irritate players during a complicated battle. Spectacle and legibility are separate achievements. [S1, S7]

The third-person camera and counter prompts support crowd fighting, but contextual movement can select a route different from the one intended. Dense fort battles can obscure who needs help. Gear comparison and army screens clarify numbers and relationships while also suspending action. These are presentation costs documented in the reviews, not proof that complexity itself is undesirable. [S2, S3, S5]

Dr. Josh Straub's November 7, 2017 DAGERSystem review, now archived by Can I Play That?, documents a different barrier than combat difficulty. Static controls, frequent context changes and moving quickly between face buttons and shoulder buttons prevented him progressing much beyond an hour without help. He also flags very small subtitles and potentially troublesome color coding/visual blur, while explicitly limiting conclusions about parts he could not fully test. This is not a comprehensive September 2026 accessibility audit, nor a sixth full-campaign review. [S6]

### The Blade of Galadriel — a different protagonist and kit

Released February 6, 2018, this story expansion puts Eltariel's paired blades and Light abilities at the center. It adds her own gear and new orc allies rather than simply granting Talion another skill branch. The official description includes encounters with Ringwraiths and Dark Talion, so the expansion sits beyond important base-game spoilers. The core comparison is a more specialized authored combat identity within the familiar world. [S30]

An accessible negative Steam account by THE WORLD IS RJ, dated February 13, 2026, argues that the distinctive kit is underused: enemy immunities encourage repetitive basic/light-trap tactics, while the ending weakens the closure the player wanted for Talion. This is one DLC-specific account, not a base-game negative review or audience consensus. It usefully contrasts the official promise of a distinct kit with a player's experience of restricted effective choices. [S35]

### The Desolation of Mordor — mortality and a contracted force

The expansion's official description emphasizes Baranor, human mercenaries, bodyguards and Torvin's equipment. David Smith's May 18, 2018 review describes a grappling hook and physical gadgets taking over traversal functions that Talion achieves supernaturally. Baranor's death resets the hired army and outpost situation while mission/skill progress supplies continuity; this is not a claim every kind of progression is erased. Smith finds the more likable lead and real mortality strategically useful. A familiar map/action structure therefore changes meaning when the protagonist cannot simply keep the same force after death. [S31, S32]

### Slaughter and Outlaw Nemesis expansions

These are enemy/follower content additions, not two further protagonist campaigns. Tribe identities bring equipment, encounter material and changes to fortress/outpost presentation; the official Slaughter description also advertises additional legendary gear. They broaden what the generation system can assemble. Promotional counts of potential enemies or stories do not establish that every combination has bespoke authored narrative or equal play value. The official hub distinguishes both expansions from the two story packs. [S33, S34]

## 7. Production, distribution and commercial evidence

### Primary creator accounts

In Matt Kamen's 2017 WIRED interview, studio head Kevin Stephens describes roughly three years of work, the lack of outside feedback during secrecy and the intention to preserve personality after recruitment. He distinguishes camaraderie from a direct romance system. This is an intent account, not proof that every generated relationship is convincing. The interview's planned August launch is historical; the finished game released in October. [S8]

In Dean Takahashi's October 17, 2017 postmortem interview, Michael de Plater discusses investing in unusual encounters despite concern that few players would see them. Early discovered stories encouraged the team, but that does not measure return on each feature. His 'six years' covers work across the two-game series, not six years exclusively on Shadow of War. Balancing constrained trait combinations and accommodating several playstyles took substantial iteration. Publicly recoverable material did not establish a reliable game-specific budget, full team count or proprietary implementation architecture. [S27]

The same interview records de Plater's denial that the endgame was designed to require cash purchases. That is an attributed 2017 account of intent, not an independent finding about motives. The later removal of the Market and epilogue revision are observable design changes. Their existence does not require retrospectively treating either the original defense or every criticism as wholly accurate. [S28, S10]

### Promotion and circulation

The official site supplies story and gameplay trailers, separate tribe spotlights, character-focused material such as the tribute to Az-Laar, and downloadable visual material. These are concrete promotional units: the army premise can be demonstrated broadly, while an individual orc can become the memorable subject of a story. Their existence does not establish which channel caused sales. A surviving mobile promotion is historical material, not confirmation of a currently available mobile service. [S34]

**Interpretation:** the especially shareable unit is a short causal history—'this enemy killed me, returned scarred, joined me, then betrayed me'—rather than a generic victory screenshot. Procedural variation lets another player's story feel different while the rules remain recognizable. This depends on clear callbacks and enough continuity to identify the same individual. Excessive randomness, repeated fake deaths or interruptions without consequences can make the mechanism feel manipulative instead.

### What is actually known commercially

The NPD account published November 16, 2017 placed Shadow of War first in its tracked US October 2017 game-sales ranking. That is a dated, market- and coverage-bounded launch result, not a global lifetime total. The article compares launch-month sales with the predecessor but does not provide an absolute unit count suitable for deriving lifetime revenue. Its industry-wide totals are not the game's revenue. No verified lifetime profit, budget or retention series was found in this pass. [S29]

Premium storefront distribution, optional paid expansions and the historical removal of in-game cash purchases describe the business model more reliably than inventing a unit total from review counts. Continued availability of forums or a store page also does not prove the cost or service level of every online feature. [S9, S10]

## 8. Reception: five independent reviews and player testimony

These are substantive original accounts rather than aggregate scores. They mostly describe launch builds; current accessibility, bugs and post-update progression require separate evidence. Each critic's prose was read.

### Andy Kelly — PC Gamer, October 5, 2017

Kelly values expressive orcs, tactical weaknesses and the pleasure of an established bodyguard more than Talion's solemn plot. His named examples include frightening Lûga Skull-Cracker with flies and summoning Az-Grels Mountain-Eater. He finds the loot comparisons and map tasks excessive, and regards the bright vegetation of Núrnen as welcome relief from duller environments. His basic information box says no multiplayer; this must not erase the asynchronous online modes documented elsewhere. The launch review also predates the Market's removal. [S1]

### Robert Ramsey — Push Square, original 2017 text republished October 28, 2020

Ramsey enjoys the expanded Nemesis relationships, smooth fighting and large battles on his reviewed PS4 Pro. Unlike Kelly, he finds the wider authored cast and plot reasonably involving, while criticizing routine objectives between scenes. He regards loot as a welcome but shallow addition. The article explicitly republishes archived prose, including complaints about the Market; its 2020 header is not evidence those monetization systems still existed then. [S2]

### Chris Carter — Destructoid, displayed October 11, 2017

Carter finds movement, counter-based fighting and learning an enemy's vulnerabilities satisfying. The expanded army/fortress structure supports memorable self-directed stories despite a plot he finds convoluted and a protagonist he finds bland. He particularly dislikes stopping the action to pick up and compare many similar items. He objects to the single-player loot shop while reporting that he never needed to buy power. The current original page displays October 11; some indexes give October 5, so the discrepancy is preserved rather than silently resolved. [S3]

### Peter Taylor — Digital Chumps, October 16, 2017, Xbox One

Taylor praises consequences within orc society, battle heft and strongly performed introductions. His actual play account includes a grunt earning a title after killing him and an opponent returning repeatedly with worsening wounds. He finds the Talion/Celebrimbor conflict interesting but strongly objects to the adaptation of Shelob. Asset glitches sometimes cost him captains. He rejects the need for paid chests without endorsing their inclusion. These are launch observations, not verified current bugs or current purchasing options. [S4]

### Paul Bird — Horror Geek Life, October 21, 2017

Bird is much more enthusiastic about the expanded plot, collection rewards and size than Kelly or Carter. He specifically describes attachment to followers and distress when they die or betray him. The expanded stealth and power options support different approaches; fort assaults are impressive but sometimes hard to navigate amid the chaos. His broad claim that committed Tolkien fans will welcome the twists directly conflicts with other reviewers' reactions. His loose region/fortress wording and misspelled currency are not used as precise rules. [S5]

### Helpful Steam testimony

The English **Most Helpful (All Time)** surface was read September 26, 2026. Sraxton's September 16, 2022 account follows Krosh from killer to fire-scarred recurring enemy to recruited captain, then mourns his loss in a fight pit. Vvhispr's April 29, 2021 account describes repeated shaming of Dûsh, attachment, betrayal and regret after killing him. Michael {United Critics}'s February 6, 2021 recommendation praises the breadth and performance while criticizing unskippable introductions, misdirected contextual movement and badly timed ambushes; it is marked as a free copy. [S7]

These are player-reported stories, not independently reproduced outcomes or evidence that a particular sequence is guaranteed. Displayed current hours are not publication-time hours. Positive recommendations contain both enthusiasm and friction; they do not establish prevalence. Vvhispr's description of Iron Will as forever unrecruitable is too broad to adopt as a rule. The negative-filter retrieval failed; no inaccessible base-game negative body is counted. [S7]

## 9. Transferable patterns and limits

**Research interpretation, not implementation requirements:**

- **Make identity causally useful.** A name becomes memorable when its owner has a distinct capability, remembers an encounter and returns in a changed state. Cosmetic randomization alone does not create a relationship. A smaller cast with better continuity is an alternative to maximizing population.
- **Let failure change the situation rather than erase it.** A survivor or promoted rival can turn loss into a new objective. This needs bounded recovery costs and comprehensible causes; repeatedly removing progress without new meaning is merely punitive.
- **Connect information to a different plan.** Intelligence is valuable when it changes the chosen target, route, tool or ally. An information screen that reveals numbers but leaves one dominant action unchanged adds overhead instead of strategy.
- **Keep delegation emotionally and tactically consequential.** An autonomous follower should contribute something visible and be exposed to understandable risk. Too little control can turn a meaningful loss into frustration; rescue, retreat or limited orders can be better alternatives than either perfect control or pure randomness.
- **Distinguish relationship-building from ownership.** Players may become attached even inside a coercive mechanic. That does not mean domination is a general-purpose template for friendship. Cooperation, obligations and reciprocal choices could support similar continuity without copying the game's premise.
- **Budget acknowledgment carefully.** Voice, scars, names and short callbacks can validate memory. They must not constantly seize the camera or repeat an introduction during urgent play. Presentation can acknowledge history without treating every encounter as a mandatory cutscene.
- **Protect closure from retention loops.** A satisfying ending and optional continued play serve different needs. The shortened epilogue is a useful reminder that more required encounters can dilute the very stories the system is meant to support.
