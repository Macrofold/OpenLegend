# G92 — Final Fantasy XV

**Status: substantive research checkpoint, not a completed R01–R14 pass.** Research date: September 26, 2026. [Requirements](../research-requirements.md) · [Roster](../research-roster.md) · [Resume ledger](../research-progress.md).

## Identity and edition boundaries

The original game released **November 29, 2016 on PS4 and Xbox One**. Its central promise is a journey with Noctis and his three friends through a contemporary-looking fantasy world while seeking the strength to reclaim his homeland. Publisher language about revolutionary technology is a marketing claim, not a measured result. [S1](#s1)

Launch reviews must not be treated as reviews of later **Royal/Windows** content. Square Enix identifies the alternate Chapter 13 route, off-road Regalia and character switching as post-launch additions included in Royal. The March 6, 2018 release adds expanded Insomnia, a controllable boat, enhanced Armiger and first-person play. Original season-pass episodes and Comrades are included; that wording does not establish that every subsequent DLC or expired event is included. [S2](#s2)

The Royal Q&A deliberately rejects the name Complete Edition while further service was planned. Tabata describes first-person warp-striking and chocobo riding as difficult implementation work, and underwater exploration as an unrealized wish displaced by other priorities. A proposed feature is not a shipped mechanic. [S3](#s3)

**Episode Ardyn** was separately announced for **March 26, 2019**. The first-party description places it 35 years before the main game and gives Ardyn Shadow-Stepping movement and daemonic attacks. The separate **Dawn of the Future novel**, credited to Jun Eishima and the development team, follows Ardyn, Aranea, Lunafreya and Noctis through a different confrontation with fate. Its English digital and print release dates are June 23 and July 14, 2020 respectively. A novel is not a playable completion of the cancelled DLC. [S22](#s22) [S23](#s23)

**Service-retirement precision:** the April 1, 2020 official notice, effective June 24, removes PC avatar replacement, other-player ghosts, user/official treasure, user photographs and online Mod Organizer builds. It separately ends **Origin Comrades matchmaking and Steam–Origin matching**, while preserving NPC participation. This does not establish that all Steam Comrades multiplayer ended. Present matchmaking population and reliability have not been tested. [S21](#s21)

## Combat, progression and contextual cooperation

**Warp-striking** consumes Noctis's MP and closes distance; **point-warps** use designated locations, restore MP and allow recovery. Phasing spends MP to avoid eligible attacks, whereas a prompted parry creates a counter opportunity. Commanded techniques can also provide protected animation windows. These are guide descriptions of observed rules, not an audit of collision or AI code. [S9](#s9)

The control vocabulary also includes four equipped weapon slots, directional movement within combinations, blocking, target lock, cover, ally rescue and consumables. **Blindside link-strikes** combine a rear attack with a nearby eligible companion and weapon; merely having a friend somewhere in the encounter is insufficient. **Cross Chains** were added after launch and involve warp-striking a Vulnerable target before a timed party sequence. The difference matters: a launch review can legitimately omit a later cooperation feature. [S10](#s10)

Core attributes include HP/MP, Strength/Vitality and Magic/Spirit, plus elemental and ballistic resistance. **Danger** is not simply immediate death: current HP reaches zero while maximum HP drains and actions become restricted; rescue or items can prevent a terminal result. **Stasis** instead constrains Noctis after MP depletion. These distinguish loss of health, loss of recovery capacity and loss of mobility resources. Exact status percentages are not necessary to the design comparison and are not generalized across every controlled character. [S10](#s10)

**Ascension** spends AP across several named categories, not a freely assignable job system. It adds new behavior as well as numerical benefits. Airstep develops into further aerial movement; Osmostrike recovers MP through aerial hits. Teamwork purchases unlock conditional companion responses. Techniques such as Ignis's Regroup or Gladiolus's Royal Guard create recovery or protection options rather than only another damage attack. Later character-control nodes allow direct play as Ignis, Prompto or Gladiolus. Those are additions to the launch model, not evidence that all four were originally switchable. [S11](#s11)

## Magic and object combinations

**Elemancy** consumes gathered Fire, Ice and Lightning energy to make limited-use spells in flasks. A catalyst can change potency, the added effect and the number of uses. A majority element controls whether the result stays single-element; without an absolute majority, Unicast randomizes the element. A Potion gives Healcast, an Antidote gives Venomcast, and an Elixir gives Tricast. The source's tables also distinguish Expericast, resource-saving Freecast and high-damage Blastcast with a self-HP penalty. Multicast's name is not a guarantee that every possible additional cast occurs. These are explicit recipe rules, not freeform spell invention. [S12](#s12)

This already creates an important analytical distinction: the same inventory object can have an immediate conventional use or become a component of a different action. A player choosing a catalyst is also choosing what not to retain for later healing, trade or another recipe. The final pass will add Royal Arms, Ring magic, named equipment and broader inventory/economy examples rather than treating Elemancy as the whole item system.

## Camping, meals and companions outside combat

**Experience is settled on resting.** Camps apply the normal amount while paid lodgings can multiply it. The DLC **Nixperience Band** prevents settlement while equipped, allowing camping without cashing out accumulated experience. It must be removed before deliberately using a paid multiplier. Thus the same rest action participates in travel rhythm, recovery and level growth; the band separates goals that otherwise compete. [S13](#s13)

**Ignis cooks at Havens**, not every lodging. Recipes come from ingredients, reading material, restaurants and other discoveries. Meals produce temporary party benefits; favorite foods also interact with techniques. The historical guide's total recipe count is not adopted as a current complete catalog because Royal added further food. Gathering an ingredient is sometimes a different unlock trigger from buying it. This makes a journey's discoveries relevant to what the group can prepare later. [S14](#s14)

**Gladiolus's Survival** develops through travel on foot, not riding the Regalia or a chocobo. It can produce additional items after a completed battle; running away does not supply the same result. Higher ranks improve the possible finds. This is a bounded looting proficiency rather than a simulation of hunger, shelter building or an independently acting wilderness expert. [S15](#s15)

**Prompto's photography** has an explicit production rationale. The GDC talk abstract by Prasert Prasertvithyakarn describes an AI companion taking context-sensitive snapshots so players can revisit and share distinct adventures. Only the written abstract was read; the talk was not watched and no uninspected implementation details are asserted. It is a strong reading/viewing route for studying an NPC who helps document experience rather than merely describing it in dialogue. [S16](#s16)

### Constructed situation: one rest, two incompatible goals

**Intention:** obtain a useful meal before an expedition but save accumulated experience for a better lodging multiplier. **Conditions:** the player owns the Nixperience Band and the recipe's ingredients. **Sequence:** equip the band, camp and cook, undertake the activity, then remove the band before the intended paid rest. **Interaction:** accessory configuration decouples food preparation from experience settlement. **Result:** a familiar social stop remains useful without immediately consuming the experience bank. **Next decision:** decide when the lodging cost is justified. **Limit:** forgetting the accessory at either rest can defeat the plan; this is a constructed rule example, not a reported play session. [S13](#s13) [S14](#s14)

## Production and discovery evidence

In **Miho Aoyagi's December 22, 2016 interview**, Tabata dates his involvement to around 2012 and distinguishes the engineering demands of a large real-time game from expertise in producing attractive CG. He describes working backward from a high-end-PC target to identify necessary technology and organizational changes. The prior Versus XIII announcement does not mean one unchanged game received ten years of continuous full production. [S17](#s17)

The second interview page describes a team growing from fewer than 100 to several hundred internal/external contributors, then focuses on decision-making: clarifying authority, avoiding advice that merely stalls colleagues, and changing leadership responsibilities to fit different production phases. These are the director's retrospective account, not an independent demonstration that those management choices caused the final result. [S18](#s18)

The third page connects simultaneous worldwide release with the desire for players to share distinct experiences. Tabata explicitly aimed between a fully open-world game and traditional Final Fantasy structure. His rough percentages about success are impressions, not telemetry. That distinction is important when connecting photographs, fishing detours, streaming and commercial reach. [S19](#s19)

Square Enix's **December 1, 2016 announcement** reports more than **five million first-day units**, explicitly defined as initial packaged shipments plus digital sales. It is not five million verified retail purchases or a profit figure. No private product budget, net margin or customer-acquisition attribution is established here. [S20](#s20)

## Reception — five written critical accounts

The substantive written bodies of these five accounts have now been inspected. They span different editions and publication dates; the sample is qualitative, not a pooled score. Video embeds are not counted as watched evidence.

**Peter Brown, GameSpot, November 27, 2016:** praises the open world's appearance, fluid fighting, monsters and optional endgame. He criticizes predictable plotting, narrowly characterized companions and unreliable Astral access. Ordinary activities can weaken the urgency of reclaiming the kingdom. His Noctis-only control account belongs to launch. An erroneous family-relationship sentence in the review is not used as canonical lore. [S4](#s4)

**David Roberts, GamesRadar+, launch PS4 review:** strongly values shared meals, photography, travel behavior and coordinated fighting. He finds the larger plot undercontextualized, its supporting cast neglected and the camera unreliable. Even the divisive final stretch retains an emotional payoff for him. This directly contrasts with Brown's dissatisfaction about the friends' depth; everyday behavior is doing more character work for one critic than the other. [S5](#s5)

**Hannah Dwan, PC Gamer, March 13, 2018:** finds the Windows release more complete, visually impressive and enriched by Royal's finale. She still dislikes shallow sidequests, restricted development choices and repetition in extended battles. Her SSD/HDD and team-hardware observations are dated tests, not current performance guarantees. She also regards Wait Mode less favorably than a reader seeking respite from real-time action might. [S6](#s6)

**Alex Donaldson, RPG Site, November 28, 2016:** distinguishes an open-world portion from a wholly open campaign. He praises tactical preparation, grounded friends and optional dungeons, but objects to the camera, awkward magic preparation, fetch quests and unclear importance of sellable loot. His production-history explanation for narrative gaps remains speculation, even though his account of experiencing those gaps is firsthand criticism. [S7](#s7)

**Mike Epstein, Digital Trends, page displayed August 23, 2019:** values the world and personable ensemble but finds the plot awkward and some genre-changing set pieces unsupported by the normal action vocabulary. Streamlined RPG systems can become forgettable, and chaotic fighting does not always reward the intended rhythm. The page date is not proof of a new Royal-edition test; its apparent XI/XIV mix-up and misspelled names are not factual authorities. [S8](#s8)

## Steam player testimony and access limitations

The main store encountered an age gate. The community **Most Helpful (All Time)** route was accessible and read. Separate negative-only routes, including an English-language variant and a review API request, repeatedly failed. This is an access limitation, not evidence that negative reviews do not exist.

The accessible helpful feed contains substantial criticism even in recommended reviews. A **June 28, 2023** account explicitly dislikes repetitive tasks and a thin world/narrative, yet finds the three companions make those activities worthwhile. A **September 21, 2018** account similarly recommends the game while criticizing repetitive optional work and abrupt storytelling; its theory of unfinished development is not adopted as a production fact. A **March 7, 2018** account describes successful play on its own hardware while acknowledging other users' problems; its later retraction of an unsupported frame-rate configuration tip is another reason not to generalize player fixes. [S24](#s24)

These are selected accounts, not a representative sentiment survey. Displayed lifetime hours may have accumulated after publication. A negative-only sample must remain explicitly unavailable unless a later access attempt succeeds; recommendation labels must not be silently changed to manufacture balance.

## Preliminary implications, not accepted OpenLegend design

Friendship can be expressed through repeated functional actions: preparing a meal, recording a trip, protecting someone or exploiting a shared combat opportunity. This does not automatically solve thin dialogue, weak narrative continuity or a desire for relationship choices. The review disagreement is therefore part of the lesson, not noise to discard.

The rules also show several forms of agency that should remain distinct: choosing a route, choosing a loadout, timing a spatial recovery action, transforming a resource and choosing when to settle rewards. None demonstrates that ordinary NPCs possess open-ended goals or that the authored world supports arbitrary invention.
