# Why Umamusume made billions — and what OpenLegend can take forward

[Research library](../README.md) · [Ten full dossiers and completion record](../research-progress-mobile-2026-10-17.md) · [Research requirements](../research-requirements.md)

**Research date: October 17, 2026.** The ten underlying dossiers are complete within their recorded evidence limits. This synthesis connects their mechanics, reported pleasures and commercial outcomes to possible OpenLegend experiences. Design interpretations and constructed examples below are proposals; their enjoyment and commercial value have not been tested in OpenLegend.

## The question the comparison needs to answer

The useful question behind the quoted $2.5 billion is what people value enough to spend their attention, affection and money on. For OpenLegend, the strongest opportunity is to connect an enjoyable activity with a particular ambition, a method the player can make their own, and an outcome that matters afterward. Commercial viability adds a further question: can we repeatedly deliver that value at a supportable cost?

Our working explanation for Umamusume is that character attachment, repeated improvement, persistent ownership and renewed goals can reinforce each other. The dossiers document the relevant mechanisms; the strength of each mechanism's contribution to revenue remains an inference. Public spending estimates cannot tell us how much income came from affection for a character, competitive ambition, convenience, an expiring offer or another motive. The final comparison therefore distinguishes the observable product from the proposed explanation of its results.

For a design question, start with [what all ten games can contribute](#what-all-ten-games-can-contribute), then [OpenLegend's particular opportunity](#openlegends-particular-opportunity). The [canonical fun synthesis](fun-and-sustainable-engagement.md) owns the broader principles; [current gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) own selection for the playable game.

## Why the parts fit together in Umamusume

The game gives its numerical goals personal stakes. Its Main Story starts with a threatened Team Sirius and Mejiro McQueen helping rebuild it. Rice Shower pursues unbeaten Mihono Bourbon; Winning Ticket brings a Derby dream; Silence Suzuka struggles when chasing results displaces her joy in running. These are particular ambitions and relationships that can make a result mean more than its reward. They do not establish how attached every player becomes. [Official Main Story](https://umamusume.jp/contents/game/mainstory/part1/).

The URA career makes preparation a sequence of tradeoffs. Trainees have different aptitudes and goals. Training raises attributes and skill points, but low energy risks injury. Bonds improve training opportunities; a summer camp rewards arriving prepared. Race schedules and suitable skills constrain the plan, and the career can be attempted again. These are the current overview's rules, not a claim that every later addition existed at launch. [Official URA overview](https://umamusume.jp/contents/game/scenario/ura/).

The account connects those attempts. Previously trained runners contribute inheritance; support cards offer different training effects, and duplicates strengthen them. Trained teams compete in Team Stadium. Collection can therefore affect preparation across many careers: a support may improve future possibilities, while another trainee can offer a different person to develop. This distinguishes acquiring a reusable trainee from producing a particular trained runner. [Official system overview](https://umamusume.jp/contents/game/system/).

Separate Scouts offer trainees and support cards. Featured-item exchanges require 200 banner-specific points; unused points become Clovers when the banner ends. [Dated Scout explanation](https://www.destructoid.com/umamusume-pretty-derby-pity-system-explained/).

The proposed commercial connection is straightforward: **attachment supplies a reason to care; preparation makes the outcome feel partly earned; retained possessions can make another attempt and another acquisition relevant.** A disappointing result can suggest a different plan, while a satisfying result can validate the time spent learning. A purchase can be attractive because of what the player hopes to do with it afterward. This is an interpretation of how the documented systems fit together, not a measured decomposition of revenue.

That interpretation also has limits. Unclear outcomes can make another attempt feel arbitrary. An obligation to repeat can crowd out curiosity. A technically retained possession can have little practical value. Revenue establishes that substantial spending occurred; it does not establish that every player enjoyed the repetition, that purchases were necessary for enjoyment, or that any one mechanism would succeed in another game.

### Audience, purchases and ongoing production

Music, manga and TV anime preceded the mobile release. [Cygames launch announcement](https://www.cygames.co.jp/en/news/id-22022). That gives a plausible route by which someone could arrive already interested in the characters. It does not supply a measured conversion rate from viewers into players or purchasers.

Players can buy currency for randomized acquisitions. [Developer interview and commercial description](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/umamusume-to-be-honest-we-werent-sure-how-a-game-inspired-by-japanese-horse-racing-culture-would-be-received-overseas/). One interviewed spender described payment as affection and appreciation; this is individual testimony, not a representative payer profile. [Reuters reporting](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/reu20211213KBN2IS00Y/). The third anniversary coincided with a Japanese iOS grossing-chart surge. [Sensor Tower event analysis](https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/umamusume-3rd-anniversary). Together, these observations support investigating how prior attachment, desired acquisitions and renewed occasions interact. They cannot assign revenue shares to those motives or separate an event's many simultaneous changes.

A production report describes concept work, choreography, motion capture and cutscene work behind victory concerts. [Secondary production account](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2023/06/06/21791.html). Presentation is therefore part of the work required to deliver the promise. A commercial explanation needs both sides: why the offer may be valuable to a player, and what the organization must repeatedly produce to sustain that value. The complete title-specific cost account remains unavailable.

## Comparing the promise with the work required

A useful analysis begins with the experience a player wants, then asks what work stands between that desire and a satisfying result. Some work creates the pleasure: recognizing a pattern, choosing a plan, taking a risk, coordinating with someone or discovering a useful interaction. Other work exists mainly because a player must clear it before reaching the part they came for.

The distinction cannot be settled by counting systems or minutes. A long session may be absorbing; a brief session may feel empty. A demanding preparation chain may make an achievement meaningful for one audience and obstruct it for another. A repeated action can provide mastery when its circumstances change, or feel like an obligation when the decision has disappeared.

This suggests separating four questions in further research:

1. **What result does the player care about?** Identify a person, object, place, discovery, contest or social contribution rather than only a reward currency.
2. **Which decisions make reaching that result enjoyable?** Explain what the player notices, chooses and learns.
3. **What survives the session?** Distinguish durable ownership, improved understanding, social history and temporary numerical gains.
4. **Why would another visit be welcome?** New decisions are one possibility. Familiar mastery, a chosen ritual, companionship, beauty or finishing a project can also justify returning. A satisfying ending is a valid result.

The answers should remain specific to the intended audience. A design that requires coordination, uninterrupted attention or comfort with a large interface asks for a different commitment than a short solitary puzzle. Neither commitment is inherently superior; the commercial question is whether enough of the intended audience values the actual experience under real conditions.

## OpenLegend's particular opportunity

The [current selection policy](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md#the-game-we-need-to-be-able-to-play) calls for a complete, enjoyable adventure with a real early instance of useful invention, independent character choice and persistent consequence. Those qualities belong in connected play from the start. The [design foundation](../../../../docs/repertoires/design-foundation.md#how-systems-produce-a-situation) supplies a broader range of motives, including curiosity, mastery, possession, affection, power, beauty, humor and conflict.

Our strongest proposed direction is **an adventure in which the player's chosen method changes both what they can do and what happens among particular people and places**. Its appeal needs to be visible in play: a clever maneuver, a surprising discovery, a hard-won object, an expressive creation or a rival's understandable response. The engine's breadth earns its place by making those experiences possible.

The distinctive bet is a useful invention beyond the world's prewritten answers, combined with characters whose own purposes can change the outcome. These ten commercial contrasts do not establish market uniqueness. Judge a proposed situation alongside the [existing systemic, narrative and creative comparisons](../../../../docs/repertoires/design-foundation.md#what-the-research-actually-makes-available), and ask what additional experience OpenLegend makes possible and why someone would prefer it. Breadth of possibility matters when a player can recognize and enjoy the difference.

### Four positive design perspectives

**Give an adventure a method the player can own.** A desirable destination, readable opposition and useful rewards can make the player want to act. Timing, positioning, observation, equipment and invention offer different kinds of competence. Preserve a viable known approach while allowing a supported new idea to produce a real alternative. The important result is that the player recognizes how their method affected events. A larger menu of nominal choices adds little if every approach feels the same.

**Let people change the situation through their own purposes.** A capable ally, jealous competitor, principled resident or dangerous enemy can make an otherwise familiar task particular. Participation may involve practical help, rivalry, affection, refusal or a bargain. A character's response should follow what they know and want; the player can win, lose, cooperate or walk away. The references' human cooperation and authored character stories are useful comparisons, but they do not establish that OpenLegend's independent characters will work. That remains part of the experience to demonstrate.

**Make progress visible, usable and personally desirable.** A discovery can open an approach; an earned object can change a fight; a finished home can express a taste; a remembered event can change a relationship. These are different kinds of value. A beautiful room, a performance or an amusing invention can be satisfying in itself. Let successful projects remain finished, and let players choose which new ambition deserves their attention.

**Make the moment feel distinctive.** The pleasure of a well-directed action, an enemy's readable threat, an unexpected sound, an arresting view or a comic reaction matters alongside persistent systems. Choose a coherent mood and presentation for the authored world. Give anticipation, action, payoff and quiet enjoyment room to breathe. A proposed interaction needs an account of what the player sees, hears, directs and feels; a database of possible consequences does not supply that account.

These perspectives extend the existing [fun principles](fun-and-sustainable-engagement.md#3-eighteen-central-principles). They do not require a new general system for each pleasure. In particular, fixed combinations in the source games are not evidence of live invention, and human social behavior is not evidence of autonomous character behavior. OpenLegend must earn those parts of its promise in its own playable situations.

### Two concrete situations to explore

**The telescope in the ruined watchtower.** In a hypothetical authored world, the player wants a fine telescope for their hilltop home. A territorial creature occupies the approach, and a rival scavenger wants the same prize. A prepared fight is a viable method. The player might instead attempt a supported invention that changes how they cross the broken approach or distract the creature, with understandable costs and limits. The rival pursues their own aim and responds to events they actually witness.

The intended pleasures are anticipation, executing a plan, surprising oneself with a useful idea, and winning something desirable. The telescope can make the home more personal and reveal an appealing distant landmark. A later encounter with the rival can carry the consequence of this one. To be compelling, the approach and confrontation must be enjoyable to direct, the invention must change an actual possibility, and the prize must be worth having. A scripted speech celebrating ingenuity would not demonstrate these results.

**An evening in a wind garden.** A player wants to make a small place whose shapes, movement and sound please them. They arrange available materials, try a supported new instrument or mechanism, and invite someone to experience the result. A visitor might enjoy the spectacle, prefer quiet or bring a conflicting taste. The garden can remain a satisfying finished place.

Here the intended pleasures are making, perceiving a difference, expressing taste and sharing an occasion. Its value does not depend on raising combat strength. The creation should respond clearly enough that experimenting feels playful, and a visitor's reaction should be specific enough to feel like an encounter with someone. This is also a useful authoring question: can a world maker arrange a modest set of capabilities into an experience another person wants to inhabit?

Both examples are original hypotheses, not accepted quests, implemented capabilities or a compulsory template for every scene. Their transferable unit is the connection between a wanted outcome, enjoyable participation and a consequence the player can recognize. The chosen world's rules determine what is possible.

### Evidence that would help choose among ideas

The following comparisons sharpen judgment without turning this research into another acceptance checklist. Ask them of a particular experience and its intended audience; use the [canonical evaluation guidance](fun-and-sustainable-engagement.md#6-how-to-evaluate-a-prototype-without-mistaking-attendance-for-quality) for the broader method.

| Proposed value                     | What would make the idea worth pursuing?                                                                         | What would make us revise it?                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enjoyable action and discovery     | The player can point to a maneuver, realization, sensory moment or encounter they enjoyed while it happened.     | Their account concerns only the eventual reward, while directing the activity is confusing or dull.                          |
| A method that feels their own      | A choice or useful invention changes the approach, and the player understands enough of the result to act again. | The result changes only in prose, depends on a secret answer, or requires repeated negotiation with the interface.           |
| A particular person worth noticing | An actor's aim, contribution or refusal changes the player's decision and makes later conduct intelligible.      | Generic approval substitutes for a purpose, or the actor reacts to information they could not have acquired.                 |
| Progress worth having              | The player uses, enjoys, shows or remembers the result, and can finish the undertaking satisfied.                | Possessions accumulate without a desired use or meaning, or every completed project immediately becomes another upkeep task. |
| A welcome return                   | The player wants a new challenge, familiar mastery, a person, a place or an unfinished ambition.                 | Return mainly serves an attendance obligation, and the player cannot identify an experience they look forward to.            |
| Creation worth sharing             | A maker can shape the intended experience, and a visitor finds something enjoyable to do or perceive.            | Producing more content improves the author's output count while visitors still lack a reason to participate.                 |

Player testimony, observation and repeated behavior can inform these judgments together. None should be reduced to a single engagement number, and a pleasant finite visit should not be marked down merely because the player is ready to stop.

## Commercial perspectives that serve the intended game

The mobile results make the relationship between value, audience and delivery worth examining. They do not select OpenLegend's financing or purchase model. Our proposed commercial questions are:

1. **Who wants this particular experience?** Describe a recognizable adventure and the kind of participation it offers. Check whether prospective players find the actual situation appealing and can explain its appeal. A promise to support any imaginable reality leaves this question unanswered.
2. **What can communicate the appeal faithfully?** A readable moment of discovery, invention, mastery or character consequence can be a candidate for showing what a world offers. Evaluate whether the moment is understandable and representative before assuming it will attract an audience. The research supplies no measured acquisition rate for OpenLegend.
3. **How much worthwhile play can a bounded addition support?** A place, object or character may participate in several situations when goals and circumstances change. Study whether that reuse creates desired play. Also leave room for an authored surprise or beautiful moment whose value is concentrated in one encounter.
4. **Can both playing and making be worthwhile?** Players need a good experience; world makers need to realize an intention, try it and improve it. A useful authoring capability should eventually help someone deliver enjoyable participation. Author convenience and visitor enjoyment require separate observation.
5. **Can we support the experience we offer?** Examine ongoing authoring, presentation, generation where used, operation and support alongside what the audience values. If purchases are introduced, identify the benefit someone affirmatively wants at the offered price. A large gross-spending example does not supply our demand, cost structure or willingness to pay.

Location dependence, licensed attachment, randomized acquisitions, purchase pressure and a frequent release schedule belong to specific source businesses. Transfer the useful player experience deliberately, and decide separately whether any surrounding commercial mechanism fits OpenLegend. The immediate benefit of this research is a better candidate experience and a clearer account of what would make it desirable and supportable.

### The arithmetic of repeated purchases

An illustrative accounting identity is:

**Cumulative player spending = the sum, across periods, of paying players × average spending per paying player.**

This is a way to organize the question, not an estimate of Umamusume's actual payer population. For example, both hypothetical businesses below would collect $2.5 billion:

| Hypothetical monthly payers | Hypothetical average monthly spending per payer | Duration  | Cumulative spending |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------- |
| 1,000,000                   | $50                                             | 50 months | $2.5 billion        |
| 250,000                     | $200                                            | 50 months | $2.5 billion        |

Real populations fluctuate, individuals spend different amounts, and a person can move between paying and nonpaying periods. The illustration simply explains why a free game does not need billions of purchasers to accumulate billions in spending. It also explains why downloads alone cannot answer the business question. A download does not specify continued use, whether the person pays, how often they pay or what serving that person costs.

For the publisher, several further quantities matter: amounts retained after store arrangements and taxes, contractual revenue shares, development and ongoing production, marketing, infrastructure and support. Gross customer expenditure cannot be substituted for profit. Nor can a corporate segment's profit be assigned to one game without an allocation that the company actually discloses.

## What the closed services change about the explanation

### Separate an attractive premise from a repeatable reason to stay

The unsuccessful group makes brand recognition an inadequate explanation by itself. Harry Potter, Minecraft, Apex Legends and Final Fantasy VII all supplied recognizable starting points, yet their mobile adaptations ended. That comparison does not estimate the value of a license; it shows that recognition cannot be treated as sufficient evidence of a sustainable service. The relevant question is what a particular adaptation lets the player enjoy after the initial curiosity has passed. [Individual evidence and edition boundaries](../research-progress-mobile-2026-10-17.md#subject-status).

The dates also resist a single external explanation. Wizards Unite's reported commercial slowdown was visible in 2019. [Dated spending evidence](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/harry-potter-wizards-unite-sharp-decline/). Minecraft Earth's publisher cited disrupted movement/cooperation and resource reallocation. [Publisher explanation](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-earth-coming-end). The pandemic belongs in the latter account, but cannot explain a pre-pandemic deterioration in the former. Neither observation supplies a complete account of management's alternatives or every player's reason for leaving.

### Useful systems do not establish sufficient demand

Durango is especially relevant to OpenLegend because its dossier records extensive specialization, production, settlement and player creation. Those systems offer concrete ways for possessions and other people to matter. Their presence did not ensure that the online business continued. Its final offline island modes preserved a bounded experience. [Dossier](../dossiers/durango-wild-lands.md); [preservation account](https://www.inven.co.kr/webzine/news/?news=231121).

This makes a better research question than either praising or rejecting complexity in general: which decisions did the complexity enable, which people valued those decisions, and what work had to be sustained around them? The same question applies to a small puzzle and a large simulated world. A count of features cannot substitute for an account of their use.

### Continued delivery has its own failure conditions

Respawn cited shortfalls in content quality, quantity and cadence. [Archived publisher statement](https://devtrackers.gg/apex-legends/p/0a52ffaa-a-development-update-on-apex-legends-mobile). Ateam's cost-recovery evidence is recorded in the financial section above. These are different kinds of evidence from a reviewer's judgment that a match was enjoyable. The game, its operation and its commercial continuation need separate evaluation.

For an operator pursuing a financial return, continued operation requires the expected value of future receipts to justify the obligations needed to earn them. A publisher can also consider other uses for the same staff and capital. The public record here does not disclose all of those alternatives. It therefore supports examining production burden and cost recovery without inventing a numerical break-even point or treating a shutdown announcement as a full postmortem. Other financing arrangements can support a game; the selected mobile businesses do not settle OpenLegend's model.

Both groups supply worthwhile experiences and counterexamples. An ended service can contain a compelling encounter or creative possibility; a large business can contain exhausting obligations. The positive opportunity map and the operating evidence answer different questions. Neither should erase the other.

## Evidence and scope

The dossier set includes major mechanics inventories, concrete constructed interactions, fictional economies, narrative and creator-tool boundaries, production and distribution, historical purchases, commercial evidence, independent written reviews and original player accounts. Early builds, later updates, original games, adaptations and unofficial reconstruction are explicitly separated where the evidence requires it.

Written reviews provide situated accounts, often with limited hours or a particular device. Player posts provide reasons someone gives for staying, paying or leaving. Neither is a representative retention study. Publisher and developer statements establish their reported intent or explanation; vendor revenue models estimate defined slices of the market. None alone establishes a causal model of success.

Missing quantities remain missing: title-specific profit, complete cost structures, payer distributions, acquisition costs, cohort retention and the financial contribution of individual mechanics cannot be manufactured from the recovered public evidence.
