# Shared-world sandbox MMOs and OpenLegend

**Completed research comparison — November 14, 2026, America/New_York.** Retrieval continued into November 15 UTC. [Batch scope, dossier links and completion](../research-progress-shared-world-sandbox-mmos-2026-11-14.md). This document records analysis and proposed positioning, not accepted new product scope or proven market demand.

## The question and the current answer

Mike asks which of nine shared-world sandboxes succeed, what players actually value and reject, how graphics affect the experience, and whether OpenLegend's documented vision gives people a compelling reason to choose it despite a smaller team and graphics budget.

The nine completed cases rule out an easy answer. Autonomous characters, remembered relationships, useful occupations, persistent landscapes and unexpected player inventions all have serious competitors. OpenLegend has a plausible opportunity in how these elements connect during an embodied adventure. A distinctive feature list, commercial demand and a reliably distinctive playable experience are three separate claims.

**AI characters alone are not a defensible distinction.** SEED directly overlaps with the proposed people and memory; Stars Reach contests systemic freedom; the older games already supply useful work, authored places and personal histories. OpenLegend could become a less satisfying version of these experiences if its main activities remain conventional while its novelty lives chiefly in conversation. Its credible opportunity is a connected experience of practical discovery, particular independent people and lasting consequences. That remains a proposition to demonstrate, not an established competitive advantage.

## Use the actual OpenLegend vision

OpenLegend's [gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) select a connected survival-adventure: useful making, a home, danger, discoveries, independent people, practical invention and persistent consequences. The first playable is supposed to contain that distinctive combination. A generic survival loop with the interesting people and invention deferred would not meet the existing target.

Peaceful work, companionship and beauty can be satisfying purposes. They coexist with danger and difficult expeditions. The research should not narrow this into a mandatory settlement-management game, a conversation simulator, or an exclusively gentle social world. Nor should every successful camp have to become a city. A player may choose a modest life and enjoy having achieved it.

The [selected language](../../../06-marketing/language-and-elevator-pitches.md) positions the game as a living-world survival MMO whose people have their own lives and whose useful inventions become lasting parts of the world. It distinguishes the playable game from the wider engine and creator proposition. This essay interprets that promise's competitive burden; it does not replace accepted copy.

The [current art direction](../../../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-direction-at-a-glance) requires believable grounded stylization and rejects visible facets, blockiness and toy proportions. A smaller production budget does not authorize an unrelated low-poly style. The [creative brief](../../../../art-direction/final-board/creative-brief.md) connects beauty to actual people, materials, lighting, weather and world state.

Current capability must remain separate from ambition. The main-branch [README](../../../../README.md) documents an active game pause when all human connections leave. The [continuing-lives design](../../../../docs/projects/continuing-lives-feature-spec.md) is a delivery target, not proof that a continuously operating, large-scale MMO already exists.

## What the nine cases establish

### SEED competes directly on people

The [SEED dossier](../dossiers/seed.md) establishes substantial overlap with autonomous characters, personality-conditioned LLM dialogue, remembered relationships, offline work, generational histories and social institutions. “We have AI characters” is insufficient differentiation. Its [official relationship explanation](https://seed.game/news-from-avesta/how-to-have-a-relationship) makes that overlap explicit.

SEED's player-managed society and OpenLegend's intended direct adventure have different centers of play. That difference may matter more than either technology label. The test is whether a player wants to be in OpenLegend because of what they actually do there.

The reception sample contains both useful social roles and burdensome administration. In the frustrated accounts, conversational expressiveness did not reliably offset characters needing rescue, invented guidance, empty friendliness or difficulty carrying out a chosen activity. Its funding and staffing establish production investment, not sales success. Steam's absent review corpus does not mean there are no standalone players.

### Stars Reach competes directly on embodied systemic freedom

The [Stars Reach dossier](../dossiers/stars-reach.md) covers direct adventure, a reactive environment, occupations, settlement society and inventive uses of physical rules. Koster's [June interview](https://gamesbeat.com/playable-worlds-stars-reach-mmo-launches-into-early-access-this-summer/) attributes unexpected constructions to players; generic “invention” is therefore also contested territory.

Its strongest contrasting accounts concern purpose. One player sees unfinished tools and lonely work. Another joins a human society and discovers useful work, local identity and shared projects. A system becomes meaningful partly through the people and desired outcomes connected to it. That is an interpretation of the [reception evidence](../dossiers/stars-reach.md#reception-interesting-worlds-difficult-entry), not a measured causal effect.

OpenLegend's particular inhabitants could help provide intelligible local life before a newcomer finds the right human organization. They must do more than explain a task. Their own decisions, knowledge, commitments and practical contributions need to change the adventure.

### Wurm establishes a paying niche and the burden of attachment

The [Wurm dossier](../dossiers/wurm-online.md) establishes persistent ownership and meaningful skilled work as serious prior art. Its operator reported approximately SEK 2.1 million in November 2024 sales, while current group reporting shows financial pressure; these support neither dismissing it as dead nor declaring it consistently profitable. [Game disclosure](https://www.mfn.se/cis/a/game-chest-group/gamechest-okad-forsaljning-och-fler-spelare-for-wurm-online-5f8ef835.iframe) · [Group report](https://www.mfn.se/a/game-chest-group/kvartalsrapport-2-2026)

The useful distinction is between effort that gives something value and procedure that delays any wanted result. Historical reviewers and current player accounts disagree because goals, available time and social access differ. A slower pace may be the desired experience. OpenLegend should make quiet inhabitation enjoyable in its own right, alongside adventure, rather than treating every minute without a new unlock as failure.

Wurm also shows that a persistent place can carry human memories without AI memory. The prospective contribution is richer encounters with particular inhabitants, not the invention of a world with history. Care can motivate return and also create unwanted obligation. Test voluntary desire to revisit separately from time spent protecting earlier investment.

### Restoration makes ordinary life and human service valuable

The [Restoration dossier](../dossiers/star-wars-galaxies-restoration.md) concerns the particular fan service, not every version of Star Wars Galaxies. Its independent accounts value surveying, making, visiting cities and encountering other people's activities. That is evidence for enjoying inhabitation, rather than needing every session to produce a dramatic personal triumph. [Contemporary firsthand account](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/mmo/i-want-mmos-to-waste-our-time-again/)

Its [Doctor redesign](https://swgr.org/post/doctors-and-a-living-galaxy.7787/) exposes a tension between automated availability and meaningful human participation. OpenLegend should preserve that distinction when evaluating its autonomous inhabitants. Efficient service, companionship, another person's recognition and shared authorship are different benefits. It may deliver some strongly without replacing all of them.

The inherited commercial world, established fiction and volunteer work also matter to comparison. A modest operating budget for maintaining that service cannot establish the cost of creating an original world, attracting its audience and paying its contributors. The case supports learning from social design while keeping production economics separate.

### Foxhole connects individual action to an intelligible shared purpose

The [Foxhole dossier](../dossiers/foxhole.md) provides a strong original-game demand example. Steam Charts records roughly 1,704 average concurrent players in October 2026, following a much larger February expansion month. These demonstrate durable participation, not unique customer counts or verified profit. [Dated concurrency series](https://steamcharts.com/app/505460)

Its firsthand accounts connect ordinary work to visible help and human recognition. The [historical LOGI letter](https://logiunion.com/archive/openletter/) also documents how cumulative logistics overhead can make that work frustrating. Later changes matter; the old dispute does not establish that every complaint remains current.

The design lesson is to connect effort to a wanted consequence while scrutinizing the cost of supplying it. OpenLegend should ask whether an invention, expedition or resident's contribution changes something the player values. More production stages alone do not establish greater depth. Nor does replacing a human contributor with automation preserve the same social experience automatically.

Foxhole also shows why presentation deserves deliberate scope decisions. Its developer's [foliage discussion](https://www.moddb.com/games/foxhole/news/devblog-anti-tank-rifle-foliage-art-and-more) treats readability at the gameplay camera as a design problem. OpenLegend's personal relationships imply different visual priorities, but the discipline of testing the actual view transfers.

### Mortal Online 2 combines real demand with demanding access to value

The [Mortal Online 2 dossier](../dossiers/mortal-online-2.md) finds historical paid demand alongside recent company losses. Its [first-half 2026 report](https://news.cision.com/se/star-vault/r/halvarsrapport-2026-01-01-till-2026-06-30-star-vault-ab--publ--556709-1169,c4384172) predates July's expansion, so it cannot establish that expansion's financial outcome. Company results, Steam participation and the whole service's subscribers remain different measures.

A [recent dungeon account](https://www.reddit.com/r/MMORPG/comments/1vttid7/the_art_of_exploration_and_organic_progression_in/) describes discovery prompting new skills and a return expedition. That satisfying sequence is already competitive territory. Learning an existing technique, configuring authored components and inventing a genuinely new usable method are distinct forms of creation; OpenLegend must show what its own form adds to play.

The reception evidence also separates wanted risk from blocked opportunity. Strong loss can make preparation and allies meaningful, while discouraging experimentation or use of valued equipment. A particular player's appetite for that tradeoff is not the whole audience's preference. OpenLegend should preserve its documented danger and adventure while observing whether consequences generate wanted next decisions or merely postpone the experience.

For presentation, a [starter-area account](https://massivelyop.com/2022/01/26/fight-or-kite-starting-a-journey-in-mortal-online-2/) found a missing skinning animation disruptive to embodiment. It is a dated observation, but a useful design question: does the player's action have a convincing visible response? Environmental detail cannot answer that by itself.

### Haven & Hearth already makes a modest life into an absorbing world

The [Haven & Hearth dossier](../dossiers/haven-and-hearth.md) is particularly relevant to the intended opening: establishing a hearth, gaining practical competence, shaping a home and discovering how a place works already constitute a substantial game. Its interdependent quality, food, study and production systems make small improvements useful. Their existence rules out claiming the basic home–exploration–making loop as OpenLegend's exclusive territory.

A [current newcomer account](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=979652) values the presentation and discoveries but nearly leaves when mining rules become unintelligible. The implication is a design distinction: mystery can encourage investigation; missing actionable information can interrupt it. Independent inhabitants could help make knowledge situated and discoverable, provided their advice and actions are reliable.

The small founding team and long continuity are encouraging precedents for original production. A [2016 founder statement](https://www.havenandhearth.com/forum/viewtopic.php?f=39&t=52550) also makes the economic boundary clear: dedication can keep development alive while income fails to fund the desired effort. That historical shortfall is not a finding about current profit. OpenLegend should learn from the achieved depth without treating years of personal commitment as a free resource.

### Life is Feudal exposes social access and trust as product requirements

The [Life is Feudal: MMO dossier](../dossiers/life-is-feudal-mmo.md) separates the restored subscription game from the distinct free-to-play Arden. The publisher [described its development focus](https://ltg.com/news/2025/qa-session-season-1-approaches-answers) as Arden and seasons while retaining the subscription audience. Their rules, reviews and participation should not be combined into one supposedly continuous product history.

Its strongest lesson concerns access to the payoff. Collective construction can express skill, cooperation and belonging. The same production requirements can leave a newcomer dependent on finding an organization before doing something they value. The evidence does not show that removing every dependency would preserve the appeal.

A [2024 firsthand evaluation](https://roundtablecoop.com/reviews/life-is-feudal-mmo-review-now-back-on-steam/) enjoyed learning and community help while expressing doubt about paying to continue. That makes a useful commercial distinction: liking a session is not the same as preferring the recurring commitment over available alternatives.

Service restructuring also makes trust part of persistent-world design. Players invest in a particular place, not an abstract promise that some server will remain online. OpenLegend should communicate what continuity means and make useful participation reachable. Autonomous residents could support that goal, but merely supplying labor would not reproduce a guild's voluntary human recognition.

### Boundless joins visual authorship to the cost of maintaining a world

The [Boundless dossier](../dossiers/boundless.md) shows how appearance can motivate action. A [detailed player account](https://store.steampowered.com/app/324510/?cc=uk&l=english) connects the search for a desired material palette with travel and encounters with other people's creations. Art can generate a goal and a reason to trade, beyond making an already chosen activity look attractive.

Human participation also supplies destinations and infrastructure. A shop, route or remarkable building acquires value through someone choosing to create and sustain it. Autonomous residents could complement that activity, but a world full of available services would not automatically reproduce its social authorship.

The economic evidence is unusually direct: in [June 2025](https://forum.playboundless.com/t/please-opensource-the-game/68160?page=8), an operator's developer reported losses and connected costs to continuously simulated worlds. It is dated testimony, not an audited November 2026 result. [October 2026 maintenance](https://forum.playboundless.com/t/maintenance-today/69528) establishes continued service, which is a different claim from financial health or expanding development.

For OpenLegend, a persistent living world requires both a compelling reason to inhabit it and a cost structure that can support its commitments. A small, appealing place is a sensible proposition to test; neither its appeal nor its affordability follows merely from reducing its area.

## How to compare success without manufacturing a league table

The comparison preserves five separate questions:

| Question | Useful evidence | Common error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the game attract people? | Paid units, verified account acquisition, relevant storefront demand | Calling funding or wishlists sales |
| Do people value playing it? | Specific independent accounts, dated review distributions, return behavior where available | Treating selected enthusiasts as the whole market |
| Does its society endure? | Continuing participation, maintained institutions and community projects | Calling one launch peak a durable population |
| Does the business work? | Game-specific receipts, costs and profit where disclosed | Treating gross spending, company-wide accounts or longevity as game profit |
| Is it a useful small-team precedent? | Actual labor, existing technology/assets, live-service obligations and audience expectations | Treating a revival built on a large inherited game as an equivalent original-development budget |

Steam concurrency is a platform-specific simultaneous-use measure. It is neither unique humans nor monthly active accounts. An official cross-launcher snapshot is still not a retention cohort. Donation-supported preservation projects require a different economic assessment from a new commercial game. A modest audience can support worthwhile work if cost and expectations fit it; that possibility must be evidenced, not assumed.

## What makes the experience worth returning to

These mechanisms recur across the attributed accounts; they are qualitative hypotheses rather than measured predictors of retention. The linked dossiers retain contrary experiences, dates and implementation changes.

| Mechanism | Why it can work | How the same system can fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wanted usefulness** | Supplies let someone fight in Foxhole; a service brings someone to a Restoration specialist; a Wurm project changes an inhabited place. Work has a beneficiary and a visible result. | Prerequisites, maintenance and awkward controls can consume the session before the result. Removing all effort can also remove mastery, specialization and the value of trade. [Foxhole](../dossiers/foxhole.md) · [Restoration](../dossiers/star-wars-galaxies-restoration.md) |
| **Growing practical understanding** | Discovering a place suggests a technique, that technique enables a return, and the return changes what is possible. | Opaque rules, incorrect guidance or inaccessible information turn investigation into dependence on external help. The player cannot tell whether another attempt is worthwhile. [Mortal](../dossiers/mortal-online-2.md) · [Haven](../dossiers/haven-and-hearth.md) · [SEED](../dossiers/seed.md) |
| **A particular place and history** | A recognisable home, public route or remembered collaboration gives prior actions lasting meaning. | Threatened loss and upkeep can turn attachment into avoidance or obligation. Recovering resources does not necessarily restore a composed place or its relationships. [Wurm](../dossiers/wurm-online.md) · [Boundless](../dossiers/boundless.md) |
| **Participation with other people** | A group gives effort context; ordinary services and neighboring projects can create lighter, more gradual relationships too. | Opportunity may depend on recruitment, schedules or gatekeepers. Empty public space can fail to deliver the advertised society. Automated availability alone does not reproduce chosen human attention. [Stars Reach](../dossiers/stars-reach.md) · [Life is Feudal](../dossiers/life-is-feudal-mmo.md) |
| **Consequences that create a next decision** | Risk makes preparation, companions and local knowledge valuable. A setback can open a new plan. | A player may stop experimenting, avoid using prized gear or leave after a loss. Different risk appetites are legitimate; harsher consequences are not automatically richer ones. [Mortal](../dossiers/mortal-online-2.md) · [Haven](../dossiers/haven-and-hearth.md) |
| **An appealing, legible result** | Desired colors, materials, silhouettes and places can motivate travel and making; visible actions help players understand what they accomplished. | Unappealing style, poor movement or weak feedback can stop people before systemic depth matters. A detailed environment can still feel uninhabited. [Boundless](../dossiers/boundless.md) · [Stars Reach](../dossiers/stars-reach.md) · [Mortal](../dossiers/mortal-online-2.md) |

Progression is most useful to this comparison when it changes reachable choices: a new destination, a better method, a wanted service, an alternative way to cooperate. A longer skill bar is not itself evidence of a better next session. Equally, a player who enjoys a settled routine need not be forced into perpetual expansion. OpenLegend's accepted room for a modest fulfilled life fits an actual set of pleasures in these cases, although that overlap makes it familiar territory rather than exclusive positioning.

The same distinction applies to departure. Completing a goal, taking a restorative break, losing friends, fearing another loss, encountering a technical blocker and rejecting a subscription are different outcomes. Review hours and concurrent-player counts cannot distinguish them without more evidence. A useful play study should record what the player wanted next and what prevented or enabled it, not reduce every departure to insufficient content.

## The candidate differentiation to test

The strongest provisional proposition is a world in which a player's practical discoveries change life with particular independent people. This joins the existing vision's adventure, invention, relationships and consequences. It does not require claiming that any one component has never appeared elsewhere.

| Proposed distinction | Competitive reality | What would make OpenLegend's version matter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autonomous people and memory | SEED already competes here; a remembered line can have little effect on play. | A particular person's knowledge, purpose and later decision change an activity the player cares about. |
| Invention | Competitors offer recipes, component combinations, visual construction and emergent engineering. Stars Reach's physical constructions are already inventive play. | A newly admitted useful method changes an achievable goal, rather than renaming a familiar object. Native behavior must remain reliable and understandable. |
| A persistent world | Player construction, institutions and shared history already supply persistence. | Returning reveals a meaningful consequence involving both the place and particular people, with clear rules for absence. |
| An inhabited world at modest human population | AI can potentially improve availability, but human social value is not interchangeable with simulated activity. | Residents make an enjoyable local life while human cooperation remains valuable; their presence must alter choices beyond adding service coverage. |
| Small-team focus | Years of accumulated work, inherited assets, volunteers and operating costs complicate all apparent precedents. | A bounded place delivers a complete desirable experience at verified production and operating cost. |

Learning an authored recipe, choosing materials or attributes, arranging structures, discovering an unexpected physical interaction and adding a new native rule are different kinds of creation. Their technical novelty does not rank their enjoyment. A reliable authored system may support more meaningful creativity than an unreliable generative one. OpenLegend should describe precisely what changed and why a player values it, while retaining its current limits on supported invention.

The distinction would be weak if ordinary play consisted mainly of gathering, filling skill bars and assembling familiar recipes, with occasional AI conversation beside it. The distinction would be stronger if the same activity regularly produced consequences unavailable from a static recipe catalogue or a compliant companion: someone chooses a different approach, useful knowledge changes what can be made, a joint risk creates a remembered event, and returning to the place reveals a durable result.

This is a target experience, not a report that these outcomes already occur reliably. Nor is adding every competitor's successful system a sensible response. More professions, bigger maps and more simulated inhabitants also increase explanation, art, balancing, moderation and operating work.

Availability and independence can also conflict. A resident with competing purposes may create waiting, gatekeeping or negotiation overhead. A resident who always accommodates the player loses much of the independence being promised. The design needs intelligible boundaries, useful alternative actions and the freedom to proceed alone, so an understandable refusal can create an interesting choice without blocking the session. This is a central product tradeoff, not just a problem of improving dialogue.

There is an important counterargument: human cooperation carries another person's voluntary attention, reputation and recognition of shared authorship. AI inhabitants might improve availability without supplying that same value. A lonely player's criticism does not establish demand for an AI substitute. OpenLegend should examine whether its inhabitants complement human play and provide an independently enjoyable experience; it should not assume that a populated-looking world solves the social problem.

### What a convincing demonstration would contain

The existing [demonstration structure](../../../06-marketing/positioning-and-copy.md#hero-demo-concept) connects a goal, obstacle, choice, working invention, independent response, payoff and later consequence. That is a stronger competitive communication unit than a list of systems.

Consider an illustrative target episode: the player has a camp and wants to reach a useful expedition site. A resident knows part of the route but has a competing purpose. Their willingness and contribution change the preparation. The player invents an admitted alternative within the game's supported capabilities, makes the actual object and uses it. An encounter permits an intelligible choice among cooperation, negotiation, avoidance or fighting. Returning brings a real benefit to the home or a particular person, and a later visit reflects what happened.

This is a proposed arrangement of the existing vision, **not a claim of an implemented or naturally observed complete session**. It does not authorize arbitrary generated mechanics or require every resident to agree. Learning an existing method and making a genuine new invention remain different outcomes. The episode matters only if its native effects work and ordinary people enjoy and understand it.

A well-authored RPG quest could deliver much of this episode too. That is a serious benchmark. OpenLegend's approach must add worthwhile unscripted variation, agency or consequences, at acceptable cost and reliability. One attractive story does not establish that autonomy and invention were necessary to produce it, or that their implementation is preferable to a more tightly authored alternative.

Several weaker results could resemble success in a curated video. A resident might simply repeat a remembered fact without changing any decision. A supposedly invented tool might be an existing item with different prose. A later scene might be manually arranged instead of following from play. Or the systems might work correctly while a newcomer finds the process tedious. Those possibilities are why existing implementation evidence, uncoached play and comparative preference must remain separate.

### Who might prefer the combination

My provisional audience hypothesis is players who want direct adventure and an inhabitable shared world, value personal continuity and useful creativity, and would appreciate interesting local lives without needing a large human organization to supply every opportunity. Some may prefer company with fewer scheduling demands; others may care principally about human cooperation. The sample does not establish the size or willingness to pay of either audience.

This proposition also competes for attention with single-player and cooperative adventures, not only the nine MMOs. Being unusual within this comparison is not a claim of worldwide originality. A smaller world must offer a positive reason to choose it that outweighs less content, a smaller human community and any presentation limitations.

## Graphics: protect desire to inhabit the world

The research does not support dismissing graphics as a superficial concern. Style preference, asset quality, animation, atmosphere, readability and responsiveness can fail independently. A person may like a stylized landscape while disliking unfinished faces, stiff movement or a confusing interface.

For OpenLegend, the relevant question is whether people want to spend time looking at and acting in its particular world. Coherent light, a recognisable person, convincing motion and a place shaped by use can support that desire with bounded scope. A low asset count can be a production decision; it is not automatically an appealing aesthetic.

The case evidence supports treating presentation as three connected jobs: **attract attention, communicate action, and make outcomes desirable**. Boundless's palettes illustrate the third; Foxhole's camera-oriented art illustrates the second; the mixed reactions to Stars Reach show that interesting systems cannot cancel aesthetic preference. These are attributed lessons, not findings from a comparative visual benchmark. [Boundless review](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/boundless-review/) · [Foxhole art process](https://www.moddb.com/games/foxhole/news/devblog-anti-tank-rifle-foliage-art-and-more) · [Stars Reach reception](../dossiers/stars-reach.md#reception-interesting-worlds-difficult-entry)

Within the accepted grounded stylization, a practical priority order to test is:

1. **A place worth inhabiting.** A coherent palette, useful lighting, believable materials and a recognizable local composition should make the camp and its surroundings desirable. Beauty can supply a purpose alongside survival pressure.
2. **People worth noticing.** The initial four or five residents concentrate attention on recognizable appearance, posture, ordinary work and readable responses. Their claimed independence will be harder to believe if their physical presence barely changes with their activity.
3. **Actions with visible effects.** Gathering, making, helping, refusing, fighting and using an invention need sufficient motion, sound and state feedback to communicate what happened. Rich prose cannot replace a missing practical result.
4. **Understandable goals and consequences.** Players should be able to recognize a useful material, a dangerous situation, a changed object or a newly usable route without interpreting every state through text panels.
5. **Consistency at the playable view.** Evaluate actual movement and interaction, not only an isolated portrait or a flattering still. Check the same sequence on the intended hardware before expanding the asset set.

This is an analytical priority proposal for existing art and first-playable owners, not a new accepted production plan. It does not require copying another game's camera or abandoning the selected body proportions, painted material treatment and coherent lighting. Nor does it establish that the current OpenLegend build looks better or worse than these competitors: this research did not perform that comparison.

This differentiation may shift production expense rather than remove it. Personal relationships concentrate attention on characters and their actions; practical invention requires understandable effects and dependable validation. Fewer locations can still carry demanding animation, feedback, simulation and operating requirements. The proposed focus is potentially distinctive for a small team, but its economic advantage remains unproved.

No conversion experiment in this pass isolates the sales effect of graphics. Reviews can identify barriers and pleasures, but cannot calculate how many people rejected a store page without purchasing. Surviving communities therefore do not prove that visual shortcomings were commercially harmless.

## Apply findings through existing owners

The [first-playable validation owner](../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-validation.md) already distinguishes correctness, invention/independence, uncoached enjoyment, return value, demand and cost. FPV07 covers ordinary danger and returning consequences; FPV09 retains the complete target rather than accepting an easy subset.

[CP10](../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-cooperation.md#cp10--whole-episodes-and-player-value) already asks whether players understand and benefit from particular people's voluntary cooperation, recall a shared occurrence and make a later choice affected by it. It explicitly retains repetitive begging, excessive administration, stalls and meaningless friendship claims as failures. Those are unusually close to the competitive risks found here.

[Invention work](../../../../docs/maintainers/inventions-and-world-evolution.md) separates native authored methods, admitted new definitions and broader live-generation quality. The commercial question belongs to [CO01 and its decision brief](../../../../docs/projects/initial-commercial-offer-decision-brief.md), including real operating costs and the distinction between the game and creator offer.

Passing the existing cooperation and first-playable gates would establish valuable behavior, not competitive preference. SEED overlaps with independence and memory; Stars Reach and Wurm already support meaningful shared activity and lasting places. The separate question is whether relevant players prefer OpenLegend's connected experience enough to choose it. That belongs in existing audience/commercial learning, rather than being inferred from technical acceptance or a memorable NPC anecdote.

No new implementation backlog is needed to make these findings actionable. The immediate competitive burden is already present in the complete first-playable target: a manageable inhabited place, direct adventure, voluntary cooperation, useful invention and a later consequence. Keep those elements connected before enlarging the world. If players mainly remember conversation quality while the practical choices remain conventional, the proposed distinction has not yet been demonstrated.

## Evidence limits and what this research does not settle

Each of the nine dossiers covers R01–R14 and records the conditional R15 determination. Independent written evaluations, selected helpful positive/negative reviews and current firsthand accounts were read where accessible. Where five full independent current reviews do not exist, the dossier identifies older reviews, previews, shorter accounts and the remaining gap; it does not invent a quorum. Helpful-review and forum samples are self-selected and reveal mechanisms and disagreements, not population prevalence.

Primary patch notes and disclosures take priority for shipped behavior, dates and financial scope. Historical criticism is not automatically a current defect; a roadmap is not a released feature. Source registers retain access limits. No firsthand gameplay, audio audition, installed mod verification, creator-income audit, OpenLegend graphics benchmark or causal marketing experiment was performed.

The work establishes a competitive landscape and a concrete direction to test. It does not establish an uncontested market, a profitable price, the scale of a potential audience, or that AI inhabitants can replace desired human relationships. It also does not show that OpenLegend must become a larger MMO to compete. The strongest plausible route is to make its already documented combination unusually coherent and enjoyable in a place small enough to understand, then observe whether relevant players choose to return and whether delivering that experience is economically sustainable.
