# Shared-world sandbox MMOs and OpenLegend

**Research comparison in progress — November 14, 2026.** [Batch scope and completion](../research-progress-shared-world-sandbox-mmos-2026-11-14.md). This document records analysis and proposed positioning, not accepted new product scope or proven market demand.

## The question and the current answer

Mike asks which of nine shared-world sandboxes succeed, what players actually value and reject, how graphics affect the experience, and whether OpenLegend's documented vision gives people a compelling reason to choose it despite a smaller team and graphics budget.

The completed SEED, Stars Reach, Wurm Online, Star Wars Galaxies: Restoration and Foxhole cases already rule out an easy answer. Autonomous characters, remembered relationships, useful occupations, persistent landscapes and unexpected player inventions all have serious competitors. OpenLegend has a plausible opportunity in how these elements connect during an embodied adventure. A distinctive feature list, commercial demand and a reliably distinctive playable experience are three separate claims.

The other four dossier passes and the final comparative judgment remain unfinished at this checkpoint. These preliminary findings are preserved so a restart cannot turn incomplete research into a completed market assessment.

## Use the actual OpenLegend vision

OpenLegend's [gameplay priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) select a connected survival-adventure: useful making, a home, danger, discoveries, independent people, practical invention and persistent consequences. The first playable is supposed to contain that distinctive combination. A generic survival loop with the interesting people and invention deferred would not meet the existing target.

Peaceful work, companionship and beauty can be satisfying purposes. They coexist with danger and difficult expeditions. The research should not narrow this into a mandatory settlement-management game, a conversation simulator, or an exclusively gentle social world. Nor should every successful camp have to become a city. A player may choose a modest life and enjoy having achieved it.

The [selected language](../../../06-marketing/language-and-elevator-pitches.md) distinguishes the playable game from the wider engine and creator proposition. The endorsed line, “Create a world by playing it. Share what you discover,” remains among the selected wording. This essay interprets its competitive burden; it does not silently replace accepted copy.

The [current art direction](../../../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-direction-at-a-glance) requires believable grounded stylization and rejects visible facets, blockiness and toy proportions. A smaller production budget does not authorize an unrelated low-poly style. The [creative brief](../../../../art-direction/final-board/creative-brief.md) connects beauty to actual people, materials, lighting, weather and world state.

Current capability must remain separate from ambition. The main-branch [README](../../../../README.md) documents an active game pause when all human connections leave. The [continuing-lives design](../../../../docs/projects/continuing-lives-feature-spec.md) is a delivery target, not proof that a continuously operating, large-scale MMO already exists.

## What the first five cases establish

### SEED competes directly on people

The [SEED dossier](../dossiers/seed.md) establishes substantial overlap with autonomous characters, personality-conditioned LLM dialogue, remembered relationships, offline work, generational histories and social institutions. “We have AI characters” is insufficient differentiation. Its [official relationship explanation](https://seed.game/news-from-avesta/how-to-have-a-relationship) makes that overlap explicit.

SEED's player-managed society and OpenLegend's intended direct adventure have different centers of play. That difference may matter more than either technology label. The test is whether a player wants to be in OpenLegend because of what they actually do there.

The reception sample contains both useful social roles and burdensome administration. In the frustrated accounts, conversational expressiveness did not reliably offset characters needing rescue, invented guidance, empty friendliness or difficulty carrying out a chosen activity. Its funding and staffing establish production investment, not sales success. Steam's absent review corpus does not mean there are no standalone players.

### Stars Reach competes directly on embodied systemic freedom

The [Stars Reach dossier](../dossiers/stars-reach.md) covers direct adventure, a reactive environment, occupations, settlement society and inventive uses of physical rules. Koster's [June interview](https://gamesbeat.com/playable-worlds-stars-reach-mmo-launches-into-early-access-this-summer/) attributes unexpected constructions to players; generic “invention” is therefore also contested territory.

Its strongest contrasting accounts concern purpose. One player sees unfinished tools and lonely work. Another joins a human society and discovers useful work, local identity and shared projects. A system becomes meaningful partly through the people and desired outcomes connected to it. That is an interpretation of the [reception evidence](../dossiers/stars-reach.md#reception-interesting-worlds-difficult-entry), not a measured causal effect.

OpenLegend's particular inhabitants could help provide intelligible local life before a newcomer finds the right human organization. They must do more than explain a task. Their own decisions, knowledge, commitments and practical contributions need to change the adventure.

### Wurm establishes a paying niche and the burden of attachment

The [Wurm dossier](../dossiers/wurm-online.md) establishes persistent ownership and meaningful skilled work as serious prior art. Its operator reported approximately SEK 2.1 million in November 2024 sales, while current group reporting shows financial pressure; these support neither dismissing it as dead nor declaring it consistently profitable. [Game disclosure](https://www.mfn.se/cis/a/game-chest-group/gamechest-okad-forsaljning-och-fler-spelare-for-wurm-online-5f8ef835.iframe) · [Group report](https://www.mfn.se/a/game-chest-group/kvartalsrapport-2-2026)

The useful distinction is between effort that gives something value and procedure that delays any wanted result. Historical reviewers and current player accounts disagree because goals, available time and social access differ. A slower pace may be the desired experience. OpenLegend should make quiet inhabitation enjoyable in its own right, alongside adventure, rather than treating every minute without a new unlock as failure.

Wurm also shows that a persistent place can carry human memories without AI memory. The prospective contribution is richer encounters with particular inhabitants, not the invention of a world with history. Care can motivate return and also create unwanted obligation. Test voluntary desire to revisit separately from time spent protecting earlier investment.

### Restoration makes ordinary life and human service valuable

The [Restoration dossier](../dossiers/star-wars-galaxies-restoration.md) concerns the particular fan service, not every version of Star Wars Galaxies. Its independent accounts value surveying, making, visiting cities and encountering other people's activities. That is evidence for enjoying inhabitation, rather than needing every session to produce a dramatic personal triumph. [Contemporary firsthand account](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/mmo/i-want-mmos-to-waste-our-time-again/)

Its [Doctor redesign](https://swgr.org/post/doctors-and-a-living-galaxy.7787/) exposes a tension between automated availability and meaningful human participation. OpenLegend should preserve that distinction when evaluating its autonomous inhabitants. Efficient service, companionship, another person's recognition and shared authorship are different benefits. It may deliver some strongly without replacing all of them.

The inherited commercial world, established fiction and volunteer work also matter to comparison. A modest operating budget for maintaining that service cannot establish the cost of creating an original world, attracting its audience and paying its contributors. The case supports learning from social design while keeping production economics separate.

### Foxhole connects individual action to an intelligible shared purpose

The [Foxhole dossier](../dossiers/foxhole.md) provides a strong original-game demand example. Steam Charts records roughly 1,704 average concurrent players in October 2026, following a much larger February expansion month. These demonstrate durable participation, not unique customer counts or verified profit. [Dated concurrency series](https://steamcharts.com/app/505460)

Its firsthand accounts connect ordinary work to visible help and human recognition. The [historical LOGI letter](https://logiunion.com/archive/openletter/) also documents how cumulative logistics overhead can make that work frustrating. Later changes matter; the old dispute does not establish that every complaint remains current.

The design lesson is to connect effort to a wanted consequence while scrutinizing the cost of supplying it. OpenLegend should ask whether an invention, expedition or resident's contribution changes something the player values. More production stages alone do not establish greater depth. Nor does replacing a human contributor with automation preserve the same social experience automatically.

Foxhole also shows why presentation deserves deliberate scope decisions. Its developer's [foliage discussion](https://www.moddb.com/games/foxhole/news/devblog-anti-tank-rifle-foliage-art-and-more) treats readability at the gameplay camera as a design problem. OpenLegend's personal relationships imply different visual priorities, but the discipline of testing the actual view transfers.

## How to compare success without manufacturing a league table

The final comparison will preserve five separate questions:

| Question | Useful evidence | Common error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the game attract people? | Paid units, verified account acquisition, relevant storefront demand | Calling funding or wishlists sales |
| Do people value playing it? | Specific independent accounts, dated review distributions, return behavior where available | Treating selected enthusiasts as the whole market |
| Does its society endure? | Continuing participation, maintained institutions and community projects | Calling one launch peak a durable population |
| Does the business work? | Game-specific receipts, costs and profit where disclosed | Treating gross spending, company-wide accounts or longevity as game profit |
| Is it a useful small-team precedent? | Actual labor, existing technology/assets, live-service obligations and audience expectations | Treating a revival built on a large inherited game as an equivalent original-development budget |

Steam concurrency is a platform-specific simultaneous-use measure. It is neither unique humans nor monthly active accounts. An official cross-launcher snapshot is still not a retention cohort. Donation-supported preservation projects require a different economic assessment from a new commercial game. A modest audience can support worthwhile work if cost and expectations fit it; that possibility must be evidenced, not assumed.

## The candidate differentiation to test

The strongest provisional proposition is a world in which a player's practical discoveries change life with particular independent people. This joins the existing vision's adventure, invention, relationships and consequences. It does not require claiming that any one component has never appeared elsewhere.

The distinction would be weak if ordinary play consisted mainly of gathering, filling skill bars and assembling familiar recipes, with occasional AI conversation beside it. The distinction would be stronger if the same activity regularly produced consequences unavailable from a static recipe catalogue or a compliant companion: someone chooses a different approach, useful knowledge changes what can be made, a joint risk creates a remembered event, and returning to the place reveals a durable result.

This is a target experience, not a report that these outcomes already occur reliably. Nor is adding every competitor's successful system a sensible response. More professions, bigger maps and more simulated inhabitants also increase explanation, art, balancing, moderation and operating work.

There is an important counterargument: human cooperation carries another person's voluntary attention, reputation and recognition of shared authorship. AI inhabitants might improve availability without supplying that same value. A lonely player's criticism does not establish demand for an AI substitute. OpenLegend should examine whether its inhabitants complement human play and provide an independently enjoyable experience; it should not assume that a populated-looking world solves the social problem.

### What a convincing demonstration would contain

The existing [demonstration structure](../../../06-marketing/positioning-and-copy.md#hero-demo-concept) connects a goal, obstacle, choice, working invention, independent response, payoff and later consequence. That is a stronger competitive communication unit than a list of systems.

Consider an illustrative target episode: the player has a camp and wants to reach a useful expedition site. A resident knows part of the route but has a competing purpose. Their willingness and contribution change the preparation. The player invents an admitted alternative within the game's supported capabilities, makes the actual object and uses it. An encounter permits an intelligible choice among cooperation, negotiation, avoidance or fighting. Returning brings a real benefit to the home or a particular person, and a later visit reflects what happened.

This is a proposed arrangement of the existing vision, **not a claim of an implemented or naturally observed complete session**. It does not authorize arbitrary generated mechanics or require every resident to agree. Learning an existing method and making a genuine new invention remain different outcomes. The episode matters only if its native effects work and ordinary people enjoy and understand it.

Several weaker results could resemble success in a curated video. A resident might simply repeat a remembered fact without changing any decision. A supposedly invented tool might be an existing item with different prose. A later scene might be manually arranged instead of following from play. Or the systems might work correctly while a newcomer finds the process tedious. Those possibilities are why existing implementation evidence, uncoached play and comparative preference must remain separate.

### Who might prefer the combination

My provisional audience hypothesis is players who want direct adventure and an inhabitable shared world, value personal continuity and useful creativity, and would appreciate interesting local lives without needing a large human organization to supply every opportunity. Some may prefer company with fewer scheduling demands; others may care principally about human cooperation. The sample does not establish the size or willingness to pay of either audience.

This proposition also competes for attention with single-player and cooperative adventures, not only the nine MMOs. Being unusual within this comparison is not a claim of worldwide originality. A smaller world must offer a positive reason to choose it that outweighs less content, a smaller human community and any presentation limitations.

## Graphics: protect desire to inhabit the world

The research does not support dismissing graphics as a superficial concern. Style preference, asset quality, animation, atmosphere, readability and responsiveness can fail independently. A person may like a stylized landscape while disliking unfinished faces, stiff movement or a confusing interface.

For OpenLegend, the relevant question is whether people want to spend time looking at and acting in its particular world. Coherent light, a recognisable person, convincing motion and a place shaped by use can support that desire with bounded scope. A low asset count can be a production decision; it is not automatically an appealing aesthetic.

No conversion experiment in this pass isolates the sales effect of graphics. Reviews can identify barriers and pleasures, but cannot calculate how many people rejected a store page without purchasing. Surviving communities therefore do not prove that visual shortcomings were commercially harmless.

## Apply findings through existing owners

The [first-playable validation owner](../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-validation.md) already distinguishes correctness, invention/independence, uncoached enjoyment, return value, demand and cost. FPV07 covers ordinary danger and returning consequences; FPV09 retains the complete target rather than accepting an easy subset.

[CP10](../../../../docs/maintainers/first-playable-cooperation.md#cp10--whole-episodes-and-player-value) already asks whether players understand and benefit from particular people's voluntary cooperation, recall a shared occurrence and make a later choice affected by it. It explicitly retains repetitive begging, excessive administration, stalls and meaningless friendship claims as failures. Those are unusually close to the competitive risks found here.

[Invention work](../../../../docs/maintainers/inventions-and-world-evolution.md) separates native authored methods, admitted new definitions and broader live-generation quality. The commercial question belongs to [CO01 and its decision brief](../../../../docs/projects/initial-commercial-offer-decision-brief.md), including real operating costs and the distinction between the game and creator offer.

Passing the existing cooperation and first-playable gates would establish valuable behavior, not competitive preference. SEED overlaps with independence and memory; Stars Reach and Wurm already support meaningful shared activity and lasting places. The separate question is whether relevant players prefer OpenLegend's connected experience enough to choose it. That belongs in existing audience/commercial learning, rather than being inferred from technical acceptance or a memorable NPC anecdote.

No new implementation backlog is needed to make these findings actionable. The final essay will connect all nine competitors to these existing decisions after the remaining dossiers are complete.
