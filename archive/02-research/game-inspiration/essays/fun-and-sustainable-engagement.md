# What makes games fun — and worth returning to

**Canonical research synthesis · October 14, 2026**

This is the game-inspiration library's core research document on **fun, meaningful experience, sustained engagement and pressure to keep playing**. It synthesizes all **148 roster subjects plus the additional Dragon Age series dossier**, and incorporates Mike's supplied Roblox video transcript. It replaces neither the individual dossiers nor the [earlier fun essay](what-actually-makes-these-worlds-fun.md), which remains a preserved precursor. Canonical here means the owner of this research synthesis—not an approved architecture, product specification or implementation backlog.

**Central position:** design for participation people value, progress that preserves or expands that value, and return they can freely choose. Being hard to put down is not sufficient evidence of success. A game can be enjoyable without demanding another session; it can sustain attention after enjoyment has faded; and an enjoyable game can still be played in ways a person regrets.

## 1. What this synthesis is based on

The [research roster](../research-roster.md) owns subject identities. The topic-focused review is complete across the corpus: [G01–G80 and the Dragon Age supplement](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md), followed by [G81–G148](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md). Every subject has an inspected-section record, a contribution and a counterweight. This includes small spin-offs, creator platforms and the unreleased EverQuest Next proposal; these do not all provide equivalent evidence of actual player experience.

The corpus was taken from a branch beginning at GitHub main commit `7a576214fbb9891b2e9f742db56bbeccaa3a58a1`. This review inspected relevant dossier mechanics, interpretations, worked situations and limitations. It did **not** replay 148 games, rerun their original research passes, independently verify every external source, or turn constructed examples into observed sessions. Existing facts retain their version, audience and source boundaries. The 22 selected video categories/entries are a supplemental account, not 22 newly completed dossiers.

The [foundations paper](../references/fun-engagement-and-compulsion-research.md) separately records selected primary research on motivation, meaningful entertainment, playtime and the clinical boundary. It distinguishes published findings from our design interpretations. The principles below are comparative hypotheses and recommendations: recurring cases make them useful to investigate, not universal causal laws or statistically ranked effects.

## 2. Fun, meaning, return and compulsion are different questions

Use four questions throughout a review:

| Question | What to examine |
| --- | --- |
| **Is the activity enjoyable?** | Handling, feedback, discovery, humor, expression, challenge, companionship, comfortable routine or another identifiable pleasure. |
| **Was the experience worthwhile?** | Satisfaction, a meaningful ending, an accomplished project, a memorable relationship or a new understanding—even when parts were sad or difficult. |
| **Why would the person return?** | A chosen next goal, another interesting attempt, friends, a familiar place, new content, a reward, obligation or fear of losing an opportunity. |
| **Can the person stop?** | Clear saving and persistence, acceptable departure, boundaries around real-world attendance, and whether continuing conflicts with the person's own intention. |

These are not four scores that should be added into an invented health metric. They prevent one observation—such as a long session—from standing in for every desirable outcome. The foundations distinguish enjoyment from appreciation, harmonious from obsessive engagement, and ordinary involvement from gaming disorder. None allows diagnosis from a video, achievement count or playtime alone. [Foundations and primary sources](../references/fun-engagement-and-compulsion-research.md)

Our preferred design sequence is **an inviting situation → worthwhile participation → an understandable result → learning, change or discovery → a personally meaningful next possibility**. A satisfying stopping point belongs inside that sequence. A contrasting sequence is **a threatened loss or expiring opportunity → another required check → postponed departure**, which can raise attendance without establishing equivalent value. These are analytical descriptions, not claims that every game or player belongs wholly to one category. The same game can support both motives. [Roblox account](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md) · [evidence map](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

## 3. Sixteen central principles

### P01 — Decide what kind of value the game promises

There is no single desired intensity or universal definition of a good session. A hard contest, a quiet visit, a personally arranged home, a frightening expedition and a moving story can all fulfill different promises. The relevant question is not whether every game maximizes strategic depth, creativity or session length, but whether its repeated activities support the experience its audience came for.

Animal Crossing and The Sims make personal arrangement and ordinary life relevant; Outer Wilds makes discovery and eventual resolution central; League of Legends emphasizes repeated competitive mastery. The Witcher 3 and Final Fantasy XVI also warn against assuming a valued story proves equally enjoyable repeated combat or sidequest actions. [G03, G08, G24, G34, G72](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G93](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** “players differ” should not excuse poor execution. Identify the promised pleasure, then investigate whether controls, repetition and progression deliver it. A story-first player and an optimization enthusiast can reasonably need different settings or activities rather than one compromised average.

### P02 — Make the activity itself good, not merely its reward

The interval between rewards is most of the experience. Responsive movement, readable impact, sound, atmosphere, conversation and the physical satisfaction of arranging something deserve evaluation in their own right. An action can be enjoyable without requiring a fresh strategic decision every second. Conversely, making a chore economically necessary does not make it enjoyable.

Hades distinguishes weapons through handling; Battle Brothers makes shield outcomes visible and audible; Black Flag lets a collected song change later travel. The video praises Pet Simulator 99's presentation while finding the subsequent activity hollow, and finds comforting companionship in Treelands without a strong urge to continue. Those contrasts separate sensory quality, activity quality and persistence. [G01, G22](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G98, G121](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md) · [Roblox study](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md)

**Review question:** with the next payout temporarily removed, what would still be satisfying, expressive, funny, beautiful or interesting? This is a diagnostic thought experiment, not a demand that every game remove progression.

### P03 — Supply direction without unnecessarily prescribing the method

A comprehensible goal gives possibilities a purpose. Method-level freedom lets the player make that purpose their own: choosing a route, loadout, ally, investment, style or interpretation. The number of visible options is less important than whether the player can explain why one approach suits this situation.

The transcript's Bee Swarm example combines overlapping goals with different hive and field choices. Retail Tycoon turns a money shortage into a strategy to test. Deus Ex supplies partly substitutable routes with different costs; Scribblenauts shows why a broad vocabulary still needs a worthwhile situation. [Roblox comparisons RV-A and RV-B](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md) · [G39](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G117](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** maximum freedom is not the rule. Final Fantasy IV's authored role changes and Mass Effect's directed dramatic structure can support meaningful participation. Agency can concern conduct, timing and interpretation even when the destination is fixed. A thousand equivalent or unusable options are not a thousand good choices. [G81, G101–G104](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

### P04 — Make causality learnable; preserve mystery where discovery is the activity

A player needs enough evidence to form and revise a plan. Distinguish not knowing the answer from not knowing what the controls did, which prerequisite was missing, why an apparently valid action failed, or whether progress was saved. These require different remedies.

Slay the Spire's intentions and Final Fantasy X's turn order support planning under uncertainty. Outer Wilds rewards learning rather than simply supplying every answer. Deus Ex and Prey make information practically useful, while also requiring readable limits on apparently flexible tools. Infiltrator exposes the cost of confusing encounter completion, collected loot and an external account reward. [G32, G34, G43](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G87, G106, G117–G118](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** exposing every hidden fact can remove investigation, suspense or deduction. Explain the action's contract and provide discoverable evidence; do not automatically reveal the solution. A camera failure, inconsistent affordance or unregistered input is not productive uncertainty.

### P05 — Make progress change possibilities, and keep its parts in proportion

Progress may be new knowledge, a useful route, a relationship, a changed home, a reusable design, resilience or a new action—not only a larger statistic. Numerical growth earns its place when it changes what the player can meaningfully attempt or how a familiar activity feels.

Cyberpunk's action-changing perks, Final Fantasy V's portable expertise, Octopath II's restoration projects and Rain World's learned routes illustrate different growth forms. Raft's receiver and engine change navigation rather than simply multiplying income. [G68](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G82, G108, G123, G141](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

The transcript's Mining Simulator and Pet Simulator 99 cases expose the opposite: catch-up power can erase interesting obstacles while leaving a capacity or waiting bottleneck. An upgrade must be evaluated relative to enemies, carrying capacity, acquisition cost, travel and goals—not in isolation. **Counterweight:** newcomers may genuinely need accelerated access. Preserve opportunities to understand and participate; do not force every historic grind merely to protect a notion of earned power. [Roblox comparison RV-D](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md) · [Terraria and Path of Radiance counterweights](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

### P06 — Seek useful combinations, not maximum system count

Depth often comes from a few understandable parts changing one another's value. A weapon can open a vulnerability, a spell can change target scope, a helper can make a route feasible, and a carried object can be worth more as a solution than as money. Ownership, permission, compatibility, readiness and effectiveness are separate properties.

Noita and Caves of Qud emphasize cross-system consequences. Linked Materia makes combinations visible; V Rising pairs broad reconfiguration with few simultaneously active abilities. Starsector and Battle Brothers make equipment suitability depend on costs and context, not one ascending quality ranking. [G32–G33](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G84, G120–G121, G146](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** a combination space can contain one dominant answer, mostly irrelevant options, or so much dependency work that ordinary play becomes consulting a guide. Fire Emblem Awakening's bundled Pair Up benefits and Final Fantasy VIII's incentive to retain spell stocks illustrate this risk. Add a rule because it creates a valued distinction, not because it increases theoretical complexity. [G62](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G85](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

### P07 — Use constraints to create worthwhile choices, not repeated administration

Scarcity, weight, time, durability and vulnerability can make preparation matter. Their value depends on what the player chooses because they exist. A journey that trades equipment against mobility is different from moving the same items among inconvenient menus after the decision is already settled.

DayZ and Starsector make taking more equipment potentially costly. Oxygen Not Included turns a local solution into connected heat, water and power questions. Kingdom Come: Deliverance II makes learning a procedure distinct from owning its ingredients. These are different uses of friction. The inventory criticism in Ultima VII and Green Hell shows that physical presentation can be immersive and cumbersome simultaneously. [G114, G120, G128–G129, G132, G144](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Review question:** after the player understands the solution, what remains valuable about performing it again? Sometimes the answer is a chosen calming routine, not another puzzle. Sometimes it is nothing. Compress the latter without automatically erasing the former.

### P08 — Give play a rhythm, not uninterrupted escalation

Immediate feedback, a local goal, an expedition and a long-lived project operate at different scales. They can reinforce one another without every quiet moment demanding another task. Preparation gives danger context; relief lets achievement be felt; returning home makes small changes visible.

Hades links attempts with relationships at home. Red Dead Redemption's travel and Red Dead Redemption 2's camp give atmosphere and ordinary life room to matter. Final Fantasy XV combines rest, food and selected photographs; Dragon's Dogma 2 separates winning a fight from restoring expedition capacity. [G22, G69–G70](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G92, G127](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** bundling several rewards into a rest point can produce awkward optimization, while a quiet interval can become dead time. Do not infer a universally ideal session length, upgrade interval or number of concurrent goals. The proposed test is whether the player understands the current purpose and welcomes its pace—not whether the screen never rests.

### P09 — Let both authored surprises and player-created situations carry value

Creative play has several forms: solving a problem in a personal way, constructing something, improvising with friends, interpreting a character, or discovering an author's unexpected invention. These are complements, not a hierarchy with unrestricted sandboxes always on top.

Raise a Floppa is the transcript's clearest correction to its own creativity thesis: the next authored surprise sustains curiosity through a finite experience. Among Us and Lethal Company generate social situations from bounded responsibilities. Wildermyth and Shadow of War combine authored material with conditions that make an event fit a particular history. [Roblox comparison RV-C](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md) · [G02, G25, G38](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G116](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** more generated events can expose repetition, contradiction or intrusive interruptions. More authored content creates production and pacing costs. Surprise should invite recognition or a new response, not merely arrive more often. Creativity is not a guarantee against staleness.

### P10 — Make investment particular, useful and accurately remembered

A person, place or possession becomes more than a category when its history matters: this companion helped, this ship survived, this home records a project, this object changes an ordinary action. Practical usefulness can reinforce characterization without replacing it.

RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress and Project Zomboid show how care and specific history can organize goals. KOTOR companions offer perspectives as well as skills; Shadow of War makes repeated encounters recognizable; Sons of the Forest distinguishes a commanded helper from a less directed ally. [G21, G23, G37](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G109, G116, G138](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** a supply bonus does not automatically create affection, and remembered history must be correct. Nor does a large simulated biography guarantee an experienced relationship. A small reliable callback may serve the activity better than extensive inaccessible state. Attachment can also cause avoidance when a player fears losing what they value, as the Steal a Brainrot counterexample illustrates. [G06, G26](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

### P11 — Make social dependence serve wanted participation

Complementary abilities, unequal information and opportunities to help can make someone matter to a group. But people must want compatible activities and have a fair chance to participate. Matching a tank and healer does not establish that both want a fast completion, an exploratory run or the same risk.

Dota 2's rescue opportunities, Lethal Company's distributed attention and XCOM's role composition expose the value of interdependence in different forms. Final Fantasy XIV and World of Warcraft show that coordination and attendance are separate design problems. Dragon Age Legends supplies a counterexample in which friend availability becomes an access wait. [G09, G38 and Dragon Age supplement](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G91, G97, G125](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Review question:** is another participant contributing something they want to do, or serving as a gate? Experienced friends can teach or erase a newcomer's discovery; independent co-op action can create freedom or exclude someone from a scene. Agreeing on spoilers, imported power, acceptable loss and session commitment is part of the social experience, not merely an administrative detail. [G26, G29, G31, G57](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

### P12 — Make failure intelligible, proportionate and recoverable under the chosen contract

A setback can renew purpose by changing a plan, teaching a rule, producing a rescue or leaving a meaningful history. The lost value must be understood relative to what the player agreed to risk. Losing one attempt, a possession, a companion or a whole campaign are not equivalent treatments of failure.

Hades preserves selected progress between attempts; Kenshi makes recovery a project; Battle Brothers adds durable company development to make rebuilding less demoralizing. Warcraft III can preserve a hero without restoring the lost army. Crusader Kings III makes a life end while a dynasty continues. [G22, G36](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G96, G121, G126](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** an interesting disaster story does not prove its owner enjoyed the loss. The transcript explicitly contrasts Apocalypse Rising's renewed purpose with Deepwoken's excessive stress for its narrator. Repeated approaches, hidden rules and technical faults do not become valuable merely because they are punishing. Recovery settings, retries and practice modes can change the cost of learning without removing the underlying problem. [Roblox study](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md) · [G47, G75–G76, G80](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

### P13 — Automate execution without accidentally taking away the desired role

Automation can replace repeated input with configuration, supervision, organization or a new creative goal. It can also erase the very competence someone wanted to exercise. Evaluate the responsibility remaining after automation, not simply the number of clicks removed.

Factorio and Satisfactory relocate work into production design. Final Fantasy XII's Gambits make priorities inspectable policies; Final Fantasy XIII concentrates control on changing team roles. Vampire Survivors automates attacks while preserving movement and build choices. Kelvin and Raft's collection systems remove ordinary labor while leaving plans to their players. [G27–G28, G44](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G89–G90, G138, G141](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** imported blueprints can bypass valued discovery or another player's authorship. A helper that chooses every goal may turn an intended adventure into spectatorship. Spectatorship and co-authoring can themselves be legitimate choices, as AI Dungeon suggests, but they have a different authority contract from acting inside a persistent world. [G27, G40](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md)

### P14 — Renew the problem, not merely the amount of content

Repeated play stays different when a familiar vocabulary meets a changed situation, relationship, constraint or interpretation. A different map, draft, role, opponent or self-chosen goal can matter more than another tier of numerically larger objects. Ending a solved activity can also be preferable to extending it.

Balatro, Slay the Spire and Against the Storm make offers and context affect the next plan. Rain World's different bodies reinterpret a known world; Warcraft II changes strategic problems through map arrangements; Mass Effect Infiltrator separates surviving a room from mastering style, time and health goals. [G42–G45](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G95, G106, G123](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** random deprivation is not automatically interesting variation. Replaying social preparation, leveling reserves or revisiting a long known route can become a tax on the genuinely new part. New Game Plus, alternate starts, rematches and saved designs should preserve the right work without assuming everything must persist. [G64, G76](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G108, G124, G147](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

### P15 — Make leaving, returning and finishing trustworthy

An interruption is not automatically an in-fiction failure. A player should understand what persists, what expires, what can be recovered, and whether a return still supports the activity they valued. Completing a finite goal can be success rather than churn requiring repair.

PEAK's save/interrupt distinctions, Old School RuneScape's crop behavior and Cataclysm's persistent unfinished work offer different ways to avoid unnecessary attendance burdens. Outer Wilds supplies finite resolution; Bannerlord supplies voluntary retirement; Core Keeper lets an automated project finish. [G34, G46–G47, G49](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G115, G122](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

**Counterweight:** shared worlds and chosen timed challenges cannot always pause every consequence. Make that contract explicit rather than presenting it as universal necessity. In live games, a saved character is not necessarily a preserved build, and a saved design is not an unchanged world. Service support, version changes and creator dependencies affect the credibility of long-term investment. [G11, G13–G15](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [G131, G147](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence-g81-g148.md)

### P16 — Treat rewards and retention measures as evidence to interpret, not ends in themselves

Randomness, status, completion and scheduled occasions can provide real excitement, identity or coordination. They can also substitute repeated checking and obligation for worthwhile play. The relevant distinction is the complete activity and the person's motive—not an automatic judgment that all randomness is gambling, all rewards are manipulation or all routine is compulsion.

Balatro uses uncertain offers within decisions; Bee Swarm combines rerolls with planning; Fire Emblem Heroes separates a compact tactical game from its acquisition economy. The transcript's Grow a Garden account describes a cost of leaving that session length alone cannot distinguish from enthusiasm. Its Sol's RNG account is one person's weak response, not proof of every participant's motives. [G42, G65](../references/fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) · [Roblox study](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md)

**Design position:** do not optimize unwanted persistence. Evaluate pressure from limited access, repeated uncertain acquisition, social obligation and threatened losses alongside the value offered. Platform discovery, creator compensation, sales and player satisfaction are related but different outcomes. Neither a popularity peak nor a published incentive establishes that the underlying experience is good. The source study also keeps Roblox's media policy separate from unverified claims about a particular takedown. [Roblox dossier](../dossiers/roblox.md) · [policy/source boundary](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md)

## 4. What changed when the Roblox account met the wider corpus

The narrator's most useful discovery is that a recognizable income loop, attractive feedback and an invested community did not reliably predict their own enjoyment. Their later praise moves toward player strategy, social improvisation and authored surprise. The corpus supports investigating those possibilities, while qualifying five stronger conclusions:

| Tempting conclusion | Better-supported research position |
| --- | --- |
| More frequent rewards make a game better. | Examine the activity between rewards and what the reward changes. Disproportionate catch-up can remove play. |
| Maximum creative freedom is the answer. | Meaningful participation can come from chosen methods, authored roles, expressive arrangement, observation or surprising story. Raise a Floppa itself qualifies the sandbox claim. |
| Losing valuable things makes players return. | Loss can create learning and purpose, or avoidance and regret. Match stakes, information and recovery to the intended experience. |
| All waiting is bad; eliminate every pause. | Travel, anticipation, reflection and a comfortable routine can be valuable. Empty checks and repeated solved work are different. |
| The most addictive game is the most successful design. | Separate enjoyment, appreciation, chosen return and unwanted persistence. Successful closure may produce less playtime, not more. |

These are our cross-case interpretations, not claims that the video experimentally demonstrated them. Historical firsts, dopamine measurements, universal brain adaptation and exact audience/decline figures are not adopted without adequate evidence. [Complete transcript study and limitations](../references/roblox-addictiveness-video.md)

## 5. OpenLegend: hypotheses worth testing, not approved features

The corpus does not imply that OpenLegend should collect every mechanic. A useful initial test is a small, coherent experience in which several forms of participation reinforce one another. The following constructed examples are **proposals for research**, not statements about existing implementation or confirmed player outcomes.

**One worthwhile situation before an enormous world.** Give a player a concrete purpose—help someone reach a place before an in-world danger arrives—with a few understandable approaches. A repaired route, negotiated assistance and a prepared expedition should differ in cost and consequence. Success should visibly change something the player cared about. Test P03–P07 before assuming more locations solve weak participation.

**A companion who matters in action and memory.** A helper might carry an awkward object, recognize a route or refuse a request for an understandable reason. Later acknowledgment should refer to what actually happened. Test whether usefulness and a specific callback produce interest; do not assume a larger generated biography, a generic affinity number or simulated offscreen busyness improves the relationship. This applies P10–P11 without requiring an unrestricted social simulation.

**A project with an end and another possible use.** Repairing a boat could open a route, make cooperation useful and leave a recognizable personal place. After completion, let the player enjoy it, depart satisfied or choose another undertaking. Do not immediately invalidate the boat with a superior tier. Test whether acquisition, use and remembrance connect before adding another reward ladder. This applies P05, P08 and P15.

**Delegation that preserves the intended role.** Once routine hauling is understood, a helper or saved work order might perform it. The participant should still control the project and consequential choices unless they deliberately choose an author/manager role. Test whether automation removes repetition or removes purpose. Separate a generated description of success from an actual persistent result. This applies P04 and P13.

**A setback with a legible next move.** A failed crossing could cost supplies or require a rescue while preserving the project's identity. The player needs to know why it happened and what might work next. Interrupting for dinner should not silently be treated as choosing that fictional risk. Test both the dramatic value and the owner's willingness to continue; do not assume a retellable disaster was welcome. This applies P12 and P15.

No example commits the project to these particular boats, companions, timers or rules. The reusable proposal is to test an intelligible connection between activity, consequence, enduring value and freely chosen continuation.

## 6. How to evaluate a prototype without mistaking attendance for quality

This is a proposed research procedure, not a validated psychological instrument. Keep the initial questions short, obtain appropriate consent, avoid collecting unnecessary personal information, and do not turn player interviews into diagnosis.

### Before and during play

Establish what the participant hopes to do and their familiarity with the genre. Observe the first understandable goal, first meaningful action, first useful feedback and first opportunity to form a different plan. Record confusion separately from execution difficulty. When a player repeats something, determine whether they are practicing, enjoying a routine, testing a hypothesis, waiting, or performing known work to reach something else.

Capture specific incidents rather than only averages: an upgrade changed a route; a surprise invited an experiment; a companion did something useful; a menu obscured a known solution; a reward removed the challenge; a timer postponed a planned departure. Classify these against the linked dossier examples, while permitting the participant to contradict our interpretation.

### At a natural stop and afterward

Ask which moments were enjoyable, which felt worthwhile, which they would remove, and what they would choose next. Ask whether they continued longer than intended and what made stopping easy or difficult. A later reflection can distinguish satisfaction with the experience from excitement during its last reward reveal. None of these answers alone establishes a disorder.

Report behavioral measures—session length, return, completion, repeated failures and purchases where relevant—alongside these accounts. Define the population and window. Do not combine them into a universal “fun score,” or infer that the person who finished and left disliked the game.

### Compare a change against the problem it claims to solve

| Suspected problem | Informative comparison | What must remain distinct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unclear rules | Better action feedback or prerequisite explanation. | Teaching the contract versus revealing a discovery's answer. |
| Repetitive labor | Saved configuration, batching or delegated execution. | Removing repeated execution versus removing valued authorship. |
| Punishing retries | Closer restart, optional recovery or an isolated practice encounter. | Reducing learning cost versus removing the skill being learned. |
| Hollow catch-up | Staged access with coherent power/capacity and a retained learning sequence. | Skipping obsolete repetition versus erasing the next interesting problem. |
| Attendance pressure | A safe interruption or non-expiring claim window while retaining the activity. | A wanted shared occasion versus a penalty for absence. |
| Late-game stagnation | A changed context or an explicit satisfying conclusion. | More content versus a genuinely different reason to participate. |

Choose comparisons appropriate to the experience; do not introduce a harmful pressure mechanic merely to test whether it raises retention. Compare novice and expert accounts, solo and group contexts, and different motivations where relevant. A builder's valued routine may be a combat-focused player's tax. No dossier supplies a universal optimum for upgrade timing, difficulty, session duration or loss severity.

## 7. Coverage, confidence and maintenance

All 148 subject records contributed a positive possibility, a counterexample, a boundary or a useful absence. Coverage is not an assertion that every subject appears by name in the sixteen principles or supports every principle. The evidence map is the complete audit trail; the body selects representative comparisons rather than becoming another 148-entry catalogue.

| Evidence group | How it informs the synthesis |
| --- | --- |
| G01–G20 | Action, social contests, creator platforms, meaningful tools and the distinction between proposed and delivered systems. |
| G21–G40 | Persistent projects, systemic discovery, authored/generated stories, creative authority and knowledge progression. |
| G41–G60 | Bounded randomness, collection, return schedules, RPG identity, cooperation and recovery. |
| G61–G80 | Tactical contracts, care, world rhythm, role development and progression that changes actions. |
| G81–G100 | Fixed and flexible roles, automation, readable timing, MMO commitments and world-scale activities. |
| G101–G120 | Relationships, finite closure, political consequence, access alternatives and expedition economics. |
| G121–G140 | Company/dynasty continuity, physical worlds, knowledge, risk, survival configuration and authoring. |
| G141–G148 | Labor reduction, preparation, persistence boundaries, coherent fantasy and the costs of routine. |
| Dragon Age supplement | Smaller games and historical services that distinguish tactical value, commercial waiting, authored identity and preservation limits. |

The strongest conclusion is not an effect-size ranking. It is a set of distinctions that recur across unlike cases: action versus reward; knowledge versus stats; ownership versus usable capability; preparation versus maintenance; authored purpose versus free method; consequence versus error; repeated context versus repeated content; social contribution versus social gate; desired return versus costly departure.

**Remaining uncertainty:** the corpus was selected for game inspiration, not randomly sampled; editions and audiences differ; testimonials and developer intentions have different evidentiary strength; many titles share design lineages; long-term clinical and commercial outcomes were not measured here. Cross-case agreement generates better questions, not proof of causality. In particular, 148 dossiers are not 148 independent experiments.

When new evidence changes a principle, update this canonical document and its case records together.
