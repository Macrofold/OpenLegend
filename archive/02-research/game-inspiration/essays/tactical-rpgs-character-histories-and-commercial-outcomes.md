# Character histories, tactical clarity and commercial outcomes

[Library](../README.md) · [Five-game completion and evidence limits](../research-progress-tactical-rpgs-2026-10-20.md)

**Research synthesis, October 20, 2026.** These findings are reference material for OpenLegend, not approved gameplay requirements. The five dossiers own detailed mechanics, lore, development, economics, reviews and sources: [Battle Brothers](../dossiers/battle-brothers.md), [Solasta: Crown of the Magister](../dossiers/solasta-crown-of-the-magister.md), [Colony Ship](../dossiers/colony-ship.md), [Battle Chasers: Nightwar](../dossiers/battle-chasers-nightwar.md) and [Dark Deity](../dossiers/dark-deity.md).

## What the supplied video helps us notice

Mike supplied [this RPG compilation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kb9QxBxN5sg) and its transcript, `Pasted text(2).txt`. The complete supplied text was read. Direct YouTube retrieval failed; no footage, timestamps, upload date or channel identity are claimed. Its enthusiastic first-person accounts provide questions to investigate, rather than representative player sentiment or commercial evidence.

The useful common thread is that one result creates another wanted decision: keeping a valued survivor alive, using a new capability, reconsidering the party, investigating another place or returning with a different approach. Calling these games addictive does not explain which decisions remain enjoyable after the novelty wears off.

| Video emphasis | What the research establishes | Useful qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battle Brothers: an ordinary recruit becomes a valued, injured veteran | Recruitment, equipment, chosen advancement, survival and persistent consequences can make a particular person matter. | Attribute/perk advancement is largely chosen at level-up; this is not a general skill-by-use or autonomous personality-development system. |
| Solasta: readable tactical rules, vertical spaces and scavengers | Spatial conditions and clear feedback support learning; delegated loot handling can remove repeated hauling. | The original uses the licensed SRD ruleset and its own content, not every D&D option. Fixed personality flags and authored banter are different from evolving personality. |
| Colony Ship: learning through use and several routes through problems | Relevant practice, earned conduct-based feats, near-death traits and faction decisions can change later possibilities. | Finite opportunities and hidden thresholds can make a plausible roleplaying idea difficult to sustain; not every mechanical bond produces new narrative content. |
| Battle Chasers: Overcharge and complementary characters | Giving ordinary attacks a route to stronger abilities supports an engaging resource rhythm. | Attractive combinations still need affordable experimentation; repeated leveling and slow encounters can undermine the invitation. |
| Dark Deity: injury instead of deleting the character | Grave Wounds preserves the person while imposing a continuing cost. | Some players still reload. Survival and a future the player wants are separate outcomes. |

The source and version qualifications for each row are in the linked dossier. None of the games demonstrates all of OpenLegend's intended independent character behavior.

## Lasting experience should leave a future worth playing

**Synthesis:** Battle Brothers is the strongest reference here for attachment to a life accumulated during play. Colony Ship is the strongest complementary reference for explicit changes earned through actions. Dark Deity supplies a useful counterexample: a gentler death rule can still feel like damage to an intended character.

The Battle Brothers developers explicitly connected permanent injuries with retaining important fighters and making survivors recognizable. That is stronger evidence of design intent than the video's anecdote, but it does not prove that every player enjoys the result. ([Developer injury account](https://battlebrothersgame.com/dev-blog-79-progress-update-injury-mechanics/); [dossier](../dossiers/battle-brothers.md).)

A useful distinction for future design discussions is:

| What changes | What can make it meaningful | How it can disappoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | An experience changes what the person can attempt or how they approach it. | An unexplained numerical loss makes the person simply less useful. |
| Knowledge | The person learns something usable in another situation. | A decorative diary entry never influences a decision. |
| Relationships | People who experienced or learned of an event may interpret it differently. | Everyone somehow knows, or one universal relationship score replaces distinct reasons. |
| Responsibilities | New circumstances create credible opportunities to help, withdraw, teach, explore or pursue another aim. | The game automatically assigns a new occupation or reduces the person to compulsory camp work. |
| Self-understanding | The person can reconsider their experience in light of values and later events. | One wound mechanically installs cowardice, revenge or gratitude. |

These are analytical distinctions, not a proposal for five new stored systems. Existing OpenLegend owners already distinguish [authored identity, lived experience and changing personality](../../../../docs/projects/compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#character-authorship-and-changing-personality). The research should help evaluate their delivered play, not create a second character-history mechanism.

**Hypothetical OpenLegend example:** a person survives an encounter because another resident returns to help. The injury changes what is physically comfortable; the rescue is something the participants actually experienced. During recovery, the person might practice a different weapon, help prepare equipment, seek treatment, accompany a less demanding journey, or insist on returning to the original activity. Later, the two may disagree about whether the rescue was loyalty, recklessness or repayment of an old obligation. A recovered person may return to their former role. The world supplies capabilities, circumstances and remembered facts; it should not force a predetermined emotional arc.

The payoff is a recognizable history that can matter in another adventure. A scar can help communicate it, but the scar alone is not that history. Nor should an injury have to grant a compensating advantage to justify preserving the person. Treatment, adaptation, accommodation, unchanged valued relationships and noncombat aspirations are also possible continuations.

Positive experiences deserve equal attention. Successfully navigating a difficult route, making a useful tool, saving someone time, earning trust, finishing a beautiful shelter or discovering a shared interest can change later play. If injury is the most reliable way to become interesting, players may seek harmful optimization while ordinary competence feels narratively empty.

## Independent people change the transfer

The mercenary-company comparison has a limit: OpenLegend's residents are intended to have purposes beyond the player's preferred roster. Their willingness to risk themselves, accept assistance or change responsibilities cannot be assumed from their utility to the player. A system that invites the player to retain an injured person solely because they still supply a bonus would miss that distinction.

Colony Ship's companion evidence is particularly useful here. Its documented bonds have concrete benefits but do not automatically create new companion questlines or banter. A player/developer discussion about Faythe shows how a chosen identity can conflict with later permitted conduct. The developer's explanation identifies the cost of handling that contradiction consistently across different kinds of violence. ([Bond limits](https://steamcommunity.com/app/648410/discussions/0/3887227420042385244/); [Faythe discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/648410/discussions/0/4034725629420334730/).)

**Implication:** coherent character evolution depends on the connection between what happened, what a person knows, what they believe about it and what they can choose next. More generated dialogue does not by itself make that connection reliable. Different observers may hold different accounts, and an interpretation may change without rewriting the underlying event. Those boundaries already belong to OpenLegend's character and memory specifications.

## Make choices inviting, understandable and worth repeating

The five games suggest complementary strengths rather than a common template to clone.

| Question for an actual OpenLegend activity | Supporting comparison | What would count against the idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the player see why an approach succeeded or failed? | Solasta makes spatial and rules interactions central to learning. | Information arrives after an irreversible commitment, or presentation reveals facts the character could not know. |
| Does an improvement enable a wanted action? | Battle Chasers and Dark Deity make a new combination or promotion an immediate reason to try another encounter. | The improvement only compensates for rising upkeep or another compulsory grind. |
| Can a specialist contribute without becoming universally competent? | Colony Ship allows party members and alternative approaches to cover different needs. | One character must maximize every skill, or every choice has one dominant solution disguised by wording. |
| Does repetition retain a decision after the player learns an efficient method? | Changing mercenaries and encounters can renew Battle Brothers; reviews of Nightwar show how familiar encounters can instead become delay. | More procedural rearrangement leaves the same answer and more walking. |
| Does convenience preserve the interesting constraint? | Solasta's scavenging and Nightwar's later rebalancing distinguish logistics or grind from tactical challenge. | Automation removes the expedition's meaningful risk, or realism adds chores without payoff. |

These questions connect to the existing [playable-game priorities](../../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md), which already require useful invention, independent people, challenge, recovery and reasons to continue. They do not promote elaborate injury simulation, universal classes, hundreds of traits or a new maintenance system into prerequisites.

The positive opportunity is a small number of activities whose consequences cross into other activities. A difficult journey can produce knowledge useful in building; a practical invention can help a willing companion; that cooperation can change a later encounter. New characters need more than different adjectives: they can notice different possibilities, care about different outcomes and contribute in ways the player recognizes. That is a hypothesis to examine in play, not evidence that a particular trait count will make the game compelling.

## Commercial outcome is relative to costs and ambitions

The research does **not** establish that these five games are commercial failures. It establishes different audiences, different financial visibility and, for Colony Ship, an explicit shortfall against the desired scale of the next project.

| Game | Strongest relevant reported evidence | Appropriate conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battle Brothers | Early Access enabled the small team to work full time; subsequent expansions, ports and publishing support show continued product investment. | A durable specialist product. Lifetime profit and studio receipts remain undisclosed. |
| Solasta | Tactical Adventures reported more than **1.5 million players** in December 2024. | Substantial reach; players are not a disclosed paid-copy total. |
| Colony Ship | The developer says sales were good for its niche but could not fund the larger planetfall sequel. | Product demand and desired studio expansion diverged. This is a concrete funding constraint, not proof that the whole game lost money. |
| Battle Chasers | Its director reported PC leading copies and the later Switch version exceeding the other consoles. | Evidence of an audience across platforms; no disclosed total or profit result. |
| Dark Deity | A publisher/developer announcement reported more than **100,000 copies** by November 2022, beyond the director's initial expectations. | A real paying audience and favorable comparison with stated early expectations. Later player-reach claims do not multiply those sales. |

The dossiers preserve source dates, attribution and scope. For direct reading: [Solasta's studio account](https://www.solasta-game.com/news/180-meet-the-tactical-adventures-team), [Colony Ship's February 2025 plans](https://store.steampowered.com/oldnews/?appgroupname=Colony+Ship%3A+A+Post-Earth+Role+Playing+Game&appids=648410&feed=steam_community_announcements&l=english), [Nightwar's director interview](https://nintendoeverything.com/battle-chasers-nightwar-console-sales-were-the-best-on-switch-raced-past-ps4-and-xbox-one/) and [Dark Deity's attributed sales announcement](https://finalweapon.net/2022/11/18/dark-deity-epic-games-store/).

### What the revenue models can and cannot tell us

The dossiers also record available third-party Steam estimates, because scale matters to the question. Their observation dates, covered products, assumptions and confidence differ. Some combine ownership estimates with generic pricing adjustments; free claims, keys, discounts, regional prices and promotional access complicate the conversion into spending. Modeled net receipts still are not the studio's actual payout or profit. The calculations are neither financial disclosures nor independent evidence that a feature caused sales.

The useful economic comparison is therefore **distinctive appeal, cost to deliver it, friction that prevents experiencing it, and the scale the developer needs to sustain**. Review complaints can identify a plausible source of lost value. Without conversion data or a credible comparison, they cannot tell us how many additional copies a fix would have sold. Likewise, the existence of a sequel does not publish the original game's accounts.

The supplied video's explicit below-expectations example, **SteamWorld Heist II**, is independently supported by Thunderful's [Q3 2024 report](https://thunderfulgroup.com/media/bmfce0rk/thunderful-group-interim-report-q3-2024.pdf) and [presentation](https://thunderfulgroup.com/media/pvgbxmio/thunderful-group-q3-2024-presentation.pdf). This is a limited commercial comparison, not a sixth full dossier. The publisher's disappointment alongside critical acclaim illustrates why quality, discoverability, cost and expectations must be considered separately; it does not establish a single cause or a lifetime profit figure.

## Production lessons worth retaining

**Protect the central pleasure while reducing the cost of reaching it.** Nightwar's team reduced grind while preserving tactical difficulty. This suggests examining travel, preparation, menus, inactive-companion catch-up and repeated animations together with combat. A demanding choice can be worthwhile; time spent reaching an already-known answer needs a different justification.

**Build enough variety to make the choices distinct.** Dark Deity's developer retrospectively identified the costs of weak maps, unclear calculations, uneven balance and a very large writing commitment. More classes and conversations did not automatically produce better encounters or stronger key scenes. ([June 2023 retrospective](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?enddate=1686157224&feed=steam_community_announcements).) OpenLegend's distinctive promise is more likely to be demonstrated by a few coherent people in meaningful situations than by a large cast whose behavior fails to reflect what happened.

**Reusable systems can support more authored adventure.** Solasta's campaigns and Battle Brothers' maintained mod communities offer evidence of people making further use of the underlying games. Their dossiers distinguish official editors from community code modification, dependency management and support burden. This supports examining reusable world content, not treating another game's executable mod access or compatibility arrangements as an OpenLegend policy.

**A completed adventure can be successful.** Some players want to finish a finite campaign and leave satisfied; others want a fresh company, a different build or another community campaign. Returning after a setback, restarting and continuing forever are different experiences. None should be inferred from a high review score or the narrator's late-night enthusiasm.

The main research question to retain is practical: **after an important event, does this particular person have understandable, worthwhile things to do—and does their history meaningfully affect those possibilities?** The answer should be observable in play, with room for both continuity and change.
