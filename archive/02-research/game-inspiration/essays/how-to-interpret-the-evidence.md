## 1. How to interpret the evidence

### Success is not one variable

Separate six outcomes:

| Outcome | What would establish it | What does not establish it |
|---|---|---|
| Moment-to-moment enjoyment | Observed play, player testimony, repeated voluntary engagement | A large possibility space or impressive technology |
| Sustained appeal | Returning players, durable communities, repeat purchases/content interest | One launch spike |
| Cultural reach | Sharing, recognizability, creator coverage, discussion | Sales alone |
| Commercial success | Revenue relative to costs, durable cash generation | Positive reviews or high concurrent-player peaks alone |
| Creator viability | Reach, reuse, earnings or other valued rewards relative to effort | A theoretically powerful editor |
| Production sustainability | Ability to support and improve the product within resources | A devoted but economically insufficient audience |

A game can succeed on several axes and disappoint on others. Dreams’ support decision is not evidence that its creations lacked artistic value. Spore is not a clean example of universal dislike. The failure of Ultima Online’s original ecological subsystem is not the failure of Ultima Online. A successful finite game need not retain its launch audience forever. ### Evidence hierarchy and limits

The research favors official game descriptions, developer interviews/retrospectives, dated corporate disclosures, and original criticism. The expanded source register explains what each source can and cannot support. Store descriptions establish features; their promotional claims are not independent validation. Steam review summaries are self-selected reception snapshots, not representative player surveys or sales estimates.

Where a case says **“my interpretation”**, it means a reasoned design explanation rather than a documented marketing experiment. In particular, a game’s suitability for streaming is not proof that streaming caused a particular share of its sales. No private cohort data, comparable development budgets, or channel-attribution dataset was available.

The four anchor families and newly expanded requested comparisons receive deeper treatment. The remaining cases are deliberately selected to test different hypotheses, not to claim that all products have comparable economics. Some are mass-market successes; some are strongly received specialist games; some are useful counterexamples whose commercial outcome is not established here.

### What was added in the second research pass

This expanded edition adds deeper creator-history, qualitative reception and growth analysis for the requested non-FPS comparisons, plus adjacent breakout cases. It retains the earlier cautionary cases and product tests rather than replacing them with a popularity list.

New review evidence is **purposive qualitative sampling**: accessible most-helpful Steam positive/negative listings, original critic reviews, developer interviews and selected discussions. It is not a random sample, a sentiment classifier over thousands of reviews, a cross-language survey or an estimate of how common any complaint is. No game was played as part of this research.

The [review evidence notebook](../references/review-evidence.md) names the source, period, theme and limitation. Long reviews that praised a game while criticizing particular systems were especially useful. One-line jokes, brand-war statements and unsupported numerical claims were not treated as design evidence. A reviewer’s displayed present playtime is not necessarily their playtime when the review was first posted.

Coverage depth is unequal and disclosed. Hades II, Project Zomboid, Stardew, Terraria, Diablo IV and Palworld have directly inspected player-review material. Other cases rely more on original criticism or creator accounts. Fortnite, PEAK and Animal Crossing are less complete on directly accessible player-complaint evidence. Several current sales, profit and age breakdowns were not public or could not be independently retrieved; “unavailable” is preferable to inventing a comparable statistic.

Historical reviews are tied to their versions. A 2018 League re-review is not an audit of 2026 balance; a Hades II early-access complaint may have been addressed; a July 2026 Zomboid stable release changes the interpretation of earlier unstable-build discussion. Current review approval and earlier criticism can coexist without contradiction.

The original 55-source pass is retained with its September 22 access notes. New sources and dated captures are labeled September 23. The source register documents actual access and distinguishes full articles, accessible excerpts, developer abstracts and secondary references. It does not claim every external link in a bibliography was independently read.

### Key versus less important

“Less important” means **not a prerequisite for the specific value being discussed**. It does not mean worthless. Music, art direction, authored details, accessibility, and character expression can be decisive—even when they are not mechanically necessary. Kasavin’s comments on Hades explicitly warn against removing every apparently unnecessary detail in pursuit of a purely functional game. [HD2](../references/sources-01.md#source-hd2)
