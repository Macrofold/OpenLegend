## 7. Virality and discovery

### 7.1 Design a shareable unit

Different games naturally produce different objects of attention:

| Shareable unit | Illustrative cases | OpenLegend possibility |
|---|---|---|
| An impressive construction | Minecraft, Satisfactory | A visible, working invention or transformed settlement |
| A surprising causal story | RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, Kenshi | A promise, unexpected collaboration, rescue, or invention backfire |
| A social reversal | Among Us, Lethal Company | A disagreement caused by partial knowledge or conflicting priorities |
| A character moment | Hades, The Sims, Wildermyth | A specific remembered consequence involving a recognizable NPC |
| A reusable playable artifact | Roblox, Garry’s Mod, Dreams | A mechanic plus an example scene another creator can adopt |
| A discoverable system interaction | Noita, Qud | A supported combination with understandable causes and limitations |

These are analytical categories, not measured channel rankings. Minecraft’s documented YouTube milestone supplies unusually direct evidence of cultural scale; most cases do not offer comparable attribution data. [MC3](../references/sources-01.md#source-mc3)

A useful sharing unit is comprehensible without a long preamble. “This character remembered that I abandoned her friend” is stronger than “our cognition pipeline now supports a larger memory schema.” A working demonstration of a reusable mechanic is stronger than a screenshot of generated JSON.

### 7.2 Separate five steps

**Encounter → understand → care → participate → return.**

A clip may achieve the first three while the actual game fails at participation. A creator tool may enable participation while offering little reason to return. A good game may retain a small audience but be difficult to explain to new people.

For each step, ask a separate question:

- What makes a stranger stop and watch?
- Can they understand why the event matters?
- Do they imagine themselves doing something in that world?
- Is joining, installing, or beginning straightforward?
- Does ordinary play deliver the promise after the clip ends?

Do not optimize only the first step. An exceptional demo that cannot be reproduced in normal play creates expectation debt.

### 7.3 Familiar frame, surprising payoff

A recognizable frame lowers the explanation cost: build a shelter, rescue a friend, return with supplies, fulfill a promise. The unusual interaction supplies the surprise.

For OpenLegend, the novel part should often be **how the world and characters respond**, not a completely unfamiliar interface or ten-minute explanation of the ontology. A familiar premise gives the player enough knowledge to recognize why a result is remarkable.

This is a design recommendation, not proof that familiar premises always outperform novel ones. Qud is an important counterweight: strong strangeness can be part of the appeal when the world supports it coherently. [QU1](../references/sources-01.md#source-qu1)

### 7.4 Creator ecosystems have several feedback loops

Do not collapse these into “network effects”:

**Player loop:** good experience → return → invite others.

**Creator loop:** achievable creation → useful feedback/audience → improvement → another creation.

**Reuse loop:** understandable artifact → compatible adoption → meaningful variation → credit and further discovery.

**Platform loop:** enough appealing content → better matching → player satisfaction → demand for more content.

Roblox’s discovery documentation illustrates the importance of player engagement and repeated participation. Dreams’ support decision shows that a valued creation environment alone does not guarantee an indefinitely sustainable platform. [RB2](../references/sources-01.md#source-rb2) [DR1](../references/sources-01.md#source-dr1)

OpenLegend should prove a small reuse loop locally before building marketplace economics. A second creator successfully modifying and using a mechanic is better evidence than a large catalogue of generated artifacts nobody runs.

### 7.5 A spike can expose the product’s weak point

Innersloth’s 2020 announcement described server work consuming development time during a large influx. Worlds Adrift’s founder described a different trap: limiting sales to protect infrastructure also constrained buzz. These are specific accounts, not universal growth laws. [AM1](../references/sources-01.md#source-am1) [WA1](../references/sources-01.md#source-wa1)

The actionable lesson is to know the supported launch envelope. OpenLegend should have a clear single-world experience and operating limits before promising massive shared populations. Fast code generation does not remove support, reliability, moderation, or onboarding obligations.

### Delayed discovery is possible, but not a strategy to assume

Among Us demonstrates that a product can find a much larger audience well after release. YouTube’s account also shows that creators need not merely record the game; they can turn its recognizable characters and situations into new cultural material. [AM3](../references/sources-02.md#source-am3) My recommendation is to enable understandable, reusable sharing units while keeping the business viable without waiting for a rediscovery miracle. “Keep developing until a streamer finds us” is not an evidence-based operating plan.

### 7.6 Virality should be optional to viability

A sustainable specialist audience may be a better initial target than a cultural phenomenon. A premium single-player game, a cooperative world service, and a creator marketplace have different cost structures and acquisition requirements. Do not require Minecraft-scale discovery or Roblox-scale participation for the first business model to function.

For OpenLegend, avoid designing an operating model that needs a breakout before the core experience can improve. That is a strategic recommendation informed by the cautionary cases, not a financial forecast.

### 7.7 Eight growth mechanisms—not one “viral game” formula

The table separates observed evidence from proposed mechanisms. A product can use several mechanisms, and the public record rarely isolates their individual financial contribution.

| Mechanism | Strong comparison | Evidence or interpretation | OpenLegend application |
|---|---|---|---|
| A personally made artifact | Minecraft / Terraria | Large documented creator culture; reviews describe return projects and friends | A working invention or changed settlement, understandable without an engine lecture |
| A retellable consequence | RimWorld / Zomboid | Player stories center on characters, mistakes and practical chains | Show what happened and why; do not make bugs the source of surprise |
| A recognizable emotional cast | Hades / BG3 | Developer/critic focus on specific characters and reactions | Authored identity plus selective memory-driven behavior |
| A playable demo with combinations | Balatro | Creator diary links demo iterations, creators and growing wishlists | Let visitors try the real invention/decision loop, not only watch generation |
| A scheduled return cohort | PoE / large updates | Developer account of release cadence; repeat peaks | Optional communal projects or scenario seasons without mandatory world erasure |
| A low-friction group predicament | Among Us / PEAK / Lethal Company | Simple social situations and major growth; exact channel weights unknown | A friend can join an intelligible shared problem quickly |
| A familiar hybrid with a new reward | Palworld / Core Keeper / Against the Storm | Clear combinations described by creators/critics | Pitch the resulting activity, not “AI plus sandbox” |
| A platform discovery and social loop | Roblox / Fortnite | Company/platform accounts and dated reach | Prove consumer value and reuse before marketplace scale |

### 7.8 Cohort return is not uninterrupted retention

A premium game can create value, reach a satisfying ending and remain successful while its concurrency declines. A new patch can bring back groups without converting all of them into daily users. A live-service game can have intentionally cyclical participation. A platform can transfer attention among experiences while one title falls.

For OpenLegend, decide what return means. Is the player returning to a person and place, beginning a new scenario, meeting friends for an occasion, or trying a new reusable construct? These can coexist, but each needs a clear promise. Do not optimize a daily-attendance metric if the best intended experience is an occasional meaningful expedition.

### 7.9 A more honest acquisition test

Record the sequence: where a participant first heard about the game, what they expected, what made them actually try it, what they did during the first session, and why they returned or did not. This separates attraction from delivery. A funny clip, a recommendation and a sale can all touch the same purchase; last-click attribution alone will not explain the product’s appeal.

A demo should contain the distinctive behavior often enough that it is not a lottery. If your marketing shows a remembered social consequence, the ordinary newcomer should have a realistic path to one. If it shows modular invention, trying a supported variation should not require developer assistance.
