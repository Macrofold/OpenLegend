## 11. Prioritized study itinerary

**Updated short list for OpenLegend:** Palworld for useful autonomous inhabitants; Project Zomboid for consequence and partial knowledge; RimWorld for particular people under pressure; Hades for selective continuity; Balatro/Noita for reusable combinations; Core Keeper for top-down home/exploration; Stardew for multiple motives and social-reciprocity gaps. Study Roblox/Fortnite primarily when validating discovery and reuse, not as reasons to postpone the default game.

This order is a design judgment based on relevance, not a sales ranking or a request to buy/play every title. The table below retains more specific observation prompts.


This is a recommended learning order, not a requirement to finish every game. The observation prompts are more important than collecting hours.

| Priority | Study | What to watch for | Question to bring back to OpenLegend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RimWorld | A colonist difference changes a practical decision; an event creates aftermath | Which three character differences actually matter in our default world? |
| 2 | Hades | A repeated encounter acknowledges specific history; failure produces something new | Where can our game make a player feel selectively remembered? |
| 3 | Minecraft | A personally chosen project grows from a simple need | Are we preserving the player’s authorship or automating it away? |
| 4 | Wildermyth | An authored situation produces a distinctive persistent consequence | What reusable situations could support our generated characters? |
| 5 | Scribblenauts | An open-language solution is delightful, trivial, or frustrating | Which constraints keep our invention system interesting? |
| 6 | Factorio or Satisfactory | A visible bottleneck leads to an improvement the player understands | Can players explain why their invention works and how to improve it? |
| 7 | Caves of Qud | A different capability changes exploration or problem-solving | Are our organisms and senses mechanically different or merely descriptive? |
| 8 | Valheim and Stardew Valley | Preparation, home, return, and quiet progress | What makes our world worth inhabiting between dramatic moments? |
| 9 | Roblox and Dreams | Discover an appealing creation as a non-creator; inspect reuse paths | Is the creator ecosystem serving players as well as authors? |
| 10 | Outer Wilds | A piece of knowledge changes a future action | Are our mysteries useful discoveries or just hidden information? |
| 11 | Kenshi / Project Zomboid | A setback becomes a choice rather than an inexplicable failure | Which kinds of hardship support our intended audience? |
| 12 | Garry’s Mod / Among Us / Lethal Company | A short playable convention creates group variation | Can one simple OpenLegend situation generate many social outcomes? |

Pair the play study with three short readings: the Worlds Adrift founder retrospective, the Dreams support announcement, and the EverQuest Next cancellation statement. They keep tool admiration, community affection, technical capability, and sustainable integrated fun from being conflated. ### Final recommendation

Build the default OpenLegend experience around **particular people, useful inventions, understandable consequences, and a place worth returning to**. Let the engine grow as the substrate for those experiences.

The ambition of reusable mechanics, unusual organisms, new senses, and player-authored realities is not the problem. The recurring risk is mistaking a growing space of technically expressible possibilities for a growing supply of enjoyable decisions.

The strongest long-term version of OpenLegend is not simply a world that can answer any request. It is a world that gives players reasons to care what they ask, reasons to choose among answers, and consequences worth remembering.
