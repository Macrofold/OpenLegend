# Deadfire: expedition, relationship and recovery rules

**G55 operational supplement — September 27, 2026.** Read with the [full Deadfire dossier](../dossiers/pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire.md). This supplies the R02/R04/R06 explanations identified in the [coverage audit](../references/games-51-60-coverage-audit.md#g55--pillars-of-eternity-ii-deadfire), with additional R05 examples. The original ten cases, reviews, expansion analyses and production research remain in their existing owner. [Progress](../research-progress.md) owns task state; this is researched gameplay, not an accepted OpenLegend specification.

Scope is the unmodified, post-update Deadfire campaign. Original 2018 guides and later rules accounts are differentiated below. No gameplay, video viewing, proprietary code inspection or present console/network test was performed. Named people and objects identify the reference game, not proposed OpenLegend assets.

## 1. A vessel is a loadout and a workplace

Obsidian describes the Defiant as transport, home and a platform for a separate naval combat system. Its March 2017 crowdfunding update made recruitable, assignable crew a specific additional production commitment, not merely decoration aboard a larger map. The released system separates the adventuring party from hired sailors. [E1](#e1) [E2](#e2)

The ship-management interface exposes hull, sails, installed equipment, occupied jobs and stores of ammunition, medicine, repair material, food and drink. The initial Dyrwoodan Sloop can be replaced by vessels with different gun arrangements, crew requirements and maneuver times. More guns therefore do not constitute a free upgrade: they arrive with different staffing and handling requirements. A Voyager's forward/aft armament is not the same firing geometry as a sloop's broadsides. [E3](#e3)

Seven job categories matter in different contexts. A cook reduces provision consumption; a navigator improves travel; cannoneers operate weapons and improve accuracy; a helmsman enables turning; deckhands support movement; the boatswain assists handling ship events; a surgeon helps injured sailors recover. Sailors have role-specific experience, not the protagonist's class progression. Wages, provisions and morale connect a voyage to future resupply rather than disappear when the party leaves a town. [E3](#e3)

**Interpretation:** the useful decision is not just which person has the highest number. It is which tasks must remain staffed simultaneously and who can replace an unavailable specialist. A more expensive hull can worsen a poorly staffed expedition. This is a concrete alternative to treating a ship as a fast-travel skin.

## 2. Naval encounters have their own action economy

On encountering another vessel, the player chooses whether to engage, close to board or attempt escape as the situation permits. The interaction screen changed in the official May 2019 5.0 update; a launch screenshot is not today's exact interface. Selecting the naval sequence enters an alternating command exchange, not an ordinary party battle with ship-shaped characters. [E4](#e4) [E5](#e5)

The principal commands change distance, orientation or staffing: sail ahead at full or half speed, hold position, turn to port/starboard, jibe, fire an available battery, or use **Report to…** to reassign people. A jibe turns the ship around so another battery can face the enemy while the first reloads. That also consumes maneuver time; presenting it as a free extra attack would erase the tradeoff. [E6](#e6)

Weapon range is an accuracy consideration, not a binary guarantee of a hit. Inspecting the enemy's guns can reveal a distance where one's own weapons work better. Holding steady before firing improves accuracy; firing directly after a jibe incurs a penalty in the later rules account. Closing too far can invite boarding and abandon the favorable cannon-range problem the player was trying to solve. [E7](#e7)

**Version correction:** the 2018 Shacknews guide includes **Brace for impact**. The later solipsa guide reports that the player option appears to have been removed, although enemies may still use it. It is retained as historical evidence rather than put into an unqualified current command list. This illustrates why copying the oldest complete-looking guide can introduce a new error while filling a coverage gap. [E6](#e6) [E7](#e7)

### Damage, ammunition and repair are different decisions

Hull depletion sinks a ship; sail damage restricts movement. Cannonballs primarily attack hull, chainshot sails, and grapeshot exposed crew. Special fire weapons have different targeting behavior, so choosing ammunition is not guaranteed control of every gun's target. Damage can also create events such as flooding or fire. [E6](#e6) [E7](#e7)

An injured gunner creates a staffing problem, not simply a smaller ship-health bar. **Report to…** can move a replacement into the station and an injured sailor into the hold for treatment. Personnel assigned to emergency work are unavailable for their previous jobs. Thus continuing to fire, restoring maneuverability and addressing a spreading crisis compete for the same people. [E6](#e6)

The later guide distinguishes in-combat treatment from fully clearing a sailor's injury after the encounter. It also reports event-driven hull/sail repairs with different economics from repairs on the travel map, including an unusual ability to perform emergency repairs without sufficient stock. This is not generalized into a law that repair materials never matter; exact exceptional ratios and exploit-like advice are not prerequisites for the research example. [E7](#e7)

**Interpretation:** a repair order should name what it restores—hull, sails, an occupied job or long-term readiness. A generic repair percentage obscures why an intact vessel can still be unable to perform a useful maneuver. The same principle applies to delegated work: repairing the machine and restoring its operator are different interventions.

### Boarding changes the problem instead of merely finishing the animation

Boarding moves from the naval exchange to a deck fight involving characters. It can let a strong adventuring party substitute for weaker guns, while surrendering the advantage of fighting at range. A displayed sailor count should not be interpreted as an exact prediction of the entire opposing boarding force. Contemporary reports distinguish sailors from defenders and also document bugs around the transition; a May 2018 Obsidian support reply acknowledged one such report. The audit did not reproduce a current boarding transfer or infer a universal spawn formula. [E8](#e8)

Likewise, sinking, boarding and taking spoils should not be assumed to preserve exactly the same state merely because each can end the encounter successfully. The research question is what survives a change of resolution system. Ship combat's separate machinery is a useful reference precisely because it creates this boundary, not because it proves the boundary was always flawless.

## 3. Romance is not synonymous with party approval

The established romantic options are **Aloth, Maia, Tekēhu and Xoti**, with **Serafen's casual encounter** a narrower route. Edér and Pallegina are not alternative romances unlocked by raising the same meter further; sidekicks do not automatically inherit the full companions' relationship scripts. The May 2018 guide describes availability to male and female Watchers while explicitly qualifying its then-unsettled trigger and bug reports. [E9](#e9)

Each route combines conversation choices, relationship state and authored progress. Aloth's *The Painted Masks*, Xoti's responses concerning Edér or Maia, and Maia's interest in how Ishiza is treated supply different contexts. These should not be flattened into “complete any personal quest and romance begins.” The early guide's suggested precise timing for Aloth remains launch-era advice, not a newly verified all-version trigger table. [E9](#e9)

Maia's reference makes the overlap especially tangible: **three Aged Shark Meat for Ishiza** participate in the courtship sequence, while *The Courier's Calling* and discussion of her service shape other relationship and ending states. Maia and Xoti can also form a relationship when the relevant Watcher routes are not pursued. Romance therefore exists alongside relationships between companions, not solely as a reward reserved for the protagonist. [E10](#e10)

Political affiliation is another variable. Maia's position in the Royal Deadfire Company and her response to its methods are not erased by affection. The reference describes conditional opportunities to keep her when choosing a different faction; it does not justify either absolute claim that romance guarantees loyalty or that any contrary faction choice always loses her. [E10](#e10)

**Interpretation:** separate eligibility, interest, chosen intimacy, political commitment and continued availability. A gift-like object can express attention without constituting universal consent. A useful generative adaptation would explain which relationship changed and why; it should not silently convert an affectionate exchange into permission to command someone's entire future.

## 4. Knockout, injury, healing and death

Deadfire does not retain the first game's separate Endurance/Health structure. Ordinary damage reduces health. Reaching zero usually causes a knockout: a suitable ability may return that person to the current fight, or a surviving party can regain them afterward. The knockout also leaves an injury with persistent penalties. Restoring health is not the same as removing that injury. [E11](#e11)

On the ordinary lethal-injury contract, another knockout after three injuries can kill the character permanently. Injuries can also arise through traps or scripted interactions, whose exceptions must not be assumed identical to combat. Specialist documentation identifies easy-mode protection and particular death effects, so “every fourth injury always kills on every setting” is too broad. Milward specifically describes Story/Relaxed substituting Maimed, while the community status reference explicitly documents Story protection and scripted exceptions. These are rule references with stated scope, not newly reproduced edge-case tests. [E12](#e12) [E13](#e13)

Outdoor rest uses food/drink assigned **to each character**. It does not require the first game's generic Camping Supplies. Assigning food to someone else is not sufficient to clear the injured person's condition. An inn provides another recovery route; prepared meals can add benefits as well as recovery. Stocks earmarked for travel and stocks consumed by the party need to be considered together. [E14](#e14) [E15](#e15)

Luminous Adra Potions and the limited-charge Bone Setter's Tarc supply additional injury remedies. Their existence does not turn a normal healing spell into resurrection after permanent death. Nor does a surgeon treating a hired sailor use precisely the adventuring party's food-and-injury system. [E12](#e12)

**Interpretation:** the player is trading immediate capability against future safety. A combat revival can rescue an attempt while increasing the importance of recovery afterward. A fair interface should show both outcomes instead of celebrating restored health while hiding that another knockout has a different consequence. Optional god challenges and Iron modes deliberately alter the contract; they are not evidence for ordinary defaults.

## 5. Four additional constructed cases

These supplement the dossier's original ten examples without renumbering or claiming personal play.

### E01 — an equipment mismatch becomes a distance problem

**Intent:** use the Defiant's guns effectively. **Conditions:** a Dyrwoodan Hog Nose and an Aedyr Channel Gun are installed, with crew and ammunition. The guide lists optimal ranges of 150–400m and 350–600m respectively. **Action:** compare an engagement at 250m with one at 450m. **Result:** neither distance places both guns in their preferred range; moving into their overlap is a different plan from simply buying the gun with the largest damage. **Next choice:** alter distance, replace a gun or accept poorer accuracy. **Limit:** optimal range is not a guarantee of a hit, and the enemy can maneuver too. [E3](#e3)

**Transfer hypothesis:** compatibility can be spatial and temporal, not just a boolean fitting check. A valid collection of components may still lack a good common operating envelope.

### E02 — rescue the station before ordering another broadside

**Intent:** keep fighting after a cannoneer is injured. **Conditions:** a replacement and a usable hold/treatment path exist. **Action:** use Report to… rather than repeatedly select a battery whose operator is unavailable. **Result:** staffing changes can restore the attack while the wounded sailor recovers. **Next choice:** commit another person to an emergency or retain a reserve. **Limit:** emergency assignments consume actual role capacity; the order does not create another sailor. [E6](#e6)

**Transfer hypothesis:** useful error messages identify an unmet dependency, not only a failed output. A worker's state belongs in the explanation of a machine's behavior.

### E03 — a relationship involves someone else's relationship

**Intent:** pursue Maia's courtship. **Conditions:** the relevant authored sequence is available and Ishiza's requirement has been presented. **Action:** acquire the three Aged Shark Meat and continue the conversation rather than merely maximize a generic approval number. **Result:** attention to her companion becomes part of the route. **Next choice:** pursue or decline intimacy and separately respond to her political obligations. **Limit:** supplying the food is one condition, not purchase of permanent loyalty or a complete romance trigger by itself. [E10](#e10)

**Transfer hypothesis:** recognizable care can make an ordinary item meaningful without turning every useful gift into a universal relationship currency.

### E04 — a revived specialist is not necessarily expedition-ready

**Intent:** retain a specialist for the next encounter. **Conditions:** that person survived the current battle with three injuries under a lethal-injury setting. **Action:** compare pressing on with resting and assigning food to that individual. **Result:** recovery changes the consequence of another knockout, not only the current health bar. **Next choice:** spend preparation resources, return to an inn or deliberately accept the risk. **Limit:** easiest difficulties and challenge modifiers require their own contract; the example is not advice for every setting. [E11](#e11) [E14](#e14)

**Transfer hypothesis:** show changes in failure severity before the player commits. Repeated temporary losses can eventually become a qualitatively different outcome.
