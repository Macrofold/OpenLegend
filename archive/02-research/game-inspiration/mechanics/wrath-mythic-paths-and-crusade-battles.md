# Wrath: mythic paths and crusade battles

**G59 operational supplement — September 27, 2026.** Read with the [full Wrath dossier](../dossiers/pathfinder-wrath-of-the-righteous.md). This addresses the [remaining R02–R04 audit findings](../references/games-51-60-coverage-audit.md#g59--pathfinder-wrath-of-the-righteous), with five additional R05 cases. The original ten situations, five reviews, six premium expansions, ordinary character rules and late-path costs remain in their existing owner. [Progress](../research-progress.md) owns task state.

Scope is the computer adaptation, not tabletop mythic rules, Kingmaker's administration or optional rule-changing mods. These are documented operations and conditional design interpretations, not a played campaign, watched footage, inspected proprietary implementation or accepted OpenLegend plan. Mythic and strategic story spoilers are limited to explaining the systems.

## 1. Early paths change the activity, not just its name

The main dossier already distinguishes ordinary levels, story-granted mythic ranks and the late Legend, Devil and Swarm transformations. The six early paths likewise need different operational explanations. Their menus do not imply that every feature is received at rank 3 or that all ten paths have equally long campaigns. The following complements the existing Angel merged/unmerged treatment rather than replacing it.

### Demon: decide when to commit a limited burst

Demonic Rage is a limited daily activation, initially three uses, increasing attack, damage, caster checks and spell difficulty during its window. Later ranks increase its uses and strength. This is not an unlimited permanent stat package or automatically the same per-round pool as a Barbarian's class rage. Demonic Charge supplies teleporting repositioning through a move action, leaving the player to consider what useful action follows arrival. [P1](#p1)

Selected demonic aspects change the burst's properties and the roles it favors. Babau's package includes sneak-attack interactions; other aspects support different strengths. Progression expands which aspects can operate together. The question becomes which encounter warrants the limited transformation and which ordinary class tools remain effective between uses—not merely whether the protagonist selected an evil dialogue label. [P1](#p1)

**Interpretation:** a strong temporary identity can be interesting because its timing is a commitment. The interface should show remaining uses, eligible effects and the post-burst plan instead of implying every challenge is meant to be solved in the empowered state.

### Aeon: alter the rules around a group

An **Enforcing Gaze** can simultaneously benefit nearby allies and impair enemies that fail its saving throw. Attack and caster-level gazes therefore support different party plans. Uses are limited early; later progression expands simultaneous gazes and removes the daily limit at rank 8. A gaze's range and enemy save remain meaningful even when activation becomes easier. [P2](#p2)

**Aeon Bane**, acquired at rank 4, has a separate round budget. It supports weapon and spell effectiveness and can strip magical protection through qualifying attacks; later upgrades deepen that dispelling role. Selecting a gaze is consequently not the same decision as spending Bane. Nor should every dispel be advertised as unconditional removal of every enemy effect. [P2](#p2)

**Interpretation:** support can make a whole composition work differently without replacing its members. A useful explanation should identify the affected people, duration and eligibility rather than crediting every subsequent success to an invisible general aura.

### Azata: choose which form of collaboration to amplify

Azata gains **Aivu**, a dragon companion whose growth follows mythic ranks rather than the protagonist's ordinary experience. Songs supply group benefits, while selected **superpowers** intervene in different rules. Favorable Magic improves the odds on relevant casting checks and worsens enemies' saving outcomes; it does not remove the checks altogether. [P3](#p3)

**Zippy Magic** makes a qualifying single-target spell affect another nearby appropriate creature. Its spell restriction matters: it is not blanket duplication of every spell-like ability or Kinetic Blast. A supportive spell and an offensive spell also need appropriate recipient types. Life-Bonding Friendship instead belongs to the teamwork/survival side of the path, not a stronger version of the same targeting operator. The exact survival arithmetic is unnecessary to establish that distinction and is not guessed here. [P3](#p3)

**Interpretation:** the useful design pattern is choosing an operator that changes a familiar activity—more reliable casting, another recipient, or a different form of mutual support. A companion's visible growth can give that abstract progression an emotional counterpart.

### Trickster: make an existing skill do another kind of work

Mythic Tricks have tiers and limited upgrade opportunities. They are selected additions, not automatic supernatural powers for every skill on the character sheet. **Mobility II** turns a successful check to avoid an opportunity attack into an opportunity to strike back. **Trickery I** substitutes a penalized Trickery check for the caster check in a dispel-like operation; the upgraded version broadens it. Their skill investment and reach remain relevant. [P4](#p4)

**Perception II** opens additional feat choices to the party; it does not automatically award all those feats. **Knowledge (Arcana)** tricks alter qualifying newly identified equipment, making identification part of acquisition rather than permission to rewrite every already-owned object. The path also has sneak-attack progression and its own spells. Its comic tone should not obscure these concrete changes to timing, eligibility and rewards. [P4](#p4)

**Interpretation:** a familiar skill becomes more interesting when its purpose changes. The danger is confusing a defined exception with unlimited author authority: “trick reality” is a fantasy, not an engine command admitting any narrated result.

### Lich: coordinate a different population and repertoire

Lich can merge its spellbook with supported full arcane classes, while an unmerged route remains distinct. Its **Skeletal Champion** begins with a selected combat role and receives later upgrade choices. Lich powers also differentiate builds: **Deadly Magic** provides a limited window for bypassing Spell Resistance, while Death of Elements changes a damage relationship rather than simply increasing a printed number. [P5](#p5)

Negative-energy abilities can harm living creatures and restore undead; the target population therefore changes preparation and support. Selecting the path early is not identical to already having completed the protagonist's later full undead transformation. The main dossier's companion and transformation costs remain part of the identity, not a cosmetic sequel to the spell menu. [P5](#p5)

**Interpretation:** a change of being is stronger when it affects who can help, what recovery works and which tools fit. The system still needs to disclose those differences before a benevolent intervention produces harm.

## 2. The army is not the adventuring party at larger scale

The crusade recruits units into stacks and moves armies along the world map using daily movement. Different unit types occupy different formation slots; adding soldiers to an existing type differs from making room for another type. Normal recruitment, mercenary availability and construction create different supply routes. The recruitment reference records weekly additions and accumulated unpurchased ordinary recruits; waiting is not automatically forfeiting every earlier available soldier. [C1](#c1)

A general initially leads three unit stacks, with **Master of Maneuver** expanding the roster capacity. Attack and Defense support the army's checks; **Power** affects the general's abilities. The general's personal spell **Energy** is not the strategic **Energy Points** spent on buildings and projects. Similar labels conceal different owners and replenishment rules. [C2](#c2)

**Interpretation:** composition involves population, capacity, travel and command. A fourth useful unit type can be impossible to field even when the player can afford its soldiers. A procurement assistant should diagnose the missing command capacity rather than recommend buying more of the wrong resource.

## 3. Battles use a visible turn sequence

Army battles take place on a tactical field with initiative ordering. A stack can move and attack in melee, shoot when its ranged conditions permit, use a supported ability, defend, or wait until later in the round. Speed constrains reach, and nearby enemies can prevent an ordinary ranged attack. These are not the party's standard/move/swift action rules copied unchanged onto bigger tokens. [C3](#c3)

Different bodies and abilities make position matter: a ranged group needs access to its attack, while a durable melee group can occupy a threatening approach. The displayed force strength is not a complete tactical forecast. Lost members can weaken the remaining stack, so preserving a damaging unit can improve both this battle and the next. The contemporary guide describes unit-specific tools such as Clerics' Lay on Hands and Smite rather than one identical attack for every army icon. [C4](#c4)

Once per round, before an allied unit acts, a general can supply an ability or spell. Wizard-type generals spend Energy; other ability families have their own limits. This intervention sits above the unit sequence rather than giving every stack another personal spellbook. The transcribed help describes daily Energy regeneration and a delay before reassigning a general after defeat; losing an army is not permanent death of the general. [C2](#c2)

**Interpretation:** a commander can be useful without personally appearing as another moving unit. However, an exceptionally powerful command spell can reduce the soldiers to bodies waiting for it. That is a balance and pacing question, not a reason to pretend the two action layers are identical.

## 4. Healing in battle and the infirmary are not interchangeable

**Cure Wounds** can restore troops during the engagement. The launch guide recommends delaying a final blow to spend additional healing; that is the author's optimization, not a mandatory or universally enjoyable routine. Spending the general's remaining resource now may preserve a force while constraining subsequent battles. Unit abilities can have different refresh boundaries from general spells. [C4](#c4)

After victory, the general's **infirmary** restores casualties within a limited capacity, prioritizing value rather than promising an identical number of bodies of every type. A win can therefore leave lasting losses. This is why an adequate victory and a sustainable sequence of victories differ; the correct response might be preparation, recovery capacity or postponement rather than another immediate attack. [C2](#c2)

The **Hospital** makes construction relevant to this loop: its documented benefit is **+5% infirmary size for all generals**. It does not simply refill the Commander's personal HP, and the construction takes resources and time. A council choice can likewise increase infirmary capacity instead of general experience or an additional stack slot. These are competing improvements to how the army survives and develops. [C5](#c5) [C6](#c6)

**Interpretation:** loss mitigation is a strategic capability with an owner. The UI should explain whether an apparent recovery came from a battlefield spell, the victory infirmary or later recruitment; those routes imply different future costs.

## 5. Morale operates at two scales

Campaign morale affects the wider crusade's effectiveness and recruitment. Its banners reward continued progress and defense. The Act II march banner and Act III conquest/defense structure are not an identical permanent interface. In Act III, victories extend one banner, taking eligible new enemy forts extends another, and protecting one's forts sustains defense. Reclaiming the same lost fort should not be assumed equivalent to conquering a new one. [C7](#c7)

Combat morale instead concerns a unit's behavior during the tactical battle. The help describes favorable morale enabling another action and poor morale disrupting action. This is not the same statistic as a general's Power or a decree's income. The **Monument** specifically raises the chance of acting twice through positive combat morale, illustrating the difference between improving battlefield behavior and merely adding a campaign-map morale point. [C3](#c3) [C8](#c8)

**Interpretation:** a shared word does not guarantee one shared variable. A useful design makes the relationship explicit without compressing institutional confidence, recruitment and an individual formation's action into one unexplained happiness bar. Historical launch reports of banner bugs remain historical, not proof of today's default behavior.

## 6. Five additional constructed situations

These extend the main dossier's ten cases; none is a new play report.

**W01 — reserve the transformation for a meaningful obstacle.** A Demon has one remaining Rage use and an ordinary encounter before a larger threat. The player compares spending it now with relying on class tools, then considers whether Charge supplies a useful position. The next decision concerns the resource window, not a permanent mode toggle. **Limit:** a strong burst still needs a valid target and a survivable destination. [P1](#p1)

**W02 — a support selection changes which plan works.** An Aeon chooses a gaze suited to the party's next activity and separately decides whether to spend Bane rounds stripping enemy protection. The next choice is to retain the current configuration or adapt to another threat. **Limit:** range, saves and dispelling conditions remain; the path is not an automatic win aura. [P2](#p2)

**W03 — acquisition order matters.** A Trickster invests in the relevant Knowledge (Arcana) trick before personally identifying an eligible enchanted item. The identification produces the supported improvement, rather than a general menu for revising every possession afterward. The next choice is who should use the resulting equipment. **Limit:** the identifier and qualifying item matter; ownership alone does not trigger the operation. [P4](#p4)

**W04 — make room for another contribution.** An army has three useful stacks and access to another type. The player chooses Master of Maneuver rather than simply enlarging an existing stack. The result changes composition capacity, while the extra soldiers must still be recruited and brought into the army. **Limit:** an empty slot supplies no troops and does not eliminate travel. [C2](#c2)

**W05 — a hospital changes the price of the next victory.** A player whose wins leave costly casualties invests in a Hospital, waits for construction and reassesses the next engagement. Its broader infirmary benefit can preserve more force after victory. The next choice is further mitigation or a different recruitment/combat plan. **Limit:** a percentage improvement is not unlimited resurrection and does not guarantee a lost battle becomes recoverable. [C5](#c5)
