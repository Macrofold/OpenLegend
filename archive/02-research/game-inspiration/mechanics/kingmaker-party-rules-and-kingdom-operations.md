# Kingmaker: party rules and kingdom operations

**G58 operational supplement — September 27, 2026.** Read with the [full Kingmaker dossier](../dossiers/pathfinder-kingmaker.md). This supplies the concrete R02–R05 explanations missing from the [manuscript audit](../references/games-51-60-coverage-audit.md#g58--pathfinder-kingmaker). The original nine cases, six publications' reviews, later reassessment, three expansions and production evidence remain in their original owner. [Progress](../research-progress.md) owns completion state.

Scope is Owlcat's Kingmaker, including its native turn-based edition, not tabletop Pathfinder or Wrath's mythic/crusade systems. Sources are versioned rule references and firsthand guides. This is documentary research, not gameplay, a source-code audit or an approved OpenLegend specification. The extra examples are constructed from the cited rules rather than invented sessions.

## 1. A turn is several different permissions

A normal round permits a standard action plus movement, two move actions, or one full-round action; a swift action and eligible free actions are additional categories. Ordinary movement can therefore consume an opportunity that would otherwise support a full attack. A five-foot step is a narrower exception, not an unlimited free move. Some summoning and modified spells also require the longer action category. [K1](#k1)

The practical distinction is between **one attack now** and **all attacks available to the build**. A character with several attacks cannot assume that walking across the battlefield and pressing attack performs the same sequence as remaining in place. Quickened casting uses the swift category rather than creating a second unrestricted turn. These relationships also exist beneath real-time presentation; changing the interface does not import another game's action-point pool. [K2](#k2)

**Interpretation:** the useful preview is the intended complete action, including movement. A command can be valid but achieve less than expected because the actor spent the required opportunity reaching the target. Explain that cost before accepting the move, not afterward through a vague claim of poor tactics.

## 2. Hitting, penetrating magic defenses and resisting an effect differ

An attack checks a d20 plus attack bonuses against the relevant Armor Class. Full AC, touch AC and flat-footed AC include different protections. Touch attacks normally ignore armor, shield and natural-armor bonuses; they do not erase every defense. Flat-footed vulnerability concerns the ability to react, not whether armor has physically disappeared. Multiple bonuses of the same type generally do not stack, with documented exceptions such as dodge bonuses. [K3](#k3)

A spell can separately require an attack roll, a caster-level check against Spell Resistance and a target saving throw. Fortitude, Reflex and Will are different defenses. Raising the chance to hit does not necessarily improve the other checks. Equally, an effect that has no applicable saving throw should not be interpreted through a generic “all spells can be resisted” model. [K4](#k4)

**Ray of Enfeeblement** makes the distinction concrete: its ranged touch attack can connect, yet Spell Resistance and a Fortitude save still have separate consequences; the save reduces the Strength penalty rather than making armor relevant again. Its standard-action cost is another independent condition. [K5](#k5)

**Interpretation:** these layers can create useful alternatives rather than pure complexity. Change the defense being tested, improve the correct check, or use another tool. A combat log should distinguish a miss, resisted spell, reduced effect and damage prevention; identical “nothing useful happened” feedback cannot teach the required response.

## 3. Spell repertoire and spell supply are separate resources

A Wizard learns a repertoire, including adding supported spells from scrolls, and prepares particular spells in available slots. Intelligence, class advancement and specialization affect that progression. A known spell is not necessarily prepared for the current expedition, and learning a higher spell level is not identical to gaining the same numbered character level. [K6](#k6)

A Sorcerer instead casts spontaneously from a more limited known repertoire, spending the available daily uses of the appropriate spell level. The ordinary class uses Charisma; archetypes can alter its governing attribute. The two casters therefore make different decisions before and during an encounter: prepare particular uses in advance versus choose among known spells when spending a shared level-specific supply. Neither is accurately described as a universal mana bar. [K7](#k7)

**Interpretation:** an inventory of powers should distinguish knowing, preparing, qualifying and affording. Rest replenishment creates a reason to anticipate what lies ahead, but repeated safe resting can turn scarcity into a time cost. Whether that cost is meaningful depends on travel, threats and the kingdom calendar rather than the spell screen alone.

## 4. Equipment and teamwork alter what a build can actually do

Weapon proficiency is an eligibility constraint, not a synonym for possessing the object. Weapon categories also differ in reach, handedness, critical behavior and damage type. Armor can add protection while limiting Dexterity contribution, burdening relevant skills and increasing arcane spell-failure risk. Class features and equipment materials qualify those rules; divine casting should not be given a universal wizard-style armor penalty. [K8](#k8)

**Outflank** requires base attack bonus 4 and a qualifying ally with the feat. Flanking together improves the attack bonus, and a critical hit can produce an opportunity attack for the partner. This is a relationship between builds and position, not a passive bonus given to every nearby ally. **Precise Shot** addresses ranged attacks into melee, including relevant ranged spell attacks; selecting a high-damage bow does not itself remove that penalty. [K9](#k9)

**Interpretation:** useful equipment comparison includes what the person is trained to do and who will act beside them. A larger displayed damage number can be the wrong upgrade. Conversely, a familiar companion can become newly valuable because a shared investment now connects the two builds.

## 5. Exploration skills and camping connect the party to the calendar

Trickery handles locks and traps; Perception reveals hidden hazards and objects. Athletics and Mobility supply different obstacle solutions. Knowledge and Lore skills interpret the world, while Use Magic Device supports otherwise constrained scroll/wand use. Training requirements matter for several of these skills. The protagonist and party can contribute differently depending on the actual check; a group inventory does not mean one universal pool of expertise. [K10](#k10)

Camping assigns distinct jobs: Lore (Nature) supports hunting, Stealth conceals the camp, Knowledge (World) supports cooking and Perception supports watches. Hunting can take additional time and is unavailable in dungeons; carried rations therefore provide predictability rather than merely another collectible. Companion-specific camp actions add further differences. **Jubilost's Effective Management** reduces camping time, whereas **Ekundayo's Wilderness Survival** reduces ration consumption. [K11](#k11)

**Interpretation:** food, expertise and elapsed time are substitutable only in limited ways. A competent hunter can reduce carried supplies but cannot make every underground rest self-sufficient. Distinct support roles are useful when the player can see which uncertainty they reduce, not when every rest repeats identical administration.

## 6. Unconsciousness, fatal damage and recovery need different tools

At zero or lower HP, a character can be unconscious without yet reaching the ordinary fatal threshold. Negative HP reaching the relevant Constitution threshold creates a different outcome. Restoring health before that point is not equivalent to reviving a dead companion. The guide's example distinguishes a Constitution-12 character at −11 from −12; it is illustrative of the threshold, not a forecast for every special effect. [K12](#k12)

**Death's Door** is a difficulty option, not an intrinsic permanent shield for all campaigns. Under that option, surviving an otherwise lethal incident can leave the condition; another lethal incident before it is cleared may kill. A safe rest at Oleg's Trading Post or the capital differs from assuming any field rest removes it. Other settings can restore dead companions after combat or alter negative-effect recovery. Instant-death effects and exceptional character rules require separate checks. [K13](#k13)

The **Signed Scroll of Raise Dead**, an early reward associated with Jamandi Aldori, is a named scarce recovery object. Its effect returns an eligible dead companion but carries negative-level consequences. That is not the same operation as ordinary healing, and the item's anomalous duration metadata should not be read as making the revival last only a few rounds. More powerful resurrection and restoration options have their own access and cost. [K14](#k14)

**Interpretation:** failure severity is state-dependent. A person who stands up again may still be less safe for the next encounter. Good feedback distinguishes readiness restored, persistent condition retained and a resource consumed; a single green health bar can conceal all three.

## 7. Kingdom values, ranks and advisers are different layers

The kingdom tracks ten development dimensions. The five initial offices are Regent, Councilor, General, Treasurer and High Priest; later offices handle relations, arcane matters, stability, culture and espionage. A stat's accumulated value and its rank are not the same number. Meeting a value threshold creates an opportunity for an advisor conversation and rank project, not an automatic completed upgrade. [K15](#k15) [K16](#k16)

Advisers have relevant attributes and perspectives. A companion's equipment can improve the relevant attribute, whereas non-adventuring NPC advisers have training projects. A custom mercenary can fill a missing office but brings a documented event penalty and different handling of rank conversations. These options do not have identical consequences merely because the vacancy disappears. Repeated disagreement can also cause resignation; the guide's uncertain exact hidden-counter claims are not adopted as a universal formula. [K16](#k16)

**Interpretation:** an adviser has at least three values: competence, available time and willingness to support the ruler's decisions. Optimizing only the first can create a brittle institution. A meaningful administrative interface makes the other two visible enough to plan around without pretending to expose a fully simulated government.

## 8. Events are uncertain; projects are commitments

**Problems** and **Opportunities** present eligible adviser choices, a deadline and a difficulty check. Starting an assignment matters; merely placing an adviser on the card is not the same as beginning it. The resolution combines a d20 with the relevant adviser bonus, including rank and attribute contribution. Crisis Points can improve the roll. Success, triumph, failure and disaster are different result bands; problems generally make neglect or failure more dangerous. [K17](#k17)

A working adviser can still accompany the adventuring party, but cannot simultaneously serve another administrative assignment. This is the relevant scarcity—not their physical absence from all combat. Cancelling after time has passed can itself produce failure, so “interruptible” is not equivalent to “costlessly reversible.” [K17](#k17)

**Projects** instead spend BP and occupy the appropriate participants without the event's random success roll. Rank-up projects initially require the ruler and adviser for fourteen days, automatically advancing the calendar; later development can shorten them. Economy, training and curse research are other project families. Curse research can affect narrative possibilities, but an ambiguous guide recipe for the “best” ending is not treated as a complete validated ending formula. [K18](#k18)

**Interpretation:** a scheduler should distinguish a risky outcome from a known commitment cost. The player can knowingly accept either, but an unexplained time jump that consumes a response window is a different failure from an unlucky adviser roll.

## 9. BP, unrest and the meaning of expansion

Build Points fund administration and construction; gold belongs to the adventuring economy. Conversion connects them, and periodic income depends on several kingdom factors. A treasury deficit can worsen unrest rather than simply prevent the next purchase. Unrest progresses through named states from Serene to Crumbling, altering event performance; unchecked collapse can end the campaign unless a selected difficulty/automation contract prevents that outcome. The Stability development stat and the kingdom's current unrest state should not be conflated. [K15](#k15)

Claiming a region enables further administrative and settlement opportunities after its prerequisites, assignment and costs. Buildings occupy specific sites and can improve stats, provide local event bonuses or enable practical services. Adjacency and alignment restrictions make placement contextual. For example, the **Mint** has distinct benefits beside a Palace, Castle or Cathedral; that is a constrained construction interaction, not arbitrary emergent urban economics. [K19](#k19)

Settlements also matter to expeditions: rest, merchant/cleric services and eligible travel infrastructure create usable destinations. Teleportation Circles and Mage Towers can connect established places, while an Aviary extends where administration can be accessed. A village-to-town project and the subsequent settlement upgrade are separate steps with requirements; a map label alone does not instantiate every urban service. [K20](#k20)

**Interpretation:** territory gains meaning when it changes future actions. The strongest loop is not simply accumulating map color: it is opening a workshop, shortening a risky journey or making a distant problem governable. That usefulness must still be weighed against upkeep and the attention consumed by another place.

## 10. Bokken connects a particular person to the realm's production

Bokken is an alchemical artisan, not a generic crafting tab. Developing an Outskirts settlement and his Alchemical Shop establishes the relationship. His crafted delivery can open **An Ancient Formula**, which involves the three volumes of *Of Transmutations and Bodily Poisons* and later events. Bringing the books is therefore progress in a sequence, not a guarantee that his masterpiece instantly appears. [K21](#k21)

Artisan quality responds to personal quests, workshop and kingdom development. The recurring equipment/alchemical rewards connect territorial investment to the adventuring party. Requests also distinguish desired product categories, rather than enabling the ruler to type any imagined recipe and receive it. [K22](#k22)

**Interpretation:** this is a grounded procurement loop: a named maker, a place to work, acquired knowledge, time and a delivered object. It supplies stronger inspiration than an unspecified “artisan gives a better weapon,” while remaining an authored system rather than an independently simulated industry.

## 11. Five additional constructed cases

These extend the original nine, including both preserved reviewer incidents. None is a new play report.

**K01 — hit the right defense, then inspect the remaining check.** A caster selects Ray of Enfeeblement against an armored opponent. Touch targeting changes which protection matters, but a successful hit does not remove Spell Resistance or the Fortitude reduction. The next decision is to improve the relevant weakness or choose another effect. **Limit:** changing attack channel is not guaranteed success. [K5](#k5)

**K02 — retain a partnership for a reason.** Two qualifying Outflank users coordinate against one foe. The attack bonus and critical-triggered response make replacing one member a party-level decision. The next choice is another partner or a different tactical plan. **Limit:** a companion lacking the feat does not inherit it merely by standing nearby. [K9](#k9)

**K03 — an improvement can consume the preparation window.** The journal shows the next An Ancient Curse deadline approaching. Before committing the ruler to a fourteen-day rank-up, the player accounts for the earlier crisis activation and travel needs. The next choice is to handle the crisis or postpone development. **Limit:** a deadline's displayed final date is not necessarily the first moment inaction has consequences. [K15](#k15) [K18](#k18)

**K04 — preserve a rescue resource without mistaking it for readiness.** A party uses the Signed Scroll to restore an eligible dead companion, then addresses the remaining negative levels rather than assuming ordinary health recovery completed the job. The next choice is restoration, safer travel or a different expedition. **Limit:** eligibility and special death effects remain constraints; possession is not universal resurrection authority. [K14](#k14)

**K05 — a book becomes another person's productive capacity.** A ruler funds Bokken's shop and retrieves the required volumes for his personal research. The result is progress in an artisan relationship whose later outputs can aid the party. The next choice is further local investment or an immediate adventuring purchase. **Limit:** the authored sequence has further conditions; completing one procurement step is not a simulated invention of any desired elixir. [K21](#k21) [K22](#k22)
