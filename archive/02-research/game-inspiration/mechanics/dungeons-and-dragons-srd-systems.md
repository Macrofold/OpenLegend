# D&D SRD 5.2.1: systemic possibilities beyond the character sheet

**Research date:** 2026-09-27. **Basis:** Mike's uploaded, 364-page SRD 5.2.1, read through all sections and catalogues. **Status:** reference research and original adaptation proposals; no OpenLegend rule, implementation, or acceptance criterion is adopted by this document.

[Packet hub](../games/dungeons-and-dragons.md) · [Current-edition dossier](../dossiers/dungeons-and-dragons-2024.md) · [DM adjudication](dungeons-and-dragons-adjudication.md) · [Worldbuilding dossier](../../worldbuilding/worlds/29-dungeons-and-dragons.md)

## 1. What the complete reading changes

The first dossier established the basic game and selected interactions. This pass asks a different question: **what recurring design machinery becomes visible when the spell, item, hazard, monster, and animal catalogues are read together?** The answer is richer than an ability-check simulator. The same underlying ideas recur as professional knowledge, borrowed perception, altered bodies, stored work, contingent actions, territorial rules, dependencies, transformations, and consequences that survive an encounter.

The opportunity for OpenLegend is to make those relationships playable. A player should be able to learn why an unfamiliar creature behaves differently, use a mundane object intelligently, make something that remains useful, or solve a problem by changing its dependencies rather than merely increasing damage. These are hypotheses about a good game, not evidence of measured player preference or a demand to implement every D&D subsystem.

**Reading convention.** `[S, p. N, Entry]` refers to the printed page and named entry of the supplied [SRD 5.2.1](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf). Source descriptions are rules-based observations. Paragraphs marked **Adaptation**, **Constructed situation**, or **Interpretation** are our synthesis, not additional D&D rules. Exact source wording, rather than remembered 2014 rules or a wiki's older statistics, controls this pass.

## 2. Knowledge can be equipment, treasure, and a profession

### A book that changes what you can do

The equipment chapter gives consulting a suitable Book a benefit to relevant Intelligence checks; a Map similarly helps with the area it depicts. Tool descriptions include investigation as well as production: identifying what a container held, examining a document, or making a useful object is tied to a particular craft. These are unusually concrete ways to make a scholar or artisan useful outside combat. `[S, pp. 93–99, Tools; Book; Map]`

**Adaptation:** distinguish possessing information, understanding it, and being able to use it under present conditions. A field guide could help identify a fungus without teaching every property of every plant. A survey could reveal a pass without making the pass safe. A potter's examination of a vessel could supply evidence that a generic perception check would not. The benefit should follow the content and provenance, not merely the item being named “book.”

**Constructed situation:** a party finds an empty jar near a sick caravan. A craftsperson identifies its former contents; an apothecary interprets the implications; a witness places the jar with a particular merchant. Each contribution changes the next decision. None requires awarding the entire mystery to the highest Intelligence score. A forged label and a genuinely contaminated jar can coexist, so evidence should remain separate from inference.

### Learning leaves artifacts and obligations

A Wizard's spellbook is an actual object. Copying an unfamiliar eligible spell costs more time and money than copying an already understood spell into a backup. Losing the book does not erase every prepared spell, but neither does a new blank book reconstruct its entire contents. `[S, p. 78, Expanding and Replacing a Spellbook]`

**Adaptation:** make discoveries portable and teachable without equating a document with instant mastery. The useful sequence is discovery → interpretation → practice → reliable use → teaching or publication. Different worlds can collapse or expand those stages. Teachers, libraries, expeditions, manuscript theft, and incomplete notes can become activities rather than decorative backstory. Avoid a universal grind: repeat practice only while it creates a choice or visibly improves competence.

The Trinkets table supplies a complementary pattern: incomplete songs, unfamiliar deeds, missing diary pages, receipts, and enigmatic objects are modest possessions with unresolved relationships. They need not be powerful to generate curiosity. `[S, pp. 26–27, Trinkets]`

**Adaptation:** a player's keepsake can connect to a person, institution, place, or unfinished project. Do not automatically turn every trinket into a world-saving artifact. Sometimes its payoff is recognition, a restored friendship, a useful address, or a piece of music the player chooses to complete.

## 3. Progression should change decisions, not only probabilities

### Trade one kind of effectiveness for another

The Rogue's Cunning Strike exchanges Sneak Attack damage dice for effects such as poisoning, tripping, or withdrawing; its poison option requires a Poisoner's Kit. The Fighter's Tactical Mind can use Second Wind to improve a failed ability check, without expending that use if the check still fails. The Monk's Stunning Strike has a lesser effect even when the target saves. These are three different ways to spend competence: trade damage for control, push toward success with bounded resource risk, or retain a reduced result against resistance. `[S, pp. 48, 51, 63]`

**Adaptation:** give developed characters better ways to pursue an intention. An experienced swordsman might choose between a damaging strike, a opening for an ally, or a safer withdrawal. An accomplished craftsperson might trade scarce material for speed or spend extra time to protect quality. Those choices are more expressive than simply making all their numbers larger.

Keep their restrictions meaningful. “I trip him while striking” should not grant both full damage and an additional effect unless the character has that capability. Conversely, present available tradeoffs in ordinary language rather than requiring the player to memorize a technical move name.

### Reliability is a form of power

Reliable Talent raises the Rogue's floor on qualifying skill or tool checks. Hunter's Lore supplies an explicit way to discover a marked target's defenses. Neither is merely a damage upgrade: one changes predictability, the other changes information. `[S, pp. 61, 63]`

**Adaptation:** expertise can mean fewer failures at routine work, better diagnosis, reduced collateral damage, more efficient resource use, or a wider set of feasible approaches. A player's progress should be visible in how confidently they can make plans. An unchanged bridge should not become harder to cross because their skill increased.

### Deliberate simplicity is compatible with depth

The book contains classes with very different decision burdens and recommended starting options. It does not require every character to maintain every available resource. `[S, pp. 19–25, Character Creation; pp. 28–82, Classes]`

**Adaptation:** allow straightforward competence and elaborate invention to coexist. The player who wants to be a reliable guard or explorer should not need the same interface as a player building a magical workshop. Depth can exist in the world and reveal itself when a player pursues it; it need not all appear on their first screen.

## 4. Invention becomes compelling when a capability can be embodied

### Small devices are an excellent starting scale

Rock Gnomes can spend time making a small clockwork device that reproduces one selected Prestidigitation effect. It has activation, quantity, and lifetime limits. This is not permission to build any imagined machine. It is a compact example of turning a known capability into an object someone else can operate. `[S, p. 85, Rock Gnome]`

**Adaptation:** begin an invention repertoire with a few understandable effects—produce a light, make a sound, move a small load, record a message, filter a specified substance—and let players combine them with bodies, containers, switches, materials, and power supplies. A useful invention should expose what it does, how it is activated, what it consumes, and what stops it. New trusted effect families can expand the repertoire without making arbitrary generated prose authoritative.

**Constructed situation:** a player builds a festival lantern that displays a learned color sequence when a handle is turned. Another player uses it as a signal at a dock. The lantern does not become a remote enemy detector simply because that would be convenient. Its value comes from a supported effect finding a new purpose.

### Delegated power is not automatically learned power

A Ring of Spell Storing and a Shield Guardian allow previously supplied magic to be used later under their own rules. The guardian retains parameters from the original caster and loses the stored spell after use. Possessing the item or giving a command is different from knowing how to create the effect independently. `[S, p. 239, Ring of Spell Storing; p. 323, Shield Guardian]`

**Adaptation:** distinguish the inventor, operator, owner, beneficiary, and source of stored energy. This supports apprentices using a master's tool, expedition supplies prepared by a specialist, rented equipment, and workshops serving other players. It also supplies meaningful failure cases: the object is empty, an operator lacks authorization, the input is incompatible, or the prepared effect no longer fits the situation.

### Interfaces can themselves be gameplay

The Apparatus of the Crab is a machine with two occupants, multiple levers, environmental limits, and a finite air supply. The controls are part of understanding and operating it, not a generic “use vehicle” button with every result implied. `[S, p. 210, Apparatus of the Crab]`

**Adaptation:** unfamiliar machinery can reward experimentation, documentation, teamwork, and training. Controls should reveal a coherent system; random punishment for touching a lever is not the same as discovery. A skilled operator might perform familiar sequences quickly, while a novice can still infer the essentials from visible feedback.

Marvelous Pigments provide a more extraordinary example: artwork becomes material objects within explicit limits on quantity, time, scale, and value. `[S, p. 230, Marvelous Pigments]`

**Adaptation:** expressive construction could let a player sketch or describe a supported object, preview its geometry and costs, and then create it through the world's actual process. The attractive part is turning an idea into something usable. The limits must follow the admitted creative power, not a narrator's changing generosity.

## 5. Preparation can become bounded automation

Alarm, Magic Mouth, Glyph of Warding, Contingency, and Programmed Illusion store different kinds of future work. Their triggers, senses, movement restrictions, durations, costs, and outputs differ. A Magic Mouth's sensory trigger does not establish that it can read moral character. A Glyph's restrictions do not make it an unrestricted portable explosive. `[S, pp. 107, 119, 136, 147, 156]`

**Interpretation:** these are small authored processes embedded in the world. They provide a useful alternative to both hand-authored one-off scenes and an always-thinking omniscient agent.

**Adaptation:** let players specify an observable trigger and a supported response: ring a bell when this gate opens, show a welcome performance when someone reaches this courtyard, release a prepared light when water reaches a mark. The device needs an actual sensor, detection range, power or charges where applicable, and a policy for repeated activation. Natural-language triggers should be translated into inspectable conditions rather than interpreted anew whenever the player needs a favorable result.

**Constructed situation:** a merchant installs a warning device at a storeroom. It detects an opening, not theft as an abstract intention. A worker opening the door legitimately can activate it; an intruder entering elsewhere may not. The player can improve the design by changing placement, adding identification, or coordinating with staff. The interesting learning is about coverage and false alarms, not memorizing the spell name.

Do not turn bounded automation into arbitrary generated code. Conversely, do not make every trigger require a paid language-model judgment. Settled conditions should remain cheap and stable. This is a proposed design direction, not a benchmark or a claim about OpenLegend's current implementation.

## 6. Lasting magic makes infrastructure, economies, and politics playable

### Created things have different kinds of persistence

The spell catalogue distinguishes temporary material, persistent rooms, conjured hospitality, repaired objects, and construction that becomes permanent. Creation's material lifetime is unlike Wall of Stone's completed permanent result; a Magnificent Mansion's provisions are unlike ordinary exported trade goods; a Demiplane's room persists independently of its temporary doorway. `[S, pp. 121, 123, 147, 173]`

**Adaptation:** record whether an output is actual persistent matter, an ongoing magical manifestation, a sensory appearance, or an object that expires under a specific condition. The distinction changes building, trade, security, and trust. A customer buying temporary material should not discover its expiry only because the narrator needed a twist; a fraud plot requires someone actually concealing a knowable property.

### Do not defeat abundance by inventing costs

The SRD contains genuine sources of abundance. A Decanter of Endless Water produces water. Plant Growth can enhance an area's yield for a year. Wall of Stone can create lasting construction. Their explicit constraints should be honored rather than quietly replaced with universal resource conservation. `[S, pp. 153, 173, 217]`

**Adaptation:** when a world admits those powers, let successful invention change it. The next interesting problem may concern distribution, maintenance, quality, land access, drainage, transport, expertise, or competing preferences. A water source can be plentiful while delivery to an upper district is difficult. A magically constructed wall can be real while its foundation, placement, and effect on a neighbor remain important. These are proposed consequences, not an assertion that the SRD models a complete economy.

Elementalism's short-lived conjured water supplies a useful contrast: an effect that resembles water need not have the same persistence as the decanter's output. `[S, p. 127, Elementalism]`

### Investment can change the map

Teleportation Circle combines a destination's known sequence with repeated work to establish a permanent circle. The ordinary listed procedure requires daily casting for a year and 50 GP of consumed material each time: 18,250 GP over 365 castings, before considering feature-specific exceptions. `[S, p. 168, Teleportation Circle]`

**Adaptation:** allow a long project to create a new route, not just improve a travel-speed statistic. Who owns the endpoints? Who knows the destination? Who maintains access? A new connection can revive a town, bypass a toll, attract visitors, or expose a secure site. The contribution of builders, cartographers, suppliers, and negotiators remains legible even when the final journey is instantaneous.

Fabricate and Move Earth provide different production limits: craftsmanship and raw-material quality still matter for fine goods, while terrain manipulation does not grant arbitrary precision engineering. `[S, pp. 129, 151]`

**Adaptation:** magic should have a useful domain of competence. It can remove a labor bottleneck without automatically replacing every profession. Avoid both extremes: making artisans irrelevant by granting an unlimited fabrication verb, or making the magical ability useless to protect an unchanged crafting grind.

## 7. Bodies, identities, senses, and control must remain distinct

### Transformation is more than replacing a model

Wild Shape preserves personality, memory, speech, mental ability scores, and specified other features while changing bodily capabilities. Its equipment can be worn where practical, dropped, or merged; the new anatomy determines object handling. Polymorph and True Polymorph have different replacement and control rules. `[S, pp. 42–43, 153, 170]`

**Adaptation:** a transformation should explicitly answer what happens to identity, learned techniques, mental capacity, locomotion, senses, speech, carried gear, wounds, and ongoing effects. Becoming a bird might enable a new route while preventing operation of a familiar tool. A changed appearance should not automatically copy another person's memories or social authority.

The “Animals” section demonstrates why visible category names are insufficient. A Giant Owl and Giant Eagle are Celestials, while other listed forms are Beasts or Monstrosities. Wild Shape eligibility depends on the actual required creature type and other limits, not the section containing the illustration. `[S, pp. 42, 348–354]`

**Adaptation:** distinguish biological form, culture, supernatural type, occupation, and faction. Do not derive every rule from a loose label such as “animal,” “monster,” or “humanoid-looking.” Those distinctions should improve outcomes, not burden the player with a hidden taxonomy puzzle.

### Borrowing perception is not omniscience

Gaze of Two Minds, Find Familiar, a Homunculus's bond, and a Vampire Familiar's connection provide distinct mechanisms for sharing senses or messages. Range, plane, willingness, maintenance, and the capability's exact wording matter. `[S, pp. 73, 130, 299, 334]`

**Adaptation:** shared perception should originate from a real observer. A scout's message, a borrowed visual feed, a remembered impression, and a magical location detector are different evidence. An observer cannot transmit a hidden fact it never perceived merely because the controlling player knows it from another screen.

Likewise, special knowledge is permitted when an actual capability grants it. A Planetar's Divine Awareness detects a heard lie; a Sprite's Heart Sight has specified emotional and alignment information; a Rust Monster locates nearby ferrous metal. These are supernatural or specialized rules, not general permissions for every insightful character. `[S, pp. 315, 320, 329]`

**Adaptation:** support exceptional information powers deliberately. A magical truth sense can exist without turning ordinary Insight into a universal fact checker. A detector should expose its range and subject matter rather than answer every question the player associates with it.

### Speech, understanding, and mimicry are separate

The catalogue contains creatures that understand languages but cannot speak, creatures that communicate telepathically, and a Raven that imitates sounds it has heard. A Giant Owl's magical capabilities differ sharply from an ordinary Owl's. `[S, pp. 299, 313, 352, 358–359]`

**Adaptation:** give nonhuman companions useful individuality. A mimic can repeat a warning without understanding it; an intelligent nonspeaking mount can consent or refuse through other communication. Neither silence nor an animal-shaped body establishes lack of personhood.

## 8. Cooperation, coercion, and personhood are different game systems

Gate can bring a creature without granting control. Planar Ally involves an independent entity that can negotiate or decline. Planar Binding is a distinct coercive magical arrangement. Geas imposes consequences for disobedience rather than simply deleting the possibility. `[S, pp. 135, 152–153]`

**Adaptation:** summons, employment, favors, bargains, loyalty, fear, magical compulsion, and direct control should not collapse into one “companion” state. A player can pursue power and bargain hard; the world should still know which mechanism produced obedience. This creates plots about promises, leverage, liberation, betrayal, and earned trust without requiring every encounter to be benevolent.

Awaken and the permanent creation cases of True Polymorph introduce beings whose subsequent attitude or independence matters. The end of a control period is a meaningful event, not merely an expired combat buff. `[S, pp. 111–112, 170]`

**Adaptation:** creating a person can create a relationship rather than perpetual ownership. The character might remember kindness, exploitation, shared accomplishments, or incompatible goals. Exact rights and constraints belong to the authored world; this research does not declare every setting's law or metaphysics identical.

Sentient magic items have senses, communication, purposes, and possible conflicts with a wielder. A Ring of Mind Shielding can hold a departed wearer's soul. These are different forms of companionship than an inert equipment bonus. `[S, pp. 207–208, 238]`

**Constructed situation:** a celebrated weapon wants its creator's lost techniques rediscovered. It assists a competent wielder but resists an unrelated campaign of conquest. The player can negotiate, fulfill part of the purpose, find another tool, or seek to alter the relationship. The item needs its own bounded knowledge; it should not produce a complete quest solution whenever convenient.

The Vampire's charm and lycanthrope curses also show that D&D sometimes imposes substantial loss of control. Their exact effects must not be mislabeled as ordinary attraction or voluntary agreement. Whether OpenLegend uses such mechanics, provides warnings, permits player-controlled cursed forms, or restricts them is a separate game-design choice. `[S, pp. 335–339]`

## 9. Creatures are libraries of interaction, not just enemies with different HP

### A creature can change the landscape

An Ankheg or Purple Worm can leave a usable tunnel, while an Earth Elemental or Xorn can pass through particular material without disturbing it. A Treant animates existing trees that later take root where possible. These are materially different ways of moving through and changing a place. `[S, pp. 259, 282, 317, 333, 343]`

**Adaptation:** creature behavior can create paths, hazards, shelters, and opportunities that persist. An expedition might follow a tunneler, protect a nesting ground, or redirect a dangerous migration. Do not invent a full natural-history simulation from a combat block: food, breeding, population limits, habitat, and ordinary goals need additional authored rules.

### Knowledge changes the right response

| SRD interaction | Why it creates a different decision | Adaptation and counterexample |
|---|---|---|
| Iron Golem absorbs Fire; Shambling Mound absorbs Lightning. `[S, pp. 302, 323]` | An apparently powerful attack can aid the target. | Let observed effects teach a creature's nature. Do not make all metal or all plants inherit these supernatural exceptions. |
| Hydra loses and regrows heads with timing and Fire conditions. `[S, p. 300]` | Coordination and damage timing matter, not just total damage. | Visible bodily state can create a tactical puzzle. Do not attach universal dismemberment rules to every creature. |
| Roper tentacles can be destroyed to free a captive without damaging the main body; they regrow. `[S, p. 320]` | Rescue and escape can be objectives independent of killing. | Let players act on the dependency producing a condition. Preserve the time window before it returns. |
| Rust Monster senses ferrous metal and damages qualifying nonmagical equipment; Mending can remove its penalty. `[S, pp. 320–321]` | Equipment choice, approach, and repair change the encounter. | Loss can create work without destroying all investment. Detecting ferrous metal and damaging a metal object are not the same predicate. |
| Giant Spider's web is a destructible object and a sensory network. `[S, pp. 353–354]` | The environment both restrains prey and informs its owner. | Cutting a path, crossing carefully, and creating a diversion are distinct approaches. A web signal need not reveal a person's identity. |
| Gelatinous Cube's engulfing permits risky extraction of another creature. `[S, pp. 286–287]` | Rescue exposes the rescuer to harm. | Make aid an active, intelligible decision, not always a free interaction or a generic healing spell. |
| Night Hag's Soul Bag holds a dependency for haunting and trapped souls. `[S, p. 311]` | Stealing or destroying an object can change a supernatural threat. | A rescue mission can target custody of souls rather than the enemy's HP. Do not assume every hag shares this feature. |
| Mummy Lord reforms while its specially vulnerable heart survives. `[S, p. 310]` | An apparent victory can require discovery and a second objective. | Give persistent adversaries a knowable cause and an achievable resolution instead of arbitrary respawning. |
| Octopus releases obscuring ink and moves away. `[S, pp. 352, 358]` | A defense changes visibility and permits escape. | Withdrawal can be a competent goal. The small and giant versions have different triggers and breathing rules. |
| A Shrieker reacts to proximity or bright light and broadcasts an alarm. `[S, p. 286]` | A noncombat organism changes nearby behavior. | Treat an alarm as a real signal reaching possible listeners, not instant universal aggro. |

### An encounter's consequences can survive into tomorrow

The Troll can produce severed limbs that remain active; a surviving limb has a delayed chance to become another Troll. Wights and Wraiths have different ways and limits for creating additional undead. A Lich's Spirit Jar and a Vampire's resting place supply different restoration conditions. `[S, pp. 304, 333, 335–336, 341–343]`

**Adaptation:** distinguish a combat ending from a threat ending. A hurried victory may leave cleanup, containment, information, or a future adversary. Conversely, discovering the right dependency can let an underpowered party succeed. These consequences need to arise from established mechanics, not a director restoring enemies to keep content available.

The monster chapter also warns against a tempting transfer error: extra damage in a creature's weapon attack is not automatically an intrinsic property that transfers with the looted weapon. Equipment and monster abilities have their own rules. `[S, pp. 254–257]`

**Adaptation:** OpenLegend may prefer more symmetric item behavior, but that is a deliberate departure. A recovered sword should clearly distinguish its own powers from the skill, physiology, or temporary enchantment of its former wielder. Do not promise players every number they saw on the enemy's attack.

## 10. Death, curses, injuries, and containment can produce adventures

The SRD does not reduce recovery to one operation. Healing HP, removing a condition, ending a curse, freeing an attunement bond, releasing a soul, repairing a body, and restoring life have different scopes. Remove Curse does not generally turn a cursed item into an uncursed one merely because it frees a wearer. The Dead glossary permits a soul to refuse restoration. `[S, pp. 158, 176–191, 206]`

**Adaptation:** recovery can involve diagnosis, specialists, equipment, a location, and someone else's choice. Give the player visible progress and alternatives; an opaque affliction that disables participation indefinitely is not automatically interesting. A dangerous fantasy world can retain serious consequences without making every setback a permanent deletion.

Gentle Repose changes the time available for some restoration efforts; a Clone provides a prepared body with its own maturation and identity conditions. A Vampire must reach its resting place within a specified period after being reduced to mist. `[S, pp. 115, 135, 335]`

**Constructed situation:** a party recovers a companion's body far from help. One character preserves it, another finds a safe route, and a third negotiates access to a capable practitioner. Scarce time matters without everyone rolling the same Medicine check. The companion's soul and the cause of death remain relevant. The group's plan can work; the GM substitute should not add a new arbitrary obstacle every time they satisfy a requirement.

The containment items provide another family. A Mirror of Life Trapping holds occupants under particular release rules; an Iron Flask's captive and the consequences of release are different; a Portable Hole's air and exit conditions differ from a Demiplane's. `[S, pp. 123, 228, 230, 234]`

**Adaptation:** prisons, vaults, refuges, and extradimensional homes can have discoverable access rules. Escaping might mean obtaining a key, reaching an opening, persuading an operator, breaking a dependency, or waiting for a known expiry. Containment is not permission to remove an entity from persistence or forget that someone else is inside.

## 11. Regions and planes should alter possibilities, not only scenery

The SRD supplies concrete cross-plane mechanics without being a complete planar gazetteer. Etherealness distinguishes observation, movement, and interaction with the originating plane. Wall of Force reaches into the Ethereal Plane. Plane Shift, Gate, Teleportation Circle, Demiplane, and Well of Many Worlds offer different kinds of access, targeting, destination knowledge, and duration. `[S, pp. 123, 128, 135, 152, 168, 172, 253]`

**Adaptation:** a plane can be a different set of meaningful relationships: what can be perceived, which material blocks passage, whether a route is one-way, how one returns, and which powers operate there. A ghostly overlay of a familiar town can be more interesting than a much larger map filled with renamed terrain. However, seeing another layer does not necessarily grant the ability to touch it.

A permanent destination, a temporary doorway, and a portal-generating object should remain distinct. That distinction supports travel knowledge, lost connections, defended entrances, and people living on the far side. Cross-world travel also needs an explicit distinction between a fictional doorway and authorization to enter another player's hosted world; the former must not silently grant the latter.

**Constructed situation:** smugglers exploit an overlapping realm to bypass a wall. They still need an exit, a way to move their cargo, and knowledge of which wards affect the alternate layer. A settlement can counter the route through a particular defense rather than by declaring all clever travel impossible. The plan's feasibility should follow established properties, not whether the narrator likes the smugglers.

The companion worldbuilding study separates these SRD mechanics from older-edition planar-trait catalogues and setting-specific lore. An old description of timelessness, a local magical ward, and a current spell's timing cannot be merged without an explicit world choice.

## 12. Pleasure, mastery, hospitality, and ambition deserve mechanics too

The SRD's powers are not solely weapons or safeguards. Small sensory effects can support performances and decoration. Hallow can support shared understanding. Heroes' Feast makes communal provisioning extraordinary. A Rod of Security creates a chosen refuge that can take the form of a garden, palace, carnival, or another imagined paradise, with capacity and duration constraints. `[S, pp. 138, 140, 154, 242]`

**Adaptation:** let players organize a festival, establish an inn, host visitors, build a beautiful workshop, create music, cultivate a garden, or gain recognition for excellent work. These can connect the same systems as an expedition: materials, timing, travel, trust, expertise, invitations, and the physical environment. A pleasurable project need not conceal a disaster to become meaningful.

**Constructed situation:** a player hosts a multilingual fair. A communication effect helps visitors understand one another, but it does not erase incompatible tastes, price disagreements, scheduling, or the desire to impress a rival craftsperson. A temporary performance device, a distinctive meal, and a newly opened route each contribute something different. The reward can be reputation, profit, friendship, spectacle, or the satisfaction of making the event happen.

Power and ambition also matter. A player may want a formidable weapon, an independent settlement, a spectacular transformation, or a dangerous bargain. The Oathbow's focused commitment, sentient items' purposes, and the Mysterious Deck's extreme consequences show very different fantasies of power. `[S, pp. 207–208, 231–233]`

**Adaptation:** allow mastery and consequential risk rather than reducing every system to caution. Make exceptional powers explicit enough that pursuing them is a plan. An intentionally chaotic artifact can exist in a chosen campaign without making arbitrary retroactive changes the default rules of a shared world.

## 13. The less visible rules that make combinations trustworthy

The previous adjudication study owns the general authority proposal. This reading adds specific reasons it is needed:

| Distinction | Source evidence | Consequence for a proposed implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Suppression versus removal | Antimagic Field suppresses effects while durations continue; Dispel Magic has its own spell-specific scope. `[S, pp. 109, 124]` | Exiting a field can restore an effect that was never deleted. Do not make every cancellation the same operation. |
| Failure stage versus blanket refund | Interrupted long casting, an unused readied spell, and Counterspell do not have identical resource outcomes. `[S, pp. 104–106, 120, 186]` | Define reservation, expenditure, completion, interruption, and expiry by capability. |
| Matter versus appearance | Minor Illusion and Mirage Arcane do not grant the same physical consequences. `[S, pp. 148–149]` | Evaluate the actual effect, not a broad “illusion” keyword. |
| Damage threshold versus reduction | Object thresholds ignore subthreshold damage rather than subtracting a constant from every hit. `[S, pp. 176–180, Breaking Objects; Damage Threshold]` | Similar-looking defensive values can have different semantics. |
| Current capacity versus lifetime capacity | Rod of Absorption limits total energy absorbed over its lifetime as well as stored energy. `[S, p. 241]` | Spending charges must not accidentally reset a lifetime limit. |
| Replacement versus multiplication | Simulacrum has explicit recursion and replacement constraints; animated trees cannot repeat the Treant's animation. `[S, pp. 163, 333]` | Combinations need identity, ownership, population, and reproduction rules. |
| Movement versus teleportation | A Shield Guardian summoned by its amulet travels to the wearer; it does not automatically teleport. `[S, p. 323]` | A goal does not guarantee instantaneous fulfillment. |
| General repair versus a specific exception | Mending's ordinary narrow effect differs from the Rust Monster's explicit penalty-removal clause. `[S, pp. 147, 321]` | Preserve justified exceptions without making them universal. |
| A condition versus its cause and duration | Different petrification, poison, restraint, and charm effects end differently. `[S, pp. 176–191; monster entries]` | Shared status vocabulary can coexist with source-specific recovery. |

**Interpretation:** D&D's apparent natural-language freedom depends heavily on consistent distinctions of this kind. A simulation can make the accounting easier, but it still needs to represent the distinctions. Merely prompting a narrator to be reasonable does not establish persistence, fair resource use, or compatible effects.

## 14. What to pursue first, and what not to copy

These are prioritization judgments for consideration, not scheduled implementation tasks.

**First: make ordinary ingenuity consistently rewarding.** Knowledge-bearing objects, professional investigation, useful equipment, reliable expertise, meaningful time costs, and understandable outcomes support a wide range of play without needing a giant spell catalogue. Include examples where a mundane approach beats a costly magical one and where the magical solution genuinely works better.

**Second: introduce a small set of composable extraordinary effects.** A temporary light, a message, a force, a container, a transformation, a sensor, and a stored action can generate many distinct tools. A creator or player needs a discoverable repertoire and honest limits, not an invisible list of specially recognized recipes.

**Third: connect encounters to persistent places and people.** A creature's habitat, a regional dependency, a captured ally, a workshop, a library, and a new route make separate systems reinforce one another. A victory or invention should leave an intelligible trace.

**Later or world-specific:** large-scale planar travel, transferable souls, permanent reality alteration, independent sentient artifacts, recursive creation, and extreme time behavior. These can be excellent content, but their persistence and agency implications deserve explicit rules before admission.

Do not automatically copy adventuring classes onto every inhabitant, treat challenge rating as an economy model, turn all life into combat turns, equate Charisma with consent, or import a fixed cosmology into the engine. Conversely, do not discard a useful supernatural mechanic merely because it is not realistic. The question is whether its authored constraints produce interesting, coherent choices.

## 15. New constructed combination studies

These are unplayed proposals, not source scenes or verified engine behavior.

### A. The archive under siege

A town stores its seed records and repair manuals in a damp riverside archive. The players want to save the collection without burning the invading organism or flooding the building. The scenario combines valuable documents, consultation, environmental vulnerability, a monster-specific defense, controlled access, and mundane transport. A strong combat spell could be useful outside but destructive inside. A specialist can identify the threat; a craftsperson can protect containers; an athlete can move heavy shelves; a magical utility can buy time.

Success leaves an archive and relationships intact, not just an XP award. Failure can destroy particular knowledge or alter access without magically erasing every copy elsewhere. The world must know which materials were actually present and which records were backed up.

### B. The expedition that becomes a trade route

Explorers discover a passage created by a burrowing creature. They map it, assess its destination, establish safe resting points, and arrange services with people at each end. Later they may invest in a faster magical connection. Discovery, cartography, construction, provisioning, diplomacy, and capital each have a role. The route can change settlement life; it should not exist only while the quest is active.

A fresh tunnel is not necessarily stable, defended, or legally open. Those are additional authored questions, not automatic facts supplied by the burrowing trait. The point is to make a new possibility available, not to declare that every monster-generated hole is a completed road.

### C. A victory whose dependency survives

A party defeats a recurring enemy but learns that its restoration depends on a hidden object. A captured witness knows only part of the route. An information spell narrows the search without revealing every obstacle. A companion is held in a separate magical container. The players can rescue, investigate, bargain, destroy the dependency, or choose temporary containment.

The enemy's return time must follow its actual rule. The narrator cannot shorten it to punish delay or extend it to save the party. That reliability makes planning possible even when the world is dangerous.

### D. A player's first commercial invention

A player develops a reusable signaling device from a known effect and a supported trigger. The first customer wants it for a performance; the second wants it for a warning system. The player discovers that the sensor, activation frequency, weather exposure, and ownership controls matter more than the decorative casing. Improvements can lead to a workshop, teaching, licensing within the fiction, or rival designs.

This should not require an arbitrary number of failed crafting rolls. Experimentation is meaningful when it answers an unresolved design question. Once the process is understood, production can become reliable work with genuine costs and resource constraints.

### Do not silently reconcile the source

Two internal inconsistencies are worth preserving as source questions: the equipment table and entry disagree on Block and Tackle's price, and the travel chapters do not present land-vehicle pace consistently. `[S, pp. 12, 95–96, 192]` This study does not choose an official erratum on the publisher's behalf. A future implementation must explicitly decide its rule rather than pretending the source is unambiguous.

Several familiar assumptions are also unsafe: Grease is explicitly nonflammable; Daylight produces sunlight; Wild Shape retains speech; the revised Rakshasa is not described by the familiar old low-level-spell-immunity shortcut. `[S, pp. 43, 122, 137, 317–318]` Other entries omit restrictions remembered from older versions. Absence of remembered wording is not permission to manufacture an official correction. Reopen the named entry before converting it into a mechanic.

The SRD supplies a large playable vocabulary, not a full account of agriculture, regional pricing, reproduction, civic life, invention discovery, autonomous motivation, or narrative quality. The adaptation proposals fill some of those design questions openly; they are not presented as hidden systems discovered in the source.
