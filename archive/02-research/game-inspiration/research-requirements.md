## Requirements for each subject

The IDs below are stable evidence/coverage dimensions, not runtime tasks. A finished dossier must map each ID to its substantive section or existing canonical research. Merely including a heading, a source link, or a disclaimer does not satisfy a category.

| ID | Required research | What usable coverage contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01 | Identity, scope, and player promise | Exact game/edition, relevant versions and expansions, platform and mode boundaries, genre/camera only where useful, the fantasy and goals, first-session versus established play, and a clear account of what the player does. Separate announced, demonstrated, released, and retired features. |
| R02 | Player actions and major mechanics | The main action vocabulary, inputs and decisions; traversal, combat, production, discovery, social/management/creation systems as applicable; prerequisites, resources, timing, failure, feedback and consequences. Explain the principal systems, not just one favorite mechanic. |
| R03 | Items, entities, and composition | Major useful item/resource/equipment/ability families; acquisition, creation, modification, use, storage, exchange, loss and persistence where applicable; named examples; compatibility, counterplay, limitations and combinations. For an item-light game, explain the actual equivalent rather than inventing loot. |
| R04 | Progression, economy, and time | What changes within a session and across sessions; unlocks, knowledge, skills, collections, upgrades, resets, death/recovery, calendars, scarcity, currencies and sinks; early/middle/late play and what sustains purpose after immediate needs are solved. Separate fictional economy from monetization. |
| R05 | Interactions and concrete situations | Several fully explained examples spanning different major systems, not cosmetic variations of one. Describe intention → conditions → actions → interaction → result → next decision; include a limitation or counterexample. Keep all earlier concrete examples. Label constructed rules-based illustrations versus attributed actual play accounts. |
| R06 | People, AI, social systems, and multiplayer | Character individuality, relationships, factions, routines, communication, cooperation/conflict, roles, information asymmetry, group entry and continuity; solo value, native versus modded multiplayer, shared versus personal resources/progress. State when a system does not exist rather than substituting another game's behavior. |
| R07 | Art, audio, interface, and feel | Concrete visual language, silhouettes, camera/readability, animation, effects, music, sound, interface and control choices; how they communicate rules, atmosphere, identity and emotion. Include production evidence where available and accessibility/friction tradeoffs, not only “the art is charming.” |
| R08 | Story, narrative, and their connection to play | Premise, memorable named people/places, authored structure, procedural or player-authored stories, environmental storytelling, repeated encounters and consequences; how gameplay enables or obstructs narrative. Clearly mark meaningful spoilers. A game without a conventional plot still needs an account of its framing and player-created meaning. |
| R09 | Production and development | Developers/publisher, origins and influences, prototypes, team and collaboration where documented, development/release sequence, tools or technical implementation only when actually evidenced, iteration, testing/feedback, constraints, abandoned ideas, ports, maintenance and major changes. Do not infer proprietary code from visible behavior. |
| R10 | Marketing, distribution, promotion, and virality | Documented release/access model, storefronts/platforms, demos, early access/crowdfunding where relevant, positioning, community/creator/streamer activity, events, updates, sharing units, word of mouth and reuse. Distinguish recorded events from hypotheses about why they mattered. No invented channel-attribution percentages. |
| R11 | Commercial and participation context | Dated, correctly defined available milestones; business/monetization model, pricing structure rather than an unverified current price, support obligations and sustainability. Preserve existing economic history. Units, players, visits, views, concurrency, revenue, bookings and profit are different measures. Missing private financial or retention data is not evidence of failure. |
| R12 | Reception, reviews, and player feedback | Substantive original criticism and direct player accounts where accessible, positive and negative/mixed perspectives, specific praised and frustrating interactions, audience/playstyle differences, dated versions and developer responses. Separate design preference, onboarding, bugs, balance, monetization and group-access issues. Do not manufacture consensus or prevalence. |
| R13 | Transferable inspiration and limits | Concrete patterns OpenLegend could draw from, why they may work, what they depend on, failure modes, alternatives and what not to copy automatically. Named items/characters are reference examples, not proposed copyrighted assets or names. Distinguish observations and attributed intent from the researcher's interpretation. |
| R14 | Sources, viewing routes, preservation, and navigation | Adjacent citations for detailed factual claims; annotated sources with dates, type and access limits; useful viewing/reading routes and spoilers; links to prior chapter and mechanics study; requirement-to-section map; preservation check and working internal links. No unreviewed source represented as read. |

These categories demand comprehensive treatment of the game's major systems and the full requested research dimensions. They do not demand copying every item stat, wiki page, line of dialogue, or copyrighted review. A source unavailable after a real search is an evidence limitation to record, not permission to invent its contents or silently skip an otherwise researchable category.

## Research and evidence rules

Use current web research for external facts, with primary developer documentation, original interviews, manuals, patch notes and firsthand criticism/player reports preferred. Community mechanics references can support documented rules with version and source-type qualifications. Use secondary reporting when necessary and label it. An attractive theory about success is analysis, not a measured cause.

Read substantive source content, not just search titles. When only an abstract, indexed excerpt, archive, or partial text is accessible, identify that limit. Seek alternate sources for missing facts. Do not pretend a store description is independent reception. Do not infer that a review's displayed present hours were its hours at original publication.

Videos are useful sources and reading routes, but metadata does not establish scene contents. Inspect a transcript or footage before attributing specific spoken/visual findings; written primary accounts can independently establish the same fact. Keep untouched original video recommendations labeled metadata-only until actually inspected. Do not invent timestamps, watched durations, firsthand gameplay or reproduction of anecdotes.

Version boundaries matter particularly for live-service games, sequels, ports, expansions and mods. Never transfer a rule from a sequel, a mod, an unreleased proposal, a community server, or an old patch without saying so. For unreleased/canceled games, research the documented design, demonstrations, production and reception of the promise; there cannot be a fabricated shipped-game player review.

Art/media is linked as reference, not copied into game assets. Paraphrase sources and respect quotation limits. Preserve specific facts while avoiding close reproduction of protected articles. Do not retrieve unrelated private files, credentials, saves or financial records.

## Per-game execution and completion gate

1. Read the game's prior chapter, relevant mechanics study, packet passages and their evidence limits. Identify gaps against R01–R14.
2. Mark that single game **in progress**, recording concrete gaps and the intended output path. Do not count its earlier selective study as a fresh full pass.
3. Research the missing dimensions. Follow material disagreements, retrieve actual source bodies, and retain useful detail. Check claims against the correct edition/date.
4. Write substantive sections and worked interactions. Retain prior material in its owners, link it, and add evidence where required. Check factual specificity, conflicting rules, unsupported causal claims and copyright limits.
5. Commit substantive progress no later than five minutes after the preceding work commit. A checkpoint is allowed, but must be labeled incomplete with exact remaining sections; never pad progress with empty files or false completion.
6. Verify the finished dossier against all R01–R14, its links and source anchors, the game's prior material and the actual diff. Commit it, then update the ledger with the output and commit reference. Only then advance to the next subject.
7. After all games, verify the complete roster, combined-chapter boundaries, cross-game navigation and packet-wide preservation. Report completed coverage separately from legitimately unavailable evidence.

**A completed pass** means the requested research categories have been substantively addressed for that subject and its documentation reviewed/committed. It does not mean every scientific cause of commercial success is proven, every private statistic is public, or every future update is covered. **Incomplete** means any applicable required category was not actually researched/documented, preservation remains unresolved for that subject, or the output has not been committed/reviewed.

Read this file and `research-progress.md`, inspect the current remote branch head, then fetch the active game's committed dossier and any named source checkpoint. Check for another writer's changes before updating shared files. Resume the exact unfinished sections, not the entire research from memory. Use the current blob SHA for each update and preserve unrelated work.

Keep the ledger's active subject, next action and unresolved evidence precise enough for a fresh conversation with no prior context. Source notes and substantive drafts belong with the relevant dossier; the ledger links them rather than becoming a second bibliography. Do not claim background work or future delivery. If execution is interrupted, the remote branch and this research-only ledger must truthfully show what is and is not complete.
