## 1. Campaign builds: identity, allocation, equipment and preparation

### Advancement is not Origins' attribute-allocation screen

Combat, quests, rifts, codex discoveries and creature research supply experience. Level advancement gives an ability point and increased health. The documented cap is 27; this is not a claim that an ordinary first campaign will reach it. Story-granted Inquisitor powers are another acquisition route, not simply purchased from the ordinary class trees. [B1](#b1)

Primary attributes are assigned automatically on leveling rather than freely distributed as in the earlier games. Equipment and selected passives add further attribute contributions. Thus a weak build can need a different allocation, better materials or more suitable equipment; another level is not the only possible response. [B2](#b2)

The Hero menu separates learned active abilities, their upgrades and always-active passives. The campaign permits **eight mapped active abilities**. Knowing a power and preparing it for convenient use are different states; a build cannot place every learned action on the bar simultaneously. Character-record changes also have an out-of-combat boundary. [B3](#b3)

**Interpretation:** a smaller active set can make a large tree usable without making the unused learning meaningless. It also creates a teaching obligation: players need to understand whether a problem is missing knowledge, a missing mapped action, insufficient resources or an incompatible target.

### Classes and specializations remain real commitments

Warrior, rogue and mage have distinct base ability families. Their specialization choices are:

| Base class | Specializations |
| --- | --- |
| Warrior | Champion, Reaver, Templar |
| Rogue | Artificer, Assassin, Tempest |
| Mage | Knight-Enchanter, Necromancer, Rift Mage |

After the relevant Skyhold progression, an operation brings the appropriate class's trainers. Accepting several training quests or preparing their items is not the final commitment: confirming the selected training supplies the specialization. Named companions have fixed specializations but can distribute their other abilities differently. A Tactician's Renewal resets eligible ability allocation, not race, class, biography or the specialization decision itself. [B4](#b4) [B5](#b5)

Specialization is more than a label. A **Knight-Enchanter's Spirit Blade** gains charge through other damaging abilities and spends it on its own attacks; related protection and mana-recovery passives favor particular positioning and activity. This is a feedback loop connecting offense, proximity and defense, not permission for every mage to become an invulnerable melee fighter. Patch-specific numerical rankings and claims that one build can solo everything are excluded. [B6](#b6)

### Resources, modifiers and institutional growth have different owners

Mage abilities use mana; warrior and rogue abilities use stamina, with cooldowns and eligible targets imposing additional restrictions. **Focus** is separate: party damage can fill each person's own meter, but spending one person's Focus does not consume everybody else's. Later institutional perks extend access to stronger Focus tiers. The Inquisitor's story-specific abilities have exceptions, so the guide's broad statement that every Focus power has three equivalent tiers should not be universalized. [B7](#b7)

Equipment composition supplies another layer. A schematic's primary slot determines basic attack or armor properties; utility materials add attributes, while offense and defense slots supply other benefits. An optional masterwork effect is not an ordinary utility-slot statistic. Choosing compatible materials therefore changes the resulting build even without spending another ability point. [B8](#b8)

**Interpretation:** personal level, prepared repertoire, equipment, an individual's combat resource and institutional perks are connected but not interchangeable. A player's request to become better at one role should identify which layer actually constrains that role. More Power at the War Table is not automatically more spell damage.

## 2. Multiplayer: a separate acquisition and return loop

### What a participant does

EA explicitly announced a four-person cooperative mode separate from the campaign, made by a dedicated group drawing on Dragon Age and Mass Effect experience. Participants play Inquisition agents rather than jointly controlling one friend's Inquisitor and companions. Missions involve cooperating through combat areas, meeting objectives and resolving a final confrontation. Their progression does not import the campaign's romantic or political decisions. [C1](#c1)

The operational loop is **select an unlocked agent → allocate abilities and equip supplies → join a mission → fight and assist the group → receive earned progress → reorganize, acquire or salvage equipment → try another mission**. The documented mode allows **four active abilities**, not the campaign's eight, and equipment/ability choices cannot be freely replaced in the field. Experience is settled after success or failure rather than granting mid-battle level-ups. Limited resupply and downed-player assistance make preparation and cooperation consequential. [C2](#c2)

**Interpretation:** a smaller active set makes another human's complementary tools matter. It can also make a poor lobby decision costly for the whole mission. A fair interface needs to communicate missing coverage before launch rather than only explain the failure afterward.

### Unlocking a person through equipment

The initial agents are the Legionnaire, Keeper and Archer. Other agents can be unlocked by acquiring or crafting the appropriate starting armor. For example, **Reaver's Mail** is an access object as well as equipment. Salvaging unwanted drops provides the material categories needed for another agent's armor; it is not merely deleting trash. A directly obtained qualifying armor can also unlock its character. [C3](#c3)

Armor and upgrades are craftable, but **multiplayer weapons themselves come from loot**. Some early guides loosely describe crafting weapons and armor together; that wording must not import the campaign's weapon-manufacturing system into this mode. Likewise, multiplayer armor-upgrade replacement has different removal rules from ordinary campaign equipment handling. [C4](#c4) [C5](#c5)

The initial twelve-character inventory is historical. **Dragonslayer**, released May 5, 2015, adds a dragon-battle map and the Avvar, Virtuoso and Isabela/Duelist. Later additions include Pala/Silent Sister and Hissera/Saarebas. These are agent-role additions, not three more campaign companions or proof that every original-console configuration received every later change. [C6](#c6) [C7](#c7)

### Currency, randomness and persistence

Mission gold buys chests and supplies; **Platinum** is purchased currency. Chests supply randomized equipment or potions, not a guarantee that spending money yields the particular item a player wants. The multiplayer inventory and potion stock support its agent roster, with class/weapon restrictions still applying. They are not the campaign's pantry, crafted schematics or political resources. [C8](#c8)

At **level 20**, an agent can be promoted and returned to level 1. Promotion grants a class-related Constitution, Cunning or Willpower improvement shared by the multiplayer roster, alongside Prestige. A personal restart therefore coexists with longer-lived account-level growth. It does not send a newly controllable companion into the single-player campaign or confer current matchmaking readiness by itself. [C8](#c8)

**Interpretation:** there are at least three reward horizons: finishing this mission, developing this agent and improving the roster across resets. That structure can make repeats worthwhile, but random drops and repeated early levels can also feel like a gate before the desired play. A cooperative system should distinguish voluntary experimentation from obligatory grinding for acceptance by a group.

## 3. Three additional cross-system situations

These supplement, rather than replace or renumber, the main dossier's ten cases. They are constructed illustrations, not observed play sessions.

### S01 — a specialization changes the useful sequence

A mage gains Knight-Enchanter training, prepares Spirit Blade and complementary tools, then uses other attacks before spending the accumulated charge. The next choice concerns when to approach and when to preserve protection rather than simply repeat one strongest-looking spell. **Limit:** knowledge, mapped slots, mana, position and target eligibility all remain constraints; a specialization does not erase the ordinary class's resource problems. [B6](#b6)

### S02 — surplus becomes a new participant

A multiplayer player wants a Reaver but lacks the necessary materials. They salvage unwanted drops and craft its starting armor. **Result:** an equipment decision expands playable roles. **Next choice:** develop that agent or continue using the old one with a better understanding of what the group needs. **Limit:** random chest acquisition and actual material requirements remain; merely knowing the armor's name cannot create it. [C3](#c3)

### S03 — accept a local reset for a broader benefit

An agent reaches level 20. Promoting lowers its immediate level while improving the multiplayer roster's persistent attributes. **Next choice:** replay that agent at suitable difficulty or switch to another developed one. **Limit:** a shared bonus is not a substitute for a coherent current build or coordinated play, and campaign progression is unaffected. [C8](#c8)

**Transfer hypothesis:** separate the identity being preserved from the capability being reset. A system can support learning, equipment recovery and roster-wide growth without pretending that a character's entire biography was rewritten. Different currencies of progress need visible owners.

## 4. Original completion evidence, preserved as a historical access record

The following findings were recorded during the earlier September 26 completion pass. “Newly inspected” in that checkpoint meant new to that pass, not fresh retrieval during this operational remedy.

### Trespasser: power after victory

Nicholas Tan, [GameRevolution review](https://www.gamerevolution.com/review/72685-dragon-age-inquisition-trespasser-review), September 10, 2015; substantive PC review body inspected. The Exalted Council asks what legitimacy the Inquisition retains after defeating Corypheus. Ferelden worries about sovereignty; Orlais wants influence over the organization. The epilogue advances two years and closes access to the base campaign's open activities. Tan values the character farewells, interlocking levels and stronger resolution, while criticizing a consequential betrayal's abbreviated reaction and the Anchor's tendency to dominate combat. His roughly ten-hour experience is an individual review duration, not a completion-time distribution.

**Research interpretation:** gaining authority and surrendering authority are different playable problems. A leadership system needs a constituency, jurisdiction and sunset condition, not merely a larger resource bar. This interpretation does not establish that Inquisition simulates independent states or that OpenLegend implements such a system.

### Sound and promotion

Conal Pierse, [Inside the Temple of Dragons with Composer Trevor Morris](https://blog.bioware.com/2014/11/12/inside-the-temple-of-dragons-with-composer-trevor-morris/), BioWare, November 12, 2014; page text inspected. The official account identifies Ocean Way Studios, Nashville, and strings, brass and choir. It also says the behind-the-scenes video was first shown to Dragon Age newsletter subscribers. This establishes a documented promotional touchpoint and orchestral production, not an audience-conversion rate. Video footage has not been watched in this pass.

### Commerce and product boundaries

[EA's current Inquisition product page](https://www.ea.com/games/dragon-age/dragon-age-inquisition), retrieved September 26, 2026, describes the Game of the Year package, storefront links, EA-account/app requirements, optional virtual-currency purchases and paid random items. Its displayed October 6, 2015 date is a package date, not the original 2014 release. The former `/dlc/single-player/trespasser` URL redirects to this general product page and was not a freshly inspected detailed Trespasser specification.

[WN Hub's September 18, 2024 report](https://wnhub.io/news/investment/item-45517) reproduces Mark Darrah's September 17 statement that Inquisition had sold over 12 million units and exceeded EA's internal projections. Treat this as an attributed developer statement relayed by reporting, not an independently audited 2026 financial disclosure. Do not convert lifetime units into active users, full-price revenue or profit; platform and discount mix are not supplied.
