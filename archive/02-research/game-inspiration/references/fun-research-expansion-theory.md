# Beyond the dossiers — critique, design theory and evaluation

**Research expansion, October 14, 2026.** This paper reviews the earlier [main synthesis](../essays/fun-and-sustainable-engagement.md) rather than assuming its conclusions are correct. Companion papers cover [mobile and recent games](fun-research-expansion-mobile-and-recent.md) and [AI-era play](fun-research-expansion-ai.md). These are research interpretations and candidate experiments, not new OpenLegend feature requirements. The earlier [foundations](fun-engagement-and-compulsion-research.md) remain the owner of basic terminology and their five original sources.

## 1. Assessment of the earlier assessment

The earlier synthesis's central distinction survives: enjoyment, appreciation, return and difficulty stopping are not interchangeable. Its strongest contribution is separating things that superficially resemble one another—learning versus grinding, useful cooperation versus an access gate, a fictional setback versus an interface failure. Its weaknesses concern explanatory precision, evidence strength and omitted contexts, not a need to reverse every principle.

## 2. Design theories as lenses, not laws

<a id="t01"></a>
### T01 — MDA: connect a rule to an experience through actual play

**Source:** Robin Hunicke, Marc LeBlanc and Robert Zubek, *MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research* (2004). [Author-hosted paper](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf) · [AAAI record](https://new.aaai.org/Library/Workshops/2004/ws04-04-001.php).

**Inspected:** paper text and rendered pages 1–3, including the diagrams. **Type:** design framework, not a controlled test. Mechanics are the specified parts and rules; dynamics concern their behavior in play; aesthetics name the desired experience. Its non-exhaustive vocabulary includes sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression and submission/pastime. These are not eight scientifically established player types. The paper also considers feedback effects that can make an early advantage reduce later engagement.

**Our use:** start with the experience, then work backward to a plausible playable cause. For an OpenLegend rescue, “fellowship” might mean noticing a partner's contribution; a plausible cause is one person creating an opening another can use, with an intelligible result. Merely adding a second actor or a cooperation statistic does not establish it. Conversely, atmosphere can be an intended experience without being justified by a combat multiplier. The framework organizes a hypothesis; observing players must still challenge it.

<a id="t02"></a>
### T02 — Pattern learning: preserve something worth getting better at

**Source:** Raph Koster, [*An Atomic Theory of Fun Game Design*](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/01/24/an-atomic-theory-of-fun-game-design/), written in 2004 and posted in 2012. **Inspected:** author's article. **Type:** historical practitioner theory. The page explicitly says it no longer represents his current understanding.

The essay treats challenges as nested patterns involving preparation, interaction and feedback. A visually new enemy that accepts exactly the old solution may add little new challenge. This is a useful account of learning and recombination, not sufficient evidence that all enjoyment is learning, that every game needs punishment, or that solved activities should always become obsolete.

**Our use:** distinguish recognizing a rule, executing it and applying it in a different situation. An invented device is stronger evidence of new play when the player subsequently applies its learned behavior somewhere else. Repeatedly changing the underlying rule merely to manufacture novelty would undermine that learning. Equally, someone may enjoy a familiar performance, social routine or landscape after mastering its rules; that is not a failure to understand fun.

<a id="t03"></a>
### T03 — GameFlow: useful inspection questions, narrow initial validation

**Sources:** Penelope Sweetser and Peta Wyeth, *GameFlow: A Model for Evaluating Player Enjoyment in Games* (2005), [DOI 10.1145/1077246.1077253](https://doi.org/10.1145/1077246.1077253); Sweetser's [2020 retrospective](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/7c1da302-bcb8-444c-acf1-adbb10503309).

**Inspected:** indexed publisher abstract and institutional retrospective abstract; direct retrieval of the original full article failed. The model organizes concentration, challenge, skills, control, goals, feedback, immersion and social interaction. Its original evaluation compared expert reviews of two real-time strategy games. Later adoption demonstrates usefulness to researchers and practitioners, not universal experimental confirmation across genres.

**Our use:** ask which demand the player is trying to master and whether attention is spent on that demand or on deciphering the interface. Do not collapse the model into “keep difficulty exactly equal to skill” or invent an optimal success percentage. A frightening contest, an easy expressive activity and a meditative routine make different promises. Flow is one useful account of absorption, not a compulsory emotional state for an entire game.

<a id="t04"></a>
### T04 — Game feel has more than one mechanism

**Source:** Martin Pichlmair and Mads Johansen, *Designing Game Feel: A Survey*, *IEEE Transactions on Games* 14(2), 2022, [DOI 10.1109/TG.2021.3072241](https://doi.org/10.1109/TG.2021.3072241) · [author-institution abstract](https://researcher.itu.dk/p/en/research-outputs/designing-game-feel-a-survey).

**Inspected:** institutional abstract and publication metadata, not the full accepted manuscript. **Type:** synthesis of more than 200 academic and practitioner sources. It distinguishes physicality/tuning, amplification/juicing and support/streamlining: coherent movement, expressive feedback and assistance translating intention into action. It is not an experiment proving more effects always improve enjoyment.

**Our use:** examine a whole action: selecting a target, moving into position, committing, seeing or hearing contact, understanding the result and recovering control. A damage flash cannot compensate for movement that feels unrelated to input. An accurate simulation can still communicate weight poorly. Conversely, added shake, particles or speech can obscure a small important cue. Prefer observable comparisons at the same task, with reduced-motion and non-audio routes considered. No universal millisecond threshold or mandatory visual-effects style follows from this source.

<a id="t05"></a>
### T05 — Suspense can be enjoyable even when dominance feels more competent

**Source:** Sami Abuhamdeh, Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi and Baland Jalal, *Enjoying the Possibility of Defeat: Outcome Uncertainty, Suspense, and Intrinsic Motivation*, *Motivation and Emotion* 39, 2015. [Institutional abstract](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/enjoying-the-possibility-of-defeat-outcome-uncertainty-suspense-a/) · [DOI 10.1007/s11031-014-9425-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-014-9425-2).

**Inspected:** primary institutional abstract and metadata. In two studies, closer competitive outcomes were more enjoyable than large victories even though the latter increased perceived competence; suspense helped explain the relationship. A subsequent choice measure favored previously suspenseful games, with performance concern moderating the effect. This is evidence from a particular competitive task, not a universal win-rate prescription or proof that uncertainty about controls is beneficial.

**Our use:** preserve uncertainty about whether a sound plan will succeed while reducing uncertainty about what an input means. Useful opposition can force adaptation without concealing the rules. For OpenLegend, a rival with a comprehensible competing objective is a better hypothesis than either an automatically compliant character or an opaque random refusal. This remains a proposed application, not a measured transfer from the experimental game.

## 3. Measure the mechanism separately from the outcome

<a id="t06"></a>
### T06 — Basic Needs in Games Scale: lack of satisfaction is not the same as frustration

**Source:** Nick Ballou, Alena Denisova, Richard M. Ryan, C. Scott Rigby and Sebastian Deterding, *The Basic Needs in Games Scale (BANGS)*, *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* 188, 2024, [DOI 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103289](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103289) · [author guide](https://nickballou.com/docs/bangs/).

**Inspected:** indexed article body, author documentation landing page and publication record; direct publisher rendering was unavailable. Five studies with 1,246 unique participants developed measures separating satisfaction and frustration of autonomy, competence and relatedness. The paper reports limitations, including weaker aspects of autonomy-frustration measurement and a sample that does not establish universality across children or mobile populations.

**Our use:** “I did not particularly connect with that character” differs from “the game pressured me into maintaining that relationship.” Likewise, a neutral task differs from feeling incompetent because the system contradicts a sensible expectation. Check both the desired experience and active barriers; do not assume removing frustration automatically creates fascination. These examples illustrate the distinction rather than reproduce questionnaire items or establish clinical thresholds.

<a id="t07"></a>
### T07 — Player Experience Inventory: usable controls and meaningful experience are different levels

**Source:** Vero Vanden Abeele, Katta Spiel, Lennart Nacke, Daniel Johnson and Kathrin Gerling, *Development and Validation of the Player Experience Inventory*, *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* 135, 2020, [DOI 10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370).

**Inspected:** indexed publisher abstract; full-page retrieval failed. Development involved 64 experts and 529 players. The scale separates functional consequences of design from psychosocial experience rather than treating usability, immersion and meaning as synonyms.

**Our use:** successful command entry can improve without increasing enjoyment; enjoyment can improve without making a system easier to operate. The AI comparison that reports better game-experience scores but no statistically significant overall usability improvement illustrates why those outcomes should be kept separate. [Talking-to-Build](fun-research-expansion-ai.md#a03)

<a id="t08"></a>
### T08 — A shorter questionnaire is not automatically interchangeable

**Sources:** [*miniPXI: Development and Validation of an Eleven-Item Measure*](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/minipxi-development-and-validation-of-an-eleven-item-measure-of-t/), 2022; [Haider and colleagues, *Test-Retest Reliability of the Player Experience Inventory and Its Mini Version*, 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19516).

**Inspected:** institutional and preprint abstracts. The abbreviated instrument was developed with expert and player studies; the later 100-participant, four-game study reports more mixed reliability for the mini version than for the longer measures. This qualifies convenience, not a blanket rejection of short feedback questions.

**Our use:** choose a small set of constructs relevant to the question and retain the instrument's instructions and scoring. Do not assemble preferred individual items and label the result a validated new scale. For early qualitative work, a plain enjoyment question plus a specific observed incident can be useful, provided it is reported as that—not a diagnosis, a causal model or a precise long-term change measure.

<a id="t09"></a>
### T09 — Designer incentives deserve their own examination

**Source:** José P. Zagal, Staffan Björk and Chris Lewis, *Dark Patterns in the Design of Games*, FDG 2013, pp. 39–46. [Primary institutional record and abstract](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/177148).

**Inspected:** institutional abstract and bibliographic record, not the full paper. The authors examine conflicts between creator and player interests and the subtleties of classifying design patterns. This is conceptual ethical analysis, not proof that any particular timer, reward or social mechanic causes harm.

**Our use:** examine who benefits when the player continues, pays or invites someone. A short match may coexist with a burdensome acquisition system; a compelling companion may coexist with pressure to buy additional conversation. Consider the complete offer, clear costs and credible exit rather than blessing an entire product because one component is enjoyable. This is an evaluation principle, not a claim that OpenLegend currently has those monetization features.

## 4. A more discriminating working model

The following is **our synthesis**, not a new validated theory:

**The player brings an intention and capabilities; the game offers a comprehensible possibility; action produces a felt and interpretable result; opposition, uncertainty or expression gives that action value; the result changes what the player knows, can do, cares about or chooses next.** Sometimes the desired next act is simply to perform it again. Sometimes it is to finish.

Four kinds of uncertainty should be reviewed separately. **Execution uncertainty** concerns whether the player can perform a known action. **Outcome uncertainty** concerns opposition and chance. **Discovery uncertainty** concerns facts or patterns not yet understood. **Interface uncertainty** concerns what the system accepted or what its controls mean. These are analytical categories: a mystery can deliberately blur some facts, but a failure to register input should not be defended as suspense. AI adds a further question—whether the same wording or world rule will be interpreted consistently—which can interfere with all four.

For a prototype, write a short hypothesis before changing it: **intended experience → proposed interaction → expected observation → competing explanation → disconfirming observation**. Example: visible cooperation should make a companion feel useful; compare equally capable help with and without a readable contribution; ask what the companion actually did and whether that changed the player's plan. More dialogue alone might increase novelty or reading time instead. A player who cannot identify a contribution, or who feels deprived of their own role, provides evidence against this particular design.

## 5. OpenLegend mapping and evidence discipline

The product mapping uses repository documentation at GitHub main **`1700f3b23a39acfdd14f3a6ab34c8ce330847bc3`**, not a fresh runtime qualification. [Playable game first](https://github.com/Macrofold/OpenLegend/blob/1700f3b23a39acfdd14f3a6ab34c8ce330847bc3/docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) prioritizes a complete adventure containing a real instance of practical invention and an independent, observation-grounded character consequence. [The README](https://github.com/Macrofold/OpenLegend/blob/1700f3b23a39acfdd14f3a6ab34c8ce330847bc3/README.md) distinguishes existing native play and finite recipe invention from broader targets.

The research therefore argues for **a complete experience comparison, not a bigger feature catalogue**: a readable obstacle, a viable known approach, an optional genuinely useful invention, an actor with their own purpose and a result used again. Review movement and feedback alongside novelty; preserve the character's ability to disagree; test a second use and a later return rather than only the first successful demonstration. Existing priorities already reject compulsory camp administration and guaranteed persuasion. These findings refine how to evaluate that direction; they do not authorize replacing it, inventing new maintenance gates or implementing a new subsystem.

**Limits of this run:** targeted rather than systematic literature search; English-accessible material; mixed full papers, indexed bodies and abstracts; no new participant recruitment, gameplay, meta-analysis or production-model calls. Study sample sizes are reported where visible, not reconstructed. Theory, human studies, vendor claims and our proposed applications retain separate labels. Additional recent cases broaden the comparison set but do not enlarge the old 148-subject completion claim.
