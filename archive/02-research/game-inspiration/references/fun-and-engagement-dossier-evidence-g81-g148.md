## G81–G100 — role identity, tactical control and world-scale activity

| Read | Subject and inspected dossier sections | Contribution to the synthesis | Counterweight / evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ✓ G81 | [Final Fantasy IV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-iv.md), §§1–8 | Fixed character verbs and story-driven party turnover renew tactical problems. Cecil's changed role makes a narrative transformation change what the player does; useful field magic connects combat capability to world access. | Authored turnover can reduce desired control over a favorite party. Active/Wait settings and save placement change decision pressure and retry cost; the same plot does not make the original and remakes mechanically identical. |
| ✓ G82 | [Final Fantasy V](../dossiers/final-fantasy-v.md), §§3–9 | Transferable job abilities make practice leave useful options after a career change. Cross-job combinations and creature-derived learning reward understanding relationships between systems, not just raising levels. | A build needs equipment, acquired spells and compatible abilities, not merely an unlocked job. The dossier preserves the designers' intention to allow default-job play; a headline customization system need not force constant switching. |
| ✓ G83 | [Final Fantasy VI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-vi.md), §§2.6–6 | An ensemble supplies different verbs and reasons to care; Magicite can be a teacher, summon and build-shaping object. Rule-changing relics make equipment alter decisions. | Shared high-end magic can blur distinctive roles, while level-up optimization encourages fiddly equipment timing. Some editions preserve EXP after defeat and others do not; reliable recovery expectations matter more than a generic claim that loss is good. |
| ✓ G84 | [Final Fantasy VII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-vii.md), §§3–7 | Linked Materia makes composition visible. Equipment trades immediate power against learning rate, while portable learned objects and character-specific Limits separate flexible roles from personal identity. | A spell's effect depends on its compatible partner, socket placement, growth and user. Choice breadth needs readable relationships; original-game mechanics are not interchangeable with the modern reinterpretations or later ports. |
| ✓ G85 | [Final Fantasy VIII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-viii.md), §§3–8 | Cards, refinement, magic stocks and junctions form alternative progression routes. An enjoyable cultural side activity can feed the main adventure without being isolated reward filler. | Stocking magic for stats can discourage actually casting it. Repeated Draw becomes tedious when alternative acquisition is poorly discovered; a rich expert route does not excuse a dull novice route or dependence on a wiki. |
| ✓ G86 | [Final Fantasy IX](../dossiers/final-fantasy-ix.md), §§4–9 | Equipment teaches durable abilities, active support choices remain bounded, and synthesis gives old objects a further purpose. Objects can teach without making every person interchangeable. | Teaching, consuming and replacing the same object creates a real tradeoff but also retention burden. Repeated failed stealing can turn a character-defining action into stalled boss pacing; role expression is not enough to justify repetition. |
| ✓ G87 | [Final Fantasy X](../dossiers/final-fantasy-x.md), §§3–7 | A visible future turn order makes complex timing inspectable. Mid-battle specialists and a guided-then-branching progression grid stage freedom instead of exposing every decision immediately. | Knowing a path, being able to move and having activation materials are separate requirements. Greater build control adds interface work; the Expert Grid serves a different starting need than the default guided path. |
| ✓ G88 | [Final Fantasy XI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xi.md), §§3–7 | One socially continuous character can pursue several careers. Primary/support jobs and practice-based expertise support changing roles without discarding the person's accumulated identity. | Preserving a career's progress is not having all careers' levels, equipment permissions and abilities active together. The dossier's account of a player-discovered tanking role inspiring a profession supports learning from play, not a claim that every emergent tactic deserves official adoption. |
| ✓ G89 | [Final Fantasy XII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xii.md), §§2–6 | Gambits move participation from repeated orders into choosing priorities, testing a policy and intervening. Saved configurations preserve work while leaving situational judgment to the player. | A general attack rule above healing can defeat the player's intended plan. Automation needs understandable priority and override; job-reset availability is edition-bound, so permanent commitment is not a timeless property of the game. |
| ✓ G90 | [Final Fantasy XIII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xiii.md), §§2–6 | Automated individual commands can leave meaningful control over whole-party roles and their timing. Restoring health between fights removes maintenance while keeping danger and decisions inside an encounter. | Removing ordinary MP does not remove item, technique or opportunity costs. Automatic command selection is worthwhile only insofar as a player values the remaining tactical responsibility; the direct sequels change that responsibility rather than simply adding content. |
| ✓ G91 | [Final Fantasy XIV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xiv.md), §§3–6 | Crafting can be an ordered resource-and-quality problem rather than a timer. Combat, clothing, homes and social occasions offer different return purposes; role matching can reduce organization work. | Matching roles does not match intentions. Sync can preserve access while removing familiar actions; housing scarcity and weekly acquisition rules impose different burdens from encounter difficulty. |
| ✓ G92 | [Final Fantasy XV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xv.md), §§3–6 | Rest connects preparation, settled experience and selecting photographs of a particular journey. Companions contribute through food, finds and memories as well as fighting. | Several valuable systems sharing one stop can also create awkward reward-timing optimization. Automatic photographs need selection, and recognizable everyday usefulness does not guarantee emotional depth for every player. |
| ✓ G93 | [Final Fantasy XVI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xvi.md), §§3–7 | Training isolates execution problems; scoring and constrained trials give a completed encounter another purpose. Assistance can shift attention while leaving a meaningful action game. | Ordinary sidequest mechanics and meaningful writing must be evaluated separately. Attachment, narrative agency and direct companion control are different achievements; action depth does not require adding a survival economy. |
| ✓ G94 | [Warcraft: Orcs & Humans](../dossiers/warcraft-orcs-and-humans.md), §§3–6 | Healing, scouting, concealment and corpse use make a small vocabulary situational. A finite-force rescue changes the value of units without adding permanent character levels. | Dominant late summons can narrow actual choices. Campaign pressure is not necessarily a symmetric player economy; expense and recognizable responses can create attachment without simulated personalities. |
| ✓ G95 | [Warcraft II](../dossiers/warcraft-ii.md), §§3–6 | Transport composition, detection and changed terrain create strategic problems beyond damage. Different map arrangements can renew familiar tools without a new art set. | Protecting an essential hero can encourage avoiding participation until an objective requires their presence. Hidden exceptions to apparently clear spell names can punish reasonable expectations; balance judgments are version-bound. |
| ✓ G96 | [Warcraft III](../dossiers/warcraft-iii.md), §§3–6 | Neutral camps exchange health, time and position for growth. Hero revival preserves identity without undoing the lost army or opportunity; counterplay can make an automatic habit worth revising. | More force can reduce future income without being worth its unused power. Recovered possessions do not restore the whole situation, and an editor's objects need actual objectives and behavior. |
| ✓ G97 | [World of Warcraft](../dossiers/world-of-warcraft.md), §§3–6 | Friends, appearances, roles and places can retain value after equipment loses relative power. Specialization and crafting orders connect one person's competence to another's goal. | Shared schedules can coordinate play or make an absence feel like debt. More weekly reward options do not mean receiving all of them; mutual need can produce cooperation, bureaucracy or exclusion. |
| ✓ G98 | [Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag](../dossiers/assassins-creed-iv-black-flag.md), §§3–7 | Land-based acquisition can advance a naval goal, and a captured ship offers competing uses. A collected song changes later travel rather than only a statistic; familiar controls can acquire a different moral purpose. | Repeated compulsory restarts can conflict with free-roaming improvisation. Crew atmosphere is not evidence of general social simulation, and changed story labels cannot by themselves correct a contradictory reward loop. |
| ✓ G99 | [Assassin's Creed III](../dossiers/assassins-creed-iii.md), §§3–6 | Helping named residents changes production capability and useful expedition equipment. Ordinary work and local pastimes make home more than a menu. | Waiting for a qualifying animation can turn observation into checklist friction. Optional mastery conditions need to be distinguished from mission failure; fast travel and dispatch clocks do not necessarily advance together. |
| ✓ G100 | [Assassin's Creed Valhalla](../dossiers/assassins-creed-valhalla.md), §§3–6 | Durable equipment, a recognizable home and episodic commitments organize a long journey. A local pastime can be worthwhile without feeding combat power; a separate run mode changes commitment and recovery. | Episodic structure still exhausts when too many arcs repeat or delay desired closure. A reset needs a changed next decision, not merely another increment of persistent power. |

## G101–G120 — relationships, discovery and consequential participation

| ✓ G101 | [Mass Effect](../dossiers/mass-effect.md), §§3–7 | A remembered favor can provide another route through a later conflict. Returning to a known crew gives discoveries social context, while nonlethal tools make a rescue intention actionable. | Frequent low-impact equipment comparisons become clerical. A finite ending can fulfill the promise, but an unclear point of no return creates accidental lost access; authored consequences are not unlimited branching. |
| ✓ G102 | [Mass Effect 2](../dossiers/mass-effect-2.md), §§3–7 | A climax can make earlier preparation and understanding a person's expertise matter. Fewer recognizable tools can preserve role identity while reducing inventory work. | Completing a mission, unlocking a power and retaining loyalty are different outcomes. | ✓ G103 | [Mass Effect 3](../dossiers/mass-effect-3.md), §§4–7 | Mixed moral expression need not weaken persuasive capability. Specific histories and later visits make a shared war personally meaningful; cooperative objectives can value support over individual damage. | An aggregate contribution score can flatten morally different events. Urgent missions use different deadline rules, and performing clue-clicking is not necessarily making a deduction. |
| ✓ G104 | [Mass Effect: Andromeda](../dossiers/mass-effect-andromeda.md), §§4–6 | Practical infrastructure changes the cost of a later journey. Tone choices and local political differences can express a character even when the broader campaign reconverges. | Several growing percentages can obscure the purpose of work. A settlement threshold does not grant every missing permission, and a cinematic resolution does not automatically complete every local project. |
| ✓ G105 | [Mass Effect Galaxy](../dossiers/mass-effect-galaxy.md), §§2–5 | Compact powers, route order and a concrete favor can change a short adventure. Assisted attacks can leave meaningful target, position and timing decisions. | Removing input is not enough when the remaining encounters repeat unchanged. Automatic transitions can penalize the sensible wish to secure a room before collecting; input calibration failures are not tactical mistakes. |
| ✓ G106 | [Mass Effect Infiltrator](../dossiers/mass-effect-infiltrator.md), §§3–6 | Separate style, speed and health goals make a replay different from merely surviving. A small arsenal can support distinct plans without endless equipment tiers. | A failure-screen purchase route does not prove paying is necessary or that the encounter teaches well. Finishing an encounter, collecting its loot and crediting another account are different events; the historical online link is not a current service promise. |
| ✓ G107 | [Octopath Traveler](../dossiers/octopath-traveler.md), combat/progression, items, quests and constructed situations | Paired town actions offer different budgets and risks; preparation can open a vulnerability rather than maximize immediate damage. Named people's possessions connect exploration with later capability. | Combat cooperation can conflict with isolated story scenes. An overdeveloped locked protagonist, neglected reserves and repeated specialist backtracking create costs; the optional roster-wide test has a different contract from ordinary chapter completion. |
| ✓ G108 | [Octopath Traveler II](../dossiers/octopath-traveler-ii.md), combat/progression, objects and closure | Personal rule-changing powers coexist with transferable expertise. Restoration, invention and a ship connect growth to tangible projects; a small schedule mismatch can become a noncombat problem. | Faster playback cannot make a solved encounter a new decision. Repeated party swaps for minor transactions become administration; individual endings, shared closure and optional superbosses are distinct goals. |
| ✓ G109 | [Knights of the Old Republic](../dossiers/star-wars-knights-of-the-old-republic.md), companions, world/quests and constructed situations | Choosing a companion for their perspective can be as legitimate as optimizing combat. An unexpected passenger or creature makes travel leave a small consequence at home; winning a fight can open a conversation. | A final allegiance choice can override an earlier pattern without erasing learned capability. Meaningful authored reactivity does not require, or demonstrate, an unrestricted social simulator. |
| ✓ G110 | [Knights of the Old Republic II](../dossiers/star-wars-knights-of-the-old-republic-ii.md), equipment/traversal, progression and people | Expertise matters across crafting, interpretation and preparation; services reinforce a particular companion's identity. Alternate relationships provide a finite reason to replay. | Repeated temporary equipment swaps can turn known solutions into bookkeeping. A repaired vehicle must solve a real travel problem, and extra branches do not prove players enjoy repeating the path to them. |
