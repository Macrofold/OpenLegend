# Fun and engagement — dossier evidence, G81–G148

**Topic-focused review completed: October 14, 2026.** This continuation of the [G01–G80 evidence map](fun-and-engagement-dossier-evidence.md) records inspected mechanics, interpretations and evidence boundaries. The [roster](../research-roster.md) owns subject identities. This is research synthesis, not a new implementation specification or a repeat of the original research-completion audits.

**Coverage: G81–G148 reviewed here; combined coverage is the original 148 roster subjects, plus the Dragon Age series supplement in the first volume.** A check means the indicated relevant dossier sections were inspected for this topic; it does not claim personal gameplay, rereading every original external source, or a clinical/causal study of player behavior. Interpretations below inherit each dossier's edition, source and access limits. | ✓ G81 | [Final Fantasy IV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-iv.md), §§1–8 | Fixed character verbs and story-driven party turnover renew tactical problems. Cecil's changed role makes a narrative transformation change what the player does; useful field magic connects combat capability to world access. | Authored turnover can reduce desired control over a favorite party. | ✓ G84 | [Final Fantasy VII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-vii.md), §§3–7 | Linked Materia makes composition visible. Repeated failed stealing can turn a character-defining action into stalled boss pacing; role expression is not enough to justify repetition. |
| ✓ G87 | [Final Fantasy X](../dossiers/final-fantasy-x.md), §§3–7 | A visible future turn order makes complex timing inspectable. Mid-battle specialists and a guided-then-branching progression grid stage freedom instead of exposing every decision immediately. | Knowing a path, being able to move and having activation materials are separate requirements. Greater build control adds interface work; the Expert Grid serves a different starting need than the default guided path. |
| ✓ G88 | [Final Fantasy XI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xi.md), §§3–7 | One socially continuous character can pursue several careers. Primary/support jobs and practice-based expertise support changing roles without discarding the person's accumulated identity. | Preserving a career's progress is not having all careers' levels, equipment permissions and abilities active together. The dossier's account of a player-discovered tanking role inspiring a profession supports learning from play, not a claim that every emergent tactic deserves official adoption. |
| ✓ G89 | [Final Fantasy XII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xii.md), §§2–6 | Gambits move participation from repeated orders into choosing priorities, testing a policy and intervening. Saved configurations preserve work while leaving situational judgment to the player. | A general attack rule above healing can defeat the player's intended plan. Automation needs understandable priority and override; job-reset availability is edition-bound, so permanent commitment is not a timeless property of the game. |
| ✓ G90 | [Final Fantasy XIII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xiii.md), §§2–6 | Automated individual commands can leave meaningful control over whole-party roles and their timing. Restoring health between fights removes maintenance while keeping danger and decisions inside an encounter. | Removing ordinary MP does not remove item, technique or opportunity costs. Automatic command selection is worthwhile only insofar as a player values the remaining tactical responsibility; the direct sequels change that responsibility rather than simply adding content. |
| ✓ G91 | [Final Fantasy XIV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xiv.md), §§3–6 | Crafting can be an ordered resource-and-quality problem rather than a timer. Combat, clothing, homes and social occasions offer different return purposes; role matching can reduce organization work. | Matching roles does not match intentions. Sync can preserve access while removing familiar actions; housing scarcity and weekly acquisition rules impose different burdens from encounter difficulty. |
| ✓ G92 | [Final Fantasy XV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xv.md), §§3–6 | Rest connects preparation, settled experience and selecting photographs of a particular journey. Companions contribute through food, finds and memories as well as fighting. | Several valuable systems sharing one stop can also create awkward reward-timing optimization. Automatic photographs need selection, and recognizable everyday usefulness does not guarantee emotional depth for every player. |
| ✓ G93 | [Final Fantasy XVI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xvi.md), §§3–7 | Training isolates execution problems; scoring and constrained trials give a completed encounter another purpose. Assistance can shift attention while leaving a meaningful action game. | Ordinary sidequest mechanics and meaningful writing must be evaluated separately. Attachment, narrative agency and direct companion control are different achievements; action depth does not require adding a survival economy. |
| ✓ G94 | [Warcraft: Orcs & Humans](../dossiers/warcraft-orcs-and-humans.md), §§3–6 | Healing, scouting, concealment and corpse use make a small vocabulary situational. A finite-force rescue changes the value of units without adding permanent character levels. | Dominant late summons can narrow actual choices. Campaign pressure is not necessarily a symmetric player economy; expense and recognizable responses can create attachment without simulated personalities. |
| ✓ G95 | [Warcraft II](../dossiers/warcraft-ii.md), §§3–6 | Transport composition, detection and changed terrain create strategic problems beyond damage. Different map arrangements can renew familiar tools without a new art set. | Protecting an essential hero can encourage avoiding participation until an objective requires their presence. Hidden exceptions to apparently clear spell names can punish reasonable expectations; balance judgments are version-bound. |
| ✓ G96 | [Warcraft III](../dossiers/warcraft-iii.md), §§3–6 | Neutral camps exchange health, time and position for growth. Hero revival preserves identity without undoing the lost army or opportunity; counterplay can make an automatic habit worth revising. | More force can reduce future income without being worth its unused power. Recovered possessions do not restore the whole situation, and an editor's objects need actual objectives and behavior. |
| ✓ G97 | [World of Warcraft](../dossiers/world-of-warcraft.md), §§3–6 | Friends, appearances, roles and places can retain value after equipment loses relative power. Specialization and crafting orders connect one person's competence to another's goal. | Shared schedules can coordinate play or make an absence feel like debt. More weekly reward options do not mean receiving all of them; mutual need can produce cooperation, bureaucracy or exclusion. |
| ✓ G98 | [Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag](../dossiers/assassins-creed-iv-black-flag.md), §§3–7 | Land-based acquisition can advance a naval goal, and a captured ship offers competing uses. A collected song changes later travel rather than only a statistic; familiar controls can acquire a different moral purpose. | Repeated compulsory restarts can conflict with free-roaming improvisation. Crew atmosphere is not evidence of general social simulation, and changed story labels cannot by themselves correct a contradictory reward loop. |
| ✓ G99 | [Assassin's Creed III](../dossiers/assassins-creed-iii.md), §§3–6 | Helping named residents changes production capability and useful expedition equipment. Ordinary work and local pastimes make home more than a menu. | Waiting for a qualifying animation can turn observation into checklist friction. Optional mastery conditions need to be distinguished from mission failure; fast travel and dispatch clocks do not necessarily advance together. |
| ✓ G100 | [Assassin's Creed Valhalla](../dossiers/assassins-creed-valhalla.md), §§3–6 | Durable equipment, a recognizable home and episodic commitments organize a long journey. A local pastime can be worthwhile without feeding combat power; a separate run mode changes commitment and recovery. | Episodic structure still exhausts when too many arcs repeat or delay desired closure. A reset needs a changed next decision, not merely another increment of persistent power. |
