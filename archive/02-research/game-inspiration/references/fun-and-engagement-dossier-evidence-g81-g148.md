## G81–G100 — role identity, tactical control and world-scale activity

| Read | Subject and inspected dossier sections | Contribution to the synthesis | Counterweight / evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ✓ G81 | [Final Fantasy IV](../dossiers/final-fantasy-iv.md), §§1–8 | Fixed character verbs and story-driven party turnover renew tactical problems. Cecil's changed role makes a narrative transformation change what the player does; useful field magic connects combat capability to world access. | Authored turnover can reduce desired control over a favorite party. Active/Wait settings and save placement change decision pressure and retry cost; the same plot does not make the original and remakes mechanically identical. |
| ✓ G82 | [Final Fantasy V](../dossiers/final-fantasy-v.md), §§3–9 | Transferable job abilities make practice leave useful options after a career change. Cross-job combinations and creature-derived learning reward understanding relationships between systems, not just raising levels. | A build needs equipment, acquired spells and compatible abilities, not merely an unlocked job. The dossier preserves the designers' intention to allow default-job play; a headline customization system need not force constant switching. |
| ✓ G83 | [Final Fantasy VI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-vi.md), §§2.6–6 | An ensemble supplies different verbs and reasons to care; Magicite can be a teacher, summon and build-shaping object. Rule-changing relics make equipment alter decisions. | Shared high-end magic can blur distinctive roles, while level-up optimization encourages fiddly equipment timing. Some editions preserve EXP after defeat and others do not; reliable recovery expectations matter more than a generic claim that loss is good. |
| ✓ G84 | [Final Fantasy VII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-vii.md), §§3–7 | Linked Materia makes composition visible. Equipment trades immediate power against learning rate, while portable learned objects and character-specific Limits separate flexible roles from personal identity. | A spell's effect depends on its compatible partner, socket placement, growth and user. Choice breadth needs readable relationships; original-game mechanics are not interchangeable with the modern reinterpretations or later ports. |
| ✓ G85 | [Final Fantasy VIII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-viii.md), §§3–8 | Cards, refinement, magic stocks and junctions form alternative progression routes. An enjoyable cultural side activity can feed the main adventure without being isolated reward filler. | Stocking magic for stats can discourage actually casting it. Repeated Draw becomes tedious when alternative acquisition is poorly discovered; a rich expert route does not excuse a dull novice route or dependence on a wiki. |
| ✓ G86 | [Final Fantasy IX](../dossiers/final-fantasy-ix.md), §§4–9 | Equipment teaches durable abilities, active support choices remain bounded, and synthesis gives old objects a further purpose. Objects can teach without making every person interchangeable. | Teaching, consuming and replacing the same object creates a real tradeoff but also retention burden. Repeated failed stealing can turn a character-defining action into stalled boss pacing; role expression is not enough to justify repetition. |
| ✓ G87 | [Final Fantasy X](../dossiers/final-fantasy-x.md), §§3–7 | A visible future turn order makes complex timing inspectable. Mid-battle specialists and a guided-then-branching progression grid stage freedom instead of exposing every decision immediately. | Knowing a path, being able to move and having activation materials are separate requirements. Greater build control adds interface work; the Expert Grid serves a different starting need than the default guided path. |
| ✓ G88 | [Final Fantasy XI](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xi.md), §§3–7 | One socially continuous character can pursue several careers. Primary/support jobs and practice-based expertise support changing roles without discarding the person's accumulated identity. | Preserving a career's progress is not having all careers' levels, equipment permissions and abilities active together. The dossier's account of a player-discovered tanking role inspiring a profession supports learning from play, not a claim that every emergent tactic deserves official adoption. |
| ✓ G89 | [Final Fantasy XII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xii.md), §§2–6 | Gambits move participation from repeated orders into choosing priorities, testing a policy and intervening. Saved configurations preserve work while leaving situational judgment to the player. | A general attack rule above healing can defeat the player's intended plan. Automation needs understandable priority and override; job-reset availability is edition-bound, so permanent commitment is not a timeless property of the game. |
| ✓ G90 | [Final Fantasy XIII](../dossiers/final-fantasy-xiii.md), §§2–6 | Automated individual commands can leave meaningful control over whole-party roles and their timing. Restoring health between fights removes maintenance while keeping danger and decisions inside an encounter. | Removing ordinary MP does not remove item, technique or opportunity costs. Automatic command selection is worthwhile only insofar as a player values the remaining tactical responsibility; the direct sequels change that responsibility rather than simply adding content. |
