## R11 — Estimate commercial results and explain economic success or failure

Every full subject pass must answer both **how much money the product appears to have generated** and **why its economic outcome was strong, weak, mixed or indeterminate**. A sales milestone, positive review score or popularity claim alone is insufficient.

### Required commercial evidence record

Use a compact table with the following fields, combining rows only when their definitions genuinely match:

| Field | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Product and scope | Exact title, edition, base game versus DLC, storefront/platform coverage, and whether the figure belongs to a game, franchise, publisher or platform. |
| Measurement period | Lifetime through a stated date, launch window, calendar/fiscal year or quarter. A retrieval date does not make an old measurement current. |
| Metric and currency | Units sold, gross consumer spending, bookings, recognized revenue, developer/publisher receipts, creator payouts or profit. Name the measure rather than calling everything revenue. Preserve original currency; date and source any conversion. |
| Reported results | Prefer primary financial disclosures, developer/publisher statements and original postmortems. Attribute unaudited self-reports. Distinguish paid units, bundles, subscriptions, keys, free claims, downloads, visits and players. |
| Estimated revenue | Give a defensible low/base/high range when evidence supports one. A high-confidence disclosed total can replace a speculative range for that exact scope; estimate only the missing scope separately. Label every modeled figure and avoid false precision. |
| Method and assumptions | Show the calculation, source inputs, discounts/regional pricing, weighted realized price, platform coverage, refunds/tax treatment and other material adjustments. State whether fees have already been deducted. |
| Confidence and uncertainty | Explain the weakest inputs, incompatible estimates and missing coverage. Do not combine apparently independent estimates that use the same review-count model as independent corroboration. |
| Costs and sustainability | Record disclosed team/budget/development duration, marketing, distribution, publisher recoupment/revenue share and continuing operating costs where available. Use explicitly hypothetical cost or break-even scenarios only when useful; never present them as actual accounts. |
| Judgment | State whose success is being evaluated: developer livelihood, project profitability, publisher return, platform income, creator ecosystem or community/non-commercial goals. Give the conclusion and what evidence could change it. |

For a premium title, a transparent starting calculation can be **paid units × estimated weighted realized sale price**. List price × every reported player is not a revenue estimate. Separate direct sales from external-key activations and subscription access; avoid counting a bundle or a DLC sale twice. For free-to-play, subscriptions and user-created experiences, identify the actual revenue mechanism rather than inventing a unit price.

Third-party revenue/ownership estimates are permitted when clearly labeled and critically assessed. Record provider, capture date, covered storefronts, metric definition and disclosed methodology. Review-to-sales ratios require a stated rationale and sensitivity analysis, not one universal multiplier. Never convert a gross-spending estimate to profit by subtracting only a storefront fee. Prefer a bounded partial-market estimate over an unjustified all-platform lifetime total.

When no defensible numerical estimate can be made after a real search, explicitly record **not estimable from available evidence**, what was searched, the available bounds or dated observations, and the missing inputs. This is not permission to skip the economic analysis. Conversely, do not fabricate a number merely to fill a required cell.

### Required economic explanation

Analyze the documented outcome through demand and differentiation, price/value, launch readiness, discoverability, wishlists or prelaunch community where evidenced, distribution/platform reach, creator/streamer exposure, timing/competition, updates and long-tail sales, production scope/cost, and ongoing support or hosting. Explain the proposed connection from a specific feature or business choice to an outcome. Separate creator testimony, observed association and the researcher's causal hypothesis.

Consider counterexplanations and failure cases: a well-liked game can earn too little for its cost; a widely distributed product can monetize poorly; a modestly selling solo project can be sustainable; a large-revenue game can lose money; a free/open-source work can succeed at its stated goals without sales. Do not label an undisclosed result a commercial failure. Distinguish initial performance, later recovery and current sustainability. Comparisons with similar games must preserve differences in scope and metric.

For Roblox and other platforms, keep **platform revenue/bookings**, **aggregate creator payouts**, **an individual experience's receipts**, **its production costs**, and **typical versus exceptional creator outcomes** separate. Virtual money earned by a player inside a tycoon is not real-world revenue for that player or its creator.

## R02, R04 and R12 — Why play is enjoyable, compelling, repetitive or abandoned

A full pass must explain **what players actually enjoy and dislike**, **why particular interactions may produce those reactions**, and **why people return, continue, finish satisfied or quit**. Do not substitute a feature inventory or average review score for this analysis.

### Required enjoyment and return-loop account

Describe the moment-to-moment activity, the repeated sequence of decisions and rewards, and the longer-term reasons to continue. Cover the opening, established play and endgame; note where the loop changes or runs out of new decisions. Trace the actual sequence: goal → choice/action → uncertainty or cost → feedback/result → changed capabilities or stakes → next wanted action.

Investigate applicable sources of appeal: satisfying controls and feedback; competence/mastery; discovery and surprise; combining items or abilities; meaningful choices and risk; gaining or expressing power; craftsmanship and visible construction; inhabiting a place; relationships and social cooperation/competition; narrative curiosity; collection; autonomy; and creative expression. These are candidate explanations to test against the game, not a checklist of assumed benefits.

Explain frustrations with equally concrete examples: repeated chores, waiting, grind, opaque rules, poor controls or performance, unfair-feeling losses, weak variety, runaway advantage, unsatisfying rewards, shallow social behavior, monetization pressure, inaccessible group entry, and lack of a worthwhile next goal. A demanding mechanic can be appealing to one audience and alienating to another. Distinguish design preferences, tutorial failures, bugs, balance, commercial friction and service limitations.

The existing minimum of five substantive written reviews from multiple sources still applies when available, plus accessible helpful/top player evidence and contrasting testimony. For each material interpretation record the actual player account or observation supporting it, version/play-stage context, a counterexample or alternative explanation, and confidence. Do not infer prevalence from selected reviews or treat a forum's name as verified demographic data.

### Replay, loss and rebuilding

Explicitly examine what is retained and lost at each boundary: death, failed mission, run end, prestige/rebirth, new season, server wipe, logout and starting a new campaign. Distinguish involuntary loss from a voluntary reset. Record restart cost, time to regain interesting choices, what varies, and what carries over as knowledge, mastery, relationships, unlocked options or possessions.

Mike's example is that **Apocalypse Rising is fun for him because he keeps dying and building up again**. Preserve this as an attributed player observation and a research hypothesis, not a verified universal cause of that game's retention. Investigate whether rebuilding restores valuable early discoveries and uncertain encounters, or merely repeats already-mastered chores. Do not import Apocalypse Rising 2 rules into the original game without explicitly marking the difference.

A restart can renew opportunity and stakes; it can also erase valued time, separate friends or invite exploitation. Explain both. Do not assume permanent progression is always desirable, or that total loss is always more interesting. A finite work that players finish happily is not a retention failure merely because they stop playing.

### “Addictive” is a question to unpack, not a success metric

When a reviewer or Mike calls a game addictive, identify the concrete behavior and proposed mechanism: wanting one more attempt, anticipating an upgrade, finishing a self-chosen project, discovering a rare combination, returning to friends, or feeling pressure not to miss something. Distinguish voluntary enjoyment from obligation, fear of missing out, sunk-cost pressure and problematic play. Do not diagnose addiction from hours played or use unsupported neuroscience explanations.

Where available, report retention cohorts, repeat-session frequency, session lengths, completion, return after failure and reasons for leaving, with definitions and sample limits. These do not independently establish enjoyment. Distinguish measured results from developer advice and hypotheses. Consider the player's stated satisfaction, ability to stop, purchase clarity and wellbeing alongside engagement. Research is not a request to maximize compulsion or exploit minors.

## R03 and R04 — Treat the fictional economy as gameplay

Record how players acquire, produce, earn, buy, sell, trade, invest, consume, lose and recover goods or currencies. Map resource sources and sinks, ownership, storage/carrying limits, prices/scarcity, automation, labor, time, specialization, bottlenecks, risk and exchange partners. Explain what an upgrade enables rather than only its price or multiplier. Distinguish player-to-player trade, NPC shops, automatic income, exchange with another settlement, and real-money purchases.

For tycoons, management games and economic simulators, explain the earn → reinvest → expand/automate → face a new choice loop, visible changes to the player's place, and whether scarcity still creates decisions after the first expansion. Treat cosmetic wealth/status, functional capability and business optimization as different rewards. Study prestige/rebirth only where actually present. For economy-light games, describe the actual equivalent or absence rather than inventing a market.

For platform studies, examine named economic game types and individual examples; do not assume their rules are platform defaults. Statements that a genre is common or dominant require a dated source and scope. A few examples do not establish its percentage of all games, players, hours or revenue.

## R08 — Deep lore and its connection to actual play

When substantive lore exists, go beyond a premise paragraph. Document the creation story or cosmology; important historical periods and conflicts; named gods, factions, peoples, cultures, places and figures; motivations and relationships; institutions and everyday customs; and unresolved mysteries or conflicting beliefs. Use a concise chronology or relationship map where it clarifies the setting. Distinguish established canon, in-world belief, developer intent, community interpretation and invented examples. Mark spoilers and edition changes.

Explain how the player encounters and uses that lore: environment and architecture, objects and crafting, religion, dialogue, quests, social relationships, economic rules, institutions and decisions. Separate written backstory from delivered quests or simulated behavior. A lore claim does not establish a functioning mechanic, and a procedural campaign is not necessarily procedurally authored mythology.

Preserve ambiguity instead of inventing a coherent timeline when sources disagree. Reference lore names for analysis, not as proposed OpenLegend assets. Record meaningful absences; a game can be enjoyable without deep lore, and lore depth alone does not explain commercial or player success.

## R09 and R13 — Explain how players and creators actually author content

For creation platforms and games with modding or in-game building, distinguish at least three levels: **playing/building within an existing game's allowed rules**, **authoring assets or reusable components**, and **creating or scripting a new game/ruleset**. State which levels are available to ordinary players, authors, approved contributors or external modders.

Describe the actual tools and workflow: editor and templates; terrain/scene/room construction; modeling/importing, animation, audio and UI; reusable models/packages/plugins and dependencies; visual configuration versus scripting; language, APIs and client/server execution; testing/debugging/profiling; collaboration/versioning; saving and persistence; publishing, permissions, moderation and discovery; and monetization when applicable. Separate native tooling, community tools, external DCC applications, AI assistance, reverse engineering and unverified promises.

Use a clear **platform/engine provides versus creator must supply** comparison. Default physics, avatars or networking do not mean the platform supplies a good economy, NPC motivation, progression, lore or fair combat. Templates are examples, not proof of a universal rule. Spell out the security and ownership boundary for shared assets, scripts, live edits and player-submitted content, without treating trusted developer scripts as equivalent to unrestricted player-generated code.

Include a concrete end-to-end example: start from an existing supported artifact, alter appearance and behavior, test a complete playable activity, package/publish it, and explain what another creator or player can reuse. Label a constructed workflow versus one personally executed. Identify authoring friction and what remains difficult after AI assistance. Derive specific OpenLegend lessons and tradeoffs, not an instruction to clone another platform or implement its architecture.
