# Worldbuilding research library

**29 dossiers, six comparative essays, and a cross-world matrix.** Initial packet: 2026-09-26. Whole-world reassessment: 2026-09-27, on `codex/world-idea-repertoires`. Dossier 29 was incorporated after the original 28-world roster and is included in the revised comparison.

This library studies fictional worlds as places where people live, work, desire, create, enjoy, believe, compete, govern, harm, protect, and change. It distinguishes the represented world from its presentation and reception. The point is not to make every setting equally pleasant, bleak, explained, or complete, but to understand what its particular arrangements make possible and why its encounters can matter.

A road, school, family name, ability, ritual, workplace, or pleasurable gathering becomes substantial when it changes a possible life. Some stories concentrate on ordinary companionship; others on mastery, domination, wonder, tragedy, or discovery. The essays test comparisons against concrete cases rather than turning a preference for one kind of experience into a universal account.

**Spoilers:** major revelations and endings are discussed within each dossier's stated corpus. Versions, adaptations, creator proposals, branching outcomes, and disputed accounts remain distinct. D&D is one comparative dossier of selected settings, not a claim that its many worlds share one continuity or received equal evidence depth.

## What a dossier contains

Dossiers retain their own structure and emphasis. They identify corpus and version; creators and production history; ordinary material life and things worth wanting; institutions, peoples, religion, and historical memory; communication; extraordinary capacities; particular people and relationships; concrete interactions and consequences; criticism; and evidence limits.

Their cases explain enough of the situation, participants, desires, means, and outcomes to support an interpretation. A celebration is not automatically a trap; a victory is not automatically reconciliation; a harmful act is not automatically a misunderstanding. A source that leaves a subject unspecified is allowed to do so. No invented sexual custom, religion, or economy fills a checklist slot.

Each revised dossier closes with a concise record of what was reviewed, which sources were actually consulted, what changed, and what remains unavailable. Named primary works and retained citations are not a claim of complete fresh reading, viewing, or play. D&D additionally separates a recurring setting practice, published alternative encounters, creator proposals, and actual fixed screen events.

## Reading routes by question

**What makes ordinary life attractive?** Compare Austen's dancing and the Crofts, the Culture's voluntary pursuits, Spirited Away's food and service, and the Tain Gala. Look at what the activity supplies before asking what later crisis might threaten it.

**What makes mastery or adventure worth seeking?** Compare Pokémon's championship, Zoro's challenge, Dune's worm-riding, Warhammer's tactical reversal, and Fallout's recovered aircraft. Skill, victory, prestige, wonder, and a changed material possibility are different rewards.

**How can affection coexist with real disagreement?** Begin with the character essay, then Star Trek, Earthsea, The Witcher, Austen, and the D&D film case. Distinguish love from trust, consent, obedience, forgiveness, and a desire to continue a relationship.

**How do institutions work, fail, or deliberately harm?** Begin with the institutions essay, then Discworld, The Wire, the Wizarding World, The Broken Earth, and Spirited Away. Separate public purpose, actual operation, effective provision, and the distribution of costs.

**How does history become a present choice?** Compare Middle-earth, Tamriel, Foundation, Macondo, and Eberron. Follow the name, document, office, ritual, or remembered place through the person interpreting it.

**How can wonder remain partly unexplained?** Compare Earthsea, Narnia, Spirited Away, and Pokémon. Then use the magic essay's distinction between an open cosmology and an unprepared solution to the current problem.

**What can success leave unresolved?** Compare the Shire's repair, Bubbles's recovery, Chihiro's release, the Expanse's separation, and the Broken Earth's planetary change. Specify the beneficiary and the continuing loss rather than declaring an ending wholly redemptive or meaningless.

**Why do audiences disagree?** Use the authorship essay, then the Bards College, Narnia, Macondo, the Culture, and D&D's film comparison. Distinguish what happens from how it is presented and what a particular critic values.

## Evidence and limits

The library preserves original works as bibliographic anchors while adding selected primary passages, creator testimony, substantive criticism, and explicitly labeled reference material. Some source access improved substantially; other claims still rely on secondary checks or limited fragments. The [source audit](source-audit.md) makes those differences visible.

There is no claim of a complete fresh rereading of every franchise, exhaustive current canon, an equal-depth survey of all D&D settings, or empirical proof that a technique caused popularity. Interpretations of appeal are argued readings, not universal audience preferences. The source base remains selective and predominantly accessible English-language material, with identified translation and transcription limits.

The comparative conclusions can inform possible transferable questions, but the library is not a proposal to implement game systems, rewrite accepted engine contracts, or expand idea repertoires. Fictional authority and conduct remain distinct from actual platform permissions and treatment of participants.
