# Source audit and evidence limits

**Original audit: 2026-09-26. Reconciled reassessment: 2026-09-27, covering 29 dossiers.** This file summarizes evidence layers, specific improvements in access, and remaining limits. It is not a claim that every sentence received new independent primary verification or that every external URL was rechecked.

The [method](research-method.md) owns standards. Each [dossier](README.md) owns its selected corpus, annotated bibliography, and precise closing record of reading and revision. The [reassessment ledger](reassessment-progress.md) records current completion and verification; the [historical ledger](research-progress.md) preserves the earlier packet's evidence rather than applying it retrospectively to new work.

## 1. What each evidence layer establishes

**Primary fictional works** establish events within their versions. Named chapters, episodes, films, issues, and games remain useful anchors without implying that the entire work was freshly consumed. Selected actual passages and dialogue have stronger proximity to a specific claim than a contents page or a remembered synopsis, but still do not certify an entire franchise.

**Creator testimony** establishes what an identifiable creator says they intended, remembers doing, or proposes. It can simplify collaboration or impose retrospective order. Baker's personal Eberron interpretations, for example, are not automatically binding published canon; Rath's imagined example is not an event in his novel.

**Official and institutional material** can establish credits, a published structure, a stated editorial policy, or the institution's own presentation. A publisher's promotion is not independent reception research. A catalogue, contents page, interview index, or GDC listing is not the unavailable work behind it.

**Substantive criticism and references** support attributed interpretations and bounded event checks. Their quality and proximity vary. Fan references can supply chapter locators while also containing speculation or errors; a critic's quoted primary fragment is not the full primary scene. A guide describes possible routes rather than an executed playthrough. A readable transcription may still contain mistakes or translation choices.

**Comparative interpretation** explains why particular dynamics may be engaging. Repetition of a claim in an essay does not improve its underlying evidence. The library does not contain a systematic audience survey, matched comparison group, independent peer review, or causal explanation of commercial success.

## 3. What improved without becoming a full-corpus claim

The reassessment does more than add bibliographic titles. Several new cases are checked against selected primary passages, especially Austen, Macondo, Narnia, the Culture, Foundation's opening, and the D&D adventure. Other cases use substantial criticism and reference material where complete originals were unavailable. Their different proximity to the work remains visible.

The distinction between **retained** and **newly read** is important. Earlier production and linguistic sources were not all redundantly revisited. New access to one item does not certify every old citation, and a failed URL does not mean a source never existed. Redirects, mirrors, limited previews, and excerpts are recorded for what they actually yielded.

The added material corrects substantive selection: celebrations and musical work; competence and desired achievement; functioning institutions; wanted intimacy versus coercion; intentional wrongdoing versus structural failure; and victories that endure alongside specific losses. The essays were rebuilt after the revised corpus was read. That is a new comparative interpretation, not proof that every inherited primary anchor received another independent audit.

## 4. Critical disagreement and claims not justified

A review can dispute narrative framing, repeated structure, pacing, characterization, or the limited activity a game permits. It does not become a reason to omit the criticized subject from the world. Examples include Macondo's treatment of sexual violence, the Bards College's musical limitations versus its historical puzzle, and conflicting responses to the D&D film's characters.

Sources are predominantly accessible English-language material, even for Polish, Japanese, and Spanish works. Selected translated and parallel passages improve particular checks without eliminating mediation. Complete original-language scholarship and archival research remain unavailable for much of the roster.

The packet does not establish one causal formula for popularity. It selects notable worlds without a controlled comparison, representative reception sample, or matched unsuccessful works. It does not verify real-world medical, legal, scientific, or political claims from fictional events. Nor does a scene's familiarity establish that every viewer or reader experiences it identically.

## 5. Continuity and outcome checks that change the meaning

The review specifically preserves comics versus screen continuities, Star Wars versus Legends, Witcher book/game layers, Pokémon versions, the Dressrosa boundary, Foundation's original trilogy versus later additions, Tolkien's editorial history, and D&D's edition/setting/campaign distinctions.

It also keeps local evidence status visible: Dumbledore's reconstruction is not a witnessed potion scene; Essun's names are one person; Kino's later fate is not settled by the selected season; a game's mutually exclusive resolutions are not one biography. A restored body is not automatically a restored relationship, a ghost's release is not resurrection, sparing is not forgiveness, and a local victory is not universal reform.

These distinctions prevent both sanitizing and overstatement. A narrator's later description of desire does not erase a depicted refusal. An implication does not authorize invented sexual details. An institution's provision can be genuinely useful without making all its conduct legitimate. A critic's moral objection does not become evidence that the depicted pleasure was never experienced.

## 6. Shared sources and verification limits

The original continuation retrieved [Jenkins's transmedia essay](https://henryjenkins.org/blog/2007/03/transmedia_storytelling_101.html) and [Sanderson's first](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-first-law) and [second](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-second-law) craft essays. They remain attributed frameworks, not universal laws or newly executed empirical tests. The language and authorship essays retain their original direct-source annotations and link newer cases to the dossier evidence.

External URLs were checked through actual selective retrieval during the relevant passes, not by an exhaustive crawler. Some returned full articles, some partial text, some metadata, and some errors. A reachable page does not establish every claim attached to it. A read-only runtime copy could not be downloaded during this session; no automated whole-library link check, formatter, game test, or screenshot inspection of unavailable maps is claimed.

Final repository inventory, navigation, content-preservation, and change-scope findings belong in the [reassessment ledger](reassessment-progress.md). Completion labels mean the selected correction and synthesis work was performed with these limits disclosed; they do not mean all independent primary-source, full-setting, or reception gaps have disappeared.
