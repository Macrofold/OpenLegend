# Source audit and evidence limits

**Review date: 2026-09-26.** This file records the evidence structure of the [worldbuilding library](README.md), representative source rechecks during its completion, and limits that remain after the documents are delivered. It is not a claim that every sentence has received a new independent primary-source verification or that every linked page will remain accessible.

The [research method](research-method.md) owns the general standards. Each dossier owns its selected primary corpus and annotated bibliography. This audit adds a cross-library view and updates access findings; it does not replace the individual source notes.

## 1. What the different evidence layers establish

**Primary fictional works:** the named novels, episodes, films, comic runs, and games establish fictional events within their stated continuities. References such as P1–P4 are bibliographic or scene-level anchors. They do not mean that the entire corpus was freshly reread, watched, or played during this session. Close readings draw on those identified works and are presented as interpretations, not as quotations from a newly inspected complete text.

**Creator testimony:** letters, interviews, essays, and recorded conversations support statements about what a creator says they intended, remembers doing, or values. Retrospective testimony can simplify collaboration or impose a clearer plan on a process than existed at the time. It is not conclusive evidence of every audience response or the only legitimate interpretation of a work.

**Official and institutional sources:** publisher pages, museums, author estates, and franchise archives can establish credits, publication context, or an institution's official presentation. Promotional praise is not independent reception research. A modern edition's publication date is not automatically the original work's date.

**Specialist and secondary sources:** critical essays, biographies, and reference articles provide context with different levels of proximity to the evidence. Wikipedia is explicitly used for orientation in some dossiers where direct material was not accessible. Its presence is not disguised as first-person testimony. An excerpt of a quoted interview is not the same as inspecting the complete interview.

**Comparative interpretation:** claims about why a scene feels inhabited, why a relationship is compelling, or how a detail creates depth are the library's arguments. They can be assessed against the identified works, but are not experimentally established rules about every reader or viewer.

## 3. Additional sources for the comparative essays

The continuation also retrieved [Henry Jenkins's “Transmedia Storytelling 101”](https://henryjenkins.org/blog/2007/03/transmedia_storytelling_101.html), [Sanderson's first-law essay](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-first-law), and [his second-law essay](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-second-law). These are respectively a critical framework and a writer's craft heuristics. They do not establish universal laws of popularity, compulsory worldbuilding methods, or proof that one amount of explanation is always superior.

The language essay adds the specifically identified Tolkien script/language distinction and Okrand's account of production-driven language growth. The authorship essay uses limited portions of creator testimony rather than attributing every comparative conclusion to the source. The essays' remaining fictional examples link to the dossiers and their primary-work anchors.

## 4. Continuity checks that materially affect the analysis

The review preserves distinctions that would change the argument if lost:

- **Version and adaptation:** Marvel comics versus the MCU; different DC continuities; Star Wars screen corpus versus Legends; Witcher books versus game and screen expansions; original Pokémon games versus remakes and anime; the books of A Song of Ice and Fire versus television's conclusion.
- **Time and corpus:** the selected One Piece analysis stops at Dressrosa; Foundation's original trilogy is distinguished from later additions; Tolkien's posthumous editorial history is identified; Star Trek's different eras and series are not one unchanging social order.
- **Identity and choice:** Damaya, Syenite, and Essun are stages of one person, not three independent samples; interactive branching outcomes are not fused into one biography; a character's account or a fictional official document is not automatically the world's objective truth.
- **Reality and representation:** fictional Baltimore and New Jersey are authored social selections; Macondo is not a documentary history of Colombia; fictional politics and religion are not evidence that all real members of a group share the represented motives; fantastic science is not real-world technical or safety guidance.

These are bounded checks, not a certification that every possible continuity disagreement in 28 settings has been settled.

## 5. Conclusions the evidence does not justify

The library does not establish a causal formula for mass popularity. It selects already notable worlds and contains no matched sample of unsuccessful works, no systematic reader survey, and no controlled comparison of distribution, marketing, performance, timing, and prior familiarity.

It does not claim equal cultural coverage. The selected works and accessible sources are predominantly available in English, even where the original work is Polish, Japanese, or Spanish. Additional primary-language scholarship could change or deepen some production and reception accounts. Translation is an acknowledged mediation, not invisible access to an identical verbal surface.

It does not claim universal completeness. The named worlds often leave government, religion, economics, language, or ordinary labor less specified than their best-known dimensions. Those gaps are reported rather than filled with fabricated institutions or rules. Likewise, a source-access failure is not evidence that the source does not exist.

## 6. What completion means here

Completion means the selected roster and thematic questions have substantial, navigable documents, source distinctions are visible, scope and continuity are stated, and the research has been committed. It does not mean every primary work was freshly consumed or that an independent scholar has peer-reviewed every interpretation.

The final structural checks and remote verification are recorded in [research-progress.md](research-progress.md). No unrun automated check, unavailable full text, or unmeasured audience effect should be inferred from a completed document status.
