# 29. Dungeons & Dragons: worlds made inhabitable through consequential differences

Research and focused reassessment: 2026-09-27. This is an independent worldbuilding study, not a proposed ruleset or implementation specification. [Library](../README.md) · [Research method](../research-method.md) · [D&D game-research hub](../../game-inspiration/games/dungeons-and-dragons.md).

**Spoilers and scope:** discusses setting premises, institutions, named characters, religious uncertainty, and the destruction of Cyre. The added cases discuss the 2016 *Death House* adventure and a relationship in the 2023 film *Honor Among Thieves*. A published adventure's possible outcomes, a film's events, and a particular table's campaign are different evidence. The deepest lived-world evidence remains Eberron creator commentary. The 2025 Forgotten Realms publications provide a dated comparison; Greyhawk and Planescape remain more selective lenses. The closing record specifies what was actually read, rather than claiming a complete franchise or sourcebook audit.

## 1. D&D is not one world, and an edition is not a chronology

Dungeons & Dragons supplies rules with which groups can inhabit many worlds. **Forgotten Realms, Greyhawk, Eberron, Planescape, and Ravenloft are not alternative names for the same setting.** A rule revision can change how a spell is represented without advancing every setting's history. A novel, computer game, published adventure, home campaign, and streamed campaign can also establish different continuities or local outcomes.

The initial packet's tabletop reference is the revised fifth-edition lineage, recorded there as **5.5e**, with SRD 5.2.1 the English reference listed during its check. That is a dated rules-version observation, not the most popular ongoing table practice or a mandatory world. The [current-edition dossier](../../game-inspiration/dossiers/dungeons-and-dragons-2024.md) owns the version verification and mechanical details; this worldbuilding reassessment does not independently certify a new rules release. [W1]

The contemporary-world anchor is the **2025 Forgotten Realms pair, Heroes of Faerûn and Adventures in Faerûn**. Their official contents were inspected in the initial packet, along with a substantive 2026 review. Complete paid chapters were not available. Older public rules provide a specifically labeled account of the Weave; they are not silently treated as an audit of every revised lore passage. Eberron material is similarly separated into published-book statements quoted by its creator and his explicitly personal extensions, often called “kanon.” [W2–W4, R1]

## 2. The audience's first foothold

**Interpretation:** a large fictional world becomes accessible when someone wants something understandable before they understand its cosmology. A traveler needs shelter. An apprentice wants a recognized qualification. A newcomer wants an invitation. A refugee wants a future that does not erase a former home. Someone facing a powerful host wants to leave safely. These are entrances into a world, not substitutes for it.

The selected settings offer different routes. The Realms' player book places character options beside a regional guide, religion, equipment, magic, and factions. The contents make belonging an alternative entrance to the atlas: a person can begin with an affiliation or occupation rather than memorizing a continent. Eberron's creator essays connect unusual magic to familiar social activities. Planescape's city-centered organization gives the cosmic traveler a place from which to orient themselves. Ravenloft's separation of horror genres makes the desired emotional experience part of setting selection. [W2, W6–W7; E1–E10]

These are formal affordances, not measured audience outcomes. A beginner can still find a long list of names intimidating. A known faction can invite curiosity or feel like homework. A spectacular map can promise exploration while concealing that most interesting choices happen between a handful of people.

**Pleasures matter in their own right.** Skillful work, delicious food, a memorable performance, a kindly mentor, recognition, adventure, and the prospect of winning offer different reasons to participate. Comfort need not conceal a sinister transaction, and combat need not be described as a failure to find the truly worthwhile activity. The examples below distinguish a recurring celebration, a desired relationship that cannot be recovered, and a place where the threat really is predatory. None supplies the tone of every D&D world.

## 3. Authorship, collaboration, and the production of a setting

Ed Greenwood identifies himself as the original creator of the Forgotten Realms. The setting's subsequent life is not simply one author's uninterrupted account: the 2025 books are published by Wizards and organize a large body of material. This dossier does not infer Greenwood's biography, private intentions, or control over later editorial decisions from that credit. A complete production history of the Realms, including early publication and later collaborative revisions, remains beyond the primary-source material inspected here. [P1, W2–W3]

Eberron offers unusually visible ongoing authorship through Keith Baker's essays. His discussions frequently identify what a published source says, where published sources disagree, and how he would handle an issue in his own campaign. This makes the difference between **canon, interpretation, and usable invention** observable. A secondary bibliographic reference credits Baker, Bill Slavicsek, and James Wyatt for the 2004 setting; the original production archive and complete collaboration history were not recovered. [P2; E7–E10]

Visual authorship also matters. Tony DiTerlizzi describes using Bristol board, sepia dip-pen ink, watercolors, diluted inks, and colored-pencil detailing for his 1993–1996 Planescape work. That is direct production testimony, not a claim that every later illustration uses the same technique. His Monstrous Manual retrospective preserves an editorial demand for reference images that remained serious rather than comic. These are actual negotiated presentation choices, not evidence that a distinctive style caused commercial success. [P3–P4]

**Interpretation:** at least four authorship layers matter: original conception, published edition, presenter or adapter, and the table that realizes particular events. They can cooperate or conflict. A new visual treatment can make an old location feel different without changing its map. A campaign can preserve a nation's institutions while changing its ruler. A strong sourcebook default does not require every table to resolve an open mystery identically.

## 4. Forgotten Realms: a dated entrance into an accumulated world

The 2025 **Adventures in Faerûn** organizes regional play around the Dalelands, Icewind Dale, Calimshan, the Moonshae Isles, and Baldur's Gate. Its contents contrast desert survival and Calimport's districts, island navigation, northern settlements, and urban divisions. The **Heroes** volume includes calendars and currency, regional languages, religious institutions, equipment and vehicles, the Weave and Circle Magic, and factions including the Harpers, Emerald Enclave, Lords' Alliance, Red Wizards, and Zhentarim. These are verified coverage categories, not a claim to have read every entry. [W2–W3]

**Interpretation:** the important opportunity is not the number of regions. It is that the same intention can mean something different in each place. A journey can turn on weather, water, navigation, or access. An affiliation can help in one encounter and complicate another. A tool, expression, coin, or custom can provide orientation before a history lecture is necessary.

The public **2014** spellcasting explanation gives a concrete metaphysical account: Forgotten Realms casters identify the Weave with Mystra, and damaged access to that fabric can disrupt magic. It distinguishes raw magical potential from the means of shaping it. This is an older explicit source, not proof that every D&D world uses Mystra or that the 2025 books preserve every sentence unchanged. [W4]

**Interpretation:** magical access can make landscape, religion, expertise, and history touch the same problem. A damaged magical environment can become an infrastructure problem, theological question, or expedition. That possibility does not establish how often ordinary inhabitants encounter it, who understands it, or how each local economy responds. Those require local evidence.

The 2026 Old Game Hermit review praises regional material, introductory usefulness, and portions of the lore, while criticizing repetition and the value of dividing the material between books. The writer is already skeptical of the revision. That is one reception account, not consensus. Speculative sixth-edition rumors and a loose age description are not adopted. [R1]

The added film case supplies a more particular encounter, not a substitute gazetteer. A conversation about an ended marriage can reveal that heroic companions also have former homes, romantic preferences, and futures other people are no longer obliged to share. It does not establish the marriage customs of all Faerûn. [R3]

## 5. Eberron: magic as infrastructure and labor

Baker frames the dragonmarked houses' power around specialized capacities, infrastructure, and institutions rather than the isolated strength of individual casters. Dynastic marks connect to services and large magical devices; the houses' relationship with the former unified kingdom has political consequences. Edition-specific examples should not be converted into revised mechanics without checking their version. [E1]

**Interpretation:** possessing a power and controlling a service differ. A traveler can need an institution even where magic is common. Expertise, equipment, contracts, and distribution can remain scarce after an effect is understood. A sophisticated magical society need not give every household independent access to all its infrastructure.

Baker's **magical education** essay distinguishes a magewright's narrow professional training from an adventuring wizard's flexible spellcasting. Guilds, trade schools, and other institutions can train specialized work; academic study and battlefield improvisation are not successive steps on one universal ladder. A healer or locksmith can be highly competent without every capability of a high-level adventurer. The article's ritual costs belong to its historical rules context. [E2]

That distinction makes room for careers. An apprentice can take pride in consistency, an examiner in standards, and a customer in finding reliable help. Extraordinary capacities can supply a different material for ordinary competence rather than erasing it. The quality of a service is not measured only by how useful its practitioner would be in a fight.

The **rural Eberron** essay distinguishes widespread low-level applications from rare high-end power. Baker proposes different agricultural and household expressions in different cultures: repair, sanitation, regional techniques, and particular religious uses of undead labor. These are his elaborations, not an agricultural census or proof that every supernatural labor arrangement is benign. Their value is that magic does not produce one uniform village everywhere. [E3]

The evidence opens questions about maintenance, absent specialists, rural access, imports, and household labor. It does not supply exact wages, crop yields, coverage percentages, or repair schedules. Inventing those quantities would turn a useful distinction into counterfeit canon.

## 6. Food, leisure, class, and the small signs of belonging

Baker's food-and-drink essay describes Zilwater establishments using magical flavoring as part of the service. His further inventions include branded refreshments and distinctions between common and prestigious drinks. He explicitly does not claim a fully worked-out culinary ecology. The interest is expertise, customization, pleasure, and commerce—not an assertion that every proposed menu is official canon. [E4]

**Interpretation:** taste can carry class and place without an exposition scene. A person may know how to order, miss a familiar preparation, treat a friend, or find an expensive drink disappointing. None requires trauma. A custom becomes substantial through a particular activity, preference, or occasion rather than merely existing as an encyclopedia entry.

The **Tain Gala**, described through Baker's quotation of *Sharn: City of Towers* and subsequent additions, is a concentrated status institution. Its established guest circle marks elite belonging; Lady Celyria ir'Tain can admit unusual guests and performers. Pleasure, introductions, and patronage coexist. Optional performers and situations in the essay are proposals, not a record of everything that has happened there. The developed case in section 12 keeps that distinction. [E5]

Wealth and recognition are not interchangeable. Money need not secure the right introduction; an entertainer's temporary access is not hereditary membership. Equally, the presence of class boundaries does not make a guest's enjoyment fraudulent. Nor is a lively gathering necessarily a prelude to violence.

The evidence foregrounds guests more than cooks, cleaners, garment workers, and servants. Their particular names, schedules, and arrangements cannot be supplied by inference. That is a limit to the sample's social depth, not proof that hospitality never works or that every pleasure is exploitation in disguise.

## 7. History, law, borders, and unfinished institutions

Baker's **Treaties and Laws** essay distinguishes the Treaty of Thronehold from a constitution or uniform legal code. In his account, states retain different laws within a fragile postwar settlement; the treaty addresses warforged freedom, certain magical production, borders, and house operations. He frames its incompleteness as a consequence of an urgent settlement, while warning that his reading may conflict with other books. [E6]

**Interpretation:** history becomes active when inherited structures no longer fit the present. A former common framework can survive differently in successor states. Treaty recognition and practical protection need not coincide. A border changes what an accusation, occupation, or document can mean. Nevertheless, ending a war and establishing a legal claim can be genuine achievements without completing every reform.

Productive incompleteness differs from incoherence. An institution known to be contested can generate choices; an unexplained contradiction that prevents anyone from understanding a rule can simply be a source-management problem. A gap need not become a hidden cosmic truth.

The **Prince Oargev** essay identifies contradictory published ages, parentage, and statistics, then selects a version. Its recurring premise is a surviving Cyran royal associated with refugees and New Cyre, with his personal future entangled in reconstruction. Suggested suitors are possible campaign material, not a declaration of whom he canonically marries. [E7]

**Interpretation:** people can disagree about what deserves restoration, what must change, and which old distinctions remain legitimate. A song, friendship, practical skill, and title carry the past differently. Those forms of continuity can survive a lost territory without pretending the loss has been undone.

## 8. Religion, cosmology, and the difference between evidence and conviction

In Baker's treatment of Eberron faith, divine magic is not proof of virtue, and institutions do not exhaust belief. He distinguishes faith and community from a system in which gods continually appear to confirm doctrine. Dolurrh's relation to death and fading memory can be known in one sense while its ultimate meaning remains disputed. Non-spellcasting participants still matter. [E8]

**Interpretation:** religion can organize care, education, solidarity, ritual, conflict, and discipline without becoming only a spell list. What can inhabitants establish, how do they interpret it, and whom do they trust? A miracle can answer one question without resolving another. A priest's kindness need not depend on supernatural power; a powerful official need not be trustworthy.

Sincere disagreement is not the only available religious conflict. *Death House* supplies people who deliberately victimize others and a supernatural order that can reward an appalling choice. Its setting does not require the researcher to explain every act as a misunderstood attempt at care. That encounter also cannot be made a universal account of Eberron religion. [R2]

Cosmology remains local to its source. The Realms' Weave, Eberron's uncertainties, Planescape's structure, and Ravenloft's domain premises should not be welded into one theological textbook. An explicit crossover is different from accidental source confusion. Fictional gods, rulers, hosts, and contracts also confer no authority over actual participants or platform permissions.

## 9. Language and communication: more than species labels

Baker's Elvish essay proposes a personal Eberron interpretation connecting the language to Thelanis and magical rather than ordinary historical transmission. It acknowledges differences from published assumptions. This is an option, not a documented complete constructed language: it does not supply enough grammar, phonology, or historical texts to establish such completeness. [E9]

**Interpretation:** a permission to understand speech, naming aesthetic, linguistic system, and social practice are different achievements. A fluent speaker can still misunderstand a legal formula, ritual reference, or elite joke. Magical translation need not produce cultural familiarity or trust. Conversely, familiar speech can communicate desire, affection, contempt, or fear without requiring an invented dictionary.

Occupational and institutional registers are more richly evidenced here than syntax: guilds, treaty participants, religious officials, and invited guests use categories that carry relationships. A lack of complete grammar need not be a failure; claims of linguistic depth based only on decorative syllables would be. Performance and pleasurable conversation also deserve attention without turning every utterance into a test of social control.

## 10. Named people: institutions interpreted differently

These studies are source-grounded readings, not diagnoses or claims that an origin determines a personality.

**High Cardinal Krozen and Jaela Daran — two readings of obligation.** In Baker's personal account, Krozen is a capable, charismatic, ruthlessly pragmatic protector of a narrowly defined community, while Jaela's concern reaches more universally. Shared religious language does not guarantee agreement about whom to protect or which means to permit. The comparison makes an institution an argument rather than one moral personality. [E10]

**Zerasha — dangerous knowledge and community significance.** Baker identifies the medusa priestess as a trusted oracle in Graywall, then develops possibilities for mentorship and ambitious inquiry. Her form and affiliation do not exhaust her relationships with those consulting her. Someone feared by outsiders can possess competence her community values. These elaborations are not universally binding canon. [E10]

**Lady Celyria ir'Tain — hospitality as power.** Her capacity to include unusual guests makes entertainment and patronage overlap. The power is the ability to make someone recognizable to a consequential audience, not merely an attack bonus. Curiosity, enjoyment, and hierarchy can occupy the same invitation. [E5]

**Prince Oargev — charm, inheritance, and an unfinished future.** Baker's selected version joins artistic and social promise to refugee leadership. Courtship can concern a community's direction without making affection merely a disguise for calculation. Possible partners leave his future open; incompatible genealogy is disclosed rather than silently harmonized. [E7]

**Strahd von Zarovich — the host as threat.** The official *Curse of Strahd* premise presents the vampire lord, castle, and Barovia through a threatening invitation. Hospitality can announce domination instead of refuge. His complete biography, every portrayal, and a fixed outcome for a visiting party were not freshly read or established. [W8]

**Holga and Marlamin — affection is not a claim on a shared future.** The film case below gives a formidable fighter a life outside combat and an ex-husband whose preferences do not have to serve her adventure. The scene is not evidence that the tabletop settings grant all marriages the same history or ending. [R3]

Position supplies opportunities; interpretation and preference shape choices; relationships make those choices matter. The same institution can form rivals, allies, reformers, opportunists, and conscientious professionals. Character depth includes ordinary disagreement and deliberate wrongdoing, not only moral improvement.

## 11. Three other settings as contrasting lenses

### Greyhawk: a usable foothold rather than compulsory total knowledge

The 2024 DMG's public contents place Greyhawk within campaign construction and include the Free City as a detailed starting area. That makes it a dated contemporary reference rather than only a historical footnote. The complete paid chapter was not accessed. [W5]

**Interpretation:** a bounded setting can orient participants, supply pressures, and leave room for choices without first explaining every king or century. That is a different promise from an encyclopedia, even where an encyclopedia exists elsewhere. Contents alone cannot establish whether its residents receive equally detailed lives.

### Planescape: a vast cosmos with local addresses

The 2023 collection separates Sigil, its wards and factions, portals, the Lady of Pain, the Outlands, and gate-towns. Named urban areas include the Hive, Lower, Market, Clerks', and Lady's Wards. This is an organizational map, not a reproduction of complete cosmology or faction doctrine. [W6]

**Interpretation:** cosmic scale can become navigable through a street, contact, route, and immediate reason to go somewhere. Difference can concern access and affiliation rather than only lectures. The risk is making every philosophy a costume or every plane one mood; public contents cannot prove the book avoids those reductions. The Lady of Pain's mystery need not be resolved into either neighborly helpfulness or a conventional combat antagonist.

### Ravenloft: concentrated emotional conditions

*Van Richten's Guide* distinguishes creating a Darklord and domain, multiple horror genres, life in the domains, travelers, and preparation for and aftermath of horror. Its range includes Barovia, Borca, Darkon, and other domains, rather than only one vampire story. [W7]

**Interpretation:** a place can concentrate an emotional experience through landscape, authority, and danger. The risk is flattening residents into props for someone else's nightmare. Asking what they value beyond escape remains useful, but the answer must come from the chosen domain. *Death House* is a deliberately horrific location, not evidence that all Ravenloft households are identically cruel or that a whole D&D world should have its tone.

## 12. Systems working together: published practices and bounded cases

The earlier version supplied three explicitly hypothetical sequences about a commission, border recognition, and a displaced musician. Their useful distinctions—professional credit, legal standing, and contested cultural memory—are retained in sections 5–7. They are not counted as researched events. The following cases distinguish a recurring published practice, a fixed screen encounter, and conditional adventure outcomes.

### A. The Tain Gala: something worth attending

Celyria's household sustains an elite circle while making selected outsiders visible to it. Guests seek entertainment, company, romance, and recognition; performers can gain access without entering the hereditary circle. Baker's account offers dining, dancing, intoxicants, and social games, not a mandatory criminal revelation. His proposed acts and plot hooks remain proposals rather than a list of events that canonically occurred. [E5]

**Interpretation:** a successful gathering can simply deliver its pleasures. Access still matters, and the host can enjoy entertaining while exercising power. The source establishes a recurring institution, not the outcome of a particular character's invented evening. That is a different evidentiary object from the two narratives below.

### B. Holga's visit: an ended marriage and a continuing life

In *Honor Among Thieves*, Holga visits Marlamin, whom she still loves. Their former union cost her tribal belonging, but he has moved on and now shares a quieter life with Gwinn. He does not resume the marriage. Holga leaves with a walking stick whose later magical usefulness connects the domestic visit to the expedition, without making his present household merely a waiting room for her return. [R3]

**Interpretation:** her martial competence does not cancel vulnerability, while his refusal is not cruelty merely because it hurts. Affection, preference, and permission remain separate. A useful object can emerge from the encounter without turning emotional loss into an automatic bargain for a better weapon. The scene's particularity matters more than an invented general rule about cross-species marriage.

### C. Death House: different kinds of release

In the 2016 adventure, the Durst household's cult tortures and kills visitors while the children, Rose and Thorn, die locked in the attic. The apparent children outside are lures; the actual ghosts' remains can be laid to rest. Wealth and respectable rooms therefore connect to deliberate victimization, not merely misunderstood custom. [R2]

The cult's apparitions demand a sacrifice. Compliance permits departure; refusal brings a creature and a dangerous escape. These are authored alternatives, not one established campaign ending. Strahd's satisfaction with either course is not a moral endorsement the reader must adopt. The house's persistence means that escaping, defeating a creature, and giving the children rest do not abolish the domain's power. [R2]

**Interpretation:** the case offers investigation, dread, survival, and a limited compassionate achievement without guaranteeing reconciliation. Its treatment of the murdered nursemaid as a hostile specter allows less negotiation than the children's fear. That asymmetry is worth criticizing rather than inventing an unprinted redemptive branch. Maps and room geometry were not visually inspected in this pass.

## 13. Selective depth, contradictions, and criticism

**Not everything needs an answer.** An unspecified distant village is not necessarily a defect. An unresolved cause can support several campaigns. The question is whether missing information prevents understanding immediate choices. A mystery can remain open while local evidence and consequences stay reliable.

**Not every contradiction is productive.** Incompatible ages or parentage can be source-management errors. Baker's disclosure of chosen versions is useful precisely because he does not pretend that every inconsistency was designed as a secret.

**Species, culture, and ethics differ.** Biology should not be presumed to determine every profession, opinion, or action. Complicating one stereotype does not prove the whole franchise has resolved representation. The paired character readings support plurality, not a comprehensive cultural audit.

**Magic requires selective consequence.** A world need not be an econometric simulation, but pervasive healing, transport, or communication should not disappear whenever inconvenient. The strongest material here makes specialists and institutions part of the explanation. Calculating a spell's hypothetical output is not evidence that every society already uses it that way.

**Adventurers are not the population.** Expedition-oriented abilities need not be the only valued forms of competence. Residents can pursue maintenance, care, recognition, romance, art, and public responsibility. Conversely, a capable combatant may genuinely want a challenging contest. Calling another vocation valuable need not demote martial ambition automatically.

**An experience can work differently for different critics.** Sandie Angulo Chen values the film's friendships and the interest added by Holga's former marriage; Kevin Finnerty finds the characterization comparatively thin. Brian Eggert appreciates tactile design and charm but questions the familiar structure and repeated object-retrieval business. These are disagreements about presentation, not proof that the same scene establishes different fictional events. An editor's age guidance is not used to define this research's permissible subjects. [R4–R6]

**Popularity is not proof of a technique.** This research does not show that one setting is currently most played, that a lore feature caused D&D's success, or that published worlds are always more coherent than home campaigns. Familiarity and social investment can coexist with frustration about revisions, commercial packaging, or the limited development of particular lives.

**Possible transfer, not a prescription:** distinguish a power from access to a service, a desirable invitation from permission to control its recipient, and a local victory from complete reform. Give a setting particular pursuits rather than assuming kindness, cruelty, or moral correction is its universal purpose. These are comparative questions; they do not amend engine contracts or propose a catalogue of mechanics.

## 14. Reading routes, evidence, and completion boundary

For **2025 Forgotten Realms orientation**, the two books' contents lead toward a chosen region and institution. For **ordinary magic**, Baker's houses, education, rural-life, food, law, and faith essays provide complementary perspectives. For **character formation**, compare Krozen/Jaela, Oargev, and the gala with the film's bounded relationship. For **medium and emphasis**, compare Planescape's organization and artist testimony with the written horror encounter and differing responses to the film.

These routes do not mean that all linked paid books were read. Complete chapters, novels, videos, comments, and actual-play recordings are separate evidence surfaces. Greyhawk and Planescape remain predominantly orientation-level comparisons; adding a detailed Barovian encounter does not retrospectively deepen their evidentiary base.
