# Eastshade — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: substantial creator-reported overall success; disappointing console launch and operational strain**

## Origin, team and financial boundary

Danny Weinbaum began after professional environment-art work. By release, he and Jaclyn were the only full-time core members, with specialists contributing. His August 2020 account reports **US$2 million gross**, approximately **US$1.1 million after platform deductions**, and a **US$200,000 initial cash budget plus US$60,000 postlaunch**. He stresses that living cheaply suppressed the cash budget relative to hiring at commercial rates. These are creator figures, not audited profit. [S1]

The game released for PC on **February 13, 2019**, followed by Xbox One and PS4 on **October 21, 2019**. The same retrospective says console sales initially reached only about **10% of expectations**, with later Microsoft featuring improving Xbox results. The disappointing port launch did not make the whole project unsuccessful. [S1]

A private playable section, a public video and a separately sold spinoff are different forms of exposure. This record does not establish the exact first public downloadable Eastshade demo or the first-ever mention outside the website.

## Marketing labor and specialist roles

Weinbaum credits **Charlene Lebrun / Player Two PR** with strategy and release assets/timing, and **DO Games** with console ports. He reports streaming to around **30–50 Twitch viewers**, some becoming beta testers, plus a paid private streamer playtest. These are concrete roles and participation, not proof that a large social following was necessary. [S1]

The creator's favorable PR account is not an audited return-on-spend or a current provider recommendation. It is nevertheless useful alongside cases where an agency disappointed: “hired marketing help” can mean different deliverables and different expectations.

No Kickstarter campaign, amount or duration is established. The development account describes savings and family support rather than a documented funding publisher. [S1] Exact specialist hiring channels, contract terms, attribution and a separate marketing-team headcount remain unavailable.

## Case-specific observations

The chronology supplies a useful counterpoint to both “announce immediately” and “wait until nearly finished.” There was early public writing, later meaningful private play, then a more presentable trailer—three different commitments with different audiences.

A publisher counterfactual cannot be measured from this record. Outsourcing selected services while retaining publishing control was one workable arrangement for this team; whether it maximized income or minimized founder workload is unknown. A successful PC market also did not guarantee matching demand or economics on consoles.
