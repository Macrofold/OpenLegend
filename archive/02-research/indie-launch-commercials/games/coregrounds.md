# Coregrounds — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful crowdfunding and unsustainable multiplayer launch**

## Origin and public-development sequence

Mario Kaiser began in 2013, spent over a year developing the concept, and published a proof of concept on the game's website after another six months. Friends and family tested it; approximately six more months produced a better-looking beta. Substantial promotion followed that beta, including Berlin's Talk & Play, AMaze and Quo Vadis. [S1]

The project attracted a few dozen exceptionally committed early players. Kaiser recruited a server programmer from that community, hired an artist, took a part-time job and continued production for almost two more years. This is a concrete community-to-collaborator route, although the artist's recruitment channel and contract terms are undisclosed. [S1]

## What happened commercially

Kaiser attributes the initial loss of traffic to rushed technical/gameplay problems. The team failed to assemble enough regular players for its competitive multiplayer design and shut down the servers after the 2018 launch, also abandoning planned mobile versions. He later released the code. His accounts differ slightly on whether shutdown was late 2018 or early 2019; the precise day is unresolved. No revenue or profit figure is disclosed. [S1], [S4]

**Outcome classification:** an expressly abandoned, unsustainable service and failed campaign, not a sales estimate inferred from Steam reviews. The devoted early fans were real; they were insufficient evidence of a viable ongoing player population.

## Case-specific observations and hiring limits

**Interpretation:** the distinction worth preserving is enthusiasm versus sustainable population. A small number of people willing to theorize about a competitive game may be invaluable testers without supplying enough simultaneous opponents at launch.

The recruitment story also creates a question rather than a universal hiring rule: when a community member becomes a developer, who owns delivery responsibilities, compensation and maintenance? The available sources do not disclose that agreement. No financing publisher, hired marketing agency or full production budget is established.

The creator's retrospective opinions about frameworks and mobile performance are not independently tested technical findings. This dossier does not adopt them as an architecture recommendation for Open Legend.
