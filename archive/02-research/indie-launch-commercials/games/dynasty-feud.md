# Dynasty Feud — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful first-month commercial launch (2017)**

## Origin, team and development maturity

Jon Sanchez describes Kaia Studios as five graduates making their first commercial game over more than eighteen months. Event visitors enjoyed the playable multiplayer brawler, but the team struggled to communicate its distinctive appeal. [S1]

Steam identifies Kaia Studios as developer and publisher and dates the PC release to **May 23, 2017**. The product requires two to four players, locally or online. The later store listing is free-to-play; that must not be confused with its paid launch. [S2]

The developer attributes the signup spike largely to free-key/giveaway communities, not successful acquisition of committed customers. The studio hired a PR company but says it expected strategic guidance it did not receive. Agency identity, fees and contracted deliverables remain undisclosed. [S1]

## Funding and commercial result

No Kickstarter campaign or crowdfunding amount is established in the reviewed evidence. **Not established is not proof that no campaign existed.** The documented release route is a studio-published PC product with outside PR assistance, rather than a verified publisher-financed production. [S2]

Sanchez reports **300 first-month sales against a 3,000-copy goal** and calls the result disastrous. [S1] This is a dated developer report, not a lifetime sales estimate or audited profit figure. No complete budget, net receipts, refunds or later recovery account is available here.

A PS4 edition subsequently shipped: the US PlayStation listing gives **December 12, 2017**, with **Relevo Videogames** as publisher. That establishes continued distribution after the poor PC debut, not commercial recovery. [S3]

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** testing with strangers and earning enthusiastic event reactions were genuine achievements. The evidence is more useful than a retrospective that simply says nobody heard of the game.

**Negative interpretation:** the large registration number concealed a much smaller population actually available to play. For a game sold around multiplayer encounters, total signups and simultaneous compatible opponents answer different business questions. Neither the signup count nor a good convention session establishes a viable paid audience.

**Collaboration question exposed by this case:** a PR contractor's distribution work and a studio's need for positioning, audience validation and launch planning are not automatically the same service. The account does not establish contractual misconduct or prove that all PR agencies work alike. Recruitment methods for the five founders, additional artists and any other contractors are not documented.

The developer's 2017 explanations of Steam visibility are treated as contemporary interpretations, not verified descriptions of today's platform algorithm. This dossier does not turn them into Open Legend launch policy.
