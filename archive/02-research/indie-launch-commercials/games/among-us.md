# Among Us — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: modest initial release followed by enormous 2020 adoption and commercial success**

## Origin, team and production maturity

Innersloth was founded in **2015**, initially worked part-time, and moved to full-time development in late 2018. Its earlier work included Dig2China and the Henry Stickmin series; this was not a studio without previous games. [S1]

Among Us began from Marcus Bromander's interest in a physically played hidden-role game and The Thing. The original core was **Forest Willard, Marcus Bromander and, joining later for additional art, Amy Liu**. The mobile release on **June 15, 2018** initially supported local play. The studio regarded it as complete by January 2020 before its summer breakout prompted renewed work. [S2]

Exact first concept date, pre-beta labor months and the earliest off-platform public mention are not established. Crucially, the later viral audience encountered an already released game, not an unplayable concept seeking initial crowdfunding.

## Early community: the recoverable 2018–2019 record

| Date | Public artifact and what it establishes |
|---|---|
| **August 17, 2018** | PC/online beta announcement; explicitly links the studio's **Discord and Twitter**. Both existed by then, after the mobile beta and before paid Steam release. [S3] |
| **September 27, 2018** | An itch.io retweet produced a visible activity spike, according to the programmer's analytics account. [S4] |
| **October 4, 2018** | Developer reported approximately **1,000–1,200 daily app users** and only **US$35 earned**, primarily with mobile advertising at that stage. These are not Steam-only users or profit. [S4] |
| **November 8, 2018** | Announced the end of free PC beta access and **US$5 pricing from November 16** on Steam, itch.io and Kartridge; mobile remained free with monetization. [S5] |
| **December 2018** | Kevin Choi's December 14 video drove another influx; the developer reported sometimes reaching **1,200 concurrent users**, constrained by server capacity. [S6] |
| **May 8, 2019** | Official devlog announced **one million downloads**, long before the global 2020 breakout. [S7] |

The December account explicitly says the two artists handled Discord while hundreds joined to report connection problems. That is a concrete **support-driven community influx**, not a claim that every member joined through a carefully planned Discord marketing campaign. It also corrects a tempting misreading of a chart: the programmer says an apparent spike was partly a counting bug. [S6]

## Blog, social and video sequencing

The surviving itch.io archive begins in August 2018 and contains frequent update posts, including roughly weekly patches during parts of spring 2019 and longer gaps later. It is a dated development record, not evidence of uninterrupted weekly blogging throughout production. [S7]

In the November 2018 pricing post, the team said reduced bug severity made it more comfortable promoting the game and that growing server usage required revenue. It also added **in-game announcements** because it lacked a single channel reaching all players. That is unusually direct evidence of product readiness affecting promotion and of owned social accounts failing to reach the whole installed audience. [S5]

The dedicated **Among Us Twitter account** first posted on **November 18, 2020**, then reached approximately **one million followers within a month**, according to Victoria Tran's interview episode notes. This account is distinct from the earlier studio/creator accounts. Its large follower count followed the breakout; it was not the precondition for the 2018 debut. [S8]

A November 23, 2020 studio post introduced Tran as the new **community director and fourth team member**. Dedicated community staffing therefore expanded after the game was already widely popular. That post also publicized a map preview and official merchandise. [S9]

A complete creator-channel upload cadence, historical studio-Twitter follower series, Discord founding day/member curve, mailing-list history and campaign-level purchase attribution remain unknown. Community-created videos must not be counted as uploads on the developer's own channel.

## Scale, monetization and continued distribution

Sensor Tower's September 2020 study estimated **86.6 million mobile downloads** and **US$3.2 million in mobile in-app spending** through its reporting window. Approximately 70% of those installs occurred during August and the first half of September. These are third-party estimates, exclude a complete PC/ad-revenue account, and do not equal developer profit. [S10]

Innersloth's press kit later records almost **500 million monthly active users in November 2020** and over **four billion YouTube views by October 2020**. The views span audience-created content, not four billion unique people or paid customers. [S2]

Innersloth subsequently announced and confirmed **Xbox and PlayStation digital launches on December 14, 2021**, including Xbox Game Pass. Physical editions were coordinated separately through **Maximum Games**, with staggered regional dates. A later retail partner does not establish that a publisher financed the original mobile game. [S11], [S12]

No Kickstarter campaign, pledge total or crowdfunding duration is established. A complete initial budget, outsourced-art recruitment process, PR-agency contract and paid-marketing expenditure were not recovered. The named early artists and later community hire are established; their hiring channels and fees are not.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** this case has evidence at the scale of hundreds and thousands of players, not only at the scale of millions. It allows the researcher to distinguish a tiny revenue stream, a functioning early community, several successive discovery events and the later mass audience.

**Negative interpretation:** the eventual outcome should not erase two years of uncertainty or imply that keeping any weak launch alive will produce the same result. The 2018 records also show a real cost of success: player acquisition could immediately create support and service-capacity work for the same people making the game.

**Collaboration interpretation:** early community support was distributed among creators; specialist community leadership arrived later. That timing is an observation about this team, not a universal rule to delay hiring until a game becomes viral.
