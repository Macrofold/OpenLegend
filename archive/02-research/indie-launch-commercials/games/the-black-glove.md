# The Black Glove — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful Kickstarter in 2014; project shelved in 2015**

## Origin and team qualification

Day For Night Games brought together former BioShock-series developers around a surreal first-person game set in a theater. The team's professional credits were a prominent part of its pitch. This is not a clean example of a first-time solo creator starting without recognition. A reliable campaign-era count of full-time core members versus external collaborators was not recovered; **under-ten team status remains unverified**. [S1], [S2]

The game was publicly announced before its October 2014 campaign. Its visual environments and videos existed during fundraising, but the reviewed evidence does not establish an unrestricted public demo at campaign opening or the exact number of production months already invested. [S2]

## Campaign economics and promotion

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Campaign | **October 6–November 7, 2014**; Kicktraq labels the inclusive calendar span **33 days**. [S1] |
| Goal / pledges | **US$550,000 goal; US$216,517 pledged**. Because the goal was missed, this is not money successfully collected through that campaign. [S1], [S2] |
| Supporters | **5,961 backers**. [S1] |
| Campaign changes | New trailer and PS4 support October 27; VR announcement October 31; repeated final-week appeals. [S1] |
| Late-stage outreach | A Save the Black Glove hashtag and requests to share unreleased footage; press coverage continued during the final days. [S1] |

Kicktraq's retained update excerpts give unusually useful campaign sequencing. An October 13 update favored fewer, denser messages; the final week shifted to frequent countdown appeals. That is evidence of changing **campaign communication**, not the upload cadence of a long-running YouTube channel. [S1]

A Kickstarter-hosted creator interview discusses the distinctive presentation, writer **Charles Soule's** involvement through prior collaboration and responses to backer requests. Its accessible excerpt helps identify how contributors and platform promises entered the pitch; it does not establish their contracts, fees or a pre-existing mass audience. [S3]

## After the failed campaign

The team did not immediately rerun a lower-target campaign. Contemporary reporting described the requested budget as connected to what the proposed production actually needed. Prominent coverage and support had not closed the funding gap. [S2]

By May 2015, Joe Fielder said the developers had built a demonstration for prospective publishers and shown it privately at events including GDC and PAX East. Interest did not produce a sufficient agreement; team members moved to other paid work and the project was shelved. **That later publisher demo must not be backdated as proof of a public Kickstarter demo.** [S4], [S5]

No completed commercial Steam or console release is established for this project in the studied period. The measured failure is fundraising and continuation, not a quantified poor-sales launch. A promise of PS4/VR support is not evidence that those versions were completed.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** the campaign generated real support, recognizable visual assets and extensive public explanation. Its dated updates make it possible to study adaptation during a campaign rather than judging only the final total.

**Negative interpretation:** visible enthusiasm and impressive previous credits did not supply the production budget. Expanding the platform proposition and increasing final-week promotion also did not guarantee that enough new supporters would arrive.

**Collaboration question:** established creative relationships helped assemble the pitch, while continued full production still depended on finance. No specific artist-recruitment marketplace, PR contract, paid-ad budget or dedicated internal marketing headcount is established. The case cannot support an inference that a particular contractor would have rescued the campaign.

This is a historical comparison case, not a claim that the intellectual property could never be revived. Exact team size, complete pre-campaign audience history and all later rights/development decisions remain open evidence gaps.
