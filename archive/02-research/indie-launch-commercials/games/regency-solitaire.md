# Regency Solitaire — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: commercially successful across its intended channels, relatively weak on Steam in 2015**

## Origin and first release

Grey Alien Games' historical-romance solitaire title reached the studio website on **January 2, 2015**, then casual download portals. The creator's year-end account describes a German retail agreement and later mainstream coverage following Steam distribution. This was an established studio extending its catalog, not an unknown creator's first project. [S1]

The official Steam page dates that edition to **May 18, 2015**, credits Grey Alien as developer/publisher, and describes the same romance-and-solitaire proposition. Steam was a subsequent storefront release, not the game's first availability anywhere. [S2]

## Publicity and commercial sequence

In an **August 4, 2015** report, Birkett said casual portals had produced **ten times the Steam gross revenue**, and called the game an overall success. He reported 94% positive reception, but disappointing Steam sales. His explanation—that the theme/audience fit was stronger elsewhere—is attributed, not independently proven. [S3]

The same post compares two 25% discount periods, describes direct Twitter promotion and a small boost following a Felicia Day mention. One period also coincided with a Mac release and extra storefront exposure. These overlapping changes prevent treating the comparison as a clean causal test of discount timing. Absolute unit/revenue values had been removed from the post and are not reconstructed. [S3]

A **January 2016** studio review says the casual distributors supplied marketing to their existing customers, whereas reaching Steam press was unfamiliar work. It reports both founders receiving salaries, but that is a **studio-level result**, not proof that this game's sales alone paid every expense. [S4]

## Financing, collaborators and audience inheritance

The December 2015 archive credits **GamesLab South West**, with public-program support. The amount, payment timing and precise obligations are not disclosed here. Self-publishing therefore must not be translated into “no outside support.” No Kickstarter campaign, target, pledged amount or campaign duration is established. [S1]

The later project comparison describes about **one year of production**. [S5] Complete collaborator credits, artist recruitment, compensation, ownership arrangements and a dedicated marketing headcount were not audited. Distribution partners, public support and specialist contractors would be different relationships.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** “weak Steam launch” and “unsuccessful game” are not equivalent. A channel that adds customers but is not the dominant source of revenue can still be worthwhile; conversely, wider press recognition does not prove that channel will outperform the less visible original market.

The unanswered question for a new game is where its likely paying players already discover comparable experiences. This case does not establish that 2015 casual-portal economics, fees or audiences can be reproduced today. It supplies a historical comparison, not a current distributor recommendation.

The three Grey Alien dossiers are a related studio comparison, not three random draws from the industry. No causal success percentage should be computed from them.
