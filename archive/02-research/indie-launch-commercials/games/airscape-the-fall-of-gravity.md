# Airscape: The Fall of Gravity — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful commercial launch, 2015 evidence window**

## Origin, team and outcome

Airscape is an octopus platformer made by an international student team. The official site names **Daniel West** for design/programming, **Claudia Starke** and **Dieter Theuns** for art, and **Sam Gossner** for music/sound. Theuns was initially hired for UI work and then contributed additional environmental detail. This is a small collaborating team, not a programmer producing every asset alone. [S2]

West's September 8, 2015 postmortem reports **fewer than 150 sales across platforms more than three weeks after launch**, with revenue insufficient even to recoup PR expenditure. The classification concerns that launch, not a verified lifetime loss statement. [S1]

## Development and publicity timeline

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Development | Roughly 3.5 years, largely part-time; West spent about one year full-time. [S1] |
| Public promotion | About 2.5 years between announcement and release, including forums, press and conventions. [S1] |
| Prelaunch | Repeated Indie MEGABOOTH appearances and other recognition; an experienced games PR firm hired near release. [S1] |
| Paid launch | Steam and Humble distribution; the September postmortem assesses the first weeks. [S1], [S2] |
| Postlaunch | Developer publicly questions the mismatch between attention and purchases. No audited later recovery is established in this review. [S1] |

The exact first announcement, first freely downloadable build and earliest convention-demo dates are unresolved. Public exposure plainly preceded completion; a polished full release was not the first marketing artifact.

## Crowdfunding and funding

West says crowdfunding was not used because the team did not need it. Early Access was also considered a poor fit for this game's discovery/puzzle structure. Kickstarter opening/closing dates, goal and pledges are therefore **not applicable to the route described**, rather than missing successful-campaign data. [S1]

The postmortem does not disclose an itemized financing budget or the PR firm's fee. Do not derive one by multiplying development years by a professional salary.

This case should not be compressed into “they forgot to market.” There is affirmative evidence of sustained outreach and purchased PR. That does not prove all the outreach was well-targeted or effective.

## Launch, reception and commercial interpretation

The official site presents a distinctive gravity mechanic, carefully supplied press assets and a clear purchase route. Those are observable presentation choices, not an independent verdict on how compelling the game was to buyers. [S2]

West's explanation raises a possible mismatch between cute presentation and demanding platforming, and warns that following successful outliers can create unrealistic expectations. These are his retrospective hypotheses, not controlled causal findings. [S1]

**Case-specific positive:** bounded initial UI work grew into a broader art contribution, illustrating a real specialist collaboration rather than a hypothetical “hire an artist” recommendation. **Case-specific negative:** visibility, awards and professional PR did not establish sufficient paid demand in the measured window.

A useful unanswered question is whether the game attracted the wrong audience, lost interested visitors at the store page, or simply appealed to too small a paying niche. The sources do not provide the traffic and conversion data required to separate those possibilities. The low total alone cannot prove which explanation dominates.

## Staffing and evidence limitations

The official team biographies preserve role attribution and personal portfolio links, but not the first contact, selection process, compensation, work-for-hire terms or relationship chronology. The launch PR firm is not named in the reviewed account. Self-directed development alongside an agency engagement should not be confused with a publisher underwriting the project.

Historical wishlists, demo downloads, event conversion, social cohorts, ad spending, refund rates and lifetime receipts are missing. The dossier intentionally preserves that gap rather than estimating sales from today's reviews. The creator's belief in the game's quality is attributed, not treated as independent evidence.
