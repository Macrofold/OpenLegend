# Lemma — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: mixed — failed crowdfunding, completed commercial release, insufficient early return to fund a similar next game**

## Origin and team

Evan Todd reports **one core developer, six contractors, three years part-time and 1.5 years full-time**. His stated **US$30,000 budget** excludes a separately estimated **US$80,000 opportunity cost**. [S1] These are creator estimates, not audited accounts; forgone salary is not an additional cash payment to a contractor.

The shipped game is first-person parkour in a changing voxel environment. Steam lists **Helvetica Scenario** as both developer and publisher and **May 12, 2015** as release. [S6] Current company branding does not establish the exact name under which every earlier contractor was engaged.

## Public builds and community sequence

| Date / stage | What the surviving artifact establishes |
|---|---|
| **February 27, 2012** | Todd's blog announced a downloadable Windows alpha, embedded video, screenshots and a request for feedback. It also pointed readers to the game's website. This was playable exposure years before commercial release. [S2] |
| **March 18, 2014** | An improved public demo, Kickstarter and Greenlight were live together. The creator explicitly credited Reddit and TIGForums feedback with changes to levels and movement. Several earlier revisions had already been discarded. [S3] |
| **March 26, 2014** | The creator reported **174 backers, over 32% funding and nearly 4,200 Greenlight yes votes**. Press coverage, a creator interview, Let's Plays and a development livestream were part of the campaign-period record. [S4] |
| **May 12, 2015** | PC commercial release. Contemporary coverage identified Steam, Humble and itch.io distribution, a US$15 price and a 10% launch discount; a free demo remained available. [S6], [S7] |

The 2012 artifact is the earliest public build verified here, not proof that no earlier forum post existed. The 2014 demo announcement establishes meaningful playable development at fundraising, not an objective completion percentage or proof that all promised work was nearly finished.

## Kickstarter and financing

Contemporary campaign coverage gives a **US$10,000 target**, intended for an artist and composer rather than the creator's own pay. Rewards included a backer forum and private development streams; those were advertised benefits, not independently verified engagement. [S5]

The campaign failed. Todd continued by reducing spending, taking contract work and making some planned outsourced art himself. [S1] Exact closing date, duration, final pledges and final backer count could not be recovered from the original campaign in this pass. The **March 26 progress snapshot is not the final result**. No successful crowdfunding proceeds are inferred from an unsuccessful campaign's pledge count.

## Launch marketing, collaborators and early return

Todd found playtesters through a Columbus incubator and monthly local meetups. He reported **US$0 marketing budget**, direct press/creator outreach two weeks before launch, and announcements across social accounts, community sites, YouTube and email. More than **400 third-party videos accumulated five million views**, heavily influenced by jacksepticeye. Players also volunteered translations. [S1]

His June 2015 snapshot records **3,171 Steam sales**, **US$43,554 gross before Steam's cut**, and **10,126 demo downloads with 277 recorded conversions**. He valued completing the game but said it had not earned enough to fund another of similar size. [S1]

These are bounded early results, not lifetime profit. The public footage count is not the developer's upload count, and demo conversion is not a universal forecast for other games.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** the useful distinction is between proof that strangers would play a changing prototype, proof that supporters would pledge, and proof that sales could support continued production. The record provides evidence for all three questions without making them interchangeable.

The creator's broad views about Kickstarter and Greenlight are historical opinions, not current platform policy or recommendations adopted for Open Legend. This dossier is counted once as mixed, not once in each outcome cohort.
