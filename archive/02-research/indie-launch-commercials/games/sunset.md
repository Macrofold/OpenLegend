# Sunset — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful launch; subsequent debt recovery**

## Outcome and team-size qualification

Sunset failed its creators' commercial expectations in May–June 2015. **By August, however, they reported breaking even when art grants and crowdfunding were included.** It would be inaccurate to preserve the dramatic failure announcement while omitting that recovery. They nevertheless abandoned their intended commercial production model. [S1], [S2]

Tale of Tales' artistic core was **Auriea Harvey and Michaël Samyn**, but the official credits name numerous additional artists, programmers, audio contributors and commercial specialists. Treat this as a two-founder project with a much larger contributor network, not a two-person total production. [S4]

## Origins and sequence

The creators describe an older apartment-game idea that changed substantially before Sunset. An idea's age is not continuous production time. Their April 2015 account explains how an earlier nighttime/ghost premise became a story told through an immigrant housekeeper's encounters with an apartment. [S5]

## Crowdfunding and financing

**Kickstarter: US$67,636 pledged against US$25,000; 2,228 backers; 30 days, June 17–July 17, 2014.** These are gross campaign figures, not the game's entire budget. The official site also credits the VAF/Flanders Game Fund. [S3], [S4]

In the early failure account the developers describe spending approximately **US$40,000 extra** in expectation of recouping it soon after release. More than **4,000 copies had changed hands**, including backers and discounted copies. They had hired PR, bought advertising and received extensive advice. Their claim that those efforts failed to generate sufficient purchases is a retrospective assessment, not controlled channel attribution. [S1]

## Community channels and the actual audience relationship

The official site connected several kinds of material: a development blog, a TIGSource development thread, Steam discussion, a visual-reference Tumblr, fictional character diaries, and social accounts for in-world entities. This is an unusually elaborate storytelling campaign, rather than only repeated screenshots. It also supplied trailers and both Steam and DRM-free purchase routes. The linked TIGSource thread was not readable in this review, so its first-post date remains unknown. [S4]

The studio already had a long-running artistic identity and previous players. Following the launch disappointment, discounts, bundles and publicity about the team's withdrawal generated additional purchases. By August, **17,000 copies** had changed hands, explicitly including Kickstarter and Humble-bundle copies. Existing Endless Forest players also supported the broader studio through donations; Patreon was part of its future support model. These groups are not interchangeable with newly acquired full-price Sunset buyers. [S2]

## People, publishing and marketing work

The official credits identify **Leigh Alexander and Ste Curran / Agency** for project-direction advice, and **Haley Uyrus and Lewis Denby / BeefJack** for marketing. **Austin Wintory** supplied music and **Kris Force** sound; other named specialists supplied 3D assets, animation, systems and graphics work. The sources establish roles, not recruitment fees, employment status or contract terms. [S4]

The failure account shows that professional assistance and an existing audience were present. It should not be categorized as a developer who never tried promotion. Conversely, crediting an agency does not establish that the agency controlled scope, price or every release decision. [S1]

## Bounded observations and unresolved evidence

**Positive interpretation:** the public follow-up preserves multiple economic dimensions: debt repayment, subsidized break-even, unit distribution and willingness to continue commercial development. Those need not move together.

**Negative interpretation:** a successful campaign and sophisticated narrative marketing did not establish a sufficiently large paying audience at the required time. The available records do not isolate whether positioning, product expectations, price or reach was decisive.

Unknowns include pre-Kickstarter follower totals; exact blog, Twitter and Facebook account dates; YouTube posting frequency; Discord membership/history; newsletter size; public-demo conversion; paid-media impressions; campaign referral shares; and individual hiring terms. No claim of complete social-history reconstruction is made. The later recovery is counted within this same dossier, not as a second successful game.
