# The Ember Guardian — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: creator-reported sustainable first release; difficult launch, one-month financial window, later profitability unverified**

## Identity, source provenance and conflicting dates

The official name is **The Ember Guardian**, by **Ratbit Games**, published by **Slug Disco**. The studio identifies its solo developer as **Julien**. The supplied transcript renders these as “Amber,” “Rad Bit” and “Julian”; the dossier uses the official spellings. [S2–S5]

**[S1] is the transcript Mike supplied**, attributed to the linked developer video. The YouTube page resolves, but a separate complete transcript or the video's financial graphics were not retrieved. This is an attributed first-person account, not audited accounts or a claim that the researcher watched the video.

The transcript repeats “March 2025,” says another six months passed before the demo, and later describes nine months of demo availability by February 2026. These do not form a consistent chronology. **Steam dates the demo to April 14, 2025; Slug Disco announced signing on May 16, 2025; Steam dates full release to April 17, 2026.** These dated artifacts take precedence. The demo's historical first release is not the date of every later redesign. The studio biography says learning began in 2024, while the transcript describes three years of learning/development; the starting year is unresolved. [S1–S5]

## Origin and development maturity

Julien describes abandoning **Hidden Tactics**, an overly ambitious PvP project, then starting this game in October 2024. His premise was a Kingdom-like side-scroller with direct combat, positioned against a perceived small set of comparable games. He reports roughly eighteen months of production before release. These are his account and market judgment, not an independently measured market gap. [S1]

YouTube activity preceded this game: the transcript describes sharing the learning process and earlier projects. Thus “first Steam release” does not mean first public creation or no prior audience. Exact channel opening date and subscriber count at the first reveal are not established. [S1]

Slug Disco's **May 2025** announcement already described a tutorial, short main runs, upgrades, endless mode and streamer-safe music. The later redesign should therefore be understood as changing the demo's emphasis/progression, not evidence that all campaign-related concepts first appeared in 2026. [S4]

No Kickstarter campaign is reported. The creator initially preferred keeping all revenue but accepted a publisher to recover development time spent making trailers and contacting creators. The claim is about workload as well as reach; the public record does not isolate what sales would have been without the agreement. [S1]

## Launch, reviews and operational cost

A recording-only change made enemy hit sounds five times louder and accidentally reached the release build. A quick correction was followed by a weekend of bug reports and performance work. Subsequent patches introduced black-screen/loading trouble and enemy behavior that bypassed barricades; the team rolled back a version. The creator reports the positive-review share falling from approximately 82% to 72%, then recovering above 80% three weeks later. [S1]

The account attributes some lost momentum to the review decline, but contains no controlled measure of its sales effect. It also attributes prolonged featuring partly to a quiet release weekend. Neither explanation establishes a general rule that Fridays are best or that a fixed wishlist threshold guarantees featuring.

**Interpretation:** the small trusted Discord test group contributed valuable feedback but did not reproduce the hardware variety, late-game load and patch interactions exposed by release. A demo that recruits wishlists and a test program that protects a launch are different deliverables.

The creator calls the new job self-sustaining. That personal assessment and a limited first-month net figure can both be true without proving lifetime profit or an eighteen-month salary benchmark. Future sales are possible, not guaranteed. Dividing all first-month units by prelaunch wishlists would **not** measure wishlist conversion: launch also attracted new customers and new wishlists.

Aggregator subscriber counts displayed beside old videos are not accepted as historical counts. The dedicated publisher team is established, but artist recruitment, contractor fees, internal marketing headcount and the exact publishing contract are not.

The studio's news page retains July 2026 content updates. They establish continued support, **not** an independently documented later sales recovery or lifetime earnings. [S2]

## Case-specific questions and observations

The most useful sequence is **earlier learning/public work → visual reveal → playable discovery → publisher partnership → representative demo → paid launch → repair and continued sales**. That sequence is better supported than the headline that one video or an algorithm alone created the business.

For later comparison, preserve three distinct questions: did the demo attract the eventual campaign's audience; did extra promotional labor justify its cost; and did the release team have enough testing/support capacity for the attention it earned? The available evidence does not answer the publisher counterfactual or future revenue trajectory.
