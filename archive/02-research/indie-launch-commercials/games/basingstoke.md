# Basingstoke — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful financial return through January 2020**

## Origin, team and the audience already in place

Puppygames began the project in 2014 while seeking a smaller revenue-producing game. Its first recorded code commit was **June 6, 2014**. A project originally imagined on a much shorter schedule became four years of development, with two full-time people and increasing involvement from a third during the final two years. The retrospective approximates ten person-years of work. [S1]

This was not an unknown developer's first game. Steam's official Puppygames catalog lists earlier commercial releases, including Revenge of the Titans, Titan Attacks!, Droid Assault and Ultratron. [S4] Existing brand familiarity and previous customers must be separated from Basingstoke-specific audience acquisition.

## Publishing, marketing and launch

The creator says an anticipated publisher withdrew before release and replacements declined. A hired PR company allegedly missed the advance release and used a teaser rather than the gameplay launch trailer. These are the studio's recollections; the agency and publisher are unnamed and their perspectives are unavailable. [S2]

Steam confirms **April 27, 2018**, with Puppygames as developer and publisher. The official page sells a single-player stealth/action roguelike, supports Windows, macOS and Linux, and retains positive press excerpts. It now uses the title **Welcome to Basingstoke**. [S3] A later title, store price or review total must not be substituted for its launch presentation.

No Kickstarter campaign or crowdfunding amount is established. Exact recruitment methods, artist contracts and a dedicated internal marketing headcount remain unknown.

## Commercial results and reception

The January 2020 figures published in February report **9,415 units**, **US$70,252 after returns, chargebacks and taxes**, and **£41,296.97 net revenue including smaller sources**. Marketing cost **£8,598.50**, leaving **£32,698.47 before further tax**, not annual studio profit. The creator describes the project as financially ruinous. [S2]

The same account records a **US$29.95 launch price**, a later US$19.95 experiment, approximately **16 million Steam impressions and one million page visits**, and uncertainty about advertising's effect. Its claim that greater Steam exposure would solve the problem is a hypothesis, not demonstrated causation. [S2]

Favorable player sentiment and an actual shipped product can coexist with a poor return on years of labor. No later lifetime recovery is established here; the negative classification is bounded to the reported financial period.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** this case offers unusually useful separation of an established mailing audience, product visibility, sales and money left after marketing. The studio did not simply launch without any publicity.

**Negative interpretation:** a 20,000-person distribution list cannot establish that an expensive, unfamiliar new product has 20,000 ready buyers. The audience's original reason for following the studio, message engagement and the new game's production cost all matter to interpreting that number.

**Collaboration interpretation:** anticipated publisher interest did not supply committed funding or distribution. The PR account also raises a concrete handoff question: who checks that the correct trailer and message actually went out? The evidence does not identify the counterfactual sales impact of either problem, so neither should be presented as the sole cause of failure.
