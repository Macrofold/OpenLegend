# Neon Climber — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful mobile commercial release (2020)**

## Origin and public-development sequence

Nikita Pavlov describes a four-person team, including three game designers, spending **2.5 years part-time** on its first released indie game. An internal prototype contest selected a runner. The team initially prioritized finishing, then sought commercial success without an equivalent early market-validation process. [S1]

A playable build reached **DevGAMM 2019**. Publishers expressed interest but wanted performance metrics; the team instead spent almost another year changing an endless runner into a level-based game before testing the business premise. Event feedback also prompted changes to confusing controls. [S1]

The official itch.io page presents Neon Climber as a **mobile runner**, not a Steam product. The developer's portfolio also retains game-jam projects, so the release was not the team's first exposure to making games. Those surviving pages do not establish the size of a pre-existing commercial audience. [S2], [S3]

## Launch, financing and result

The September 8, 2020 retrospective calls the release a commercial failure: **cost per install exceeded US$1**, while **day-one retention was below 30%**. [S1] These are reported acquisition and retention measurements, not download totals, revenue, profit or timeless thresholds for all mobile games. Their sample sizes, countries, ad networks and exact testing dates are not disclosed.

The author linked iOS and Android distribution. The iOS link was inaccessible during this research; an exact first app-store release day is therefore not asserted. [S1] No Kickstarter, campaign amount, crowdfunding period, publisher financing agreement or hired marketing team is established. Do not mistake publisher conversations at a conference for a signed publishing deal.

## Artists, contractors and collaboration

The core group had no dedicated artist. **Daria Kalinova supplied protagonist concept art**; the team produced the model and used Mixamo animations. Tasks were divided partly by members' interests and skills. Compensation, recruitment channel and ownership terms are not disclosed. [S1]

**Positive interpretation:** a narrow external contribution can coexist with substantial internal production. This is a concrete credit, not evidence that free or speculative collaboration is reliably available.

**Negative interpretation:** attractive booth presentation and publisher curiosity tested different things from repeat mobile play and affordable customer acquisition. The key sequencing question raised by the documented history is why the proposed commercial test was deferred while the product was substantially reworked. This does not demonstrate that a different test result would necessarily have yielded a publishing contract.

## Limits

This case supplies unusually direct small-team production and acquisition evidence, but weak owned-community history. It is included as a mobile launch contrast, not as a Steam launch template. No console launch, lifetime sales recovery or current studio profitability is inferred from the continuing portfolio.
