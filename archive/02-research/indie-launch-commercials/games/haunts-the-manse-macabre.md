# Haunts: The Manse Macabre — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: successful game crowdfunding, stalled delivery in 2012–2013**

## Origin, team and publicity before a selected game

Mob Rules Games' three founders were designer Rick Dakan, programmer Jonathan Wills and artist Austin McKinley. Their November 2011 studio announcement asked supporters to help select the studio's first project from three concepts. The initial proposal was to raise US$20,000 for prototyping, give contributors votes and encourage discussion and referrals. Open Game Labs supplied organizational support. This was explicitly community formation around a studio and its concepts, not a campaign launched only after years of private game production. [S1], [S2]

That initial studio campaign and the later Haunts campaign are different fundraising events. The first failed; project selection subsequently became a free public vote. [S3] Do not describe the 2012 game campaign as the studio's first public appearance.

## Game campaign and development sequence

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| November 2011 | Public studio announcement and proposed concept-selection process. [S1], [S2] |
| May 7–July 6, 2012 | Haunts Kickstarter; **60 days**, **US$28,739 against US$25,000**, **1,214 backers**. [S4], [S5] |
| Campaign proposition | Turn-based horror in which the player controls either inhabitants or intruders; PC, Mac and Linux, with iPad plans. [S4] |
| October 2012 | Creator reported that funding was spent and neither programmer could continue. [S6], [S7] |
| Subsequent communication | Campaign metadata retains a March 5, 2013 update date. A timestamp alone does not establish delivery or the content of that update. [S4] |

The game campaign followed roughly six months after the studio announcement. That is **not** a verified six months of full-time Haunts development. The accessible campaign metadata does not establish when a downloadable public demo first appeared, how polished it was, or how much work remained. By the October crisis, Dakan said the systems existed but the build still contained substantial bugs. That later condition must not be backdated to campaign opening. [S7]

The evidence supports **participation-first publicity**, not a quantified claim that participation produced most of the pledges. Dakan's previous industry experience also differentiates this from an entirely unknown first-time developer. [S1]

## Delivery, collaborators and financial limits

Dakan said one programmer's departure had been expected, but hoped-for spare-time availability did not materialize; the second also moved to other paid employment. He discussed a possible rescue with former colleagues at Blue Mammoth Games and offered personal refunds to people withdrawing support. Neither a prospective rescue nor a refund offer proves completed delivery or universal reimbursement. [S6], [S7]

No completed Steam launch, audited profit statement or reliable download total is established here. The negative classification concerns the documented production shutdown and failure to deliver on the original schedule, **not** failure to reach the 2012 funding target. This dossier does not infer misconduct from a stalled project.

Artist recruitment beyond the named founding artist, employment agreements, PR hiring and a publishing contract are not disclosed. The founders' prior relationships and potential rescue contacts are concrete collaboration routes, but their availability is not reproducible for every solo developer.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** the project offers a genuine example of inviting a community in before selecting the game, rather than merely announcing a polished product. It also shows supporters remaining potential collaborators after a setback.

**Negative interpretation:** community participation and an achieved fundraising target did not secure the ongoing specialist labor needed to finish. The relevant dependency was not just the amount pledged, but whether the people maintaining the unfinished software could remain available for the actual remaining work.

No cross-game recommendation or Open Legend hiring policy is adopted here. The outstanding research questions are the original demo history, the detailed first-campaign results, source-code handoff outcomes and complete refund accounting.
