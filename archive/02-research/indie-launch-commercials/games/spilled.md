# Spilled! — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: successful crowdfunding and substantial first-month sales; full-cost profitability undisclosed**

## Origin and the audience before fundraising

Lente's January 30, 2024 announcement describes taking development seriously at the end of 2022, while renovating a boat to live on. Lower living expenses helped make the work possible; this is personal context, not a replicable funding assumption for every developer. After about a year of sharing progress on Twitter, she reported **17,000 followers and 14,000 Steam wishlists**. The campaign was intended partly to buy development and marketing help. [S1]

This is unusually useful pre-campaign evidence: the audience figures were published when fundraising began, not reconstructed from a much larger later account. They still do not reveal which posts caused purchases or how many followers were prospective players rather than other developers.

## Playable builds, campaign and specialist hiring

| Milestone | Verified record |
|---|---|
| Public demo | Steam dates the demo to **October 20, 2023**. [S2] |
| Kickstarter | **January 30–March 1, 2024; 31 days**. **€31,621** pledged against **€10,000**, from **1,721 backers**. [S3] |
| Visual upgrade | The **July 5, 2024** announcement says campaign money enabled hiring a **technical artist**, followed by an overhauled demo. [S4] |
| Additional discovery | The same announcement reports a **Wholesome Direct** appearance and **over 39,000 wishlists**. It invites press/creator beta requests and newsletter subscriptions. [S4] |
| Commercial release | **March 26, 2025**, Steam, with **Lente** listed as developer and publisher. [S5] |
| First month | Contemporary reporting reproduces the creator's announcement of **50,000 copies sold**. No audited profit figure accompanies it. [S6] |

The campaign initially advertised a 2024 release; the verified commercial date is in 2025. That delay is retained without relabeling the delivered game as a failed campaign. [S3, S5]

A demo existed before Kickstarter, but the later art overhaul did not. Looking at the final trailer and assuming all its polish had to be financed before fundraising would reverse the documented sequence.

## Marketing and collaborators

Both dated announcements were distributed through **Pirate PR**. This establishes an external promotional relationship, not its fee, exact contract or the date it began. The named campaign objective of hiring help was followed by a documented specialist contribution; the technical artist's recruitment channel and employment terms were not disclosed. [S1, S4]

The short clean-up premise, visible before-and-after environmental changes and the creator's boating story appeared together in the pitch. [S1] **Interpretation:** product demonstration and personal narrative were complementary presentation choices. The record cannot separate their sales effects or establish that an unusual lifestyle was necessary.

## Case-specific observations

The record supports **public work and sampling before crowdfunding, followed by funded specialization**, rather than either “concept-only funding” or “finish all production before asking for help.” Its small campaign target is not the game's complete cost: prior unpaid work and living expenses also existed.

The commercial milestone establishes meaningful demand at one point in time. Multiplying copies by a single list price would ignore regional pricing, discounts, refunds, platform fees and other costs. This dossier therefore preserves success in fundraising and sales without inventing take-home income.
