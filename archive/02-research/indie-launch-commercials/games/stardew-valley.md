# Stardew Valley — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: breakout commercial success; solo original production with publishing assistance**

## Origin, team and commercial result

The official press history describes **Eric Barone / ConcernedApe** making the original game himself, including programming, art and music, over roughly four years. It reports **one million copies within two months** of the February 2016 release. This establishes a commercial breakout without using current review counts as a proxy. Later development and porting involved additional people; the original solo-production description must not be applied indiscriminately to the mature business. [S1]

The tenth-anniversary announcement, dated **February 26, 2026**, reports **50 million copies**. This is a later creator-reported milestone, not an audience that existed before the original release. [S4]

This record establishes **public discussion and visible work in progress approximately three years before release**. It does not establish that the first public version was a downloadable demo, or that every feature shown early survived unchanged.

## How the early community was handled

The February 2013 announcement moves an already-existing discussion group to improved forums and a new site. That matters: the website relaunch was not the birth of all interest in the game. The retrieved record does not expose the old forum's registrations, referral sources or engagement history. [S2]

The early blog mixes progress with material people can share: screenshots, a trailer and wallpaper. Its dated posts give a more concrete answer than saying “marketing started early,” but there is no attached analytics record demonstrating how many buyers each post produced. [S3]

## Financing, artists, contractors and release ownership

The 2013 announcement is direct evidence of a **Chucklefish publishing relationship well before release**. The current Steam page names ConcernedApe as publisher; that rolling attribution does not erase the original partnership. [S2], [S5]

For the original game, the useful art-production precedent is the creator doing the work himself rather than hiring an early art department. Later multiplayer assistance and platform expansion belong to a different production phase. The press history does not disclose collaborator compensation, exact recruitment processes or the full terms of the publishing deal. [S1]

No Kickstarter campaign is established in the documented route. Campaign dates, goal and pledges are therefore not applicable to the financing evidence collected here. Neither the press history nor the partnership announcement supplies a complete living-cost budget or an itemized marketing spend.

## Reception and case-specific interpretation

The anniversary post acknowledges the many ways players kept the game alive through fan creations and community participation. That is evidence of a durable postlaunch relationship, not proof that all of those activities existed during the early blog period. [S4]

**Interpretation:** this case separates three often conflated activities: making the game, showing its unfinished development, and obtaining publishing support. They overlapped for years. The public evidence does not require a fully polished final product before the first community contact, but it also does not show an audience forming around an idea with no visible work.

Exact causation remains unresolved: the archive does not allocate sales among genre demand, creator reputation, press, publishing work, social sharing and Steam discovery. Huge lifetime sales cannot answer that attribution question by themselves.

## Source cautions

The rolling press page contains a factsheet date inconsistent with its own narrative. The February 26 date is independently supported by the anniversary and Steam; the conflicting field is not used. “Four years” is a retrospective approximation, not a verified day-by-day labor total. Contractor terms, launch-demo availability, prelaunch follower growth, PR staffing and net profitability remain unknown.
