# Core Defense — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: modest commercial success, with postlaunch setbacks**

## Origin, team and outcome

Mario Kaiser / ehmprah made Core Defense as a solo, single-player spin-off of his earlier multiplayer game. He describes an alpha produced in **two months**, then a Discord community seeded through former players and Alpha Beta Gamer. Community contact therefore began with something playable, not only a concept. [S1]

The one-year retrospective reports **US$72,916 gross across platforms**. Steam accounted for **7,872 copies and US$67,404**. The July 31, 2020 launch began with **3,632 wishlists**, **2,546 first-week units** and **US$20,186 first-week gross**. These are a credible modest outcome, not evidence of personal take-home pay or venture-scale returns. [S2]

## Release and community sequence

| Stage | Product / audience evidence |
|---|---|
| Initial alpha | Playable prototype, then Discord and targeted outreach to recruit testers. [S1] |
| January 2020 | Paid itch.io beta / “first access,” with a Steam page and wishlist widget already available. [S1] |
| Prelaunch Steam demo | Separate free application intended to attract feedback and interest before the paid release. [S3] |
| July 31, 2020 | Steam commercial launch. [S2], [S4] |
| March 19, 2021 | Mastery Expansion DLC. [S2] |
| April 21, 2021 | Freemium iOS/Android versions. [S2] |

The developer's two retrospectives give different itch.io figures: approximately **150 copies** in the earlier narrative versus **106 pre-Steam units / US$1,144** in the later numerical recap. Different periods or categories may explain this, but the sources do not settle it. Do not average them. [S1], [S2]

## Community, discoverability and cadence

Kaiser describes substantial personal participation in Discord and Steam discussions, regular Twitter posts, and genre-oriented creator outreach. This is recurring founder labor, not simply opening a server. Precise Discord counts and its calendar creation date are unavailable. He used community translation plus paid store-page translation, but later found ongoing volunteer coordination difficult. [S1], [S2]

The separately published demo essay documents the deliberate separation between the free acquisition/testing application and the paid product. It belongs to a historical launch experiment, not a statement of current Steam rules. The records do not justify copying old platform visibility assumptions without checking contemporary documentation. [S3]

Exact YouTube account dates, founder upload cadence, historical social growth and email-list size remain unverified. Other creators' coverage should not be confused with a founder-owned channel.

## Financing, marketing help and collaboration

No Kickstarter or funding publisher is established. The official Steam page lists ehmprah as developer and publisher. The launch accounts describe founder-led marketing and community work rather than an identified full-service publishing team. [S1], [S4]

Specific artist-hiring history and contractor terms are not disclosed. Translation illustrates a different collaboration problem: recruiting initial volunteers did not ensure they remained reachable for later content. The developer explicitly regretted not routing translators into his Discord contact network. [S2]

## Reception, continuation and bounded interpretation

The DLC caused a temporary review backlash: the creator reports the overall score falling from 86% to 81% and daily baseline purchases dropping during a negative-recent-review period. Mobile monetization also encountered operational difficulties. These observations qualify the success label rather than erasing the first-year commercial result. [S2]

**Positive interpretation:** the case exposes a tractable sequence—small playable alpha, reachable testers, a paid testing period and a more polished Steam debut. The inherited community is important context, not an invisible advantage to omit.

**Negative interpretation:** a helpful volunteer community also creates coordination and maintenance obligations. A successful original launch does not guarantee that DLC pricing or a mobile business model will be welcomed.

No full profit statement, labor valuation, paid-versus-organic attribution, historical Discord curve or independently audited sales record is available. The archive does not convert the creator's experience into a universal minimum-wishlist rule.
