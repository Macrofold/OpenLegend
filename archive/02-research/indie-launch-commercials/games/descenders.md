# Descenders — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: successful community-led Early Access launch, followed by further paid-sales growth alongside subscription distribution**

## Origin, team and partnership

In a 2023 original interview, publisher **Mike Rose** describes **RageSquid as three developers**, says they brought him a playable pitch, and recalls signing the project before formally founding **No More Robots**. This is a publisher relationship built around a demonstrated game, not evidence of a Kickstarter-funded concept. The quoted twenty-million-player estimate is not an audited paid-copy total. [S1]

Steam confirms **February 9, 2018 Early Access** and **May 7, 2019 full release**, with RageSquid developing and No More Robots publishing. The product is procedurally generated downhill biking with progression and consequences for failed runs. [S2]

## How the early Discord acquired a purpose

Rose's first-person Discord case study says the server was part of the original announcement plan. **Beta access**, exclusive development material and a **three-team metagame with weekly challenges** gave people reasons to join and return. It grew past **4,000 members** around the announcement period. A closed-beta weekend supplied feedback, player footage and later fan contributions. [S3]

He reports Top Sellers placement within **30 minutes of the February 2018 launch**, plus early positive reviews. Those are publisher observations, not source-attributed sales accounting. Membership is not a count of purchasers, and peer support should not be confused with coordinated pressure on negative reviewers. [S3]

The dated **July 27, 2017** announcement already directed prospective beta players to Discord. It named **Power Up Audio** and funding support from the **Creative Industries Fund NL**. The announcement was promoting a project people would be able to test, not proving that all visitors already had immediate access. [S4]

## Later growth: paid purchases and Game Pass can coexist

In **June 2020**, Rose reported that a recent week of Xbox unit sales was **five times** the weekly level before Game Pass and that total sales had tripled over the longer comparison. The public report reproduces his posts; the subscription agreement's compensation is undisclosed. [S5]

This does not isolate Game Pass causation. Full release, multiplayer, updates and an expanding audience also occurred during the period. It is a counterexample to assuming that subscription access necessarily eliminates purchases, not proof that joining a subscription service always increases profit.

A **February 2021 publisher announcement** reports over **four million players** and the game's thirteenth major update. Players include access routes other than individual purchases; the figure must not be multiplied by retail price. The record establishes sustained work after launch, not just a brief advertising spike. [S6]

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** the server was a place to do something before release, not merely another feed demanding attention. That is a stronger mechanism than “create a Discord,” but its moderation and content work also consumed resources.

The title belongs in a sustained-growth group, not a failed-launch recovery group: its initial launch was already reported positively. Later distribution can enlarge a successful game's commercial life without rescuing a failure. Treating every rise as a comeback would misstate this chronology.
