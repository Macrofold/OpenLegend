# Crumbling World — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful commercial launch, 2020**

## Origin, team and outcome

Dani Marti describes three years of solo development alongside freelance work. His postmortem reports **141 first-month purchases and US$1,380.82 revenue**, against approximately **US$10,000 marketing spending**. These are creator-reported figures, not audited receipts. The platform revenue line items do not sum exactly to the headline total; retain the discrepancy rather than silently correcting it. [S1]

## Public development and product maturity

The retained itch.io development index reaches back to March 2019 and contains frequent illustrated progress reports. Several February and March 2020 entries appeared on consecutive days. That is observed activity during those windows, not a verified daily schedule throughout production. [S2]

A **November 25, 2019 free alpha** gave outsiders their first advertised chance to play. It offered early procedurally generated stages and the knight character. Importantly, the announcement said the titular world-crumbling mechanic would arrive in a later beta. Thus even a playable demo did not yet demonstrate the complete selling proposition. A new trailer accompanied the alpha and directed viewers to Steam wishlisting, Twitter and Instagram. [S3]

## Community, marketing and collaborators

Marti reports daily social posting for approximately 18 months, around **2,000 Twitter followers**, **1,900 Instagram followers**, and **1,900 launch wishlists**. A hired agency handled promotion; release-readiness problems and a Steam integration crash damaged the launch. The retrospective identifies a conflict between meeting the announced release and finishing/validating content. [S1]

The demonstrable developer-content route was **work-in-progress posts → free alpha and video → store wishlist link → paid release → patches**, not a large personal YouTube audience preceding the project. Exact subscriber growth and video frequency are not established. [S2], [S3]

Steam identifies Marti as developer and publisher. A self-published game can therefore still buy agency services. The agency's identity, contract, staff allocation and artist recruitment process were not established in the reviewed materials. [S1], [S4]

## Kickstarter, other channels and evidence gaps

No Kickstarter campaign is documented in this production account. Campaign dates, goals and pledges are not applicable to the financing route actually evidenced. The agency expense is not the complete development budget.

The reviewed sources do not establish Discord's opening date or growth, newsletter size, historical YouTube subscribers, individual press/creator conversions, or the exact launch date of each social account. The earliest retained development post is a lower bound on public activity, not proof that no earlier site existed. The current demo and store reviews must not be used to reconstruct launch conversion.

## Case-specific observations

**What is useful:** the dated alpha announcement makes product maturity observable, including a major mechanic still missing. That is more informative than simply saying the developer had a demo.

**What warrants caution:** a low sales total cannot distinguish weak demand from lost purchases caused by release defects without funnel and refund data. The account contains both marketing activity and quality problems; it does not justify concluding that either advertising alone or more coding alone would certainly have rescued the game.

The dossier classifies the measured launch, not an independently verified lifetime loss. Later patches show continued support, but no later profitability claim is established here.
