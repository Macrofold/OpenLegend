# Shadowhand: RPG Card Game — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: respectable launch sales but disappointing early return on labor; later wishlist growth did not imply strong conversion**

## Origin, team and announcement maturity

This is **the original Shadowhand**, not the separate later Shadowhand Solitaire. Grey Alien's **September 14, 2015** reveal says the project had been private for a few months. It names **Positech Games** as partner, invites people to play an early version at **EGX 2015**, provides promotional artwork and directs press to **Indigo Pearl**. There was therefore a visible, playable and professionally promoted proposition long before release. [S1]

The developer's January 2016 report confirms Positech's funding and marketing role. No Kickstarter campaign is established; publisher finance should not be counted as campaign proceeds. Its amount and contractual revenue split remain undisclosed. [S2]

## Launch and financial interpretation

Steam records **December 7, 2017**, Grey Alien as developer and Positech as publisher. [S3] In January 2018, Birkett said he and his wife had spent **two and a half years** on the project. First-week gross exceeded Regency Solitaire's lifetime **Steam-only** gross, yet higher production expenses and the publisher split left a weak return per hour. He did not provide an audited lifetime loss. [S4]

The numerical US$100,000 example elsewhere in that essay is hypothetical, **not Shadowhand's reported revenue**. The case should not acquire a fabricated sales total by confusing the example with the studio's account.

## Wishlist growth and the long tail

An **October 7, 2019** creator analysis reports roughly **4,000 prelaunch wishlists**, **13,000 during launch week** and **21,000 through the 2017 winter sale**. Later discount campaigns averaged approximately **1.22% seven-day wishlist conversion** in the author's dataset. He suspected a 2018 visibility change hurt results, but explicitly could not establish causation. [S5]

These are not all-player conversion rates or a universal Steam benchmark. New customers could buy without having wishlisted, and increasing discounts change money earned per copy. A growing remaining wishlist population is not a receivable waiting to be collected.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** this case resists both simplistic narratives. It was not commercially invisible, but an apparently good sales comparison did not settle whether the additional scope and expense were worthwhile. The denominator matters: comparison with another game's Steam receipts is not comparison with that game's total business.

Likewise, a publisher and public demo were present, so weak early labor returns cannot be reduced to “nobody marketed it.” Neither does the record prove that the publisher caused the shortfall. The useful question is which level of production expense the demonstrated demand could support.

The related [Spooky Bonus](spooky-bonus.md) and [Regency Solitaire](regency-solitaire.md) cases preserve a different outcome within the same studio. They are not independent evidence for a statistical rule that shorter games always win.
