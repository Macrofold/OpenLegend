# Unwritten: That Which Happened — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: successful crowdfunding, original delivery missed and production stalled by January 2014**

## Origin, team and inherited advantages

This is **Roxlou Games' Unwritten: That Which Happened**, not a different project called Unwritten. Joe Houston had worked at established studios, including Arkane on Dishonored, before pursuing a procedural game about guiding a nomadic tribe and accepting consequential decisions. An interview describes prior colleagues as sources of advice and feedback. Professional experience and an industry network therefore preceded the Kickstarter, even though a large owned fan community is not documented. [S1]

Contemporary coverage identifies a three-person creative core: Houston, artist **Lee Van Wallene** and musician **Julian Culme-Seymour**. The campaign was seeking production funding near the end of preproduction, with the creators' own money running low. That is materially different from crowdfunding a nearly finished game. [S2]

## Outcome and financing limits

Houston's January 2014 account described running out of funding and a health-related family crisis. He wanted to continue rather than declare the idea permanently abandoned, but no longer had the original financial and personal capacity. Contemporary responses included sympathetic backers as well as requests for clarity or refunds. [S7]

This dossier classifies the **missed original delivery and stalled funded production**, not the artistic idea, the sincerity of the creator, or a separately verified lifetime sales total. A current complete game, Steam launch, console launch, successful rescue publisher and complete refund accounting are not established. There is no basis for treating Kickstarter receipts as profit.

## Collaboration and case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** the project assembled complementary design, art and music skills and explained a coherent creative proposition. The creator's essay and interviews offer concrete artifacts of communicating that proposition before release.

**Negative interpretation:** funding that depends on a few people's uninterrupted availability is vulnerable to circumstances outside the product's appeal. This case does not justify attributing every delivery failure to weak marketing, laziness or overscoping.

The record identifies collaborators and prior-professional feedback, but not how the artist and musician were recruited, their compensation, ownership agreements or whether they were employees. A publisher-funded fallback and dedicated marketing team are not documented. These missing commercial details prevent a reliable reconstruction of how much additional funding would have made the production resilient.
