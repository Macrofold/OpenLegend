# Adopt Me! — community and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: two-founder Roblox game expanded into a major live operation; exact game profit undisclosed**

## Origin and the important team-size boundary

Uplift Games' **May 11, 2021** announcement dates Adopt Me's launch to **2017** and its origin to **two founders**. By the announcement, the company had **40 staff**, remotely distributed principally across North America and the UK. It described itself as self-funded. The giant later audience must not be used to imply that two people were still producing, marketing and supporting everything. [S1]

The official pet catalog identifies original pets as coming from the **2019 Pets Update**. That establishes a major product addition after the original launch; this source does not establish the precise day or isolate the growth caused by pets. [S2]

An online interview attributed to NewFissy and Bethink describes complementary programming and visual roles and an earlier interest in a pet-focused game. The accessible copy is dated December 2020 but discusses pets as future work, so it is an **undated/reproduced interview lead**, not a reliable 2020 milestone. No timeline-critical assertion is based on that discrepancy. [S5]

## Growth after launch

The studio's 2021 statement reports monthly users growing from **30 million in January 2020 to over 60 million in January 2021**, revenue increasing **over 400% during 2020**, and **1.92 million concurrent players** for the April 2021 Ocean Egg event. It also reports over 22 billion visits. These are company claims: visits are repeat sessions, monthly users are a population, concurrency is simultaneous activity and revenue growth is not net profit. [S1]

The pattern is therefore not simply “first launch went viral.” Meaningful product additions, recurring events and a growing organization intervened. Whether early launch monetization was strong, weak or merely adequate is not established by the later figures.

No Kickstarter, campaign maturity, target, proceeds or campaign duration is documented. The original Roblox launch is not a Steam debut; wishlists and Steam featuring are not the appropriate historical metrics.

## Owned media: useful dated evidence, not a fabricated growth curve

A retained **July 27, 2022** recruitment page for a **Senior Social Media Manager** lists an existing audience of approximately **4.8 million TikTok followers, 2.4 million YouTube subscribers, 1.9 million Instagram followers and 1.3 million Twitter followers**. It describes joining an existing communications function. These counts belong to the retained hiring-page context, which may have been edited; they do not establish the accounts' creation dates or the starting audience in 2017. The job is explicitly closed now. [S3]

This artifact answers a different question than a first-trailer case study: a mature live game was allocating dedicated labor to a multi-channel audience. It does not prove that those channels caused its original breakout or that a new solo developer should attempt that staffing level.

## Recruiting collaborators and sustaining production

A **May 26, 2022** 2D artist advertisement requests work across interface assets, illustrations, concepts and promotional presentation, with collaboration across disciplines. This is a concrete example of specifying the outputs a visual role must support. It does not disclose who was hired, recruitment conversion, pay or contract terms. [S4]

The 2021 studio announcement also named recruitment needs in production, quality assurance, localization and social media. [S1] Growth created work beyond coding new features; the historical two-founder credit alone would hide those later responsibilities.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** an established live audience can make a product update a distribution event. The Ocean Egg concurrency milestone provides an example, but does not measure how many participants were new versus returning or how many became payers.

The platform also supplied an already operating creation and distribution environment; it is not a controlled comparison with an independent game building its own multiplayer infrastructure. Separate the advantages supplied by Roblox, the game's own appeal and the studio's later professional operations.

For future synthesis, this case supports investigating **postlaunch changes in the reason people play**, rather than assigning a permanent success/failure label from the first release. It does not support recreating scarcity-driven purchases without considering the intended audience and player welfare.
