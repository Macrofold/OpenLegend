# Midora — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: successful crowdfunding, funding and delivery failure in the 2015–2016 evidence window**

## Identity, origin and team limits

Midora was Epic Minds' colorful 2D action-adventure, publicly represented by project lead Mhyre, also identified as William Ledent. Its campaign sold a recognizable game concept and visual direction rather than a developer channel alone. The retrieved records establish a creator-led studio, but **do not reliably establish a contemporaneous core headcount under ten**, its founding date or full contractor roster. Treat that qualification as unresolved, not as a confirmed solo precedent. [S1], [S4], [S5]

## Kickstarter and what was promised

| Field | Verified record |
|---|---|
| Campaign period | **June 14–July 14, 2014; 30 days.** [S1] |
| Funding | **US$73,470 against US$60,000; 3,359 backers.** [S1] |
| Development age | Exact start, labor months and completion percentage not established. |
| Public demo | A campaign trailer or screenshots are not proof of a downloadable demo; availability at opening is unresolved. |
| Beta promise | The June 15 FAQ described a standalone Arena Mode beta, planned six months before the full release. It was a future commitment, not an already-shipped build. [S2] |
| Additional commitments | FAQs discussed an editor, map sharing and multiple console/mobile versions. [S2] |

A July 2014 announcement said console ports would be pursued even if their stretch targets were missed; additional money would allow porting specialists to work in parallel. Contemporary reporting described the campaign as only around halfway to its base goal early that month. These are useful indications of **campaign-time expansion and promotion**, not evidence that enough staff or money had already been secured for every platform. [S3]

## Funding collapse, publishing and release outcome

By April 2015, the creator reported exhausted Kickstarter funds and difficulty continuing production. In October, he acknowledged that the original funding amount had not been sufficient to finish and discussed needing substantially more money while pursuing outside support. These are attributed admissions, not an independently reconstructed studio budget. [S4], [S5]

A January 2016 update discussed eventual refunds and the founder's need to regain financial stability; prospective publisher agreements had not solved the problem. **A stated intention to refund is not proof that refunds were completed.** [S6]

No completed Steam or console launch, audited revenue, customer downloads or successful publisher financing is established in this evidence window. The campaign page displays a later **September 30, 2022** update date, but its full contents were not retrieved. Therefore this dossier does not assert that 2016 was the final communication or present a verified account of every subsequent development. [S1]

## Collaboration and case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** the project communicated an understandable visual proposition and reached its fundraising target. The dated FAQs are especially useful for reconstructing which promises were public, rather than attributing every later idea to the original pitch.

**Negative interpretation:** fundraising success is not the same as funding sufficiency. The record raises a concrete production question: which promised platforms and tools were covered by committed labor and money, and which depended on later financing?

The stated plan to hire porting specialists is an example of **intended** outsourcing. It does not establish completed hiring, where contractors were found, contract terms, delivered ports or marketing staff. A paid publisher, porting contractor and promotional partner would have different responsibilities; none is silently substituted for another here.

This case is not used to claim that crowdfunding inevitably causes scope growth or that its supporters were misled intentionally. The unresolved evidence includes the original playable-build history, full team composition, later update contents and financial/refund accounting.
