# Choo-Choo Charles — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: breakout audience and successful Steam launch; profit undisclosed**

## Origin, existing audience and reveal sequence

Gavin Eisenbeisz (Two Star Games) was a solo developer with earlier releases, not a first-time creator. His YouTube channel had approximately **8,000 subscribers at the announcement trailer**, rather than the much larger audience visible after success. [S1]

| Milestone | Dated evidence |
|---|---|
| First tracked mention | September 17/18, 2021; article and embedded tweet differ by one calendar day. |
| Steam page | September 23, 2021; approximately **500 wishlists in its first week**. |
| Trailer | October 1, 2021; approximately **14,000 wishlists that day**. |
| TikTok | Early October; a video reached approximately **1.5 million views**. |
| October 17 report | **91,738 wishlists; 5,799 Steam followers**. |

These figures come from the developer-supplied account in [S1]. They are snapshots, not a controlled attribution study.

Distribution combined YouTube, Twitter, Reddit and TikTok; press followed rather than initiating the spike. Eisenbeisz credited an early Airdorf retweet: the relationship involved his previous game and a podcast interview. He hired a horror-focused capsule artist through **Fiverr**, while using marketplace environmental assets alongside his own work. [S1]

## What was actually ready?

The announcement presented recognizable footage of the game, but the reviewed evidence does not establish a downloadable public demo at that point, the first internal prototype date, or exact production months before revealing it. A trailer demonstrating the premise is not equivalent to unrestricted player testing.

No Kickstarter campaign, goal, pledge total or crowdfunding duration is established. The documented funnel is a forthcoming Steam product, not a recovered crowdfunding campaign. Whether other financing existed is a separate unanswered question.

## Release, presentation and commercial evidence

Steam identifies **December 9, 2022** as the PC release and **Two Star Games** as both developer and publisher. Its central proposition is an open-world island explored by train while preparing to fight an evil spider-train. The store presentation therefore connects an immediately visible antagonist with an understandable activity, rather than relying only on the developer's biography. [S2]

NVIDIA's December 2022 release coverage promoted the game and its DLSS support. This is a concrete example of additional launch exposure through a platform-technology company; the article does not disclose whether money changed hands or establish a publishing agreement. [S3]

The game appeared in Steam's **December 2022 Top Releases** selection, as preserved in contemporary coverage. [S4] This supports a strong launch classification, but is **not an audited unit count, revenue statement or profit calculation**. Wishlist totals from October 2021 are not launch-day totals, and multiplying them by an assumed conversion rate would invent a sales result.

Official PlayStation listings also establish PS4 and PS5 distribution under Two Star Games LLC. Their present ratings and prices are not reconstructed console-launch metrics. The precise port production arrangements and historical sales split were not recovered. [S5]

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** this is a useful record of audience growth during a reveal, rather than merely a large follower count observed afterward. Its dated numbers allow the starting audience and subsequent visibility to remain separate.

**Negative interpretation:** neither a striking premise nor one highly successful announcement establishes that repeated posting alone will reproduce this result. The missing conversion, budget and launch-era list data limit how confidently the marketing effort can be translated into financial expectations.

**Collaboration interpretation:** the identified art commission is a narrow, paid specialist contribution, not evidence that the entire game required a large permanent team. No general hiring recommendation is inferred from one successful commission.
