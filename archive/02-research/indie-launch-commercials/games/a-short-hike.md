# A Short Hike — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: commissioned release, strong recognition and subsequent distribution; commercial magnitude and profit not established here**

## Origin and production scope

The official press kit dates the project to **December 2018**, when **Adam Robinson-Yu** took a break from a larger RPG. Credits include **Mark Sparling** for music, **Dawn Blair** for art/writing, **Andrew Wong** for additional art and **David Czarnowski** for the logo. Solo-led does not mean every contribution came from one person. [S1]

The deliberately compact game is an exploration trip centered on climbing a mountain. Its small final scope should not be confused with starting from no game-making experience or contacts.

## Public presence, commissioning and release sequence

In an original January 2020 interview, Robinson-Yu describes earlier game-making, a university game-development community, jams, software work and a subsequent move into full-time independent development. He says publishers noticed work shared on **Twitter**. The Humble deadline helped limit the project; additional ideas could wait for the later edition. Local events and jam communities also supplied relationships. [S2]

| Stage | Verified record |

| First commercial distribution | **April 5, 2019**, as a **Humble Original** included with the subscription. [S1] |

There is no recovered Kickstarter campaign, target, pledge amount or fundraising duration. The Humble arrangement was a different kind of commercial relationship. Its fee, rights, exclusivity and exact risk allocation are not disclosed in the sources reviewed.

## Community building and what the short-development headline omits

The interview supports visual work attracting professional attention before the game's broader release. It does **not** establish that a brand-new account attracted a publisher in a few weeks, or that a large YouTube channel was required. Earlier shared work and professional experience are part of the starting conditions. [S2]

The press kit supplies trailers, images and useful publication assets. This establishes presentation support, not measured traffic or the first day those assets were available. [S1] No complete prelaunch demo-player count, mailing-list size or wishlist history was recovered.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** limiting a project can make a concrete delivery commitment possible, while a later edition provides room for selected additions. That is different from using Early Access as an indefinite promise to finish a much larger design.

The useful sequence is **prior experience and public work → a bounded project and commissioning opportunity → delivered edition → expanded storefront release**. A developer can learn from it without treating acclaim as proof of a particular income level.

This dossier is retained as a financing, scope and distribution comparison, not added to a count of independently verified profitable games. The actual effect of a different deadline, marketing budget or publisher agreement cannot be recovered from the observed outcome alone.
