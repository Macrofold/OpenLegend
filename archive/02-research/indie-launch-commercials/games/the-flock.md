# The Flock — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful 2015 multiplayer commercial launch and subsequent studio funding exhaustion**

## Origin, team and proposition

Vogelsap developed The Flock from a student project over approximately three years. Creative director Jeroen van Hasselt's retrospective describes team turnover and difficulty moving from prototypes to sustained production. Exact headcount at each milestone and the complete contractor roster are not established; this is a small student-studio case, not a verified all-solo production. [S1]

The commercial hook was an asymmetrical horror game for three to five players, with a finite shared population of deaths and an intended eventual ending. Prelaunch reporting made that unusual business/design premise a major part of the story. [S2], [S3]

## Public exposure and release sequence

| Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 2013 | Official store lists a Dutch Game Awards student-game win. Public recognition therefore preceded the commercial release by years. [S2] |
| 2014–2015 | Indie showcase recognition and Indie MEGABOOTH selections are recorded on Steam. [S2] |
| Prelaunch | Beta/event enthusiasm and extensive coverage. A report of van Hasselt's conference talk gives **500-plus websites** and a tenfold increase in trailer views, not absolute unique viewers. [S1] |
| Funding | Official listing identifies an **Indie Fund** game; no Kickstarter campaign is established. [S2] |
| Release | **August 21, 2015**, Steam; Vogelsap listed as developer and publisher. [S2] |
| October 2015 retrospective | Approximately **3,000 copies sold**, according to reporting of the creator's talk; not a lifetime estimate. [S1] |

A playable public-facing game clearly existed before the commercial release. The precise first demo date, public versus invitation-only beta access and production age at the first forum announcement remain unresolved.

## Launch reception and what failed

The conference account describes severe launch bugs, immediate negative reviews, a price/content mismatch and testing that had not adequately measured when players stopped enjoying repeated sessions. Those are the creator's retrospective explanations, not controlled proof of each factor's sales impact. [S1]

More decisively, the **developer-authored Steam description** says the game did not perform well enough, Vogelsap ran out of money and the server tracking the finite population also stopped. This establishes a commercial/service failure independently of estimating sales from review counts. It does not mean every peer-to-peer play possibility disappeared or that every sale was refunded. [S2]

The 2015 talk discussed prospective patches, price changes and free access as recovery attempts. The talk alone does not verify that each planned intervention happened or succeeded. A comprehensive lifetime revenue statement and later recovery are not established. [S1]

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** The Flock shows how a small team can gain significant attention with an unusual, explainable premise and public playable appearances. The attention was real; describing the project as merely undiscovered would erase the central evidence.

**Negative interpretation:** enjoying a short session at an event is different from repeatedly choosing the game at home with enough other people available. The launch also had to work for the people broadcasting it. A marketing event and a technically successful player/creator session are different dependencies.

The finite-population concept attracted attention, but these sources cannot isolate how many people refused to buy because of it. Nor does this case prove that all finite or multiplayer games are commercially unsuitable. Its useful unresolved questions concern repeat-play testing, the cost of each promotional route and the operational capacity of the release team.
