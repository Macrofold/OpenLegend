# RimWorld — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: commercial success**

## Outcome, origin and team boundary

Tynan Sylvester reported **more than one million copies sold on January 31, 2018**, before the 1.0 release. This is a dated developer sales statement, not a current lifetime estimate or profit figure. [S5]

The early archive documents a founder-led project recruiting specialist help, not the mature studio's eventual staffing. On June 15, 2013, the studio announced its existence and the project then called **Eclipse Colony**. Subsequent June posts discuss functioning trading interfaces, raids, character histories and an in-game encyclopedia. The public conversation therefore began while a playable simulation existed, before final branding and art polish. Exact development start and hours invested before that announcement are not established by these records. [S1]

## Development and public launch sequence

| Date | Verified event and product maturity |
|---|---|
| June 15–27, 2013 | Studio introduction followed by frequent illustrated systems/progress posts; unfinished but working game. [S1] |
| September 11, 2013 | Eclipse Colony renamed RimWorld. [S2] |
| September 16–17, 2013 | First-look video and landing page published; the next day's post describes them. [S2] |
| September 18, 2013 | Public call for a 2D artist. [S2] |
| September 19, 2013 | Founder acknowledges a player-created subreddit. [S2] |
| September 21, 2013 | Kickstarter trailer recorded and sent to Alistair Lindsay for audio work. [S2] |
| October 1, 2013 | Official forum reaches 1,000 posts, involving testers, interested YouTubers and prospective players. [S3] |
| October 2, 2013 | Kickstarter and Steam Greenlight announced together; direct PayPal support and IndieDB trailer also linked. [S3] |
| January 31, 2018 | Million-copy milestone. [S5] |
| October 17, 2018 | Version 1.0 release date recorded by Steam. [S6] |

This sequence is unusually valuable because it distinguishes a functioning prototype, public demonstration, contractor recruitment and crowdfunding. It does not show the developer waiting for a finished game before talking publicly.

## Channel-by-channel community record

**Blog and screenshots.** June's archive contains six dated posts between the 15th and 27th. That is an observed short-period cadence, not a promise of daily posting throughout development. The material exposes unfinished systems and player stories, rather than requiring cinematic assets first. [S1]

**Forums.** The 1,000-post milestone is evidence of conversation, not a membership count. The named participant types matter: players, testers and video creators were already interacting before fundraising. Neither unique posters nor conversion to backers is disclosed. [S3]

**Reddit and local networks.** A player, not necessarily the founder, created the subreddit. A September 23 post records an interview arising from an Ottawa game-development community meeting. These are distinct acquisition/contact routes; no referral shares are available. [S2]

**YouTube.** The first-look video and better-produced campaign trailer are documented. A large founder-owned channel, subscriber history, weekly upload routine and individual creator conversion rates are not established. Interested YouTubers on a forum are not the same as the developer owning their audiences. [S2], [S3]

**Twitter/Facebook, newsletter and Discord.** Historical follower counts, account-opening dates, mailing-list size and dated Discord growth were not recovered. Modern links in a blog sidebar do not prove that every linked channel existed when the original post appeared.

## Collaboration, marketing and distribution

The September artist call is direct evidence of publicly recruiting help while the game remained unfinished. The trailer's audio was entrusted to a named specialist before Kickstarter. Recruitment sources beyond that call, applicant evaluation, fees and contract terms remain unknown. The founder also had a public design blog and book; the archive links them, but does not quantify an inherited audience. [S2], [S3]

Steam lists Ludeon as developer and publisher. This supports self-publishing, not a claim that the founder personally executed every asset, promotion or support task. The original Steam Greenlight effort must not be confused with the much later 1.0 release or treated as a description of today's Steam submission process. [S3], [S6]

## Case-specific observations and uncertainties

**Positive observation:** there are primary, dated examples of audience contact before finished artwork: systems posts, a video, a subreddit and a functioning tester forum. **Caution:** none provides a reliable formula converting a given number of posts, subscribers or demo players into pledges.

The long interval between campaign and 1.0 is not by itself evidence of non-delivery: the commercial milestone predates 1.0. Conversely, a million sales do not reveal profit, acquisition cost or support burden. Historical alpha-release details, precise launch headcount, launch wishlists, paid advertising, PR staffing, campaign referral analytics and contractor economics remain unresolved in this dossier.
