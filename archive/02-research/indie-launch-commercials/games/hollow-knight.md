# Hollow Knight — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: commercial success**

## Outcome and team-size boundary

Hollow Knight raised **AU$57,138 from 2,158 backers**, then became a much larger paid game business: Team Cherry reported **over 2.8 million people had bought and played it** in its February 2019 Silksong announcement. The campaign total is not lifetime revenue, and the sales milestone is not a profit disclosure. [S1], [S5]

The core began with **Ari Gibson and William Pellen**; programmer **Dave Kazi** joined after crowdfunding. Composer/sound designer **Christopher Larkin** and PR/marketing specialist **Matthew Griffin** supplied additional expertise. This is a small-core-team case with outside help, not an example of two people handling every commercial function indefinitely. [S2], [S3], [S4]

## Origin and development/publicity sequence

The founders describe the project as growing from a small game-jam idea. A Stencyl prototype existed before the post-Kickstarter move to Unity. The retrieved record supports that sequence, but does not establish an exact first day of development or quantify pre-campaign labor. [S2], [S5]

| Period | Product / community stage | What can be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| Before November 2014 | Game-jam roots and a working prototype predate the later engine migration | More than concept art existed; the exact availability and content of a public campaign demo need separate evidence. [S2], [S5] |
| November 18–December 18, 2014 | Crowdfunding with a distinct illustrated world and video presentation | An identifiable game and art direction were being sold, not only the founders' personal brand. [S1] |
| Early 2015 | Third programmer, office and Unity migration announced | Expanded funded scope created a staffing need. A lore document let higher-tier backers contribute character/boss ideas. [S2] |
| March 2015 | Composer/sound designer announced while approaching beta | Specialist audio was brought in after the campaign, not postponed until after release. [S3] |
| July 2016 | Matthew Griffin announced for PR and marketing | Dedicated commercial help arrived before launch and was intended to continue afterward. [S4] |
| February 24, 2017 | PC release | Steam lists Team Cherry as both developer and publisher. [S6] |
| February 2019 | Public sales milestone and sequel announcement | Existing customers and Kickstarter backers became an audience for the next product. [S5] |

Date caution: migrated Team Cherry posts sometimes expose only a month/day header. The early-2015 staffing post explicitly identifies 2015 in its text; this dossier does not force a conflicting retrospective day-of-month onto it.

## Artists, contractors, marketing and publishing

The audio recruitment account is unusually useful. Team Cherry received many composer submissions during the campaign, evaluated samples, and chose Larkin, whose film/TV/game work and Adelaide location supported close collaboration. They publicly shared early musical sketches. The sources do not provide his fee, ownership terms, or a general-purpose hiring marketplace. [S3]

Griffin was introduced with relevant experience on Stardew Valley, Interstellaria and Wanderlust Adventures. His remit was PR and marketing before and after release. The same announcement explained that the initial release estimate had slipped as the game expanded, and declined to promise a new date prematurely. Exact compensation, employment status and campaign deliverables are undisclosed. [S4]

Steam's self-published attribution therefore does **not** mean “no marketing professional.” Similarly, employing a composer does not make him a member of the permanent core. [S3], [S4], [S6]

## Launch, reception and continuation

The verified original PC date is February 24, 2017. By the 2019 announcement, the studio described a multimillion-buyer game and promised qualifying original Kickstarter backers a free copy of Silksong, including a PC/Switch choice. This is a concrete example of preserving a crowdfunding relationship through a larger follow-up, rather than regarding backers as a one-time transaction. [S5], [S6]

The sales milestone establishes substantial reach. It does not resolve full costs, per-platform contribution, acquisition spending or the share of customers attributable to particular influencers.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive evidence:** the records show different specialists joining at different stages: programming after expanded crowdfunding scope, audio during development, and marketing before the paid release. Community involvement included bounded creative contributions, not only follower accumulation.

**Risk evidence:** the studio itself linked scope growth to schedule extension. A funded campaign created production obligations, and public communication had to manage uncertainty rather than repeatedly invent a launch date. [S2], [S4]

**Interpretation:** this case is more informative as a sequence of prototype, campaign, specialist recruitment and release than as proof that a solo creator should first become a YouTuber. The reviewed evidence does not establish that creator-first route.

## Remaining evidence gaps

Exact development start, first blog/social post, public-demo launch date and content, pre-Kickstarter audience size, campaign referral breakdown, historical Discord growth, video cadence, launch wishlists, marketing spending and contractor terms remain unverified. No claim of complete social-history reconstruction is made.
