# Shovel Knight — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: commercial success**

## Origin, team and outcome

Yacht Club's founders had worked together at WayForward before establishing their own studio. The project therefore began with an experienced working relationship, not a group recruited entirely through a fresh crowdfunding campaign. The interview-based history advertised by Boss Fight Books documents that background; the full book was not read for this dossier. [S4]

The studio's August 2014 disclosure identifies a **six-person core** and reports approximately **75,000 first-week copies excluding Kickstarter presales**. It says the first month exceeded the team's 150,000-copy lifetime aspiration. This supports commercial success without treating the campaign's pledges as the full development budget. [S2]

## Kickstarter and prelaunch finance

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Platform / creator | Kickstarter / Yacht Club Games |
| Goal / pledged | US$75,000 / US$311,502 |
| Backers | 14,749 |
| Campaign duration | 30 days |
| Dates | March 14/15–April 13/14, 2013; campaign renderings differ by timezone; the creator's retrospective explicitly gives April 13 as the close |
| Parallel fundraising | US$17,180 from 935 PayPal supporters, reported separately |
| Public presentation | Video and identifiable 8-bit action-game pitch; exact first public demo date and pre-campaign labor hours not recovered |

Campaign metadata is first-party. PayPal amounts should not be quietly folded into the Kickstarter-only total. [S1], [S2]

The founders' financial account says deferred composer payment, reduced compensation and postrelease delivery of stretch goals were used to bridge the budget gap. They ultimately spent five months without team payments. These are unusually important qualifications to “Kickstarter funded the game.” [S2]

## Publicity and release sequence

| Period | Verified milestone |
|---|---|
| March–April 2013 | Crowdfunding campaign; an already-presentable game concept, not a proven finished product. [S1] |
| Following development year | Convention and media promotion while production continued. [S2] |
| June 26, 2014 | Initial release on Windows, Wii U and 3DS; PC and Nintendo-region availability differed. [S2], [S3] |
| August 5, 2014 | Detailed public budget/sales retrospective. [S2] |
| Later product | Treasure Trove packages the expanded collection; its current feature set must not be retroactively assigned to the original release. [S3] |

The developer says Nintendo supported store placement and marketing. It also acknowledges launching during a Steam Summer Sale despite Valve's advice. Success in this exceptional case is not evidence that competing with major discount events is generally harmless. [S2]

## Community channels and their limits

| Channel | What is established | Missing historical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Kickstarter updates | Campaign has a retained update archive and identifiable backer base. [S1] | Referral shares, subscriber-to-backer conversion and original update cadence. |
| Blog | The developer published unusually transparent sales and production accounts. [S2] | First company blog date and total prelaunch audience. |
| Conventions / press | Named in the founders' explanation of promotion before release. [S2] | Event-by-event spend, leads, follow-up conversions and incremental sales. |
| YouTube / livestreams | The official product press kit supplies trailers and media material. [S3] | Founder channel start, posting frequency and launch subscriber trajectory. |
| Twitter/Facebook / fan communities | Campaign and publisher material establish outward-facing communication, but no reliable historical series was recovered. | Exact account dates, follower increments, paid-versus-organic acquisition. |
| Discord / email | No dated launch-period growth record established. | Opening date, membership, newsletter list and retention. |

A supporter count is not an account-following count. A later collection's popularity likewise does not establish what the founders could reach on day one of Kickstarter.

## Specialists, publishing and collaboration

The team retained business, marketing, publishing and merchandise responsibilities rather than delegating all of them to a financing publisher. Composer Jake Kaufman's deferred payment is documented; a zero upfront payment should not be misdescribed as free work without a future obligation. The exact contract and recruitment channel are not available. [S2]

The official press material credits Yacht Club and supplies assets for press coverage. This gives a useful observable distinction between preparing reusable marketing materials and having a separately identified marketing hire. No launch marketing-agency fee or paid-ad budget is established here. [S3]

## Bounded observations

**Positive interpretation:** a known production partnership, a recognizable visual promise, backer commitment and platform support all appear in the record. The causal contribution of each cannot be separated from the disclosed aggregate sales.

**Caution:** the attractive revenue outcome hides a financing shortfall, unpaid time and continuing stretch-goal obligations. Pledges measure an initial commitment to the pitch, not the total cost of delivering the game and rewards.

The dossier does not turn the founders' historical preorder forecasting heuristic into a reliable modern conversion rule. Nor does it use their cost-estimation example as an audited actual budget. Exact campaign-demo availability, prelaunch follower growth, contractor rates, marketing labor allocation and final net profitability remain open.
