# I Am Overburdened — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: mixed — successful completion and favorable reception, commercial shortfall through January 2018**

## Origin, development and outcome

Solo-led developer **Spidi / Magic Item Tech**, already experienced with a Steam release, intended a four-month project. Actual production took **288 calendar days**, including **190 logged full-time days**. Testing after three months exposed weak gameplay and prompted further iteration. [S1]

Steam confirms **November 2, 2017**, with **Magic Item Tech** as developer and publisher. The proposition is a compact, turn-based roguelike built around a large inventory and artifacts, not a multiplayer service. [S2]

In January 2018 the creator reported **over 650 copies sold**, including 200 in week one and 400 by November's end. Development costs were not recovered. He considered completing the game successful but its marketing and sales disappointing. [S1] This is a dated early shortfall, not an established lifetime loss. It is included as an additional mixed case, not used to inflate both outcome cohorts.

## Publicity before the finished game

The developer-owned IndieDB archive has project posts dated **February 21, March 3, March 22, March 26 and April 3, 2017**. Topics include systems, sound, box art, tiles and sprites. The archive establishes repeated public development writing months before release, but not the first-ever post or uninterrupted weekly publication. [S3]

The **April 3** graphics post explicitly describes unfinished visual production. This is useful maturity evidence: showing the game publicly did not wait until every asset was final. Its linked or embedded presentation should not be confused with unrestricted access to a playable build. [S4]

A **September 17** post says development had been relatively quiet publicly while polishing and balancing continued. It contains a video and discusses remaining work. That directly qualifies any claim of constant posting throughout development. [S5]

## Marketing effort and response

The creator cross-posted early blogs/videos to roughly a dozen communities and posted Twitter GIFs/screenshots several times weekly. Daily item-image promotion produced only a few hundred wishlists. **900 creator keys yielded 130 activations and approximately 40 covering channels**. Reddit reportedly drove ten times the store traffic of any other social site. Paid Keymailer extras disappointed him; small Facebook ad tests had uncertain attribution. [S1]

These are distinct measures: an activated key is not a published video, and an incoming visitor is not a paid customer. Historical follower totals and overlapping audiences were not disclosed, so the record cannot supply a reliable follower-to-sale conversion rate.

## Release and reception

On **November 4, 2017**, the creator published a delayed release announcement, acknowledging that the push to finish and ship had caused him to miss a timely blog announcement. The post explicitly dates the actual release to the evening of November 2. [S6]

The surviving Steam listing shows favorable player reception and promotes short, replayable runs. Its current review totals are not backdated to release, and positive reviews alone are not used to declare profitability. [S2] The developer's own sales account above supplies the commercial classification instead.

No Kickstarter campaign, pledge total or campaign duration is established. No financing publisher, dedicated marketing employee or PR-agency contract is documented in the reviewed material. That is an evidence limit, not proof that no outside help ever occurred.

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** this is not a case of silence until launch. The record supports examining whether the audience reached by production diaries was the audience likely to buy this particular game, without presuming that the diaries themselves caused the shortfall.

The rushed communication at release is a separate operational issue from the quality of the underlying game. A useful future comparison would keep those questions separate rather than turning the creator's disappointment into a claim that every promotional channel failed completely.
