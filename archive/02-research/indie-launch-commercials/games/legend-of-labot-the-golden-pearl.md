# Legend of Labot: The Golden Pearl — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful initial itch.io launch; later Steam release, lifetime outcome unknown**

## Origin and commercial evidence

Developer `naknamu` reported seven months of development and zero downloads at the time of a 2021 Reddit post. In replies, the developer said only friends had known the game existed before the paid itch.io debut. Trailer posts on Reddit/Facebook and outreach to YouTubers happened around the release, rather than after a long public development campaign. [S1]

The source is a self-identified creator account, not an independent financial audit. This is a solo-led case; the record does not establish that every visual/audio asset was made by that person or enumerate all collaborators.

## Sequence and product maturity

| Stage | What can be established |
|---|---|
| Seven-month development period | Creator's retrospective duration; full-time hours and exact beginning date unknown. [S1] |
| Initial itch.io release | A purchasable Windows game, not a concept pitch; weak initial acquisition reported. [S1], [S2] |
| Reddit discussion | Creator planned additional polish and a Steam page. A reader offered the entry fee and the creator acknowledged the offer. The transaction itself was not audited. [S1] |
| September 14, 2021 | Retained itch.io update 1.1. [S2] |
| November 17, 2021 | Steam release under DreamJoy Studios, listed as both developer and publisher. [S3] |

The official itch.io comments include people saying they arrived from Reddit and bought or planned to install the game, with developer replies. **Zero was an initial snapshot, not a lifetime total.** The later Steam release is independently visible; a plan in an old forum post is not being mistaken for a completed launch. [S2], [S3]

Public support comments are not representative playtesting or proof that the game became profitable. Conversely, they prevent the initial negative snapshot from being presented as a permanent absence of customers.

## Funding and collaboration

The creator explicitly said they did not know how Kickstarter worked when a commenter suggested it. No campaign is evidenced; funding dates, pledges and backers are therefore not applicable to the documented route. The same discussion describes difficulty affording Steam's entry fee. [S1]

No publisher-financing deal, marketing employee, paid agency, artist-selection process, contract rate or asset-license record was recovered. DreamJoy's self-publishing attribution does not establish its exact employee count. The itch.io page's request for feedback and developer responses show direct support, not an outsourced community operation. [S2], [S3]

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** this is a useful example of public exposure beginning around a paid release, rather than of three years of community cultivation failing. It also illustrates why a distribution milestone and a commercial outcome should be recorded separately: a later Steam listing proves another release opportunity, not a financial rescue.

The evidence cannot separate the effects of store choice, presentation, price and audience targeting. Claims in commenters' advice about ideal budgets, posting schedules or guaranteed Steam wishlists are not adopted as facts. No conclusion about the creator's ability or the game's artistic worth follows from the early download count.
