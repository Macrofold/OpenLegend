# Brigador — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: weak first commercial period, later sufficient income to fund another game; cash reserve and audited profit unknown**

## Origin and what existed before launch

Stellar Jockeys' first game took approximately five years. In an original creator interview, Hugh Monahan describes a team learning to work together while building demanding production processes. Early Access feedback encouraged a directed campaign and more world fiction, but he believed publicity was spent before the game was ready. [S1]

The current official store describes a destructive isometric vehicle-combat game, credits Stellar Jockeys and Gausswerks, and dates full release to **June 2, 2016**. A present credits label does not establish employment arrangements throughout production. [S2]

No Kickstarter campaign, target or funding period is established. Exact launch-era headcount, contractor budget and first unrestricted prototype date remain unresolved in the primary material reviewed here; the project is treated as small-studio, not assumed entirely solo.

## The commercial reversal, with dates

| Period | Record |
|---|---|
| 2016 | Monahan said publicity about poor discovery improved sales, but the project still had far to go. He had actively circulated the post and contacted press; it was not entirely passive virality. [S1] |
| June 2, 2017 | **Up-Armored Edition** relaunched the game with onboarding, difficulty and content changes. This was an improved release, not simply waiting for the original build to sell. [S3] |
| March 11, 2019 | Localization collaborator Benjamin Glover reported that translations had covered their costs and broadened sales geographically; China ranked fifth for units in the period since June 2018. [S4] |
| May 4, 2019 | A Steam account marked **developer** said revenue enabled a second game, but incoming money was being reinvested and no reserve was being built. [S5] |
| 2021 | GOG's anniversary announcement records another substantial free update and a timed discount. It establishes continued product work, not its incremental profit. [S6] |

The first observation and the later funding capacity are both retained. “Failed forever” would be wrong, while “eventually enough to make another game” is narrower than rich, risk-free or fully audited profitable.

## Community, contractors and promotion

Monahan's interview identifies **Makeup & Vanity Set** for music and **Brad Buckmaster** for the companion fiction. He describes setting a creative direction and giving collaborators latitude. The source does not disclose recruitment channels, contracts or compensation. [S1]

The May 2019 Steam exchange points people to a **newsletter**, while a moderator identifies Twitter and Discord as places for occasional images/GIFs. It proves those communication routes existed by then; it does not establish their first dates, size or a steady prelaunch video schedule. [S5]

The localization essay describes production tasks beyond translating text, including font/interface testing and selecting appropriate collaborators. Its assessment that localization paid back is a participant's report rather than an independent comparison holding discounts, product improvements and press exposure constant. [S4]

## Missing measurements

No complete historic follower series, launch mailing-list size, Discord active-user curve, source-attributed paid conversions, advertising budget or later net-profit ledger was recovered. The current store's favorable reviews cannot supply those numbers. The 2017 relaunch date is corroborated by contemporary release coverage; the original linked article was inaccessible, so the date is not presented as newly inspected developer patch notes. [S3]

## Case-specific observations

**Interpretation:** a recovery case should identify what changed, not merely show two different sales snapshots. Here, renewed public explanation, a revised introduction, additional content, localization and continuing updates intervened. Their separate contributions remain unmeasured.

The decision to continue still involved risk: funding a next project without reserves is not the same situation as having ample retained profit. This is a counterexample to treating launch week as final destiny, not evidence that every struggling game should receive unlimited additional work.
