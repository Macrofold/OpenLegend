# Factorio — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: commercial success; small-origin team that later grew beyond ten**

## Origin and commercial outcome

The official December 2022 recap reports **3.5 million sales**, roughly **500,000 annual sales** at that point, and over **40,000 Switch copies**. These are dated developer disclosures; do not add the Switch figure to the headline total without knowing its scope. [S5]

The first blog entry, October 24, 2012, describes three friends—Michal, Petr and Tomas—working intermittently for almost half a year. Michal had left his job; artist Albert was improving the visuals. A basic, buggy game was playable, and a free tutorial demo was being prepared to test whether continued development made sense. Expenses, especially graphics, were self-financed. This contemporaneous description is more precise than reducing every stage to “two founders.” [S1]

## Development, funding and release timeline

| Stage | Record |
|---|---|
| May 2012 | Development start in the official retrospective press kit. [S4] |
| October 24, 2012 | First developer blog: playable basics, unfinished presentation, public demo still forthcoming. [S1] |
| Early 2013 | Successful **Indiegogo**, not Kickstarter. [S4], [S6] |
| September 27, 2013 | First Friday Facts: a commitment to weekly updates after forum complaints about communication. Version 0.7.0 already existed. [S2] |
| May 2014 | Official history identifies a trailer-driven growth event. [S4] |
| February 25–26, 2016 | Steam/Humble debut and immediate developer debrief, after direct sales had already been operating. [S3] |
| August 2020 | Version 1.0. [S4] |
| October–December 2022 | Switch release and subsequent commercial recap. [S4], [S5] |

The Steam launch was not the first opportunity to play or buy the game. Similarly, the 2020 release date should not be used as the start of its community.

## Crowdfunding record and access limitation

The campaign page now redirects to an Indiegogo page that did not expose usable original metadata during this review. The official press kit confirms successful crowdfunding in February 2013. A secondary development chronology reports **January 31–March 3, 2013**, **€21,626 raised against €17,000**, approximately 31 elapsed days. These exact dates and amounts are **secondary-reported, not independently re-read from the campaign**; backer count is left unverified. [S4], [S6]

The first blog establishes preparation of a playable demo before crowdfunding, but does not by itself establish the precise date the downloadable demo became public. Campaign advertising spend, individual referrers and pledge conversion are not disclosed in the reviewed records.

## Community and content channels

| Channel | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|
| Developer blog | First entry explicitly introduces the series in October 2012. Weekly Friday Facts began September 2013 in response to community demand. This is not proof every later week had an update. [S1], [S2] |
| Forum | An active feedback venue existed by the first Friday Facts; discussion shaped communication and release planning. No historical membership curve recovered. [S2] |
| Developer video | The 2016 post distinguishes a spectacle-oriented trailer from a gameplay explanation video showing progression and UI; production involved substantial staff time. [S3] |
| Reddit | The developer reported the new gameplay trailer near the top of r/Games for nearly a day around the Steam debut. That is exposure, not a measured number of acquired buyers. [S3] |
| Player videos / streaming | The official press kit offers review keys to eligible creators and reusable assets. Its present criteria are not asserted to be the 2013 criteria. [S4] |
| Social accounts / newsletter / Discord | Exact opening dates, historical subscribers, Discord cohorts, acquisition sources and upload frequency were not established. |

At the February 2016 debrief the developers reported 630 Steam reviews with an approximately 99% positive rating. Existing fans were helping newcomers and circulating the trailer. This is a contemporaneous reception snapshot, not a substitute for unit sales. [S3]

## Team, specialists and commercial work

The first public blog already names external/specialist graphics expenditure. Friday Facts #1 discusses Albert's art work alongside plans to improve presentation for Steam Greenlight. The record therefore supports spending on visual communication while the game was still in active development, not waiting until release to consider art. Recruitment route and fees are not established. [S1], [S2]

For the Steam launch, Wube purchased an existing strategy guide from creator **Xterminator** and made an edited online version free. This is a specific collaboration with someone already serving the player community. The developers also described a support function and heavy launch-preparation work; no separate launch PR agency or contract terms were identified. [S3]

The official press kit describes a later team of around 30 professionals/contributors. Treat the early bootstrapping sequence as the small-team precedent, not the entire mature production and expansion business. [S4]

## Case-specific interpretation and gaps

The strongest sequence here is **working prototype → public demo preparation and blog → crowdfunding/direct sales → sustained forum-supported communication → larger Steam debut**. The evidence does not require a founder to first acquire a celebrity YouTube audience.

A caution is the labor hidden by “post regularly”: launch videos, guides, art, support and release preparation consumed production capacity. The 2016 account explicitly notes development disruption near launch. [S3]

No causal experiment establishes how much success came from Friday Facts versus the game, creators, direct customers or Steam. Historical subscriber curves, public-demo conversion, original crowdfunding attribution, team size at every release, marketing costs and net profit remain unknown. The campaign access limit is substantive and is not disguised with a precise invented backer total.
