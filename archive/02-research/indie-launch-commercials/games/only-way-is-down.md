# Only Way is Down — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-16** · **Outcome: meaningful first-game revenue, weaker reception and insufficient full-time livelihood in the reported period**

## Origin, product and team

This is a solo-led cat platformer released on Steam on **April 1, 2025**, developed/published as Jilted Generation Productions. The store identifies an intentionally punishing descent from a skyscraper; a cute protagonist does not make it a relaxed pet game. [S3]

The developer reports roughly one year's work spread across eighteen months. No complete production budget or contractor roster is public in the reviewed material. No Kickstarter campaign is established. [S1]

## Verified community and development sequence

A first-person **October 1, 2024** Reddit account is valuable because it predates the eventual launch outcome. It describes a playable demo retained after June Next Fest, asynchronous creator coverage, and **7,800 wishlists** by the account. The author reports approximately **500,000 views** from several large YouTubers producing only a few hundred wishlists, and a separate **3.7-million-view Instagram post** producing around **600**. These are creator estimates, not linked-user attribution. [S2]

The same account describes trying daily posting for over a month with little success. No paid advertising had been used at that stage. That limits the tempting inference that the later paid campaign created the entire audience. [S2]

| Phase | Later creator retrospective [S1] |
|---|---|
| Publicity | Social posts and Steam page by May 2024; demo in June 2024. |
| Release readiness | 17,821 prelaunch wishlists; playable demo removed shortly before release. |
| Launch | 1,248 first-day copies; 3,091 in week one; 4,810 in month one. |
| Later window | About ten months after launch: 8,656 retained sales, 1,712 returns, approximately US$41,000 after Steam's share; not profit. |

The author's blog reports about US$5,000 of advertising, mostly before launch, and says the game did not yet support full-time development. Its proposed explanations include attracting cat/cozy audiences to a difficult game and overly punishing controls. These are attributed interpretations, not a causal experiment. [S1]

## Reception and what counts as success

The later account reports a decline from roughly 75% positive to 61%, alongside continued daily sales. [S1] That supports a **mixed** classification: real revenue and sustained purchases, but disappointing audience fit and insufficient income for the creator's objective. It does not support “no one bought it,” “the launch failed completely,” or a finding of lifetime unprofitability.

**Interpretation:** the early Reddit figures show why video views should not be used as purchase-intent units. A person who enjoys a creator struggling with a cat can still have no desire to play that experience. The developer's hypothesis about audience mismatch is plausible, but it does not isolate controls, difficulty, price or review effects.

## Channel and collaboration record

The early account identifies Reddit, TikTok, YouTube and multiple other social channels, with varying results. Creator playthroughs continued after the festival ended. [S2] The studio website describes a solo developer and links public channels. [S4]

The precise first blog/account dates, historical follower curve, Discord founding and active membership, email-list size, art recruitment and external contributor costs remain unestablished. Devlog videos exist, but there is no verified regular multi-year upload schedule. Paid creator-discovery tools are not a financing publisher or proof of sponsored endorsements by every covering creator.

## Case-specific observations

A retained demo gave creators time to discover the game after an event. That is a concrete possible benefit of persistent playable access, not proof that leaving every demo up improves sales.

The commercial question raised by this case is whether the **same promise** attracts attention, earns purchase intent and survives actual play. Here, a visually appealing cat, punishing platforming and content-creator entertainment may have served partly different audiences. A better future test would compare qualified demo players and purchasers rather than only aggregate views.

No console release is established here. The retrospective discussed a port; a plan is not a completed launch. Long-tail revenue beyond the stated window is unknown.
