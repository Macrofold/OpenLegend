# Rock Paper SHIFT — launch and commercial dossier

[Collection index](../README.md) · **Read and written: 2026-10-15** · **Cohort: unsuccessful small commercial release; successful completion goal**

## Origin, scope and result

Developer `SalmonDev` revived an older prototype during June 2020 lockdown with a deliberately modest objective: finish a game and release it on Steam. The creator describes just over a month of production and roughly eight-hour workdays. Their retrospective reports **about 150 wishlists and 40 sales**, some to friends, and explicitly calls the financial outcome unsuccessful while valuing the shipping experience. [S1]

The self-estimated £1.50 hourly return is not an audited net-profit calculation. Exact costs, tax, hours, refunds and revenue by region are not supplied. This is a solo-led account; outside asset contributions or informal assistance are not fully enumerated.

## Crowdfunding, publishing and collaborators

A Kickstarter campaign is not part of the evidenced route; opening/closing dates, pledged amount and backer count are not applicable. Steam lists A Salmon Game as both developer and publisher. A separate financing publisher or hired marketing team was not identified. [S2]

The account does not explain artist recruitment, specialist audio sourcing, contract prices or work-for-hire terms. It is therefore useful primarily for its small-project release and promotion sequence, rather than as a contractor-hiring model.

## Case-specific observations

**Positive interpretation:** the creator defines and evaluates two different goals. Shipping a bounded first game can satisfy a learning objective even when it does not generate a sustainable income.

**Negative interpretation:** a short development schedule can also compress the opportunity to discover an audience. The record does not show months of compelling player demand that inexplicably vanished at checkout.

The 40-sales/150-wishlists comparison must not be labeled a conversion rate: friends, direct discovery and differing measurement periods can contribute. Readers' suggestions that a mobile port or advertising would certainly solve the problem are untested proposals. No later lifetime revenue or financial recovery is established, and the current review count is not used as a sales estimator.
