# Indie launch, community and commercial dossiers

Research collection started **2026-10-15**. This is a separate evidence archive from [game-design inspiration](../game-inspiration/). It records individual development and launch histories; it does **not** prescribe an Open Legend marketing plan or synthesize principles across games.

## Scope and reading conventions

The requested collection covers at least 20 successful games and 20 unsuccessful cases, prioritizing solo developers and core teams smaller than ten. A small core team is not the same as a small total production: publishers, outsourced studios, freelancers, platform partners and inherited audiences are recorded separately. Larger-team exceptions must be identified rather than silently counted as solo precedents.

Outcome labels refer to a stated period and dimension:

- **Commercial success:** documented sales, revenue, profitability or a developer/publisher's explicit successful commercial outcome. Awards and positive reviews alone are insufficient.
- **Unsuccessful launch/commercial outcome:** a documented shortfall, unsustainable return or developer-described commercial failure. This is not a judgment of artistic quality.
- **Funded but delivery failed:** crowdfunding met its goal but the promised game was canceled or materially undelivered. It is not a failed fundraising campaign.
- **Mixed/recovered:** different phases or dimensions point in different directions. A failed launch followed by recovery is described in both periods, not presented as a lifetime failure or counted twice.

Dossiers distinguish **documented facts**, **attributed explanations**, **case-specific interpretation**, and **not established**. The last term means the reviewed public sources do not establish the answer; it does not mean the activity never happened. No private analytics, developer interviews conducted for this project, purchased data, or game playtesting are represented here.

Historical follower counts, Discord membership, conversion rates, paid-media spending and hiring contracts are especially incomplete. Present-day follower/review counts are not backdated to launch. Backers, wishlists, downloads, paid copies, concurrent players, gross revenue, developer receipts and profit remain different quantities. Currency amounts retain the source currency. Kickstarter periods use the campaign's displayed dates and duration; timezone-dependent dates in retrospective articles can differ by one day.

Each dossier records the earliest **verified** public touchpoint, not necessarily the first-ever post. A visible trailer does not prove that a public downloadable demo existed. Kickstarter campaign metadata can remain readable when the original description or updates do not; access limits are recorded rather than filled with remembered details.

## Dossier structure

Each game includes its outcome and evidence window; origin and team; development/public-exposure timeline; crowdfunding or other financing; channel-by-channel community evidence; launch, reception and commercial results; collaborators/publishing/marketing; bounded positive and negative observations; and unresolved evidence with a source register.

Sources are linked beside claims and listed with author/organization, date where established, source type and purpose. Developer recollections and marketing claims are attributed. Reviewers' opinions are reception evidence, not independent financial audits. The archive is a deliberately selected comparison set, not a representative sample from which a success rate can be calculated.
