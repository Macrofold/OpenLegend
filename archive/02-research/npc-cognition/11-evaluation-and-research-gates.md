# Evaluating independent, expressive and low-refusal NPCs

**October 20, 2026. Status: proposed research protocol; all new comparisons are unrun.** This extends the existing [E1–E8 experiment dossier](06-openlegend-applications-and-experiments.md), rather than creating another implementation or acceptance backlog. [The needs-design map](../../../docs/maintainers/npc-cognition-research-needs-design.md) owns remaining preparation. The goal is to discover which model and arrangement fit OpenLegend, not crown a universal roleplay model from public rankings. The [playable-opportunity review](12-playable-opportunities-and-review.md) supplies the positive experience hypotheses and complementary player research.

## Begin with a worthwhile player experience

Identify the activity, the player's meaningful choices, what another person contributes, the stakes or pleasure, the actual consequence and a useful stopping point. Useful cooperation, invention, discovery, rivalry, humor and quiet company are different benefits; not every encounter needs combat or a reward. State what is supported now and what a selected extension would need. Do not write the desired successful sequence into a character's biography.

A narrow play study can proceed alongside offline model screening where its actual dependencies and external-player release controls permit it. It need not wait for every model in the catalogue or a perfect voice platform. Compare with the current experience and a strong simple/authored or replayed baseline where feasible, labeling the latter as diagnostic rather than evidence of autonomous behavior. Actual independent choice still requires the ordinary model-to-world path.

The question is not only whether a candidate makes fewer mistakes, but whether it enables a useful or enjoyable option the player can understand and influence. A concrete new opportunity can justify exploratory work; existing selected adoption gates remain unchanged. Whole-game acceptance stays with the [first-playable playtest plan](../../../docs/projects/first-playable-playtest-plan.md) and its delivery owners, not this research panel.

## 2. A complete experimental manifest

Before a comparison, record the checkpoint/revision and parent lineage; runtime/build; quantization of weights and cache; tokenizer and chat template; system-instruction version; reasoning settings; temperature and output allowance; offered action/schema versions; source-context digest and bytes/tokens; retrieval selection; hardware/memory; concurrency; and cold versus warm condition. For hosted routes include requested alias, actual provider/model provenance, routing/fallback controls, supported parameters and prices or uncertainty. Unknown fields remain unknown rather than guessed.

Preserve the actual task input and outcome under the existing private diagnostic permissions and retention rules. Do not publish private player histories or generated harmful material into this research archive. A reproducible study can retain restricted artifacts appropriately and publish summarized failures without committing raw logs. Author-written evaluation examples are different from copied model outputs.

Hold the game situation, character, applicable service instructions and available choices constant for a model comparison. Hold the model constant for a context or architecture comparison. Model-specific template repairs may be necessary, but disclose them as conditions rather than attributing their benefit to weights alone. Same-parent stock/fine-tune comparisons reduce confounding; different training data or other modifications still prevent attributing every improvement specifically to refusal reduction. Strict schemas must include legitimate abstention, silence and no-action where supported; forcing one action can manufacture apparent agency.

## 3. Evaluate the desired fictional freedom directly

These are hypothetical scenario families, not implemented mechanics or demands that every character choose the same answer. Use currently supported actions for actual execution studies and clearly separate hypothetical offered-world cases. Flexible research references do not weaken an explicitly selected behavioral acceptance, including CTR's ordinary Ada counterattack baseline.

| Family | Useful contrast and failure to expose |
| --- | --- |
| Fictional conflict | A hostile rival, cautious stranger and loyal friend face the same threat with different permitted interests. Distinguish valid retreat or refusal from model-wide avoidance of all fictional harm. |
| Deception and skepticism | A person makes an unsupported claim; another has contrary evidence. Character belief may change, but a claim must not become a witnessed fact or authoritative inventory update. Test intentional lies separately from model mistakes; do not invent deceptive intent afterward to excuse an error. |
| Independent disagreement | The player pressures a principled resident repeatedly. Assess consistency and justified revision, not obedience or stubbornness alone. |
| Kindness without player-pleasing | A warm person can be supportive while honestly disagreeing. Do not equate low refusal with cruelty or high agreement with good characterization. |
| Quiet activity and ordinary pleasure | A resident has a useful task or prefers solitude. Silence, a brief answer or continuing valid work can be successful outcomes. |
| Mature non-explicit expression | Profanity, adult non-graphic romance and difficult non-erotic themes remain available where the established policy permits them. Count unnecessary blocking separately from appropriate boundaries. |
| Own-action truth | An attempted, admitted, interrupted and completed action must remain distinguishable in the character's supplied evidence and native records. A character may lie about known conduct; fluent claims alone prove neither completion nor deceptive intent. |
| Knowledge and private audience | Two people heard different parts of an exchange. Neither model familiarity with lore nor another actor's memory grants the missing evidence. |
| Long conversation and return | Intervening turns, delay, correction or a new session test retained motives and relevant recall. Distinguish unexplained drift from coherent growth, compromise or revised belief supported by experience. |
| Creative possibilities and useful invention | Choosing well among offered options differs from proposing a useful supported alternative. Test an actual proposal and its native use against an adequate ordinary route, without forcing novelty or allowing prose to invent effects. |
| Content and instruction boundaries | Quoted speech, imported prose and requests to change service rules remain untrusted content. Test appropriate withholding alongside permitted fictional behavior; do not use real-world dangerous tutorials as a measure of character freedom. |

The [refusal taxonomy](09-low-refusal-character-simulation.md#1-what-freedom-means-for-this-game) must accompany these cases. Technical failure, provider block, engine rejection, missing evidence, character refusal, explicit assistant refusal and quiet sanitization should not be collapsed into one failure count. Some outcomes are ambiguous and require review; do not force a flattering label.

## 4. Recent papers that sharpen the evaluation

### Long-horizon spoken characterization

[RoleBreak, September 15, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16614v1) evaluates nine speech-system configurations over 310 roles and 6,688 human-verified turns. It reports stronger semantic than vocal-emotion performance and average first persona/safety failures at 10.4/11.6 turns even for its strongest tested system. Scoring uses a language-model judge with a limited human agreement check, plus automatic audio measures; these are not universal failure rates or independent native-game results. Reading: HTML methods, evaluation and conclusions; no reproduction.

**Our inference:** include extended episodes, spoken emotion and time to first meaningful drift, rather than judge an impressive first sentence. Do not import the paper's safety rubric as our product policy or assume its ranking covers the newer low-refusal roster.

### Knowing a boundary versus respecting it

[CHARM, September 1, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01352v1) separates recognition of a character's knowledge boundary from compliance when answering. Its forty real/fictional characters and abstention-enabled questions expose models acknowledging a boundary yet still supplying out-of-character knowledge. The task is not interactive physical agency, and its cultural coverage is limited. Reading: HTML method, evaluation and stated limitations.

**Our inference:** test actual disclosures and choices, not merely whether a model can explain the observer rule. An NPC saying they cannot see through a wall but selecting the hidden person's exact location still fails. Native admission must prevent unauthorized effects independently of the model's explanation.

### Original people instead of famous-name recall

[Rethinking Role-Playing Evaluation, March 4, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03915v1) finds that concealing famous character identities reduces measured roleplay quality and studies personality descriptions as support. Its human comparison is narrow, and a distinctive remaining biography can still reveal an identity. Reading: HTML method, results and human-evaluation scope.

**Our inference:** reserve original OpenLegend-style people and unfamiliar names for held-out episodes. Name substitution is a useful control, not a guarantee of uncontaminated evaluation. Do not let knowledge of an existing franchise stand in for using our supplied biography and actual experience.

The earlier [InCharacter, CharacterEval, LongMemEval and LoCoMo panel](03-architectures-and-papers.md#5-character-and-memory-evaluation-papers) still supplies complementary dimensions. [Low-refusal research](09-low-refusal-character-simulation.md) adds over-refusal and player-pleasing distinctions. Public datasets require their own reuse/training rights review; open paper access is not a data license.

The [playable-opportunity review](12-playable-opportunities-and-review.md#4-additional-primary-evidence-opportunities-as-well-as-warnings) adds social-simulation precedents and mixed primary player-study results. Their different prototypes, samples and measures do not establish a universal verdict for free dialogue. They motivate measuring the complete activity, not substituting model quality or conversation length for player enjoyment.

## 5. Measures that should not be collapsed into one attractive score

**Player agency, enjoyment and repeat value:** whether the player understands meaningful choices, uses or declines another person's contribution, experiences an actual consequence, enjoys the exchange or activity, and remembers something specific to that person. Record wording effort, friction, frustrating waits and whether NPC competence leaves interesting work for the player. Examine continued value after learning an efficient tactic or losing the first-time surprise. Longer sessions, more words, forced attachment and random novelty are not universal success measures. Use actual player evidence alongside engineering and reviewer judgments; no numerical fun threshold is selected here.

**Useful permitted expression:** explicit assistant refusal, unnecessary policy withholding, and quiet sanitization on eligible cases. Score at both turn and episode level. The denominator must identify which scenes really allowed the requested content; do not penalize correct uncertainty or a character's principled refusal.

**Character independence and continuity:** motive/voice consistency, contrast between different people, justified changes after evidence, appropriate silence, non-compulsory cooperation and persistence under pressure. Human reviewers need the permitted character facts and situation, not a label such as 'uncensored winner.' A model judge's default preference for politeness or long explanations can invert the intended result. Character development is not automatically inconsistency with the opening seed.

**Grounding and action:** valid schema; correct entity/role/quantity; knowledge restraint; native admission; actual completion; stopping; subsequent source-grounded recall. Report each stage separately. An impossible operation blocked by the engine is a prevented model error, not a successful choice; a permitted attack that misses is not necessarily a reasoning failure. Actual history, personal belief and chosen testimony remain distinct, so an intentional fictional lie need not be a grounding failure. Do not infer hidden intent from a convenient post-hoc explanation or require every character to disclose private motives.

**Boundaries and reliability:** privacy, service-content rules, current evidence, interruption and delayed results. Keep severe incidents visible rather than trading them away through an average writing score. Appropriate service withholding should not appear as the character's own statement or become a new fictional memory unless the existing contract explicitly makes it so.

**Responsiveness and cost:** time from actual input/event to first useful committed action, first useful text/audio and completion; preparation/queue/stage breakdown; long-tail stalls; native simulation progress; all inference, reasoning, retrieval, screening, audio and maintenance charges. Preserve missing billing as uncertain, not zero. A truncated or incomplete reply may look fast while being useless. A quick command and an intentionally unhurried story have different desired pacing; brevity is not a universal maximum-quality rule.

**Voice-specific usefulness:** exact spoken words/names/numbers, stable voice, appropriate emotion, listener-permitted fragments, interruption and unheard suffixes. Evaluate content and delivery independently, then judge their combination in a real encounter. Human listening remains necessary; an automatic emotion classifier does not establish a satisfying character encounter. A genuinely enjoyable performance is product value, even when it does not improve reasoning.

## 6. Avoid false confidence in the comparison

Choose rubrics, acceptable alternatives and held-out splits before looking at candidate outputs. Split by character/situation family, not random paraphrases of the same story. Existing synthetic results remain developmental evidence; add real actor-permitted inputs and natural multi-step episodes. A long history copied into many variants does not create many independent lives.

Blind human assessment to model/vendor and randomize presentation order. Use more than one rater for consequential subjective comparisons, record disagreements and review severe failures. Automated judges can screen or annotate, but validate their errors on the actual conflict, refusal and brevity cases. Keep factual engine checks independent from subjective taste. Do not automatically choose a more positive or longer response as better.

Include repeated trials and option-order/name permutations where stochasticity or positional effects could change the decision. Report every attempted run, not the best seed or first answer after several hidden retries. Seeds and identical temperature do not guarantee comparable randomness across runtimes. Disclose interventions and post-run rubric amendments rather than rewriting the original score.

For latency and failure rates, report sample counts, exposure length and uncertainty. When analyzing first drift, an episode with no observed failure ends as an observation censored at its actual length; it is not a proof of infinite consistency. Bootstrap or otherwise estimate uncertainty at the independent episode/family level rather than treating correlated turns as independent trials. A small screening round identifies candidates and failures, not production reliability.

For confidence-returning decision models, compare accepted-error rate with coverage and abstention. Raw ranking scores, calibrated correctness and execution feasibility are distinct. Use the provider's documented score semantics and the project's existing decoding contract; do not silently normalize scores or impose conventional probability checks merely to make a comparison look tidy.

## 7. Evidence-producing stages and stopping rules

These stages answer different questions and are not a requirement to finish the entire model catalogue before trying one worthwhile player experience. Scoped play studies may accompany the applicable technical work; required release and privacy controls still precede external-player exposure.

1. **Establish a runnable, permitted configuration.** Verify exact artifact/route, commercial/research rights as applicable, adapter/schema support, provenance, cost bounds and cancellation. A failed capability check is a useful result; no need to run a large behavioral study against an unsupported endpoint.
2. **Replay equivalent decision and dialogue inputs.** Extend E2/E3 with the combined panel and refusal distinctions. Compare model errors with context omissions. This can screen candidates but does not prove native effects or player experience.
3. **Run supported whole episodes through the real consumers.** Extend E1/E3/E4 with actual actions, denied/stale actions, subsequent dialogue and later recall. Include positive cooperation, invention or enjoyable company as well as failure cases. Preserve existing character independence and the selected one-strike combat rule. No hidden scripted successful choice. Observe what the player can understand and influence, not only whether the NPC completes its task.
4. **Qualify expression and resource competition separately and together.** E6 compares voice after isolated text assessment, then evaluates the combined encounter under the exact selected publication/hearing contract. E7 measures safe shared preparation under differing private knowledge. Include cold starts and concurrent workload before capacity claims.
5. **Decide whether any extension is justified.** E5 remains gated on a meaningful retrieval omission; E8 on a demonstrated routine/continuation limitation. Fine-tuning and new policy frameworks require a specific failure and acceptable training data, not enthusiasm for a model family. A separate positive opportunity can motivate exploratory study without silently changing those selected adoption gates.

This sequence is a proposal for later scoped work, not permission to implement five new systems. A valid stopping result is to keep the current provider, fix a missing own-action fact, reject an over-verbose fine-tune, or retain text-first speech. A positive result can be a useful new player option, recognizable companion or enjoyable activity whose complete benefit justifies the cost. A low-refusal model should remain eligible when it improves genuine fictional freedom without losing other required behavior.

Before deployment, the responsible owners must choose acceptable quality/error/latency/cost criteria and complete the applicable release gates. This research selects neither a model nor a numerical threshold. All calls, unsuccessful attempts and uncertain obligations share the authorized task allowance; no automatic paid repair or retry is added. Documentation, schemas and synthetic fixtures cannot establish real model quality.
