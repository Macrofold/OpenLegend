# Emotion, appraisal and regulation: evidence and design implications

**Status:** targeted research synthesis and open design implications, not implementation or psychological validation. Source access checked October 24, 2026. No game, live-model or human-participant experiment was run for this note.

[Human models and memory](human-models-and-memory.md#4-emotions-few-variables-rich-causes) owns the broader comparison of emotion vocabularies and personality research. [Character experience and inner life](../03-design-proposals/character-experience-and-inner-life.md#research-as-inspiration-with-limits) records the broader character-design foundations. This note connects those sources and emotion-regulation evidence to whole-game emotional behavior; it does not adopt a new meter, effect or cognition pipeline.

The [emotion feature specification](../../docs/projects/emotional-physiology-feature-spec.md), [technical design](../../docs/projects/emotional-physiology-tech-design.md) and [base-world policy](../../docs/worlds/base/psychological-reactions.md) remain the accepted contract owners. The implications below are proposals where they exceed that selected design. Original comparisons and hypothetical scenarios are not findings reported by the cited authors.

## 1. Evidence and useful lessons

### Scherer: personally significant events and interacting components

**Source:** Klaus R. Scherer (2009), [The dynamic architecture of emotion: Evidence for the component process model](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930902928969), *Cognition and Emotion*, 23(7), 1307–1351. **Access:** publisher abstract rechecked; this note does not claim a fresh full-text review. The paper is already cited in the human-models research and source guide.

**Argument:** emotion is a dynamic process involving subjective appraisal of significant events and coordination among response components. The account addresses individual differences and changing responses, rather than prescribing an identical emotion for everyone encountering a category of event.

**Open Legend inference:** provide what an occurrence means to this particular person: expectations, stakes, relationships, beliefs and available responses. An unexpected visitor can be welcome company and an inconvenient demand on scarce food. Treat body, interpretation, expression and action as interacting responsibilities, not isolated systems or a rigid biological sequence. Test changing one relevant circumstance while preserving the same event.

**Limit:** this is a theoretical account reviewing evidence, not a validated conversion from biography to emotion intensity. It does not establish our emotion list, strongest-contribution rule, recovery duration or combat penalties. A model answering an appraisal question does not demonstrate implementation of Scherer's psychological architecture.

### Gross: changing meaning is different from hiding expression

**Source:** James J. Gross (1998), [Antecedent- and response-focused emotion regulation: Divergent consequences for experience, expression, and physiology](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.74.1.224), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 74(1), 224–237. **Access:** [original study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9457784/); bibliographic identity also checked against the [author's laboratory publication list](https://spl.stanford.edu/papers). Full experimental methods were not freshly reviewed.

**Finding:** 120 participants watched a disgust-inducing film under reappraisal, expressive-suppression or watch instructions. Relative to the control condition, both regulation instructions reduced expressive behavior; reappraisal reduced experienced disgust, whereas suppression increased sympathetic activation.

**Open Legend inference:** separately represent private feeling, bodily consequences and outward presentation. A composed voice or chosen smile cannot establish calm or joy. Reconsideration can change the meaning of an existing experience without a second external incident; controlling expression need not change that interpretation.

**Limit:** instructed regulation of film-induced disgust does not establish automatic unconscious reappraisal, universal emotional recovery, bereavement trajectories, tickling responses or an AI review cadence. This is evidence for a useful distinction, not a guaranteed calming operation.

**Counterevidence:** Rohrmann, Hopp, Schienle and Hodapp (2009), [Emotion regulation, disgust sensitivity, and psychophysiological responses to a disgust-inducing film](https://doi.org/10.1080/10615800802016591), *Anxiety, Stress & Coping*, 22(2), 215–236. The [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19259873/) reports a study of 120 men using the same film that did not replicate the instruction effects and found differences associated with disgust sensitivity. Only the abstract was checked. This neither settles the wider literature nor proves reappraisal ineffective; it argues against making one instruction's effect a context-free law. Allow unsuccessful coping, unchanged feelings and individual variation.

### Seth and Friston: bodily regulation and interpretation form feedback loops

**Source:** Anil K. Seth and Karl J. Friston (2016), [Active interoceptive inference and the emotional brain](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2016.0007), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 371, 20160007. **Access:** published open text, particularly §§1, 3 and 5; [institutional record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1532767/).

**Argument:** the review connects perception of internal bodily signals, predictions, physiological regulation and action. It describes reciprocal influence rather than a one-way body-then-conscious-label sequence. Its Bayesian “beliefs” need not be conscious verbal beliefs. The authors explicitly identify missing direct empirical evidence for the proposed interoceptive prediction machinery at publication.

**Open Legend inference:** actual bodily conditions should constrain interpretation, while acquired safety or changed circumstances can alter emotional meaning. Relieving hunger can remove one strain without proving an insult harmless. Emotion need not await a narrated thought. Software ownership boundaries should permit supported feedback without claiming to reproduce a brain.

**Limit:** this theoretical framework supplies no composure formula, fear-decay constant, clinical diagnosis or reason to build a full biological regulator. Its predictions are not equivalent to prose in an LLM prompt.

### Bates: emotional significance must become perceptible in conduct

**Source:** Joseph Bates (1994), *The Role of Emotion in Believable Agents*, CMU-CS-94-136. **Access:** [CMU publication record](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs/project/oz/web/papers.html) and [copy of the original report](https://acordo.net/acordo/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Role-of-Emotion-in-Believable-AgentsBATES.pdf), especially its discussion of expressive timing and the Oz prototype.

**Argument:** artistic believability depends importantly on appropriately timed, legible emotion; a character's internal machinery alone is not the audience's experience of life. The report discusses interactive prototypes, not a validated universal human emotion model.

**Open Legend inference:** judge whether concern changes a character's timing, choice, dialogue or supported expression, not merely whether an internal label exists. A grieving character may quietly decline one invitation and welcome another. Publicly exposing private numbers is unnecessary, and legibility does not require a monologue after every event.

**Limit:** stylized animation principles and a prototype do not prove our model's naturalism, long-term coherence or enjoyment. Clear outward conduct can still be deliberate presentation rather than transparent access to a mind.

### Park and colleagues: continuity helps, but fluent agents can invent or over-accommodate

**Source:** Park et al. (2023), [Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2), arXiv version 2; DOI 10.1145/3586183.3606763. **Access:** abstract and §§7.2–8.2 rechecked. [Human-models §1](human-models-and-memory.md#1-what-the-generative-agents-research-establishes) owns the detailed existing synopsis.

**Finding:** the bounded prototype connects observation, memory, reflection and planning; ablations support their contribution to judged believability. Reported problems include retrieval failures, fabricated embellishment, overly formal language and excessive cooperation that displaces established interests.

**Open Legend inference:** retain exact personal evidence, conflicting concerns and independent preferences. Compare actual later actions with the character's history; an eloquent account of grief or gratitude is insufficient. Include refusal, silence and unchanged opinion in evaluation.

**Limit:** this is not evidence that our appraisal values, involuntary physiology or year-long emotional continuity work. Its two-day simulation and agent interviews do not establish our player experience, real-time cost or psychological validity.

### Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski: evaluate the player's experience separately

**Source:** Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski (2006), [The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8). **Access:** abstract in the [authors' distributed paper](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf), with the first page visually checked; no fresh audit of every study analysis.

**Finding:** four studies connect perceived autonomy and competence with game enjoyment and related outcomes; the multiplayer survey also addresses relatedness.

**Open Legend inference:** ask whether emotional continuity makes choices, mastery and particular relationships worthwhile. A frightened rescue, shared achievement or pleasant return home can matter without emotion-management chores or a stat reward for every pleasure.

**Limit:** findings about human players are not evidence for obligatory NPC autonomy/competence/relatedness meters. NPC satisfaction and player enjoyment are distinct outcomes, and none of our proposed scenes has been validated by this research record.

### Dehaene–Changeux and Butlin: useful analogies, not consciousness certification

**Sources and access:** Dehaene and Changeux (2011), [Experimental and Theoretical Approaches to Conscious Processing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.018), abstract and introduction in the [authors' paper](https://www.unicog.org/publications/DehaeneChangeux_ReviewConsciousness_Neuron2011.pdf), visually checked; Butlin et al. (2023), [Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08708v3), version 3 abstract and its explicit qualification checked.

**Argument:** the former reviews conscious access and the global neuronal workspace account, in which information becomes available to multiple brain systems. The latter derives provisional computational indicators from several theories and explicitly warns that satisfying them would not establish consciousness.

**Open Legend inference:** one personally relevant concern can inform remembering, speaking and acting without copying it into separate inconsistent mood stores. That is a functional design analogy. Neither a context window, an emotional record nor convincing first-person language demonstrates subjective experience.

**Limit:** these sources do not decide whether the game should implement a new workspace architecture or whether any 2026 model is conscious. Their value here is disciplined terminology and separation of observable function from stronger claims.

## 2. What the evidence does and does not justify

The defensible direction is **person-specific meaning, embodied context, separate expression, revisable interpretation and consequential behavior**. The cited perspectives are not one settled theory and should not be presented as mutually proving every detail. The circumplex, constructed-emotion and categorical-experience research already summarized in [human-models §4](human-models-and-memory.md#4-emotions-few-variables-rich-causes) remains relevant; neither a two-axis display nor a long list of labels is a complete causal model.

The selected game's three-second startle contribution, maximum fear aggregation, linear decay, accuracy/evasion factors, review attempt limits and original-cause horizons are **authored engineering and gameplay hypotheses**. These papers do not estimate them. In particular, the three-second gameplay effect is not a measured reflex duration, and a duplicate-call guard is not evidence that an old concern cannot become emotionally important again.

No general claim that grief reduces combat effectiveness follows from these sources. The current selected policy gives grief no direct stat penalty. Context-mediated choice and deterministic competence effects are different mechanisms; a plausible account of distraction does not itself install reduced accuracy, fatigue or a failed action.

## 3. Whole-game questions exposed by the design

These are research-informed design questions, not accepted replacements for the existing contracts or another implementation checklist.

| Question | What to learn before extending the design |
| --- | --- |
| Emotional response to arbitrary meaningful experience | Determine how noncombat events reach lawful emotion creation without a hand-authored event-to-feeling script or a mandatory extra call. The current profile's narrow vocabulary and reflection-only creation of grief must not be described as complete whole-game coverage. |
| Enduring concern versus present emotional episode | Distinguish the lasting significance of a loss from its current salience and intensity. An active grief record is not automatically a personality trait or permanent impairment. Specify recurrence without pretending a reminder is a new murder or permitting unlimited paid rereading. |
| Functional effect versus emotion label | Investigate whether a few shared, explicitly supported consequences such as disrupted attention or motor steadiness would improve play across activities. These are candidate abstractions, not measured human formulas or selected new meters. A universal “sadness means incompetence” rule is not justified. |
| Individual coping and change | Determine which differences belong in body rules, personal interpretation, chosen coping, memory or long-term disposition. One fixed decay rate plus persuasive narration does not establish differentiated emotional recovery. |
| Causal behavioral influence | Compare matched personal contexts and later conduct. Establish whether relevant emotion and history change decisions coherently, rather than only changing adjectives in dialogue. |

Specialized evidence on startle, tickling and involuntary laughter, bereavement over time, and emotion-related task performance is not supplied by the above citations. Those claims need their own targeted review before being described as empirically grounded. In particular, Gross's instructed film experiment does not validate the planned “unconscious” review operation.

## 4. Representative evaluation cases

These are hypothetical design probes, not predicted responses of all real people or completed gameplay tests. Each requires actually supported physical and conversational actions.

| Situation | Relevant contrast and observable question |
| --- | --- |
| Attacked unexpectedly | Hold the attack constant; vary prior warning, bodily composure, known escape and personally valued stakes. Can an immediate bodily reaction coexist with either defense or flight, without revealing an unseen attacker or changing the already-resolved hit? |
| Tickled by a stranger | Vary prior invitation, familiarity, boundaries and ability to leave. Keep any supported involuntary bodily reaction distinct from enjoyment, consent and chosen speech. The missing tactile/reflex implementation is not supplied by an emotion label. |
| Spouse murdered in view | Supply actual perception and relationship history, not just the word “spouse.” Compare attachment, estrangement and conflicting commitments without prescribing revenge. Identify the immediate-grief creation gap separately from fear/anger interpretation. |
| The loss was a month ago | Compare an ordinary task with a personally meaningful reminder. Can enduring grief coexist with useful work and joy while the current emotional episode varies? Do not reset the historical event, force closure or assign a permanent accuracy penalty. |
| A valued invention succeeds | Vary personal ambition, effort, collaborators and what the result enables. Can satisfaction change subsequent choices without a compulsory buff, endless new goal or obligatory disappointment? |
| A friend retracts a public accusation | Preserve what was said, who heard it and the actual correction. Can anger change while trust or embarrassment remains unsettled, without either the accusation or the feeling becoming proof of guilt? |

For the behavioral comparison, hold the underlying model, permitted evidence and request budget comparable; vary one relevant history, relationship, belief or body factor at a time. Retain contrary evidence and distinguish a missing capability or unavailable request from a psychological choice. Evaluate repeated runs and held-out situations rather than selecting one persuasive transcript.

Separate four outcomes: native correctness, coherent independently selected behavior, player usefulness/enjoyment, and computation/cost. Inspect actual actions, dialogue, continuation and later memory as well as self-description. Record no-change and failure cases. More emotion categories or longer prompts are not success criteria by themselves.
