# Narration and conversation follow-up

Recorded September 20, 2026. This is a faithful requirements summary of the user's request, not a verbatim transcript. **Documentation and future implementation planning only were requested.**

- An agent can talk, act and/or think in response to any triggered situation. None is compulsory, and all three may occur together with proper entity targets and other necessary fields. An insult can produce only a frown, or speech (“Screw you”), an action (slap the speaker) and a private thought (“I don't like Mike”). Each accepted component becomes the actor's own remembered experience, including thoughts.
- Narration in a conversation describes non-speech reactions, actions and relevant world events, excluding internal thoughts. It can combine a cause and its consequences: “Ada slaps you. Your eye wound from yesterday starts bleeding.” Actual character impacts appear below in smaller distinct text, for example “-1 Health”.
- Actions may cause effects. In the eventual design, missing actions/effects are invented when needed, including conditional effects such as losing one health if an existing head injury is present. **Defer on-the-spot invention:** initially an unsupported slap can be expressed and remembered but has no actual mechanical effects.
- A master world-event table represents external observable occurrences: people, animals, speech, natural events and more. Actor/event awareness is a many-to-many join. Discard unwitnessed experiential events unless notable according to an importance score; notable unseen events can support narration such as “Far away, a demon awakens.”
- A Storyteller Agent, **The Narrator**, describes selected events from one or several actors' perspectives. It can be one LLM call with a specialized, preferably parameterized extension of the existing context builder. Filter by actors, purpose (such as conversation), awareness and relevance; reuse semantic/vector retrieval. Selected single-actor modes may explicitly include that actor's inner thoughts.
- Triggers include actions during conversation and standalone milestones such as meeting an important person or entering a village for the first time. Standalone narration appears as stylized book-like text across the top of the screen and remains in the Journal, privately for each player. Narrator voice is a player setting.
- Narrations describe events; they are not observable world events themselves. Store them separately in PostgreSQL, linked to players, conversations, events, actions and other relevant identities. Style may embellish presentation without becoming new world truth.
- Conversations are durable identities. Ordinarily two actors start one by talking; self-talk creates a one-person conversation. Membership can grow and shrink (two to five to two different actors). A later joiner need not know earlier speech, but may have overheard it before joining. Overhearing alone does not join; speaking does. End when all participants have closed/left.
- Every speech event belongs to one conversation. An action may belong to one conversation, and a narration may belong to one conversation. Reconstruct a transcript from its world events and narrations in chronological order, with awareness/privacy filtering.
- When someone in one conversation engages another conversation, their source conversation ends with “Joined conversation #123”; all current source participants join the destination. Keep earlier records in their original conversation rather than assigning them to multiple conversations.
- A meteor affecting several conversations remains one world event without a conversation ID. Each affected conversation receives appropriate narration for its aware participants. A many-to-many narration/world-event join records every source explicitly; transcript reconstruction need not fetch the meteor's raw event payload.
