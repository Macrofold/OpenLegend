# Gameplay, bodies and space

[Glossary index](README.md)

## Time and actions

| Canonical term                | Meaning in OpenLegend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Source                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simulation time (game time)   | Time that has passed inside the running world. A game second and a real second are separate units. A player can turn the camera while the simulation is paused.                                                                                                     | [Independent clocks](../simulation-time.md#independent-clocks)                                                                                                                                |
| Simulation interval           | An amount of game time over which already-active work advances. Movement, collision or an action completing may require the engine to split an interval. A browser frame, server wake-up or state update each has its own timing.                                   | [Interval and event ordering](../simulation-time.md#native-interval-contract)                                                                                                                 |
| Elapsed-time debt             | Game time the clock owes that the simulation has yet to complete. If the server falls behind, report the time actually completed. Skipped opportunities for optional thoughts do not add simulation debt.                                                           | [Clock, progress and debt](../simulation-time.md#independent-clocks), [Optional work](../simulation-time.md#product-scalability-time-and-detail-targets)                                      |
| Calendar time                 | Dates meaningful in the fiction. Advancing the calendar separately from bodily activity or movement is a design target. The existing speed control changes how much simulation time is owed.                                                                        | [Separate calendar proposal](../simulation-time.md#product-scalability-time-and-detail-targets)                                                                                               |
| Intention, command and action | An intention states what the actor wants. A command asks the authoritative world for a specific change. An accepted command can start an action that takes time, misses or is interrupted.                                                                          | [Grounding an intention](../action-capabilities.md#5-intent-envelope-and-semantic-fidelity), [Recorded results](../action-experience.md#what-is-recorded)                                     |
| Action capability             | Something the installed world can execute under stated prerequisites. An **action choice** is one option offered to a particular actor with particular inputs. Availability is checked again when the actor tries it.                                               | [Capability registry](../action-capabilities.md#4-one-capability-registry-several-views), [Availability and execution](../action-capabilities.md#action-availability-and-temporary-execution) |
| Activity                      | Work an actor can undertake, such as moving stock from a bag to a cache. An activity can contain several actions. Its plan records what is still to be done. Another character must decide whether to cooperate.                                                    | [Executing activities](../action-experience.md#selected-execution-and-inspection)                                                                                                             |
| Action occurrence             | The record of one actual attempt, including stages, interruption or completion, outputs and effects. An attempt to deal eight damage against two remaining health records the two health actually lost. A learned method describes steps the actor could try again. | [What an action records](../action-experience.md#what-is-recorded)                                                                                                                            |

## Bodies, abilities and survival

| Canonical term                | Meaning in OpenLegend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Body and body profile         | The physical body occupies space and can move, collide or be hit. Its **body profile** supplies dimensions and physical reference points, including where its eyes or hands reach. A mesh or sprite only depicts it.                                                                                   | [Authored terrain and bodies](../spatial-world.md#authored-terrain-and-bodies)                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Replacement life              | Under the bundled rule, a player can choose to continue after death. Their character keeps their identity, memories and relationships and begins a new physical life. Commands targeting the old body cannot silently attach to the new one.                                                           | [Player death and continuation](../worlds/base/player-death.md#death-and-possessions), [Binding a body target](../identity-and-references.md#control-and-projection)                                                                                  |
| Life stage                    | A proposed phase of biological or social development, such as childhood or adulthood, with capabilities defined by that world. This is distinct from a character's physical life ending, and age by itself grants nobody control of the character.                                                     | [Proposed stages of childhood](../projects/generations-and-family-life/childhood-and-population.md#stages-change-possibilities-not-ownership)                                                                                                         |
| Attribute                     | A value the world defines for an entity, with declared meaning, visibility and supported changes. For example, the base world's health and fullness are different attributes. Use the particular attribute's name when “stat” could mean either a game value or a measurement.                         | [Attribute meanings](../engine-and-world-boundaries.md#semantics-before-meters), [Attribute definition](../../packages/domain/src/world-modules.ts)                                                                                                   |
| Need and reservoir            | A need gives the character a reason to act. A reservoir is a quantity with bounds and rates, such as fullness. Hunger can arise as fullness falls. Feeling hungry does not make a character automatically seek food.                                                                                   | [Bundled survival rules](../worlds/base/survival.md#bodily-conditions-and-chosen-responses)                                                                                                                                                           |
| Condition and status effect   | A condition describes a state, such as “starving” at a specified fullness. A status effect applies installed rules over time or on transitions, such as periodically reducing health while starvation continues. In rule code a condition can also mean a test that decides whether an effect applies. | [Condition descriptions](../worlds/base/survival.md#bodily-conditions-and-chosen-responses), [Status effects](../status-effects.md#transitions)                                                                                                       |
| Trait and skill               | A trait describes a characteristic or disposition. A skill describes proficiency and requires a rule connecting it to action. For example, base-world sling handling improves handling in its supported context. Knowing how to make a sling is separate.                                              | [Semantics before meters](../engine-and-world-boundaries.md#semantics-before-meters), [Sling handling](../worlds/base/practical-competence.md#one-narrow-competence)                                                                                  |
| Native response               | A supported reaction computed by ordinary simulation rules, such as an alarmed hare starting to flee. A reaction can occur without a model deciding what to do, and does not give every witness knowledge of its cause.                                                                                | [Animal alarm and escape](../worlds/base/animal-responses.md#what-starts-and-renews-an-alarm)                                                                                                                                                         |
| Incapacity, death and absence | Incapacity affects what a living character can do. Death ends their current physical life under the world's rules. A player leaving the session triggers the world's separate departure rules. Reconnecting alone does not revive a character.                                                         | [Body and survival](../worlds/base/survival.md#bodily-conditions-and-chosen-responses), [Logout and return](../worlds/base/lifecycle-and-protection.md#human-logout-and-return), [Continue](../worlds/base/player-death.md#continue-near-a-rest-spot) |

For what counts as an injury and what treatment does in the proposed care system, see [danger, care and death](social-life-and-stories.md#danger-care-and-death).

## Objects, custody and work

| Canonical term                        | Meaning in OpenLegend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Item definition and item lot          | The definition describes a kind of item. An **item lot** is an actual identified holding of that kind, with its own quantity. Ten interchangeable stones can form one lot; an individual keepsake retains its own identity.                                                                    | [Item types](../../packages/domain/src/types.ts), [Item lot](../../packages/domain/src/objects.ts), [Inventory and piles](../worlds/base/items.md#ground-piles)                                         |
| Material property and ingredient role | A material property describes a supported characteristic such as flexibility. A role specifies how a recipe uses an ingredient, such as binding or shaft. Changing an ingredient’s name does not grant the properties that role requires.                                                      | [Material and work rules](../worlds/base/invention-families.md#material-and-work-rules)                                                                                                                 |
| Placement                             | An entity's authoritative physical relationship to the world. It can sit at a world position, be contained by another entity, or be attached at a supported point. Contained and equipped objects move with their parent.                                                                      | [Placement in code](../../packages/domain/src/spatial-state.ts)                                                                                                                                         |
| Assembly and shelter                  | An assembly is a physical arrangement of installed parts. Its supports and cover determine what it can actually shelter. For example, a roof can shelter an item under its covered area while leaving a nearby item exposed. A place name or permission to build grants no weather protection. | [Canopy arrangements](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md#flat-and-sloping-arrangements-using-the-same-rules), [Rain exposure](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md#rain-and-drying-for-the-first-scene) |
| Custody, ownership and access         | Custody is who holds an object or which container holds it. Ownership is a claim about whose property it is. Access is permission and practical ability to use or move it. Someone can carry a borrowed item, and seeing a bag does not reveal its contents.                                   | [Possessions and transfers](../worlds/base/social.md#offering-and-accepting-possessions), [Shared containers](../worlds/base/items.md#shared-containers-and-active-work)                                |
| Inventory and equipment               | Inventory shows what a character has in their possession. Equipment also occupies compatible body attachment points and can enable an action. Having a weapon in a bag does not make it ready for a strike.                                                                                    | [Held equipment](../worlds/base/items.md#compatible-held-equipment), [Equipped strikes](../targeted-actions.md#equipped-contact-strikes)                                                                |
| Resource source and stock             | A source can supply material under the installed extraction rules, such as reeds gathered from a patch. Stock is a quantity of items already available, such as gathered reeds in a bag. A source can be depleted and a gathering attempt can yield less than requested.                       | [Authored sources](../worlds/base/first-playable-survival.md#authored-sources), [Stock transfer](../action-experience.md#fresh-stock-binding-in-finite-methods)                                         |
| Finite attempt and yield              | A gathering or fishing attempt takes work under an installed rule. The **yield** is the quantity actually obtained. For example, a completed cast can yield no fish. Learning a method or reserving time never grants its hoped-for output.                                                    | [Finite source attempts](../food-preparation.md#finite-source-attempts), [One fishing cast](../worlds/base/river-fishing.md#one-chosen-cast)                                                            |
| Crafting and preparation              | An actor uses a supported method, materials, tools and work time to produce something. The actual output belongs to that attempt and can differ from the actor's aim. **Invention** changes the available method. **Learning** changes who knows it.                                           | [Known manufacturing](../worlds/base/first-playable-survival.md#known-manufacturing-catalogue), [Food preparation](../food-preparation.md#preparation-definitions-and-exact-choices)                    |
| Output port                           | A named result of a particular step which another step can use. A method names the result it expects. A later step can use only what the earlier step actually produced and what remains available. A step that produced nothing cannot supply a later step.                                   | [Outputs and later steps](../action-experience.md#selected-execution-and-inspection)                                                                                                                    |

## Space, movement and combat

| Canonical term                     | Meaning in OpenLegend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Source                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| World position and support surface | Position is a point in metres with height on the Y axis. A support surface identifies what a body stands on. Two actors can share horizontal coordinates while one stands on a bridge and the other underneath it.                                            | [What a location means](../spatial-world.md#what-a-location-means)                                                                                                       |
| World place and known place        | A place is an authored location, such as the camp hearth. A **known place** is a location the particular character has learned about. Remembering where the hearth is does not reveal whether it is currently lit or who is there now.                        | [Static and known places](../spatial-world.md#static-named-places-and-known-places), [Base-world places](../worlds/base/places.md)                                       |
| Terrain, blocker and scenery       | Terrain and blockers have installed physical properties such as support, collision and sight obstruction. Scenery describes what appears in a scene. An image of a wall alone cannot stop a body or block sound.                                              | [Terrain and physical bodies](../spatial-world.md#authored-terrain-and-bodies), [Static scenery](../spatial-world.md#static-scenery)                                     |
| Facing and camera                  | Facing is the direction the actor's body points. Rotating the camera changes the player's view. It cannot make a character turn, see behind a wall or redirect an attack already in flight.                                                                   | [Body orientation](../spatial-world.md#what-a-location-means), [Camera perspective](../spatial-world.md#tactical-camera-and-perspective)                                 |
| Route, movement and follow         | A route describes a possible path for this body on its available surfaces. Movement advances the body after checking current physical conditions. Follow asks an actor to maintain an allowed relationship to a target using current evidence and replanning. | [Movement](../spatial-world.md#movement), [Base-world follow](../worlds/base/navigation.md#native-follow)                                                                |
| Clearance, reach and contact       | Clearance asks whether a body fits along a movement path. Reach asks whether an actor can physically interact with a target. Contact is the actual bodies or objects meeting. A target being in view or in range alone does not establish contact.            | [Physical interaction](../spatial-world.md#physical-interaction-design-checks), [Physical contact](../spatial-world.md#physical-contact)                                 |
| Threat response                    | A character’s chosen response to evidence of danger. It can include speaking, leaving, continuing current work or choosing a specific defensive action. A purpose such as protecting someone does not itself authorize a pursuit or strike.                   | [Meaning of a threat response](../worlds/base/threat-response-options.md#1-what-a-response-means)                                                                        |
| Aim, release and impact            | Aim selects the intended point or subject. Release commits the actual projectile to flight. Impact depends on what it physically encounters, including an intervening obstacle or a moving body.                                                              | [Aim and flight](../targeted-actions.md#chosen-aim-committed-flight-and-evasion)                                                                                         |
| Defense and evasion                | A supported defense can guard against an incoming strike. Evasion changes what the defender's body does to avoid danger. A defensive intention, a feasible response and a successfully avoided hit are separate outcomes.                                     | [Contact defense](../targeted-actions.md#finite-chosen-contact-defense), [Physical evasion](../worlds/base/skilled-action-execution.md#physical-arrow-and-local-evasion) |

## Social actions

| Canonical term                              | Meaning in OpenLegend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Source                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Offer and acceptance                        | An offer asks another character to take part under stated terms. The other character can accept or decline. Acceptance of an item transfer still requires the specified items, access and the current participants.                                   | [Offering possessions](../worlds/base/social.md#offering-and-accepting-possessions)                                                                                  |
| Spoken promise                              | A supported commitment made through speech. It can matter later even if unfulfilled. A promise to give someone an item leaves the item where it is until a real transfer occurs.                                                                      | [Spoken promises](../worlds/base/social.md#spoken-promises)                                                                                                          |
| Voluntary outing                            | A proposal and, if accepted, a shared purpose for travelling together. Each character keeps their own movement, choices and right to leave. A companion's consent to travel does not transfer control of them.                                        | [Voluntary outings](../worlds/base/social.md#voluntary-outings), [Consenting travel companions](../action-experience.md#consenting-travel-companions)                |
| Biological kinship and knowledge of kinship | The world can record that two people are biologically related. Each character has their own evidence or belief about that fact. Being someone's parent alone gives no automatic right to their possessions or decisions.                              | [Objective family facts](../worlds/base/social.md#objective-family-facts), [Family life proposal](../worlds/base/generations-and-family-life.md#what-family-changes) |
| Barter and gift                             | Barter exchanges agreed items in both directions. A gift asks for nothing in return. The recipient sees the actual terms, and the transfer goes through the existing item owner.                                                                      | [Gifts, barter and counteroffers](../worlds/base/social.md#offering-and-accepting-possessions)                                                                       |
| Durable agreement                           | In the proposed commission system, parties accept terms for a later exchange. A commission for a pouch creates a commitment. The maker still needs materials and must produce and deliver the pouch.                                                  | [Proposed commission rules](../worlds/base/durable-agreements.md#first-agreement-rules)                                                                              |
| Institution and mandate                     | In the proposed equipment-sharing association, a member can accept a specific role, such as lending a named bow on agreed terms. The mandate defines that authority. Membership alone conveys neither ownership of the bow nor access to its storage. | [Proposed institution and its keeper](../worlds/base/shared-equipment-and-institutions.md#what-the-institution-means)                                                |
