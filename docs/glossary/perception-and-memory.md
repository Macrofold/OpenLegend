# Perception, attention and memory

[Glossary index](README.md)

## What happened and what reached the observer

| Canonical term                | Meaning in Open Legend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Source                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Occurrence                    | Something that actually happened in the simulation, with a cause and a game time. A request to speak alone does not establish that the character spoke.                                                                                                                                        | [Events and reactions](../events-perception-and-reactions.md#2-the-six-distinct-concepts)                                                                     |
| World event                   | An external occurrence accepted by the simulation, such as a spoken warning. It can be recorded even when nobody else heard the warning. When a listener’s awareness is recorded, it contains only what they perceived.                                                                        | [External world events and awareness](../narration-and-conversations.md#5-external-world-events-and-awareness)                                                |
| Subject (perception)          | The person, animal, object or other thing the observer is sensing or tracking. In “the guard watches a hare,” the guard is the observer and the hare is the subject. An observer can perceive an unidentified subject.                                                                         | [Observer and subject](../technical/cognition-walkthrough.md#start-here-how-the-pieces-fit-together)                                                          |
| Observer                      | The actor whose senses or experience are being considered. A hare can be the observer when evaluating what it heard.                                                                                                                                                                           | [Personal perspective and acquisition](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                        |
| Observer perspective          | An account of events from one observer’s point of view, limited to what that observer personally perceived or learned. Two witnesses can have different perspectives on the same event.                                                                                                        | [Personal perspective and acquisition](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                        |
| Perception                    | The process by which an observer senses their surroundings. Two people beside the same gate can perceive different things, for example if only one can see the person behind it.                                                                                                               | [How an observer acquires evidence](../events-perception-and-reactions.md#2-the-six-distinct-concepts)                                                        |
| Physical signal               | A physical input that can reach a sense, such as sound from a voice. Whether a particular listener detects it depends on the signal that actually reaches them and their hearing.                                                                                                              | [Perception evidence and references](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#three-representations-one-observation)                                 |
| Received evidence             | Information a particular observer actually acquired through a sense. A listener behind a wall may hear a voice without seeing or identifying the speaker. The server knowing who spoke gives the listener no extra evidence.                                                                   | [Perception evidence and references](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#three-representations-one-observation)                                 |
| Detection                     | Establishing that something was sensed. A listener can detect a voice without understanding any words.                                                                                                                                                                                         | [Shared perception contracts](../projects/stealth-awareness-tech-design.md#shared-contracts)                                                                  |
| Localization                  | Determining where a sensed signal came from, to the precision the evidence supports. A listener might know that a voice came from the left without knowing the speaker’s coordinates.                                                                                                          | [Shared perception contracts](../projects/stealth-awareness-tech-design.md#shared-contracts)                                                                  |
| Recognition                   | Matching a currently sensed subject to an individual the observer already knows. A familiar voice from the left can support recognition without a visible body or a known name. The match can be mistaken.                                                                                     | [Matching and association](../worlds/base/perception-recognition.md#matching-and-association)                                                                 |
| Source association            | The observer’s evidence-supported link between observations they attribute to one source. A listener may associate two voice samples with one speaker without knowing who spoke. The speaker’s actual identity alone supplies no such evidence to the listener.                                | [Matching and association](../worlds/base/perception-recognition.md#matching-and-association)                                                                 |
| Not detected                  | A completed check found nothing detectable by the specified sense in the area and time covered. It leaves open whether something was present but undetectable.                                                                                                                                 | [Incomplete and missed observation](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#incomplete-and-missed-observation)                                      |
| Not checked                   | The observer has no completed check covering that area, time and sense. An unfinished search cannot support a report that it found nothing.                                                                                                                                                    | [Incomplete and missed observation](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#incomplete-and-missed-observation)                                      |
| Exposure (perception)         | An observer’s encounter with a particular sensory source. After losing sight of someone, the observer can retain the encounter even though a physical action would require a fresh [current-body reference](identity-and-references.md#what-a-reference-lets-its-recipient-do).                | [Four reference roles](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#one-identity-owner-four-distinct-reference-roles)                                    |
| Speech intelligibility        | Which words a listener could make out in an utterance. `none` supplies no words, `partial` only the fragments heard, and `clear` the full wording. Understanding the speaker’s meaning is a separate matter. Seeing a speaker alone supplies neither their words nor their intended addressee. | [Listener-specific speech evidence](../hearing-and-speech.md#5-one-occurrence-listener-specific-evidence)                                                     |
| Received order                | The sequence in which observations reached the observer. Two observations with the same game timestamp can still have a definite received order.                                                                                                                                               | [Observation records](../technical/cognition-walkthrough.md), [shared temporal references](../identity-and-references.md#selected-shared-temporal-references) |
| Viewing frame                 | The observer’s viewpoint for a particular sensory sample. A later movement cannot change the viewpoint from which the earlier observation was made.                                                                                                                                            | [Observation records](../technical/cognition-walkthrough.md), [shared temporal references](../identity-and-references.md#selected-shared-temporal-references) |
| Awareness record              | The stored link between an observer and a particular event, with the detail or words they perceived. Nearby listeners can have different awareness records for one utterance.                                                                                                                  | [Events and awareness](../memory-architecture.md#5-events-awareness-and-memory-storage)                                                                       |
| Personal account (experience) | One character’s account of what they experienced. It can report another person’s words without establishing that the speaker’s claim was true. In sign-in instructions, [account](access-and-participation.md#who-can-do-what) means the player’s application identity.                        | [Personal perspective and acquisition](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                        |
| Direct observation            | What the observer personally perceived. “I heard Ada say the gate fell” reports a direct observation of speech.                                                                                                                                                                                | [Personal perspective](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                                        |
| Testimony                     | A claim someone communicates to the observer. When Ada says “the gate fell,” the listener can report what Ada said. Whether the gate actually fell requires its own evidence.                                                                                                                  | [Personal perspective](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                                        |
| Inference                     | A conclusion the character draws from evidence, such as “the gate probably broke in the storm.” Recall and retelling preserve the distinction between this conclusion, Ada’s testimony and what the listener directly observed.                                                                | [Personal perspective](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition)                                                                        |

## What receives closer attention

| Canonical term                        | Meaning in Open Legend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Source                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Broad perception                      | Sensing the surrounding scene, such as registering several people passing. A person can remain visible even when the observer is following someone else in detail.                                                                                                                                                                                                              | [Base attention capacity](../worlds/base/attention.md#foreground-capacity)                                   |
| Detailed attention                    | Tracking selected subjects more closely. It can supply observations of intermediate movement for those subjects. Selecting a subject does not by itself produce an observation.                                                                                                                                                                                                 | [Base attention capacity](../worlds/base/attention.md#foreground-capacity)                                   |
| Detailed attention capacity           | The maximum number of subjects one observer can track in detail at the same time. The Base rules call this **foreground capacity**. A limit of one incidental subject restricts that reason for selection. It leaves other priorities, such as a person addressing the observer, subject to the overall limit.                                                                  | [Base attention rules](../worlds/base/attention.md#foreground-capacity)                                      |
| Perception (Base attribute)           | The named Base ability that contributes to recognizing individuals from sensory evidence. A higher score alone does not supply a known name.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | [Recognition ability](../worlds/base/perception-recognition.md#installation-and-ability)                     |
| Focus (Base attribute)                | The named Base attribute used to calculate how many subjects an observer can follow in detail unless the world author specifies the capacity for that actor. More Focus alone does not reveal hidden objects or identify strangers.                                                                                                                                             | [Foreground capacity](../worlds/base/attention.md#foreground-capacity)                                       |
| Deliberate attention                  | A choice by the observer to follow a particular subject more closely. A guard choosing to watch one passerby has a reason to track that person apart from any sudden movement. The observer’s overall capacity still applies.                                                                                                                                                   | [Base attention priorities](../worlds/base/attention.md#foreground-capacity)                                 |
| Cue (attention)                       | Something the observer perceived that can draw attention, such as a sudden movement. The stored selection reason `cue` covers urgent cues as well as ordinary incidental cues.                                                                                                                                                                                                  | [Incidental subjects](../worlds/base/attention.md#how-many-incidental-subjects-an-npc-tracks)                |
| Incidental subject                    | In Base attention selection, a subject followed closely solely because of an ordinary cue, such as a passerby whose movement stands out. An actor using the `npc` controller can follow at most one for that reason. Being addressed, urgency, deliberate attention and current work have their own priorities within the overall limit.                                        | [Incidental subjects](../worlds/base/attention.md#how-many-incidental-subjects-an-npc-tracks)                |
| Discovery in attention selection      | Within the overall detailed attention capacity, Base reserves room for one eligible subject whose current description has yet to appear in the observer’s decision context. After briefly holding a selection, the observer gives other waiting subjects a turn in encounter order. A familiar person with a changed appearance can qualify. Selection starts no model request. | [Discovery and detailed observation](../worlds/base/attention.md#how-many-incidental-subjects-an-npc-tracks) |
| Optional discovery decision           | A chance for a base-world NPC enrolled in the decision scheduler to consider an eligible description it has not yet considered. The scheduler admits at most one such attempt per free interval, for example after the NPC’s chosen work ends. Merely seeing another new subject does not open a new interval.                                                                  | [Base decision opportunities](../worlds/base/attention.md#semantic-opportunities)                            |
| Attention during decision preparation | A relevance judgment about which already-permitted items to include for a particular decision. The items can include a recipe or an earlier conversation as well as a currently visible object. This is distinct from choosing which moving subjects to track with the senses.                                                                                                  | [Jev attention and context](../memory-architecture.md#4-jev-attention-before-context-inclusion)              |
| Urgency                               | How promptly newly perceived evidence warrants reconsidering a response already in progress. A warning to duck can be urgent.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | [Importance and urgency](../memory-architecture.md#importance-urgency-and-in-flight-response-refresh)        |
| Importance (event)                    | An event’s lasting significance to the character. Learning the name of a relative can remain important long after the meeting without needing an immediate reaction.                                                                                                                                                                                                            | [Importance and urgency](../memory-architecture.md#importance-urgency-and-in-flight-response-refresh)        |

## What the character knows, remembers or may use

| Canonical term              | Meaning in Open Legend                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Source                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiarity                 | An observer’s prior knowledge of an individual’s distinctive features, such as a familiar voice. It can come from earlier encounters or from explicitly authored starting knowledge. Familiarity with a voice alone does not establish familiarity with a face.                                                                             | [Base recognition](../worlds/base/perception-recognition.md#matching-and-association)                                                               |
| Known name                  | A name learned by this observer or supplied as part of their authored starting knowledge. Recognizing someone’s voice and knowing what they are called each require their own evidence.                                                                                                                                                     | [Spoken introductions](../worlds/base/knowledge.md#spoken-introductions)                                                                            |
| Observer-local individual   | A person or distinguishable source remembered under one observer's own identity record, such as “the stranger who helped me.” A private label or note can refer to that individual even when the observer cannot connect them to a particular identified person or current body.                                                            | [Learned individuals](../worlds/base/knowledge.md#learned-individuals-without-a-canonical-association)                                              |
| Recent history (perception) | An observer’s retained sequence of particular observations and their original times, within the installed world’s history limits.                                                                                                                                                                                                           | [Base history and memory](../worlds/base/attention.md#recent-history-and-lasting-memory)                                                            |
| Lasting memory              | A selected experience or interpretation that the character retains beyond a recent observation, with its surviving support. Displaying it again supplies no new observation.                                                                                                                                                                | [Base history and memory](../worlds/base/attention.md#recent-history-and-lasting-memory)                                                            |
| Recall                      | Retrieving eligible past experiences or knowledge for a character’s present purpose.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | [Selective recall](../memory-architecture.md#retrieval-service-and-selective-recall-tools)                                                          |
| Consolidation (memory)      | Processing eligible earlier accounts into retained memories or summaries. Summarizing one witness’s account cannot add a second witness or reconstruct words the witness never heard.                                                                                                                                                       | [Memory consolidation](../memory-architecture.md#6-hourly-consolidation-and-six-hour-raw-recall)                                                    |
| Knowledge document          | An editable personal note or authored starting text. Writing “I know how to move these berries into a cache” records the claim. A [learned activity](actions-and-learning.md#what-experience-can-teach) requires evidence from the character’s experience.                                                                                  | [Knowledge documents](../knowledge.md#documents-and-authority), [learned activities](../knowledge.md#learned-activities)                            |
| Player history              | The recorded occurrences the player is currently permitted to review for their character, including through [Events](interface-and-presentation.md#what-the-player-opens). Some can be outside the character’s current recall window. Cached history must honor correction and forgetting. Creator inspection requires separate permission. | [Perceived World Events](../perceived-world-events.md#1-navigation-and-scope)                                                                       |
| Evidence source             | The observation, event account or other record supporting a particular claim. Before using an [evidence reference](identity-and-references.md#what-a-reference-lets-its-recipient-do), the server checks the reader’s current permission and the relevant state of the source. A remembered name cannot locate a hidden person.             | [Evidence and permitted references](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#evidence-records-and-permitted-references)                    |
| Permitted evidence          | The information this recipient is entitled to receive for this use, down to the individual fact or spoken fragment. The server can retain the original utterance while supplying a partial quotation to a listener. Signing into another tab does not broaden those rights.                                                                 | [Current authority](../identity-and-references.md#control-and-projection) and [partial speech](../hearing-and-speech.md#6-partial-text-and-privacy) |
| Correction (evidence)       | An authorized change to a source that alters what it supports. A contradictory account from another witness adds a separate claim and leaves the first account attributable to its own witness.                                                                                                                                             | [Source changes and forgetting](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#correction-contradiction-revocation-and-deletion)                 |
| Forgetting                  | Explicit removal of a character’s access to particular evidence. Later recall, decisions and views must exclude facts that depended solely on it. The character may still use independent evidence that remains available.                                                                                                                  | [Source changes and forgetting](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#correction-contradiction-revocation-and-deletion)                 |
| Natural expiry (evidence)   | The end of a record’s retention or permitted use when its normal lifetime elapses. A different witness’s account can have its own lifetime.                                                                                                                                                                                                 | [Source changes and forgetting](../projects/perspective-perception-tech-design.md#correction-contradiction-revocation-and-deletion)                 |
