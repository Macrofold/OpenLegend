# World presentation

This specification owns motion presentation, mixed sprite/mesh depth, lighting, shadows, camera controls and player-controlled local read-through. [Spatial world](spatial-world.md) owns physical location, navigation and senses; [SW](maintainers/spatial-world.md) owns implementation and remaining acceptance. Visual approximations never become collision bodies, actor knowledge or permission to act. Current presentation bounds are catalogued in [interface limits](limits/interface.md#vp01).

## Camera and settings

Grounded motion and placement use continuous world coordinates. Neither camera orientation nor artwork imposes four/eight movement directions or tile snapping. Optional placement snapping is an authoring convenience, not a world law; the current default accepts fractional positions.

Right-button dragging changes yaw and pitch continuously, without snapping; holding Shift at the start of a right-drag pans instead. Middle dragging also pans; primary dragging does not move the camera or act on release. The wheel zooms, and a stationary right-click retains the action menu. Preserve drag thresholds, pointer capture, cancellation, lost focus and Control-click/keyboard menu access. Release after dragging never issues a movement command. Pitch remains bounded above the ground and below a degenerate top-down view. Projection, floor focus, recentering and optional rotation lock remain independent. Camera control should help exploration, not be necessary to discover otherwise body-visible nearby details.

Resuming the same character in the same world and saved timeline preserves the camera position and unchanged landscape/currently visible visual resources. A renewed controlling connection still clears transient captions and hover, drops now-unpermitted remembered visuals, and revokes selection if its object is no longer visible. Actual world, timeline or private-player replacement resets the scene and initial focus. This separates continuity of the player’s view from permission to retain private information; [FR10](maintainers/foundation-review-todos.md#fr10--preserve-same-character-work-and-camera-on-resume) owns the focused hardening acceptance.

Initial 3D scene construction waits until the tab has entered play, so cold graphics preparation cannot delay acquiring control. A newly opened competing tab shows the permitted interface and Resume dialog without preparing its 3D background until selected. An already-created scene still receives paused-view invalidation and retains its permitted resources; caption and perception preferences apply on first construction as well as later updates.

Local camera preferences persist separately from player-owned visibility preferences. In game settings, read-through has Off, Player only and Nearby visible objects modes, radius and strength. The current UI presets are 2/4/6/8/10/12 metres and subtle/balanced/strong; default Nearby, 6 m, 0.7 strength. Server input limits are radius 2–12 and strength 0.2–0.95. They are presentation presets, not biological sight ranges or arbitrary authored-world laws. The profile mutation and persistence owner validates them; no new preference store is introduced.

The canvas renders at one pixel per CSS pixel: a 1920×1080 viewport produces a 1920×1080 framebuffer in both shadow settings. The earlier implicit 0.8 scale is removed. Device pixel ratio remains capped at one, avoiding an unrequested Retina supersampling workload. CSS still owns layout and input coordinates. [VP01](limits/interface.md#vp01) owns this graphics budget.

## Local read-through

Eligibility is the controlled player plus **currently character-visible** entities within the configured three-dimensional neighborhood, excluding stale remembered entities. Radius selects the protected target, not potential occluders: a tree outside the radius may hide a target inside it. Same XZ on another floor is not sufficient; body visibility still gates the result. The local neighborhood appears circular in plan, not a screen-wide X-ray mode. Changing radius never extends perception, interaction range or server disclosure. Targets outside the viewport are not brought onto the screen.

Make the target readable where intervening camera scenery obscures it, with a soft feathered boundary, mild stipple and smooth temporal onset/removal. Only walls, structural surfaces and decorative trees permit ordinary read-through. People, animals and ordinary objects retain their normal occlusion: an unselected fire behind a person stays behind that person. This supersedes the earlier rule allowing read-through over any depth obstruction.

Winning world fragments record their obstruction class in the stencil buffer. A preliminary, color-free pass protects the full alpha silhouettes of nearby currently perceived characters hidden by scenery, preventing object read-through from showing through their partially blended images. Object read-through then runs before character read-through; both redraw only hidden fragments over permitted scenery against retained world depth. These layers run before CameraFrame composites the scene. This is the local visual equivalent of fading an obstructing tree over the protected silhouette, without lowering every tree's opacity, exposing unrelated unknown objects or building a target-by-occluder raycast matrix. Depth still establishes normal visibility. No character is faded to reveal an object behind them.

A target becoming unauthorized is removed immediately; only still-authorized presentation eases out. Picking must share target scope, alpha/feather eligibility and depth/reveal treatment, never a hidden rectangular hit area. Decorative canopy can obstruct the camera without acquiring physical collision or sight semantics. No artificial small count cap may silently hide eligible nearby details; GPU work scales with the eligible target meshes and screen coverage. Future mask batching/instancing is conditional on measurement. A more complete depth/mask cutaway is replaceable behind this presentation owner without changing knowledge rules.

Native visual acquisition now records observer-private evidence through EPR03; an observer noticing something is not an external act broadcasting it to neighbors. This shader still cannot sanitize any other upstream disclosure error. It also does not implement server viewport leases, distant camera knowledge or multiple controlled views (D51).

## Selection and hover

The selected, currently perceived thing is drawn in a final foreground layer, even over people and independently of the read-through mode, radius and strength. Selection changes presentation, not bodily sight. It does not bring an offscreen target onscreen. Sprite fronts retain their alpha silhouette; the block mercenary retains the nearest opaque surface of his actual geometry. Clearing selection restores ordinary depth immediately.

Canvas selection and contextual targeting require current server-projected visibility and the existing sight-focus legibility restriction. List inspection also checks the latest projection, then uses the same renderer selection check when the renderer is available. Clicking a remembered image or an image outside that legibility restriction shows “Mike can't see this.” with the controlled character's actual name, without selecting, opening target actions or issuing a ground movement command. The message does not identify the unseen target. A completely undisclosed thing supplies no image or click target. Losing current visibility or changing world scope removes foreground emphasis and selection; hover emphasis is revoked immediately.

After the pointer stays on one selectable thing for more than 0.3 real seconds, a warm glowing silhouette outline fades in over 0.25 seconds. Movement inside the same target retains the delay; leaving, dragging, cancellation, window blur, replacement or loss of visibility resets it. Reduced motion retains the delay and shows the outline without a fade. The outline uses a subdued, softly spread edge at 42% strength. Target mode shows this silhouette immediately for targets with an offered equipped-item use. Hover uses the existing picking shapes and alpha images; the animated character's existing conservative per-mesh picking remains an approximation, while its outline follows the actual opaque block geometry. Inspection remains available through selection and the In view list, independently of hover.

One renderer-owned outline camera processes only the current hovered target after the delay. It reuses PlayCanvas's outline renderer with isolated material copies and borrowed geometry/transforms; the preserved sampled-model study additionally uses its existing pixel textures. Unselected outlines cannot paint over foreground character silhouettes. The two reusable color targets and mask depth target follow canvas size; their work does not multiply with the number of scene objects. Replacement and teardown release copies, materials, layers, camera and targets. [Current interaction evidence](verification/embodied-feedback.md#browser-playthrough) and [earlier visibility evidence](verification/character-visibility.md) record the exercised cases and limits.

Press **T** with an equipped item to enter target mode. Offered targets highlight under the mouse; primary click requests that exact target/item action, including an attack for an equipped weapon. Ground clicks do not walk while targeting. Escape or Cancel leaves the mode; normal dialogs, text input and camera drags keep their input ownership. Existing native admission rechecks visibility, range, item access and all action requirements. Items without an offered target use explain that limitation. Inventory secondary-click (or the keyboard context-menu command) opens the existing item action picker, including Eat when applicable.

## Motion and depth

Overhead notices, injury bars and work progress share the existing character projection. React updates their content when it changes or expires; frame callbacks paint positions and work fractions directly, reusing one projected position per character. They do not rebuild React content merely because the camera or character moves, or a new snapshot repeats unchanged health facts. Disclosure withdrawal and timeline changes clear retained feedback. [Review evidence](verification/embodied-feedback.md#implementation-review--october-4-2026) includes moving-frame comparisons and paused progress.

Ordinary 2D sprites face the camera in both yaw and pitch. Orbit/pitch must not expose an edge, select a different artwork view or foreshorten the image; ordinary perspective size, movement animation and lighting still apply. A screen-aligned card preserves its ground anchor and image proportions while an upright virtual body supplies depth and lighting coordinates. Picking uses the same virtual-body mapping and rejects hits outside the camera’s near/far depth interval, including when the displayed image plane itself intersects a valid ray. Physical collision and bodily heading do not rotate with the picture. Sprite shadows use the displayed silhouette in that same upright body, so they follow the artwork rather than an unrelated world-heading proxy. Transparent bottom padding is derived across an asset’s animation frames to keep visible contact on its support; side padding serves the same purpose for horizontal poses. Picking uses the resulting placement as well. This is a stylized depth approximation, not reconstructed hidden geometry; close perspective intersections need their own visual qualification. Opaque/cutout/dither depth and blended effects are separate material choices. Correct ordinary crate/character ordering must not depend on turning on read-through. Ghost art is remembered presentation only and never grants live picking or animation facts. Its reduced opacity is applied to every appearance part both when the scene updates and on subsequent animation frames, preserving each material’s authored transparency; solid meshes use the same ordered-dither fade as sprites. Remembered images cannot return to the appearance of current observations merely because rendering continues. An explicit removed-body state for the controlled actor disposes its image immediately, even without a new removal event; mere loss of sight still retains remembered imagery. Previously observed fire illumination is retained as a local environmental approximation until fresh observation updates it or the remembered source is evicted.

Network snapshots may straddle ramp/ground or ramp/deck seams. Interpolate the displayed foot along the relevant physical supporting planes and their common seam; do not immediately bind the interpolated point to the destination's underlying terrain. Airborne display interpolates XYZ. When missing intermediate topology makes interpolation unsafe, snap to the latest authorized state rather than invent motion through a floor. Smooth arbitrary-angle authoritative segments remain the goal; presentation interpolation does not replace swept collision.

Future long-gap smoothing should use authorized **recent past** motion segments, not expose another actor's future route. Turn smoothing is visual, must not change bodily heading/senses, and should avoid excessive inertia or repeated destination corrections. Model articulation, directional artwork, transform replacement and richer steering remain separate capabilities. Existing ground motion already uses an arrival tolerance, exact displacement limits and bounded replanning rather than chasing tiny alternating grid waypoints.

## Sprite lighting

Ordinary world imagery is lit by default, including a newly generated image without normal/depth maps. Ambient illumination, local light color and distance falloff must affect it. Only intentionally emissive effects (fire, glow), markers and appropriate UI use unlit materials. Do not treat a flat sprite as self-emissive just because it lacks geometry.

The current fallback supplies a forgiving rounded virtual normal field and wrapped diffuse response with two-sided material support. This gives a useful lighting response on simple pixel artwork; it is not inferred anatomy, correct hidden geometry, foliage self-occlusion or a guaranteed commercial game's visual fidelity. Camera-facing artwork and world heading remain separate; sprite shadow orientation follows the displayed artwork. Normal/depth maps, cylinder/trunk versus canopy profiles, foliage translucency, per-asset surface roughness and real meshes are progressive improvements, not requirements for basic illumination. Existing painted shadows may need art adjustment to avoid conflicting light directions.

Use PlayCanvas's materials, lights, shadow maps and camera post-processing rather than a second renderer. The current budget is one principal sun, ambient day/night shading, up to eight nearby currently observed or remembered fire lights in Detailed or two in Economy, each selected light shadow-casting, restrained bloom and ACES/color grading. Direct sunlight fades to zero at night; a cool ambient approximation preserves some surface detail without a hidden nighttime sun or a new moon simulation. Losing sight retains the last observed light and freezes the source sprite at its last observed animation frame, faded to remembered opacity. Observing an extinguished source revokes its light immediately; unseen changes remain unknown. This trades exact unseen fuel/light evolution for continuous local illumination without disclosing hidden source state. World, observer, access and timeline changes clear this memory. The earlier four-source Detailed budget is expanded after measured submission optimizations; all selected sources retain occlusion: selected illumination must not pass through walls or floors. A one-metre retention bias stabilizes the selected set through small distance crossovers. Unselected fire images remain emissive but do not illuminate neighboring surfaces. Every selected point light can require six shadow views. All selected sources share the existing atlas, trading additional draws and per-light map detail for consistent obstruction within the quality-dependent source count and unchanged atlas-size budgets. Emission and illumination are different; a bright image alone does not light its neighbors. Unsupported sources never create lights; remembered sources retain only their last permitted illumination. In Detailed quality, sunlight uses PlayCanvas PCSS with a small angular spread and the engine's PCF capability fallback. Clustered local lights use PCF5 and the shared shadow atlas; per-light resolution does not control their atlas allocation. Local softness is filtered, not distance-dependent area-light simulation. [VP01](limits/interface.md#vp01) owns the settings and budget tradeoffs.

The appearance target includes readable stylized 2.5D night scenes, torch-lit foliage and strong atmospheric contrast. This does not require ray tracing, ubiquitous dynamic GI or high-fidelity rigged models. Baked environment lighting may supplement stable scenery, but must not bake a constructed/destroyed object's permanent shadow into an otherwise editable world. Ambient occlusion, environment probes, tone/quality presets, reduced-motion behavior and antialiasing need visual/performance qualification. TAA is not enabled by assumption: it can smear pixel art or moving dither.

## Shadow quality

Settings offers Detailed (default) and Economy. Both modes use the same geometry, contact placement and day/night clock. Detailed lights up to eight nearby fires; Economy lights up to two. All selected sources cast shadows. The scene supplies its real sun description even when disabled; material copies retain it so nighttime startup can prepare sunrise, including instanced grass and reveal materials. Preparation uses the actual material/mesh/camera inputs and temporary cloned directional-light descriptions, without changing active lights or visible geometry. PlayCanvas owns the cached programs; weak membership deduplicates shared programs, and quality setters share the same filter definitions. This trades bounded setup/cache work for avoiding quality-switch and day/night first-use stalls. It adds no hidden draw or synchronous readiness wait. Immediate switching, previously unseen material configurations, initial loading and context restoration still need their own qualification. ## Sprite volume

Casting sprites use a shallow relief derived from image transparency behind their original front image. Distance from a transparent edge supplies depth: thin legs and small details stay shallower than broad bodies. Equal-depth faces are merged into one mesh, sampled on a bounded grid. This is automatic derived asset data; changing a supported image or animation frame supplies the matching geometry without per-artwork metadata. The front retains the full-resolution alpha texture and existing contact padding. Ground grass and emissive fire imagery stay non-casting.

The color pass projects that relief back onto the unchanged image plane, including in perspective, while lighting, depth and shadow projection use the upright body. Picking still addresses the front image. The see-through pass draws only the front plane: back faces must not pass its occluded-fragment depth test against the sprite itself. Shared meshes are cached by texture, retained for inactive animation frames, and released with their appearance/texture; landscape rebuild and scene disposal release their resources. There is no extra draw per limb or additional shadow-only body entity.

This replaces the zero-thickness sheet with a modest inferred volume so edge-on light retains a shadow. It is not an anatomical reconstruction, directional artwork or camera-independent 3D geometry. Its depth and shadow still follow the displayed image as the camera rotates; authored 3D assets remain the path to exact volume across viewpoints. Geometry is more expensive than two triangles, so the bounded inference, shared ownership and measured cost are deliberate constraints. No collision, support, heading or visibility authority comes from the relief.

## Sun path

Sun direction and daylight strength come from one pure evaluator of the public fractional world-clock hour. The existing directional light moves along a tilted circular orbit: sunrise on +X, noon toward +Z and sunset on −X. These are the presentation preset's east/south/west directions, independent of the camera. Ground shadows lengthen toward morning/evening and become shortest at noon; they remain projections onto the actual receiver geometry. The same daylight phase controls direct intensity and ambient balance, with no direct sun at night. Sun color and the existing shadow budget remain unchanged.

The current bundled-world preset has a fixed daily arc, not a calendar/latitude/season simulation. Its settings and rationale are in [VP01](limits/interface.md#vp01). The sun's direction continues smoothly below the horizon while disabled, avoiding rotation discontinuities at noon or midnight. The renderer applies each received clock value directly, so pause, speed, navigation preparation, reconnect and restored timelines have no independent client clock to drift. A time jump intentionally changes lighting immediately; no wall-clock extrapolation invents sunlight during a stalled connection.

The client evaluator owns only presentation. Other coherent worlds can have different skies; replace the localized preset through an authorized environment description when another environment actually needs one, rather than imposing this orbit on simulation, storage or art. No per-artwork metadata is needed.

## Continuous shadows

Use actual dynamic light projection onto rendered receiving geometry for terrain, ramps, decks and substantial meshes. Sprites cast their current alpha-tested artwork with its derived shallow relief from the same upright virtual body used for depth and picking. Thin legs, holes, facing flips and current animation/pose therefore follow the image automatically; no separate limb shapes or mandatory per-image/protocol/save metadata is needed. Meshes cast their own geometry. Sprite shadow sampling uses the upright sheet's normal biased toward each light, including backlighting, rather than letting a hidden solid proxy shade its own painted surface. Sprites still receive other objects' shadows.

A single-view sprite supplies approximate shallow volume, not reconstructed 3D anatomy. Its shadow follows the camera-facing image as the camera rotates; edge-on illumination uses the inferred depth. Matching camera-independent volume requires directional artwork or an authored mesh. This intentionally replaces the previous world-heading compound proxies, which could put legs away from the visible feet and cast a dark body patch onto the artwork. A footprint can cover ground and ramp at once because receivers are per rendered fragment, not one centroid-selected support. Do not move a whole blob between floor heights or double-darken contact with another equally strong blob.

A disconnected deck naturally intercepts part of a shadow and leaves other parts below; real geometric discontinuity is not a bug to erase by interpolating through empty space. Geometry-aware positioning and receiver geometry must nevertheless agree. Floor cutaways retain shadow casting, collision and acoustic effects; hiding the camera-facing roof must not open physical sunlight or sound through it. Each casting sprite uses one texture-owned relief mesh and its current material; bounds enclose both displayed and upright geometry, including depth. The normal appearance/component owner removes replaced casters and releases derived meshes with textures. Switching to a mesh uses that mesh's geometry directly. Both remembered sprites and remembered mesh objects stop casting live shadows. Fire and ground-grass decoration do not cast. Decorative plank seams share one non-casting mesh per surface instead of one box per seam. Non-casting grass and shrub decorations use shared GPU-instanced groups with immutable foot/size data and the same lit upright-body mapping; the GPU faces them toward the camera. One conservative group bound trades per-tuft culling for fewer draws and removes per-tuft camera transform writes. The landscape owner releases the instance buffer on rebuild/disposal; entity and canopy picking remain separate. These approximations change presentation cost, not physical geometry or sensing.

Compatible current sprite casters share GPU-instanced shadow draws within spatial cells. Each instance carries its exact world transform, foot and size; geometry, alpha and source material remain shared, while opacity and shadow masks must match. Membership, animation and transforms refresh after scene animation and before shadow culling each frame. Revoked/disabled casters leave immediately; single remaining objects return to individual casting, including after re-observation. Conservative bounds include every member. The visible image, picking and read-through retain their individual owners. No shadow-map reuse, delayed light updates or per-artwork metadata is introduced. The scene-owned batch helper releases its buffers and cloned materials when groups disappear, source assets are released or the scene is destroyed. Procedural resource assets share by their actual generator inputs, preserving the artwork while avoiding duplicate textures/reliefs. [VP01](limits/interface.md#vp01) owns the cell/allocation tradeoff; [measurements](verification/eight-fire-three-times.md) own the evidence.

A clipped multi-receiver blob/decal is a fallback for low-end quality or art that rejects silhouette casting, not an additional mandatory shadow engine. Qualify bias/acne, thin floors, foliage, low sun, lit backsides, many nearby receivers and point-light cost before expanding quality budgets.

## Boundaries and extension

The renderer consumes authorized plain views and presentation settings. Authored terrain definitions select supported ground/earth/liquid rendering styles and colors; the scene and operations overview read that same projected table instead of assigning meaning to bundled tile names. An unchanged terrain table does not force a rebuild when control is renewed. PlayCanvas classes, GPU handles and shaders stay in the client. Recast and Rapier do not solve lighting, alpha depth or this reveal policy. Current sprites, simple meshes, the restored animated mercenary and an attributed generated rigid-prop pilot coexist. Both rejected block mercenaries and the earlier sampled-model profile remain preserved studies. Arbitrary generated GLB loading, general skinning/normal generation and compatible representation upgrades remain SW-owned future work. The bundled model fences late loads and owns its own replacement resources. No image generation is needed for camera rotation or native movement.

Current facts are in [Architecture](architecture.md#spatial-world-foundation); current evidence is in [Recast integration](verification/recast-integration.md). The [maintainer tracker](maintainers/spatial-world.md#sw18--world-presentation-delivery) distinguishes implemented approximations from unqualified graphics behavior and future features.
