## Simulation and cognition audit

September 24, 2026 observations used Apple M1 Pro / Darwin arm64 / Node 22.23.2, the running PostgreSQL development game, a consistent read-only copy of its mature world, and disposable in-memory runtime scenarios. The user's world was neither reset nor populated with profiling fixtures. Other checkout edits and host load occurred during this work; only the matched snapshot comparisons below establish a local before/after result. [Bounded measurements](simulation-cognition-audit.json) retain scenario counts and timings; private world snapshots, complete prompts and CPU profiles remain outside the repository.

The initial live sample showed native-step p50 about 1 ms but outliers over 500 ms, with long tick/database/GC tails. A later 15-second CPU profile spent about 80% idle, about 562 ms in sampled garbage collection, and substantial active time in draft traversal and status/perception work. These sampled times overlap application spans and are not additive causal percentages. The profile does not establish that inference network latency itself blocks native simulation.

The cold mixed fixture (10 added people, 20 added animals, 300 objects; 344 total entities) took 1,723 ms for 180 native seconds before the sight-cache fix, p95 12.26 ms and maximum 386.98 ms: only 0.58× required native throughput at 3×. A 164-entity crowded fixture (50 added people and 100 added animals) after the fixes took 2,298 ms for 180 seconds, p95 18.79 ms, maximum 39.56 ms, with 14,975 awareness records. Its 1.31× native headroom at 1× leaves little room for persistence and cognition. Neither is a capacity pass. Dense acquisition and moving-observer costs remain PF09/EPR work; no events, sensory fidelity, clock debt or authoritative persistence were dropped to improve a score.

Two browser sampling intervals of approximately ten seconds captured 583 and 601 frames: frame p50 16.7 ms and p95 18.5 ms, scene-update p50 0.1 ms and p95 0.2 ms. The first included a 100 ms outlier; the second maximum was 18.7 ms. Temporary frame logging was removed afterward. A separate 15-second stream sample delivered 336 updates/307 KB, gap p50 50.63 ms, p95 61.57 ms and maximum 103.20 ms, with no gap over 250 ms. This is limited runtime observation, not GPU qualification or proof that all jumps are eliminated.

Inspection of the latest 1,000 stored stages found 17 rejected human-gesture components from Hare, one response with both speech and action in a single operation, many pause/reload cancellations, and two provider-uncertain Jev outcomes. The fixes bind gestures/speech to actual capabilities, reject malformed envelopes in the parsing stage before optional interpretation, and make current speech history identify its speaker, perceived recipient and overheard status. Trigger source and subject visibility are separate. Redundant references, empty planning/withdrawal instructions and long floating-point meter strings were reduced without omitting required evidence. Read-only provider inspection confirmed both Jev runs failed with only `outcome: uncertain` in their run events; no finer cause or billing conclusion is supported, and neither was retried.

Two completed live-provider scenarios used 34 entities, eight added people, twelve added animals and five animals explicitly granted cognition. Eight directed responses and three autonomous responses completed; a twice-attempted out-of-hearing interaction was rejected before inference. A human correctly recalled the previous question; deer and hare used permitted speech/actions without rejected human gestures. Autonomous bystanders explicitly acknowledged overhearing when joining the exchange. A native private-thought marker appeared in its owner's first context and in none of the other actors' inspected contexts. These are limited behavioral observations, not a general privacy or model-quality guarantee. SQLite supplied the fixture repository, so these runs do not qualify PostgreSQL vector retrieval or reflection/dream execution.
