# Do ordinary animal energy changes cause repeated expensive work?

October 16, 2026. **Completed diagnostic; rejected.**

Stopping ordinary animal movement saved 24.3% of isolated simulation CPU, but improved the full game by only 2.0%. Forty bodies continued to be updated. One hare still changed 217 times and lost 1.2 energy during 2400 game seconds. This experiment separates passive tiredness from movement and reactions.

| Version    | What animals do                                              | Energy and rest tradeoff                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before     | Ordinary wandering, flight, escape and territorial reactions | Existing passive fatigue and extra fatigue for effort.                                                                                                                                      |
| Diagnostic | Exactly the same available behaviors                         | Native-controlled animals lose no energy merely for being awake. Working, flying and fleeing still use the existing effort rule. Less ordinary fatigue may mean less energy-triggered rest. |

People retain their existing energy, food and rest rules. Direct harm and ongoing health conditions are unchanged. This is a diagnostic in private source copies, not an accepted gameplay change.

Both versions start from the actual current combined opening with 17 people,40 animals,100 added objects and 3 profiling sheds. They use the same already qualified newer temporary-edit library. Old saved worlds keep their own saved rules and are not modified to pretend this new tuning was installed. Finite resources, provider limits, current validation, complete activity/survival checks and exact owned cleanup stay in force.

First verify authored configuration generation, types, actual route worker, package bindings, current save/reload and positive fatigue cases. Then compare complete native activity and within-version repeatability. Only after those gates, measure four counterless fixed-work observations in before/after/after/before order. A mean CPU saving of at least 5% is the preselected gate for four actual fresh 30 second running-game observations. Native timing alone cannot establish responsive input, stable frames or sustained 3×.

**Decision:** reject this tuning. Removing the repeated energy writes does not remove enough shared simulation work to save CPU; the additional condition and changed scheduling may contribute, but this experiment does not isolate their individual costs. The preselected 5% CPU-saving gate fails, so do not run the conditional four full-game phases. No running-game improvement, long rest behavior, browser smoothness or 3× pass is claimed. No rule or dependency change is retained in canonical runtime code.
