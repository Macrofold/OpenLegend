### Remaining structural costs

1. **Retirement prepares the ceiling rather than the eviction.** `RetentionMetadata.capture` fetches up to 8,193 oldest headers per due actor, adds every result to the segment selection and unions aliases from those rows, pending writes, both resident snapshots and support capsules across actors. For an actor below that ceiling it can fetch the entire retained history for a small eviction. It also loads all selected actors' account/support and retained-version metadata. The later survivor query expands every matching page's alias array before filtering/counting. GIN indexes do not remove that expansion. Actual removal candidates and positive resident/support/account survivor proofs are determined later in `cleanupRetiredIndividuals`. The closure can repeat preparation up to four times; the failed round, actors, key cardinality, candidate pages, plan and PostgreSQL CPU/wait split are unknown.
2. **Grouped storage still feeds expanded readers and frequent publications.** Mutable current readers rebuild per-detail views and recheck body permissions; immutable weak caches cannot cover all draft reads. Every actual unchanged selected check still appends history metadata, advances a recording cursor and changes durable current data. Grouping removes repeated membership packing, not these updates or their publication/SQL work.
3. **Changed history repeatedly prepares accumulated metadata.** `encodeEvidenceOwner` reuses prior segments but unions their indexes, compares owner headers, scans bounds and serializes segment metadata again. New segments are JSON-serialized up to four times for exact byte accounting, then prepared payloads are serialized. A new immutable append version forks ten maps, two sets and an operation-order array; chunk rollover carries selected boundary state, and retirement clears recording cursors so later selected reception rebaselines. Shared values are charged again at referencing chunk boundaries. These mechanisms explain amplification; the available profile does not assign an exclusive cost to each.
