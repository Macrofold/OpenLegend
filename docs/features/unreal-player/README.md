# Open Legend’s Unreal desktop player

**Accepted feature target.** A player can look down from a wooded ridge, choose a river crossing, walk there, meet someone with a life of their own, and use what they learn to choose where to camp. A friend can reach the same crossing from the far side, hear an exchange without seeing who spoke, and later help change that place. Both can return to the consequences of what actually happened. A player who chooses solitude can find food, make shelter and leave a mark without joining the conversation. Neither a scripted tour nor a beautiful view from an unreachable camera fulfills this goal.

The installed Unreal Engine 5 game is the primary way people inhabit Open Legend’s hosted worlds on Windows and Apple Silicon Mac. The hosted Rust world decides what happens, what persists and what each character can perceive. The desktop player lets a person experience their character’s perspective and act through it. This specification describes the destination. Implementation status, conversion work and evidence from earlier clients belong to the [migration project](../../projects/unreal-engine-5-migration-feature-spec.md) and [delivery tracker](../../maintainers/unreal-engine-5-migration.md).

**Ordinary play boundary.** A player must be able to complete the supported first-playable journeys inside the installed game. That includes movement and discovery, bodily needs, useful work and tools, equipment, dangers and combat, speech and cooperation, invention where offered, constructing a home and returning. A player may decline an encounter, abandon a draft, decline a cost, or finish a session after a modest success. The character’s independent decision, chosen risk and actual resources determine the outcome. Starting an administrative tool, buying a model call, generating a bespoke mesh or switching to the retired player must never be a hidden prerequisite for those journeys. Specific world-authoring and operator tasks may have an explicit authorized browser handoff.
