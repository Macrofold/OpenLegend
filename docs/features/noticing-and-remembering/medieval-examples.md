# 56 medieval witness decisions

These examples specify what should happen when a world supports the activity shown. Read across each row from **what this NPC can know**, through **what the game can already do**, to **what would justify asking a model for this NPC's decision**. The final column gives a condition, not a promise that a request is sent. If an already chosen response settles the matter, the game carries it out under its usual preconditions. Otherwise a fresh choice can lead to a request once evidence, authority, capacity and funding pass. One choice can produce silence, refusal or several provider stages. Another character's private decision is never shared.

Throughout, an NPC may keep warranted personal evidence without a model call. That includes exact words actually heard and the identity of a person actually recognized. Optional evidence can expire. A later question addressed to an NPC is a new reason to answer from what remains. Scene descriptions below sometimes tell the reader a secret, such as who stole a seal. Only the **Evidence** column grants knowledge to a witness. Where the player controls a character, the player chooses their voluntary response.

## At the market

| Player situation                           | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                                                                      | Without a model call                                                                                                                                       | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Pay for bread with too few coins        | The baker can count the coins received against the agreed price. The customer behind you may hear an argument without seeing the coins.                            | Count the offered coins against the price. Complete the sale only if the agreed terms permit it.                                                           | An uncovered shortfall leaves the baker choosing whether to extend credit, ask for the rest, or decline the sale. The spectator owes no reaction.                                                                                                                                                     |
| 2. Return a purse anonymously              | The owner finds the purse. Someone who actually saw the handover may know who returned it.                                                                         | Restore possession and let each witness retain their own evidence. The owner cannot credit the player from the item's hidden history.                      | Mere rediscovery needs no call. If the owner had offered a reward and must now decide how to find the finder, or a witness is asked who returned it, there is a choice.                                                                                                                               |
| 3. Send a debt collector to the wrong Mara | A lender who knows the debtor can distinguish the two women when present. A collector carrying only the written name cannot tell them apart from that instruction. | Keep the two people distinct and apply any existing collection instructions. A matching name cannot settle the debt.                                       | A disputed identity leaves the collector choosing whether to ask for identification or return to the lender. The wrongly approached Mara can respond to the demand addressed to her.                                                                                                                  |
| 4. Sell a pot with a hidden crack          | At purchase the buyer sees an apparently sound pot. At supper they see the leak. Neither sight establishes what the seller knew.                                   | Apply the physical leak, preserve the timing, and continue any adequate cooking routine.                                                                   | If the leak spoils supper or the purchase becomes disputed, the buyer can choose a substitute, request a repair or complain. The invisible crack caused no decision at purchase.                                                                                                                      |
| 5. Challenge a merchant's short measure    | An inspector sees two quantities measured against a known standard. The crowd may only hear cheers.                                                                | Record the measured difference. Carry out a previously chosen inspection or settled procedure.                                                             | A disputed discrepancy requiring a judgment or remedy gives the inspector or affected trader a decision. Cheers cannot make a spectator an expert witness.                                                                                                                                            |
| 6. Arrange to collect a violin “tomorrow”  | The shopkeeper hears the customer's intention. The instrument and promised day are specific only if identified in the exchange.                                    | Keep the literal word “tomorrow,” any supported references and the original date. Complete an authorized handover when its conditions hold.                | A missed appointment, competing buyer or request for a different instrument can reopen the shopkeeper's choice. The passage of midnight alone does not mean collection happened.                                                                                                                      |
| 7. Display a stolen toll seal              | A traveler sees a familiar seal. A clerk who learned of the theft can recognize a conflict. Nearby people need not know either its owner or its history.           | Recognize the visible mark and continue a settled toll procedure where applicable. The stolen item's identity cannot silently tell the crowd it is stolen. | The informed clerk deciding whether to challenge the bearer, or a traveler personally faced with a disputed demand, has a reason to think.                                                                                                                                                            |
| 8. Bid at a crowded horse auction          | The auctioneer hears a valid bid. A competing bidder hears that it exceeds their own. Others hear a number.                                                        | Update the standing bid and follow already chosen bidding instructions within their limits.                                                                | An outbid buyer who still wants the horse and has discretion over the price can choose whether to bid again. A firm, previously chosen ceiling can settle it without a call. A disputed bid may require the auctioneer's judgment. Hearing “six silver” alone gives the audience no bidding decision. |

## In the workshop and household

| Player situation                                       | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                            | Without a model call                                                                                                                          | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 9. Stop the millwheel during grinding                  | The miller observes their work stop. A neighbor accustomed to the noise may notice quiet without knowing why.            | Stop the work that needs the wheel; record the blocked step. The neighbor can listen for the sound again.                                     | If the miller's task can no longer continue as planned, they may inspect, seek help or change work. Quiet alone gives the neighbor no diagnosis.                                                                                    |
| 10. Hide the baker's peel on another shelf             | The baker misses the move, then sees or feels that the peel is absent where they reach for it.                           | Keep the new physical location private until observed. The attempted retrieval fails and the loaves continue baking.                          | With bread due out and the planned tool missing, the baker faces a choice. They cannot accuse the player solely because the player moved it unwitnessed.                                                                            |
| 11. Copy a smith's movements from the doorway          | The apprentice can see the grip and some strokes. The metal's temperature and the smith's reasoning may be inaccessible. | Retain the visible demonstration; continue chosen practice within the apprentice's actual ability.                                            | An unsuccessful attempt, an invitation to try, or a direct question can warrant thought. Watching alone grants neither a complete learned method nor a call per stroke.                                                             |
| 12. Serve an old family dish without announcing it     | A guest can receive its taste and smell. Recognition depends on their own established experience.                        | Serve and eat the food; a supported familiar cue can draw attention. A recipe label cannot reveal the guest's childhood or impose an emotion. | A recognized cue that meets an existing personal condition requiring a choice can prompt the guest. Otherwise its private meaning may emerge in a later conversation; merely tasting it buys no call.                               |
| 13. Show an apprentice a cloak with a strained seam    | A tailor examining the garment may see the strain. The wearer may see only the attractive finish.                        | Apply the inspection and retain the visible defect at the precision actually achieved. Expertise cannot reveal hidden damage.                 | A tailor asked to approve the work must decide whether to accept it, repair it or explain the risk. An unseen seam gives the wearer no such decision.                                                                               |
| 14. Take over kneading when the cook hurts their wrist | The cook feels pain. A helper may see the grip fail and recognize that the dough needs attention.                        | Apply the bodily effect. Continue accepted backup work or receive an offer without inventing a diagnosis or consent.                          | An unassigned helper who sees an open need and can take over may decide to offer. The cook can decide whether to accept if they retain that choice.                                                                                 |
| 15. Deliver pie to every household and get turned away | The giver hears an actual refusal at one door. The household's reasons remain private unless expressed.                  | Record that this offer failed; keep the slice and continue any accepted plan for other houses.                                                | A recipient asked to accept the gift may choose to refuse. Once refused, the giver needs a decision only if their existing plan leaves what to do next unresolved. Do not infer illness, dislike or hostility from the closed door. |
| 16. Save a seat for a friend at a packed supper        | The host knows why the chair is empty. An arrival sees an available-looking chair.                                       | Keep the host's intention and any already chosen arrangement. An empty chair needs no recurring thought.                                      | When someone reaches for the seat, conflicting plans can make the host decide whether to speak, yield it or find another. The newcomer need not know why.                                                                           |

## At the gate and on watch

| Player situation                                           | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                                 | Without a model call                                                                                                              | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 17. Walk up to a cautious guard outside the walls          | The guard may see a body getting closer, at the accuracy supported outdoors. The guard cannot see hostile intent.             | Notice the supported approach and execute any previously chosen valid response. Ordinary steps do not each restart the condition. | An unresolved close approach relevant to the guard or their post can warrant warning, making room, or holding position. Caution shapes that choice without extending sight.                     |
| 18. Follow a patrol beyond a hedgerow                      | A scout sees stretches of the route with gaps. Familiarity with the usual patrol does not establish today's hidden movements. | Retain the actually followed detail and the gaps; continue the watch while possible. No permanent story per tree.                 | A genuinely lost trail that blocks the task, a relevant deviation actually seen, or a request for a report can require a decision. Each reappearance alone does not.                            |
| 19. Wear the captain's coat in fog                         | A sentry can recognize the coat while failing to recognize the face. Their later recollection may contain a suspicion.        | Keep the clothing observation separate from personal identification. Apply a settled credential check if one exists.              | Admission that requires identifying the visitor gives the sentry a choice about asking for proof or seeking help. The coat alone cannot confirm the captain was there.                          |
| 20. Call the gatekeeper's name as a cart rattles past      | The gatekeeper may hear their name and miss “leave the gate open.”                                                            | Turn toward the voice or listen more closely if supported. Leave the unheard instruction unrecorded and unexecuted.               | A clearly received request, or unmistakable direct address that gives them reason to ask for repetition, can warrant a decision. Hearing only their name in passing cannot establish the order. |
| 21. Have a companion drop a basket to distract a sentry    | The sentry can see or hear the fall and may lose coverage elsewhere. They lack the conspirators' private plan.                | Shift warranted attention, apply the actual obstruction and keep the resulting gaps. Nobody gains automatic concealment.          | The sentry may choose to help if they perceive a practical need, or reconsider the watch if real evidence compromises the post. The unobserved coordination triggers neither.                   |
| 22. Raise a hand toward a friend standing before a guard   | The friend may recognize a greeting clearly directed at them. The guard may see only a raised hand.                           | Notice the gesture; a previously agreed signal can produce its chosen response.                                                   | An intelligible personal greeting with an open response gives the friend a decision. The guard needs some separate reason; mere alignment does not make the guard the addressee.                |
| 23. Fail to relieve a watch as promised                    | The waiting guard knows the appointment and, with adequate coverage, that relief hasn't arrived. They do not know why.        | Keep the agreed deadline, establish the missed handover and continue any chosen fallback.                                         | When the missed appointment leaves a choice between staying, finding relief and reporting the gap, the guard can think. It supplies no proof of desertion.                                      |
| 24. Agree that two lantern flashes mean the ferry is ready | A lookout who accepted the convention can count two distinguishable flashes if coverage holds. A stranger sees light.         | Resolve the chosen signal and execute its agreed feasible step. A hidden or missed flash cannot complete the sequence.            | An ambiguous signal that leaves the lookout's task blocked, or a confirmed signal requiring a fresh choice, can prompt them. The stranger gets no ferry knowledge or call from the code alone.  |

## At the fair, inn and shrine

| Player situation                                           | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                                                                     | Without a model call                                                                                                                            | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 25. Sing a satire about a tax collector in their hearing   | The collector may hear their own known name, and perhaps enough of the verse to realize it concerns them. Merely hearing the name does not reveal the joke.       | Orient toward the song and retain the words actually understood. The audience gets no private measure of offense.                               | Clear personal reference plus enough received content for a response can give the collector a decision. Whether the song is insulting, funny or harmless is for that decision.                  |
| 26. Miss an answering drumbeat during a familiar dance     | A partner monitoring the familiar sequence can perceive the expected beat's absence while able to hear. A late arrival has no baseline.                           | Mark the unmet cue and follow any chosen musical recovery. Noise that masked the interval cannot prove a missed beat.                           | An unexpected omission that leaves the partner choosing how to continue can warrant thought. A rehearsed recovery needs none.                                                                   |
| 27. Stay silent during applause                            | A performer who could see the guest’s hands through the applause may notice they never clap. A glance at their face cannot establish that omission or its motive. | Continue the performance and preserve any actually noticed outward behavior. No dislike or political allegiance is assigned.                    | If the performer was awaiting that guest's response or must decide whether to change course, the supported omission can matter. One silent face alone does not require a call.                  |
| 28. Ask a fiddler for a song your mother sang              | The fiddler hears the request and may recognize the tune. Other listeners hear the performance, with no automatic knowledge of its private history.               | Carry out an already accepted request for a known, playable piece. Do not grant an unknown tune or share the requester's personal association.  | The addressed fiddler may choose whether to play, ask which song was meant or decline. The other listeners need an independent reason to think.                                                 |
| 29. Give a bride a brooch a guest knows as family property | The guest recognizes this particular brooch. They see a gift; they may know nothing about inheritance, borrowing or sale.                                         | Bind the observation to the recognized item and retain the witnessed transfer.                                                                  | If the sight contradicts what the guest has reason to believe about the item's custody and leaves them choosing whether to inquire, offer a decision. Recognition alone cannot establish theft. |
| 30. Leave flowers at an unmarked shrine                    | The groundskeeper later sees fresh flowers. A companion who witnessed the act can identify the giver. Neither automatically knows whom they mourn.                | Keep the two accounts distinct. Follow normal shrine care without inventing an inscription or a mourner's motive.                               | No call is due for the flowers alone. A request to identify the visitor, or an actual conflict with a chosen shrine duty, gives the relevant NPC a choice.                                      |
| 31. Wear a jester's bright cap without performing          | A witness with a structured fear may recognize the attire. An unrecognized resemblance and a performer's concealed profession supply no match.                    | Apply the supported fear response once on recognition; subsequent glances during the same exposure need no new call.                            | Offer a decision if that felt response leaves a real choice about current work, company or safety. The feeling can also run its course without deliberation.                                    |
| 32. Stage a play in which the king dies                    | A seated viewer has the theatrical context. A latecomer outside may hear only “The king is dead!”                                                                 | Retain what each person heard and its source. Keep a line performed onstage distinct from confirmed news; preserve the latecomer's uncertainty. | If the report bears on an actual choice, such as whether a messenger must verify it before departing, the hearer can think. Other viewers need no decision or compulsory rumor.                 |

## Emergencies and unsolicited help

| Player situation                                        | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                            | Without a model call                                                                                                                                 | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 33. Fall from a ferry without crying out                | A passenger sees someone struggling and knows a rope is within reach. The ferrymaster below deck may hear only a splash. | Apply the fall, notice the disturbance and carry out any already chosen rescue step. Hearing a splash does not identify a casualty.                  | The unassigned passenger has an unresolved choice about offering the rope, summoning help or protecting someone beside them. No relationship or direct request is required.                                                                                     |
| 34. Shout “Somebody fetch the healer!”                  | A runner understands the open appeal and knows a way to reach the healer. Someone farther away may hear only a shout.    | Draw attention to the sound. Continue an accepted errand if it covers this need. Neither listener learns that another runner has volunteered unseen. | A listener who can practically fetch or summon help can choose to offer. Two runners can both start. A listener with no relevant means owes no reply just for hearing the words.                                                                                |
| 35. Catch a stranger's rolling cart                     | The owner sees a hand on the cart and might have missed it rolling. A second witness may see both.                       | Resolve the supported physical stop and record each viewpoint. No automatic gratitude, suspicion or accusation.                                      | An NPC who sees the runaway cart within reach may choose to help. If it rolls out of reach during deliberation, they cannot catch it retroactively. After the player catches it, an owner who sees an unexplained interference may decide to ask what happened. |
| 36. Ring a flood bell as a prank                        | Locals who know the signal hear a flood alarm. The bell supplies no direct sight of water or proof of who rang it.       | Carry out existing alarm responses and maintain the distinction between signal and flood. Repeated ringing need not restart an unchanged response.   | Someone with an unresolved safety or duty choice can decide how to respond. Learning that it was a prank is a later, separate matter requiring evidence.                                                                                                        |
| 37. Arrive from another village during the alarm        | To a newcomer, the bell may have no known meaning. Locals can see the newcomer and hear the same bell.                   | The newcomer can orient and watch people move uphill. Locals can continue their own accepted responses.                                              | A newcomer whose route is disrupted may ask what is happening. A local who recognizes the newcomer's need and can explain may choose to help. The bell cannot insert local knowledge into a stranger's memory.                                                  |
| 38. Offer to help carry an injured rival                | The rival receives an offer; visible impairment may be apparent. Private medical details and consent are not visible.    | Apply injury and receive the offer. Carry out an already accepted, physically supported joint step only while its prerequisites hold.                | A conscious rival with an open choice can accept, refuse or ask for different help. A helper can decide to offer unsolicited aid. Inability to respond cannot be counted as consent.                                                                            |
| 39. Watch a rescue begin, then lose sight behind a boat | A witness sees the rescuer reach the casualty, then loses coverage. They lack evidence of the outcome.                   | Retain the start and the gap. Continue a chosen lookout or errand without marking the casualty safe.                                                 | A witness with a practical contribution and a need still appearing open can decide whether to summon more help. A clear later sight of adequate help may settle that decision.                                                                                  |
| 40. Bring a bucket after a fire goes out                | One helper sees that the flames are out; another approaches the hidden side and still believes water is needed.          | End a conditional bucket task once its completion condition is personally met. The other helper can keep carrying water.                             | Only a remaining choice, such as a newly discovered need or what to do with an abandoned duty, warrants thought. The fire going out cannot privately inform every helper at once.                                                                               |

## In the woods and on the river

| Player situation                                             | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                                         | Without a model call                                                                                                                               | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 41. Ask “Can anyone take me across?” at the landing          | Ferrymen who understand the appeal know their own boats and availability. People unloading grain may hear it equally clearly.         | Receive the public request and keep doing unrelated work. An accepted ferry service may already cover it.                                          | A capable, available ferryman can choose to offer passage or terms. A competing ferryman can also offer or withdraw on witnessing acceptance. No refusal is due from everyone else. |
| 42. Point at startled deer and claim wolves are coming       | A hunter may see deer flee and hear the player's explanation. Neither identifies wolves.                                              | The deer follow their own supported reactions. Preserve the hunter's direct sight and the player's testimony separately.                           | If the warning changes the hunter's actual route or protective duty, they may investigate, ask for evidence or proceed. Flight alone requires no automatic wolf decision.           |
| 43. Ask two hunters whether they saw the same bear           | One saw a bear; the other recognized a familiar scarred individual. Only the latter has the individual association.                   | Keep the first observation generic and the second specific. Do not merge two sightings on species alone.                                           | Each hunter actually addressed can decide how to answer from their evidence, including “I couldn't tell.” The earlier sightings need not have caused either to think.               |
| 44. Sell a map with a ford marked in the wrong spot          | A buyer sees a marked crossing, follows it and finds deep water there. They have evidence against the map, not the seller's intent.   | Preserve the map's claim and the observed water separately. A route that actually requires a ford can become blocked.                              | Failure of the route leaves the buyer choosing a detour, verification or a complaint. Someone who merely handled the map has no such reason.                                        |
| 45. Send a guide ahead, then take another path               | The guide saw the player at the fork and later finds the companion missing. The private destination is unavailable.                   | Retain the last supported sighting and continue an agreed rendezvous plan if one covers separation.                                                | An unresolved loss that compromises the guide's undertaking gives them a choice about waiting, searching or seeking help. No hidden tracking resumes after the fork.                |
| 46. Repair a bridge above someone's home                     | A traveler sees a crossing needing work. The occupant hears work overhead and may see debris or another actual effect on their home.  | Apply material changes, sound and warranted attention. The repairer need not know someone lives below; noise alone does not identify the repairer. | Interference with the occupant's use or safety can prompt investigation or a request to stop. A repairer who learns about that conflict can reconsider their work.                  |
| 47. Carry a sealed parcel through three villages             | Witnesses may recognize a carrier, seal or wrapping. Seeing the outside cannot reveal the contents.                                   | Preserve those separate observations. Opening the parcel later gives new evidence only to those who acquire it then.                               | Passing a sealed parcel warrants no general call. A guard with an applicable inspection duty facing a disputed seal, or a witness later asked what they saw, can decide.            |
| 48. Bring home an apprentice's odd clamp instead of treasure | A millwright can test the clamp and observe its performance. A spectator who saw the apprentice praised has evidence of praise alone. | Execute an accepted test and record the actual result. Applause cannot teach a mechanism or a craft method.                                        | If the result leaves the millwright choosing whether to adopt it, commission another or question the apprentice, offer a decision. A spectator needs a separate reason.             |

## At court and in village politics

| Player situation                                                | Evidence available to the NPC                                                                                                                         | Without a model call                                                                                                                                                  | What would trigger a model decision                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 49. Hold a vote on repairing the ford (deferred)                | An eligible voter understands that an individual answer is being solicited and knows whether they have voted. A visitor can hear the same question.   | Register each actual permitted vote once. An absent answer is neither assent nor abstention.                                                                          | Each eligible, informed NPC still facing their own ballot has a decision. The first few votes cannot settle the rest. A visitor has no voting decision.                                                                                                                   |
| 50. Ask the same assembly for one cart to haul stone            | Members hear a request for a contribution. Some know they can supply an available cart; others cannot.                                                | Receive offers and record an actual agreement once. A witness who sees sufficient help accepted may continue their work.                                              | An available supplier can choose whether to volunteer or set terms. Several suppliers can respond independently; the rest of the assembly owes no individual answer.                                                                                                      |
| 51. Swear a public oath while thunder masks the deadline        | A witness may hear the promise but miss the date. The speaker and another listener may have exact words.                                              | Preserve each listener's own wording and gaps, and any separately protected terms they actually know.                                                                 | A participant who needs the missing term to accept or perform can ask for clarification. Later testimony can prompt a decision, but cannot restore a date the witness never heard.                                                                                        |
| 52. Translate an envoy's offer inaccurately                     | A bilingual listener can compare the two versions they heard. Someone who understood only the translation has the translated claim.                   | Keep the versions and their sources distinct. A supported discrepancy does not establish deceit.                                                                      | A bilingual negotiator facing conflicting terms can challenge or clarify. A recipient of just the translation may respond to those terms without gaining the original meaning.                                                                                            |
| 53. Accuse Oren of theft while addressing a customer            | At the noisy forge Oren might hear “money,” just his own name, or the full words “Oren stole the toll money.” Two people called Oren might both turn. | Keep the actual words and addressing evidence. A recognized name can draw attention; an ambiguous name cannot identify the accused with certainty.                    | A recognizable self-reference and enough personal content to respond can give Oren a decision despite his not being addressed. An actual stake in resolving ambiguity can justify clarification. A stray name or “money” alone buys no call.                              |
| 54. Bring three witnesses who heard the same ferryman's story   | Each can report what the ferryman said. A questioner can recognize a common source to the extent the accounts actually disclose it.                   | Preserve attribution; three retellings do not become three firsthand sightings. Hidden shared provenance stays hidden.                                                | When deciding a dispute, the revealed common source can give the questioner reason to seek the ferryman. The witnesses can answer direct questions without inventing personal knowledge of the crossing.                                                                  |
| 55. Publicly correct yesterday's false accusation               | Those present receive the correction. Someone upriver may still know only the original accusation.                                                    | Keep the correction as a later attributed event and preserve the earlier chronology. An authorized privacy withdrawal separately removes access to affected material. | Someone whose accepted belief, undertaking or pending accusation now conflicts with the correction can reconsider. An absent NPC gets no decision until evidence reaches them.                                                                                            |
| 56. Unveil a plaque naming the ferryman as sole hero of a flood | The ferryman, washerwomen and soldiers have different firsthand accounts. A child reading the inscription years later knows what the plaque claims.   | Preserve attributed text and each witness's own evidence and identity. The plaque grants no one the missing rescue history.                                           | A participant who recognizes their own involvement and the public contradiction can choose to protest, tell someone privately or let it stand. A player questioning other witnesses can uncover the omission; no village-wide belief update or compulsory protest occurs. |
