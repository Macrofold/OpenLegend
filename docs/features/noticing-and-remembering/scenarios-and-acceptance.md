# Scenarios and acceptance

Use these journeys to judge whether a player can surprise people, read a situation, ask a useful witness or exploit a real blind spot. A good outcome does not require a particular line of dialogue or emotion. The character's reason, available evidence and action must fit what happened.

## The jester and fifty witnesses

A costumed performer emerges, flips and raises a middle finger. Fifty capable NPCs receive the gesture in the principal example. Give it a shared importance high enough to attract notice. No one has a separate urgent need or unused discovery opportunity in this example. The following groups are disjoint so their counts add up.

| Witnesses | What they can establish                                                                                                                       | Expected outcome                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|         1 | The intended recipient can tell the gesture was directed at them.                                                                             | Offer a personal decision. A joke, offense, a question or choosing silence are all possible.                                                                                                                                            |
|         1 | A guard undertaking a watch recognizes the performer they were asked to find. Their instruction requires choosing what to do on finding them. | Offer one guard decision. Changing the instruction to “record the time I see them” can satisfy it with a record and no new deliberate choice.                                                                                           |
|         1 | Someone with a structured fear of clown attire recognizes the appearance.                                                                     | Apply the supported fear on first recognition, which can precede the flip. If the person can continue their chosen course, no immediate decision is due. If the fear presents an unresolved choice about that course, offer one.        |
|        47 | Each witnesses the gesture without an individual reason for an immediate decision.                                                            | Update evidence and applicable attention. No immediate paid interpretation solely for this event. Actual signs of noticing can be visible to other characters within their own senses. Nobody is assigned a private opinion by default. |

The performance itself gives **two immediate decision opportunities, or three if fear leaves a choice open**. Count from each person's first qualifying observation. If a frightened witness later visibly stumbles, someone able to help may face a new choice. The private fear alone gives onlookers no such evidence. Decisions, actual provider requests, applied actions and visible responses are different counts. An NPC can have several reasons at once without duplicating a response. A personalized decision may use several paid stages; refusal before dispatch may use none.

Vary the scene:

- Put a fence between the guard and the performer's face, or a tree between the recipient and the gesture. Seeing the costume does not prove identity or who was insulted. The dependent decision disappears unless other permitted evidence independently establishes it.
- Bring back the same jester after a brief occlusion, and repeat the same flip during one continuing performance. No artificial clearance, new discovery allowance or series of paid reactions. A genuinely new address, newly supported identity, worsened danger or actual new choice can still matter.
- Have all fifty deliberately watch the show. More can later describe the flip accurately. Watching does not give each person a new decision at each move. Give all fifty a real, individually received request for a choice and all fifty may qualify. If resources only serve some in time, the others were unserved; they did not agree, refuse or deliberately remain silent.
- Let one witness have only a free-form childhood memory of clowns. A later independently warranted turn may connect the memory and the costume. An immediate visceral response is not promised without a structured trigger. Conversely, a costumed stranger can trigger the structured fear; a performer known privately to be a jester in ordinary clothes cannot trigger it solely through that hidden fact.
- Put observers at different distances and angles. Some see the hand; others see only a person tumble. Others may hear a crowd react without seeing why. Hearing real laughter can become new evidence. No second wave of paid thoughts or laughter is owed simply because other witnesses reacted. A transmitted account remains testimony.

## Evidence needed for acceptance

The [56 medieval examples](medieval-examples.md) specify behavior where an activity exists. Qualify the witness rules through supported activities, using contrasts that change one relevant fact at a time.

- **A close pass outdoors.** Try walking steadily toward the guard, running across the approach between two looks, and stepping from behind a corner already close. First sight can establish nearness without establishing previous motion. A missed pass stays missed. Check sight, the closing cue, the guard’s choice and later recall separately. Repeat across an opaque wall. Record whether a player could exploit a visible pass to bypass an alert guard, and distinguish a missed observation from an NPC who saw the approach and chose to hold position.
- **Unsolicited help.** Compare reaching a rope after seeing a passenger struggle with hearing only a splash. Include an everyday courtesy such as holding a gate. If two helpers set off, let only one see the need resolved.
- **Direct conversation and public help.** Tell a cart owner “I need a way to haul this grain.” Let the owner offer terms or decline. If they promise the cart for tomorrow, ask after saving and returning what was promised. A promise alone supplies no evidence that the cart was delivered. Then explicitly ask the market for transport. Two available suppliers can make independent offers, while other listeners can carry on. Repeat with the request masked.
- **Address and overhearing.** At the same noisy forge, Oren hears a direct call with every word masked, overhears his name alone, then overhears a complete allegation about him. The direct call allows a clarification request. The overheard name can draw attention. The full allegation can give Oren a choice about answering. None requires him to speak. An undetected call grants none. Two listeners called Oren can both orient without learning the speaker's private intent.

Start with an ordinary witness, a direct conversation and an explicit request for help. Then verify a watch and a personal reaction, recall after consolidation and return, and finally the crowded and subtle cases.

For scale, fifty witnesses receiving twenty different observable action sequences per minute would create a thousand witness/sequence pairs per minute. This is arithmetic, not a prediction of latency or model demand. Count once-per-world occurrences, actual per-witness sensing, matched personal conditions, actual funded decisions and downstream requests separately. Check that sharing a public performance never shares a private interpretation.

## Questions for human playtests

Mike can use these questions in a [separate playtest](../../../AGENTS.md#completion-and-handoff--every-task) to learn whether NPCs feel observant, surprising and worth talking to. Let players watch a performance and question witnesses without telling them who should react. Can they tell when someone missed a cue, is focused on work or is trying to carry on despite fear? Does the crowd just look inactive? In the market, see whether players expect help after an indirect remark and what they do when no one offers it. Use those observations to inform the priority of indirect requests.
