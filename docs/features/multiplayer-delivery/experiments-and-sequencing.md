# Multiplayer delivery: experiments and sequencing

Part of [Multiplayer updates and recovery](README.md). [Behavior and scenarios](behavior.md) is required. The optional candidates below are **experiments**, not adopted policies, performance claims or prerequisites for a playable game. Implementation and evidence status live in [MP20–MP24](../../maintainers/multiplayer.md#multiplayer-delivery-and-recovery).

## Foundation — now or soon

The foundation consists of behavior whose value does not depend on winning a performance comparison: truthful information, private audiences, coherent outcomes, recoverable views and bounded interference from a slow recipient. It should be secured while the first playable scene is small because later interactions depend on it. Reuse established owners and valid evidence; implement only identified gaps. A clearer contract does not imply that its protections are missing from the engine.

“Now or soon” is a sequencing decision, not a claim that all listed behavior must be rebuilt before another playable encounter. Fix a demonstrated breach of a required foundation before depending on it. Otherwise keep developing the smallest coherent, enjoyable scene. Use that scene to learn where delivery is actually costly or confusing.

### What the first playable scene should establish

Players can follow another character, act, understand supported speech and outcomes, and return after interruption. Different observers receive different legitimate knowledge. A slow reader does not prevent a healthy reader from following the scene. Visibility and access changes remain truthful even when nothing moves. Existing unsupported interactions do not have to be added merely to qualify delivery.

These outcomes can be covered by a small connected journey using [the scenario matrix](behavior.md#10-scenario-and-acceptance-matrix). Reuse checks already establishing the same outcome. A matrix row is a requirement, not an instruction to build a separate test, benchmark, diagnostic interface or framework.

## Conditional experiments — usually after playable interaction

A candidate becomes worth trying when a representative supported interaction reveals a concrete cost or player experience problem that it could address. A small-scene defect can justify an earlier bounded experiment; a future large population alone does not. Passing one experiment does not select the other candidates or establish a larger supported workload.

| ID    | Candidate                                                 | Trigger                                                                                                                                    | Principal possible benefit                                                                                   | Principal tradeoff                                                                                           | Initial disposition                                                                                                  |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| MDX01 | Vary permitted presentation frequency, detail or priority | Measured delivery demand or visible congestion harms a supported interaction while some presentation is genuinely optional or less urgent. | Preserve responsiveness where players are actively interacting while reducing unnecessary presentation work. | Older views, pop-in, misunderstood motion, starvation or unfair advantage if policy is poorly chosen.        | Conditional; foundation first, ordinarily after an enjoyable connected encounter.                                    |
| MDX02 | Reuse exactly equivalent permitted results                | Repeated eligible work is a material cost in an actual multi-recipient scene.                                                              | Reduce repeated work without changing anyone's information.                                                  | Extra retained state, eligibility work, invalidation cost and accidental sharing across different audiences. | Conditional; require evidence of repetition and a new hypothesis beyond previously rejected or withdrawn candidates. |
| MDX03 | Use a smaller lossless representation                     | Measured transfer or representation cost materially affects a supported interaction.                                                       | Reduce transfer size or processing demand while preserving meaning.                                          | Recipient processing, reference/lifecycle mistakes, reduced inspectability or savings too small to matter.   | Conditional; do not replace an established representation merely because a different format is compact.              |
| MDX04 | Compress or group eligible outgoing information           | Measured byte cost or repeated small deliveries are significant and a bounded reduction could improve the encounter.                       | Reduce transferred bytes or repeated delivery overhead.                                                      | Added waiting, preparation work, temporary memory, decoding cost and larger recovery units.                  | Conditional; test compression and grouping separately before attributing gains to a combination.                     |

### MDX01 — permitted frequency, detail and priority

The experiment changes how already permitted information is presented. It does not change sensing, retained evidence, authoritative mechanics, cognition admission, social resolution, world time or participation policy. Required lifecycle and distinct-outcome semantics remain mandatory. Slower updates cannot keep an unsupported observation labeled current.

Before trying a policy, identify the affected surface and information category, what may be omitted or delayed, what always remains required, how the player's current interaction affects importance, and the permitted age/delay under the supported workload. Identify the units, real or game clock, and whether a measured age refers to acquisition of the fact, its selected publication point or delivery waiting time; these are different quantities. Define the behavior when importance begins or ends, an object crosses a boundary, a stationary fact expires, access changes, or the selected bounds cannot be met. No unspecified delay or silent fallback is admitted.

Distance can be one world-appropriate input, but is neither universal permission nor a sufficient measure of importance. Following a companion, speaking to someone, aiming at a supported target or inspecting an object can require different presentation from an unrelated visible background object. Such priority does not grant a new sense, range, model decision or action capability. A radio conversation or remote view follows its own supported world's rules.

Optional stabilization near a presentation boundary may avoid distracting oscillation. It can stabilize only the optional presentation policy. It cannot prolong a viewing right, postpone required loss of current sensory support, or suppress an entry/exit or removal required by the perception/lifecycle owner at the applicable publication point. It does not require detecting unmeasured sensory opportunities or transmitting every superseded transient view. Evaluate frequency, detail, priority and boundary stabilization as distinguishable policy choices rather than assuming one combined “distance level” is beneficial.

Acceptance includes ordinary movement, deliberate following, supported conversation, recognition changes, sight loss, stationary invalidation, interruption and competing admitted work. Players must still understand the scene and use supported controls. A lower average cost fails if a recipient receives stale private information, required work starves, or the change makes an ordinary encounter materially worse within its claimed workload.

Reducing movement evidence actually noticed or retained is a separate conditional design under [noticing and remembering](../noticing-and-remembering/what-a-witness-can-remember.md) and PS04-A. MDX01 does not authorize that reduction. Similarly, crowd summaries must originate in supported perception; delivery cannot examine forbidden individuals and invent an apparently permissible aggregate.

### MDX02 — reuse of equivalent permitted results

The candidate may reuse only a result or part that has exactly the same permitted meaning for its recipients. Equivalence covers every dependency relevant to that part, including content, scoped references, time, lifecycle, uncertainty and evidence restrictions. A change dependent on a prior view requires a matching delivery basis; independent preparation or self-contained content need not. Equivalence of surroundings, position, character identity or world revision alone is insufficient. Each recipient still requires current eligibility at release and independent progress/recovery.

The comparison must include scenes with genuinely identical permitted information and scenes with different names, recognized people, private knowledge, visibility or prior views. Access changes, correction, forgetting, restore and disconnect must not leave reused information applicable to the wrong recipient or context. Partial reuse is eligible only where the shared part remains equivalent independently of the differing parts.

The benefit must survive the work of establishing equivalence, retaining results and responding to change. Count peak and retained resources as well as completed work. A reduction in one repeated operation that increases total encounter cost is not a pass. Previous Open Legend dispositions and the live Horizon example are recorded in [the research dossier](../../../research/game-engines/horizon-server.md); a new trial must explain its changed evidence or hypothesis rather than treating a renamed candidate as untried.

This experiment does not require grouping all nearby players, keeping a long-lived shared cache or giving a viewer another person's baseline. Those are possible engineering decisions to evaluate only in a later technical design.

### MDX03 — smaller lossless representation

Every selected fact must retain its meaning, identity, applicable time, ordering, precision and omission/removal semantics. The same recipient must be able to reconstruct the same permitted result. A reference remains scoped and lifetime-bound regardless of its size. Smaller identifiers do not permit guessing another person's identity or looking up information outside the view.

Define the supported range of input values and sizes, recipient handling requirements and the honest outcome for invalid, unavailable or unsupported input. Do not silently truncate, round away required precision, reinterpret an unknown value or apply a representation to the wrong context. Exhausting a finite reference or ordering space must establish a valid replacement context or refuse further unsupported use; repeated values cannot make old information current again. A deliberate loss of optional visual precision belongs to MDX01 and must not be counted as a lossless result.

The candidate must preserve first entry, reset, removal, distinct outcomes and recovery, including inputs outside a small showcase scene. Measure the complete recipient experience and processing cost, not just a prepared example's size. Existing opaque player references remain an established owner to reuse; this experiment is not a prerequisite for introducing them.

Current-format clients and servers may change together under the project's development policy. This feature creates no compatibility matrix or legacy-format requirement. Where a current participant cannot use the selected representation, follow the declared supported-format refusal/recovery behavior; do not misinterpret the data or expose a more privileged fallback.

### MDX04 — compression and bounded grouping

Compression must be lossless for required information. Grouping may combine only eligible information without changing its audience, basis, chronology or lifecycle. Required outcomes remain distinct even when delivered together. A combined delivery must not accidentally place one recipient's private data in another's view.

Before the trial, declare the affected traffic, maximum permitted added waiting in real elapsed time, size/retention bounds and units, what triggers earlier release, and what happens when an individual item or combined result exceeds the selected allowance. A low-volume recipient must not wait indefinitely for a group to fill. A reset, revocation or changed context invalidates ineligible pending information rather than forcing its old group to complete.

The policy must distinguish preparation cost, waiting time, transfer savings, recipient processing and recovery. Include small and larger legitimate payloads, quiet recipients, active encounters and a stalled reader; compression or grouping may help one and hurt another. The rejected or non-beneficial scope stays on the established policy. There is no requirement to compress everything, wait for a universal interval or keep expanding a group until it fits.

## Common experiment and adoption requirements

Each experiment follows [performance measurement](../../performance/measurement.md) and the existing verification/spending rules. Listing a candidate authorizes neither execution, a paid run, a new automated test nor a transport rewrite.

Before execution, the experiment owner records:

1. **Question and trigger.** The observed problem, affected interaction, supported recipients/worlds, proposed change and reason existing valid evidence does not settle it.
2. **Comparison and success criteria.** The established behavior, frozen relevant inputs, measurable benefit, acceptable regression bounds and stop/refusal conditions. Select numeric thresholds and optional age/delay/loss allowances before observing the result. Unspecified thresholds leave the experiment unready, not implicitly permissive.
3. **Required semantic outcomes.** Applicable scenarios from the behavior contract, allowed optional differences and failure/recovery behavior. Compare actual recipient disclosures and completed effects; equality of counts alone is insufficient.
4. **Complete cost and experience.** State which measurements are relevant and why: server work, database work, achieved simulation progress, recipient freshness and latency, bytes, temporary/retained memory, client processing, independent healthy-reader progress and human clarity. Separate these costs rather than combining unlike units into one unexplained score. Include unfavorable outcomes and tails where they affect the selected requirement.
5. **Representative workload and execution.** Use the real supported caller, the smallest meaningful connected journey, valid source/input provenance and authorized execution environment. Choose repetition according to variance and the question. Do not impose an arbitrary benchmark population, per-row test quota or new measurement platform.
6. **Decision and scope.** Adopt, reject or leave inconclusive. Adoption requires the declared benefit and all non-negotiable semantics. Record the demonstrated workload, chosen limits, fallback and unresolved evidence; update the relevant behavior, technical and limit owners before ordinary activation. A failed or inconclusive experiment leaves established behavior in place.

A quiet encounter, a concentrated group with differing knowledge and activity spread across independent areas exercise different costs. Start with the scope that triggers the question. Expand only when the proposed claim requires it. No packet benchmark, many idle connections or accepted-request count proves successful gameplay, larger sustained population or MMO capacity.

Adoption can be narrower than the experiment: a policy may help only a particular optional surface or payload size. It must be possible to use established behavior outside the qualified scope, or refuse unsupported activation honestly. Reverting a delivery policy preserves authoritative effects, personal evidence and recovery; it does not restore incompatible old data or add legacy support.

## Later work and non-prerequisites

Broader hosted capacity, regional distribution, unattended communities and richer spectator products have their existing owners. They are not unlocked by smaller updates. The related [research](../../../research/game-engines/horizon-server.md) records why Horizon's event routing, native plugins, advertised virtual zones and example gameplay should not be adopted wholesale.

No universal update rate, distance ladder, character cap, shared-recipient grouping, new plugin/event framework or replacement network transport is selected here. Each would require its own demonstrated need and applicable design. Continue building compelling interactions while preserving the foundation; revisit an optimization when evidence identifies it as a worthwhile constraint on play or operating cost.
