# Perception and attention: scenarios and acceptance

This document specifies observable outcomes for the [perception and attention feature](README.md). The [behavior contract](behavior.md) owns evidence, attention, interruption and continuation; [authoring](authoring.md) owns supported inputs and composition. These scenarios are desired behavior and acceptance cases, not claims that the required mechanics have been implemented.

## How to read the scenarios

An attention candidate can be a person, object, place, direction, occurrence or personally inferred situation. Its priority is the strongest currently supported reason for attending to it. Several descriptions of one act do not add points; independent cues may support a more consequential interpretation when their association is justified. Each reason retains its evidence, demand for brief examination or maintained detail, and ending conditions.

Acquiring an observation, an involuntary bodily reaction, choosing closer observation and choosing a different action are separate outcomes. The scenarios never assume a universal focus count, compulsory thinking pause or fixed switch delay. Actual body, sense and activity compatibility determines what can happen together.

Each case requires its named physical and informational inputs. A container-opening case requires a supported state transition and a way to perceive the lid or opening sound. Identifying an opener additionally requires perceived evidence connecting that person to the act. A protected-person approach requires an accepted concern with an identifiable subject, observable positions or another supported separation measurement, and an authored approach condition. An accepted watch requires its subject, method, coverage, demand, ending conditions and compatible work to be defined. An unsupported prerequisite cannot be replaced with an invented observation.

Fire on a person and narrow manufacturing windows are examples of inputs a sufficiently general system must accommodate when those mechanics exist. They do not introduce thermal injury, overfiring, fishing reaction windows or compulsory precision crafting. Existing ordinary manufacture retains its own interruption and completion rules. The proposed [heat profile](../../worlds/base/heat-and-materials.md) excludes bodily combustion from its initial targets and does not permit its first clay process to overfire.

## Three complete journeys

### 1. A nuisance pursuit meets a possible battle

**Starting situation.** A guard has chosen to pursue a person for a minor disturbance. The pursuit names its actual subject and purpose. The guard is receiving sufficient current observations to steer; the person is not an eternally visible target. A separately supported explosion occurs in the distance.

| Stage | Acquired evidence and resulting behavior | What remains undecided or unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The blast is heard | The received sound can create a strong brief-examination reason. Any supported native startle occurs through the bodily-reaction rules. The guard can orient toward the available bearing or examine the observed region. | Hearing a blast does not identify its cause, victims, attackers or exact source position. |
| Observation competes with pursuit | Optional closer observation can shift promptly when the new reason warrants it. Compatible locomotion may continue. A slowdown, stop or change of route must follow the actual reaction or a chosen action. | Winning attention does not cancel the pursuit, erase its purpose, or issue an instruction to run toward the blast. |
| The guard assesses | An already accepted response policy or a newly chosen action can establish finite assessment: what to check, what work continues or pauses, when assessment ends and what fallback applies. Actual looking/listening supplies the interval's evidence. | Assessment does not guarantee a useful new observation and does not wait indefinitely for generated deliberation. |
| A help call and combat-like sounds arrive | If their personally received timing, location or bearing and recognized relationships support association, they can strengthen a possible incident. Recognizing a known guard's voice can support concern for that person. | Compatible cues do not reveal the number of combatants, confirm a large battle, or make a clipped call contain unheard words. |
| A different action is chosen | The guard may suspend or end the nuisance pursuit and investigate, help, seek support, warn others, or continue pursuit. The decision uses the supported concern and current commitments. An accepted change is reflected in actual movement and work. | The attention score does not predetermine courage, allegiance, willingness to help or the best response. |
| Contact with the pursued person is lost | The guard retains the last supported position and time. A later return to pursuit uses legitimate new observations or a bounded search based on that stale information. | The person's hidden turn supplies no updated destination or missing movement history. |

The decisive counterexample uses the same blast while the guard is trying to stop a witnessed imminent attack. Its physical noticeability has not changed, but the consequence of abandoning the current purpose has. A guard may check the blast briefly and continue the urgent intervention. Neither the word “guard” nor a globally assigned explosion score chooses the action.

**Required pass:** the guard visibly responds to the cue without automatically abandoning the pursuit; any pause or redirection has an actual cause; additional cues change only supported understanding; immediate new danger can interrupt optional assessment; loss and reacquisition of the pursued person remain honest. An assessment deadline completes with the configured outcome even when a model response is absent or late.

### 2. Useful making, a watched approach and a rescue opportunity

**Starting situation.** A player is making a useful item at camp. A willing companion has accepted a finite watch over one approach. Another person travels nearby. The maker's work, the companion's watch and each person's interests remain independent activities with their own requirements.

1. A visitor enters the maker's available view. The maker may notice once and continue making. Continued presence does not require a detailed movement history or repeated decisions. The companion's maintained watch supplies only its actual coverage.
2. A striking new event occurs: the visitor visibly collapses, or a supported hazard exposes someone to immediate danger. A supported person-on-fire observation is a valid stronger variant. It may take precedence over optional examination of the workpiece even without a prior interest in that person.
3. The maker may orient while compatible work continues, or choose to suspend the work. The crafting owner determines what has already completed, what remains, whether interruption loses anything and how resumption works. Attention invents no damage, material refund or quality penalty.
4. The companion may remain on watch, investigate, warn the maker or offer help. Seeing danger does not silently end an accepted watch. If the companion looks away or leaves, actual coverage changes; an intruder can exploit the gap through ordinary movement and perception.
5. Helping requires a supported action, usable route, necessary capability and applicable permission. Concern alone does not extinguish a fire, heal an injury or move a victim. A frightened or committed character may reasonably choose another response.
6. After the immediate situation ends, the characters can reconsider unfinished work and concerns. The maker's retained work can resume when its prerequisites hold. A watch whose original ending condition has been reached does not automatically renew. Each witness remembers only their acquired part of the episode.

A future supported manufacturing process may have a narrow intervention window. Its own rules must expose the cue, meaningful deadline and consequence. An accepted watch can make that change relevant without continually re-deciding the job. A weak external distraction need not displace it; a more consequential danger can still do so. This variation does not add a hidden failure timer to ordinary crafting.

**Required pass:** the episode demonstrates useful work, finite observation, a consequential distraction and a viable continuation. Rescue remains a real choice. Work and materials obey their owners. A character who missed the rescue can learn about it through later evidence or testimony, but does not receive the rescuers' private experience automatically.

### 3. A watched cashbox opens without an identified hand

**Starting situation.** A merchant has accepted a watch concerning a particular cashbox. The installed container mechanic exposes an opening transition through a supported sense. The merchant's interest is bound to that box; it is not an instruction to track every nearby customer continuously.

| Situation | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| A customer approaches and waits | The approach may earn notice or closer examination through a supported approach concern. Remaining stationary does not continually repeat the arrival or create detailed tracking without a continuing reason. |
| The merchant sees the customer open the lid | The observation supports both the box's change and the customer's involvement. The relevant subject may be the person, the box or the associated occurrence, as the installed concern specifies. |
| The merchant sees the lid but not the hand | Attention may go to the box. The identity of the opener remains unknown. A nearby person is not automatically assigned responsibility. |
| The merchant only hears an opening-like sound | The retained observation preserves its actual classification and localization. A legitimate association may connect it to the watched box; unsupported certainty about the source or opener is not added. |
| Hand motion, lid motion and state change describe one opening | They can support the same reason or improve the supported interpretation. They do not add three copies of relevance. The strongest supported reason determines that candidate's priority. |
| The opener had permission | Opening can remain relevant without implying theft, hostility or a duty to intervene. Permission changes supported interpretation and available responses. |
| The lid closes and opens again | A genuinely new observed occurrence can supply a new reason. A repeated report of the first opening cannot. |
| A save is restored with the lid already open | Current visible state can be observed. Restoration does not invent another opening or reset a completed response's repetition state. |
| The merchant was distracted throughout the opening | On returning, the merchant can see the current open box and investigate. They cannot retrospectively witness the act. |

**Required pass:** concern about a watched object works for a previously untracked actor, responsibility remains evidence-bound, repetition follows real occurrences, and notice does not itself select punishment or intervention.

## Scenario catalogue

The catalogue tests shared behavior through different purposes. Cases requiring a new physical mechanic are input-contract examples; they do not make that mechanic a prerequisite for delivering attention. Test supported equivalents where they preserve the particular distinction, and state the remaining coverage gap.

### Action, danger and competing commitments

| ID | Setup and meaningful choice | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PA-S01 | Nuisance pursuit; a distant explosion | Notice and finite examination can precede reconsideration. A change of focus does not automatically cancel pursuit or select a destination. |
| PA-S02 | Help call followed by combat-like sounds | Personally associated cues can support a more consequential interpretation; independent, inconsistent or unlocalized cues retain their uncertainty. |
| PA-S03 | The same blast during an imminent intervention | The current stakes can justify maintaining the urgent action while briefly checking elsewhere. Occupation alone does not determine priority or response. |
| PA-S04 | A person catches fire nearby, when the mechanic is supported | Strong acquired danger can outrank optional attention without a pre-existing watch. Bodily reaction, closer observation and chosen help remain separate. |
| PA-S05 | A new immediate attack during assessment | Applicable native reactions and supported defense are not blocked by an arbitrary thinking delay. Released effects retain their ordinary continuation. |
| PA-S06 | A pursued person crosses behind cover during a glance | Tracking becomes stale at the actual loss of evidence. A hidden turn remains unknown; later reacquisition does not fill the gap. |
| PA-S07 | A second attacker appears during a duel | Competing danger does not erase awareness of the first attacker. Actual observation and body/work compatibility constrain defense and continued action. |
| PA-S08 | An opponent retreats while a companion falls | Pursuit, securing the objective and rescue are legitimate competing purposes. The fall's meaning and the chosen response depend on acquired evidence and actual concerns. |

### Deception, stealth and shared tactics

| ID | Setup and meaningful choice | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PA-S09 | One player creates an impact while another crosses a watched route | A plausible cue can create a real coverage gap. The observer receives no hidden thrower identity or accomplice relationship. |
| PA-S10 | A resolved harmless decoy is repeated | Repetition does not endlessly renew the original strongest reason. A changed signal or actual escalation can matter; new bodily danger remains effective. |
| PA-S11 | A lookout expects trouble on the road | Expectation shapes active checks and actual coverage without suppressing strong unexpected evidence from another direction. |
| PA-S12 | A believable false call for help | Received speech and recognition can influence judgment. The observer cannot detect a lie from hidden truth, nor obtain missing words. |
| PA-S13 | An alarm supplies danger but no source location | A direction, region, report or unresolved occurrence may receive attention. Neither inspection nor navigation receives an invented exact target. |
| PA-S14 | Two players produce competing distractions | Finite supported attention and stable selection prevent update-by-update oscillation or perfect simultaneous surveillance. Trivial repetition cannot erase unresolved danger. |
| PA-S15 | An ambush is discovered before contact | Surprise consequences follow the preparedness owner. Detection leaves ordinary combat, retreat, negotiation and other supported choices available. |

### Making, opportunities and ordinary life

| ID | Setup and meaningful choice | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PA-S16 | A maker notices a visitor while working | A brief notice may leave work and detailed attention unchanged. Waiting nearby does not repeatedly reproduce the arrival. |
| PA-S17 | Danger interrupts a useful craft | Orienting, suspending and cancelling remain distinct. Actual work rules determine partial progress, costs and resumption. |
| PA-S18 | A practical demonstration interests Mara | An acquired result and a supported interest can justify examination. Watching does not automatically disclose a complete method or compel praise, purchase or help. |
| PA-S19 | A supported process exposes a closing intervention window | Relevance and urgency follow the process's actual cue, known consequence and remaining opportunity. Attention creates neither the process nor its failure rule. |
| PA-S20 | Familiar workshop noise becomes an unusual crack | Expected activity can remain ordinary; newly observed failure can change the reason. Expertise may support interpretation only from available evidence. |
| PA-S21 | Ada notices an unusual animal or a striking view | Positive interest can justify a finite pause without danger, treasure, a reward grant or permanent tracking. |

### Witnesses, investigation and generalization

| ID | Setup and meaningful choice | Required outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PA-S22 | An authorized visitor opens watched supplies | The act can be relevant without being theft. Attention does not grant enforcement authority or prescribe a moral interpretation. |
| PA-S23 | A watched container opens but the hand is hidden | The object or occurrence can draw attention while the opener stays unknown. Proximity alone does not establish responsibility. |
| PA-S24 | A door opens onto a stationary intruder | An environmental or observer change can create exposure without target movement. The observation concerns the exposed present, not an invented earlier arrival. |
| PA-S25 | A person returns to a broken gate or missing possession | Aftermath, testimony, inferred cause and directly witnessed action remain distinct. Searching can produce useful partial findings or no new information. |
| PA-S26 | A false alarm ends; real danger later appears | Actual resolution can end a reason. New supported evidence can create another. Lost perception is unknown, not proof of safety; restoration is not a new occurrence. |
| PA-S27 | A nonvisual creature and several human witnesses encounter the same event | Each receives only supported sense data and personal evidence. The creature gains no implicit human senses; the witnesses can truthfully retain different fragments. More updates or clients do not increase certainty or importance. |

## Functional acceptance

All applicable requirements below are required passes. Unsupported prerequisite mechanics must be identified explicitly; a missing producer cannot be reported as a successful end-to-end case.

| Area | Required pass evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence boundary | Detection, classification, localization, recognition and acquired detail remain distinct. Unknown people, sources and intentions stay unknown unless further permitted evidence supports them. |
| Notice without tracking | A transient ordinary observation can be retained without selecting maintained detail, repeatedly re-admitting a decision, or continuously recording the subject. |
| Meaningful capture | Strong acquired danger can interrupt optional examination even without an existing concern about its source. Native bodily effects do not depend on winning optional attention. |
| Reason selection | A candidate's priority reflects its strongest active supported reason. Repeated updates and multiple descriptions of the same occurrence do not accumulate points. Ending a strong reason can expose a still-valid weaker reason. |
| Eligibility and resources | A highest-ranked candidate need not be eligible for maintained detail. Actual method/body/activity resources govern coverage and concurrency; an empty detailed allocation is valid. |
| Stability and renewal | Small fluctuations do not cause oscillation. Genuine escalation remains responsive. Repeat handling distinguishes ongoing conditions, newly acquired evidence, new occurrences and resolved concerns. |
| Cue association | A combined interpretation identifies its acquired supporting cues and justified relationship. Contradictory or insufficient evidence does not become certainty, and unrelated events are not silently merged. |
| Assessment | Finite assessment has an accepted purpose, method, work disposition, ending condition and fallback. It collects actual evidence and ends without waiting indefinitely for a model. |
| Action continuity | Attention changes do not silently cancel work, issue movement, spend resources, choose targets, renew agreements or change ownership. Accepted action transitions use their ordinary consequences. |
| Coverage and tracking | Turning, movement, occlusion and environmental changes alter real coverage. Last-known information retains its age and provenance; hidden movement never refreshes it. |
| Life cycle | Pause, supported save/load, disappearance, death, invalidated subjects, completed work and changed concerns preserve or end reasons according to their actual conditions. Restoring state creates no fictitious occurrence. |
| Personal history | Witnesses can recount only their acquired observations and attributed information, preserving uncertainty. Detailed history has actual coverage intervals; missed intervals remain missing. |
| Player agency | Directly controlled characters retain the existing control contract. Attention supplies permitted information and applicable native effects; it does not choose a player's investigation, attack or retreat. |
| Shared events | Several players and NPCs interact with one consistent occurrence and its consequences while retaining their own perspectives. The same cue cannot be multiplied by transport or observation frequency. |
| Authoring completeness | An installed definition has supported observations, bindings, relevance, demand, lifetime and repetition behavior. Unsupported measurements or effects are reported as unsupported instead of approximated with fictional evidence. |

Exercise interruption, resumption, lost perception, later reacquisition, changed geometry and save/load on the three complete journeys. Include at least one materially different nonvisual input in authoring qualification; its physical sense remains a separately supplied prerequisite.

## Performance and responsiveness acceptance

The following are required measurements and comparisons, with the semantic properties above remaining required passes. This document does not claim a qualified population, frame budget or hardware result. The [technical design](../../projects/perception-and-attention-tech-design.md) owns the measurement procedure and work attribution.

| Comparison | Required observation and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| More unrelated installed definitions | Repeat the same walks, watch and pursuit with additional definitions that cannot consume their observations. Ordinary observation and attention work must not grow linearly with the total installed catalogue. Setup or authoring cost must be distinguished from recurring gameplay cost. |
| More genuinely relevant interests | Measure the additional work when more actual concerns apply. Do not label necessary matching or personal observations constant-cost, and do not meet a budget by silently omitting relevant evidence. |
| More real witnesses | Measure a crowded market containing pursuit, a watched object and uninvolved bystanders. Distinguish shared physical work from witness-specific observations, reasoning and retained detail. |
| Higher update frequency | Increase equivalent movement-update frequency without changing the meaningful episode. Priority, occurrence count, action outcomes and remembered coverage must remain semantically consistent. |
| Simultaneous salient events | Measure visible response, stable selection and completion of finite assessments under real competition. Strong supported danger cannot be silently discarded to protect optional detail. |
| Time and waiting | Measure native reaction, orientation, chosen-action response and simulation progress separately. Model latency is not an authored reaction delay, and faster simulation does not secretly extend an original deadline. |

A measurement that misses its selected performance objective remains a failure or open qualification; correct semantics alone does not establish scale. Conversely, fewer observations or faster execution obtained by granting hidden knowledge, suppressing danger or losing witness history fails functional acceptance.

## Human understanding and enjoyment

Mechanical passes do not establish fun. Observe ordinary players completing a wanted activity, attempting a distraction, adapting when it fails, and returning to interrupted work or a changed relationship. Include repeat attempts after the tactic is understood.

Required human-evidence questions are whether players can explain what caught attention, distinguish noticing from a chosen response, identify at least one useful alternative, understand a failed attempt, and find the time spent observing or assessing worthwhile. Check whether the resulting opportunities remain interesting after learning a reliable tactic. A rational satisfied decision to stop is not automatically failure.

Ordinary visible behavior and permitted sensory presentation must communicate useful distinctions without exposing private numeric priorities, hidden concerns or omniscient labels. Looking, continuing work, pausing, warning and leaving should have their actual meanings. Sensory information needed for play must follow the project's accessibility and perspective contracts; a developer-only trace is insufficient feedback.

Preserve counterexamples: an NPC can decline rescue, maintain an urgent pursuit, be fooled by credible evidence, investigate fruitlessly, or choose a peaceful opportunity. These are not necessarily defects. Unexplained oscillation, invented knowledge, forced action, endless deliberation and repetitive renewal chores are defects rather than sources of emergent drama.
