# Work, craft, professions and invention

[Library](README.md) · [Reading the labels](README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](selection-and-scale.md) · [Design foundation](design-foundation.md)

**Proposal catalogue: 296 selectable practices.** Work can mean satisfying mastery, a livelihood, beauty, service, ambition, martial preparation, exploitation, crime, or an experiment nobody quite knows how to finish. A profession should offer its actual activity: a musician makes music, a repairer diagnoses and repairs, and a baker produces something people can enjoy. Repeated work can become a chosen routine without remaining compulsory manual drudgery forever.

**Scope:** professions and making, modification, restoration, diagnosis, and service processes. [Objects](objects.md) owns finished possessions; [Materials](materials-resources.md) owns inputs and access requirements; [Economy](economy-logistics.md) owns exchange, labor terms, and distribution arrangements. [Abilities](abilities-progression.md) owns learned proficiency, [Technology](technology.md) technical systems, [Magic](magic.md) extraordinary effects, and [Combat](combat-rescue.md) fighting or rescue resolution. Refer to the [ownership guide](README.md#category-ownership-and-cross-references), not a duplicate classification legend. Missing dependencies are named, not assigned invented IDs.

Seed labels follow the four proposals. FA includes optional later traditions without replacing its opening finite-heat rules; MD gains no supernatural craft by association. SC work concerns inhabited places and abstract transit, not piloting or dogfighting. `—` keeps wider inventions in the library. Rows are original proposals: Inspiration identifies a motif, contrast, or retained pattern, not a claim that the exact practice is implemented in the linked source. Hazardous, medical, sexual, and criminal subjects stay at fictional design-inventory level, not operational instructions.

## Base coverage

Keep household production, agriculture, construction, arms and armor, repair, technical trades, artistry, instruction, personal services, field work, and research. Different work can require choosing a design, judging a material, coordinating stages, satisfying a client, interpreting evidence, or handling an exception. Do not turn every profession into the same timed button with a different noun. Optional hands-on controls, description, commissions, and delegation can express the same meaningful craft choices.

## The commission with room for taste

WORK-181 retains a readable rainproof market sign whose function is mandatory but style is negotiable. A disliked color is not proof of mechanical failure. Makers can ask, prototype, and offer an unexpected solution.

## Teach the workshop one good routine

WORK-182 retains demonstrated offcut sorting and repair reservations, with bounded inputs, explicit stop conditions, and visible exceptions. A helper cannot invent missing material or spend beyond its agreement. [Automation](automation-creators.md) owns the reusable routine machinery.

## Make do beautifully

WORK-183 retains festival lanterns made from available tins and cloth after a shipment fails. A substitute can establish a beloved style rather than always producing an inferior version of the unavailable original.

## The diagnostic profession

WORK-101 retains a repairer who listens, measures, and asks about changes before selecting a pump repair. Evidence must distinguish plausible faults; investigation is not a hidden counter preceding the only permitted button.

## Craft as performance

WORK-061 retains direct pottery shaping alongside descriptive design and commissioned execution. Motor speed is not the character's expertise, and accessible control routes should not erase the value of material and design decisions.

## A profession that changes its clients

WORK-145 retains an itinerant arithmetic tutor whose successful clients need fewer basic lessons and can request new ones. Success can change a livelihood instead of continually regenerating the same ignorance.

## The prototype club

WORK-184 retains small trials such as carrying soup, opening a high window, or protecting a seedling. Results distinguish actual supported tests from speculative descriptions. Friendly recoverable mishaps and genuinely dangerous experiments are different selected activities.

## Work worth sharing credit for

WORK-185 retains surveying, hauling, cooking, and negotiation as real contributions to a bridge. Credit can be coarse and contestable; it need not reduce every participant to a universal productivity score.

## An inherited method questioned

WORK-186 retains a bakery's proofing ritual as something that may regulate temperature, mark time, or bring people together. Testing its practical claim need not erase its social meaning or prove every tradition foolish.

## A master who still learns

WORK-187 retains an expert carpenter learning a folding joint from a traveling novice. Expertise is particular: the expert contributes structural judgment while the novice brings experience of portable homes. A lesson should visibly change later work.
