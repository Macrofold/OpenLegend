# Ranked coverage: the game before its embellishments

[Library](README.md) · [Priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md) · [270 patterns](ranked-patterns.md) · [384 actions](ranked-actions.md) · [Worked situations](combinations.md)

**Current survival-adventure priorities · October 3, 2026.** Coverage tables describe broader families than the illustrative pattern cards. This register decomposes those families into the basic player capabilities and later elaborations they contain. It prevents a catalogue full of interesting refinements from hiding an absent weapon, enemy, objective or reward. It is selection policy, not a second delivery tracker, implementation audit or approval to adopt an optional world's lore.

The scores are **1 Core, 2 Complete, 3 Depth, 4 Detail, 5 Specialist**, defined in [the policy](gameplay-priorities.md#criticality). Rank the smallest stated scope, not the whole domain by its most important member. A missing Core capability outranks another refinement to a working one. A representative weapon does not mean every weapon; a functional home does not mean a city simulator. Existing adequate capabilities need no reimplementation merely because they appear here.

## 1 Core: close the playable loop

These are interconnected requirements, ordered by the policy's workstreams. Deliver a thin playable combination rather than finishing one isolated subsystem exhaustively. The source links identify repertoire owners, not claims that their full proposed mechanics exist.

| Order | Player capability             | Minimum useful scope                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Owning repertoire                                                                                                                  |
| ----- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Understand and control play   | Movement, interaction, inspection, target selection, usable inventory and equipment, readable action results, stop/cancel, health/danger feedback, and an understandable first goal. Include accessible controls and legible presentation; these are not optional simulation features. | [Actions](actions.md), [Automation](automation-creators.md), [Adventure](adventure-discovery.md)                                   |
| 2     | Continue or finish a session  | A usable current-format save/load or restart path under the existing policy; clear success, loss and recovery rules. No forced new mortality or respawn rule is selected here.                                                                                                         | [Mechanics](mechanics.md), [Bodies](bodies-species.md)                                                                             |
| 3     | Encounter actual opposition   | A readable local hostile creature or person at a worthwhile site, with detection, approach, attack and disengagement. Make both wildlife and hostile people first-class early content candidates, not merely ecology or diplomacy footnotes.                                           | [Combat](combat-rescue.md), [Ecology](ecology-weather.md), [Bodies](bodies-species.md), [Factions](diplomacy-conflict.md)          |
| 4     | Use a weapon against it       | Equip and use a representative melee weapon; provide a representative ranged choice with distinct range, timing and ammunition where used. Reliable targeting, damage, attack cues and enemy response matter more than an exhaustive arsenal.                                          | [Objects](objects.md), [Combat](combat-rescue.md), [Abilities](abilities-progression.md)                                           |
| 5     | Defend, escape and recover    | At least one meaningful defensive or avoidance method, usable retreat/disengagement, understandable health and a basic recovery method. Basic protection should change outcomes; detailed wounds and permanent impairments are separate.                                               | [Combat](combat-rescue.md), [Needs](needs.md), [Bodies](bodies-species.md)                                                         |
| 6     | Explore somewhere worthwhile  | Recognizable landmarks, an interesting or dangerous destination, discoverable routes and an attainable objective. A resource site, occupied ruin or guarded cache can supply this without a procedural continent.                                                                      | [Adventure](adventure-discovery.md), [Settlements](settlements-architecture.md), [History](history-myth.md)                        |
| 7     | Win something and improve     | Useful loot or another tangible payoff, an attainable equipment/recipe/technique improvement, and a next challenge or newly useful route that makes it matter. An objective can end in a real victory without being undone by an automatic reset.                                      | [Objects](objects.md), [Abilities](abilities-progression.md), [Economy](economy-logistics.md), [Adventure](adventure-discovery.md) |
| 8     | Gather and make useful things | A short resource-to-tool/weapon/consumable chain with real input costs and outputs worth using. Harvesting, gathering and crafting serve the adventure or a satisfying creative activity.                                                                                              | [Materials](materials-resources.md), [Work](work-crafting.md), [Actions](actions.md)                                               |
| 9     | Meet basic survival needs     | Usable food access, preparation and consumption; basic rest/recovery. Where thirst is selected, supply a simple coherent collection/carrying/drinking loop rather than delaying water behind food preservation. Do not add a draining meter without its usable remedies.               | [Needs](needs.md), [Materials](materials-resources.md), [Actions](actions.md)                                                      |
| 10    | Use and improve a home        | Functional construction/placement, storage and a usable shelter/resting place. Give at least one improvement a practical or expressive payoff worth bringing materials home for.                                                                                                       | [Settlements](settlements-architecture.md), [Objects](objects.md), [Work](work-crafting.md)                                        |

The action examples include aiming, shooting and shielding but omit a plain melee-strike example. That is a **catalogue coverage omission**, not evidence of absent runtime melee. The [action tracker](../maintainers/action-capabilities.md#ac096--equipped-contact-strikes-implemented-acceptance-partial) already records a narrow implemented contact-strike slice with incomplete acceptance. Evaluate its real player path rather than inventing a replacement. Conversely, an implemented attack or a knife object alone does not prove a complete enemy encounter, satisfying combat, rewards or progression.

**First proof:** the player discovers a dangerous resource site or occupied ruin, obtains and equips suitable gear, fights or evades a readable opponent, reaches an objective, returns with a useful reward, makes an upgrade and has a reason to attempt another challenge. Failure has an understandable continuation. This is a proposed evaluation target, not a newly approved quest, guaranteed solution or completed playtest.

## 2 Complete: make the game worth continuing

| Rank | Addition                            | Scope boundary                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Enemy and encounter variety         | Different attack patterns, melee/ranged roles, a threatening elite or boss-like objective, and more than one encounter goal. Do not require adaptive personalities or regional war.            |
| 2    | More interesting destinations       | Ruins, caves, camps, valuable resource locations, alternate approaches, shortcuts, secrets and useful discoveries. New places should change decisions, not just scenery.                       |
| 3    | Useful gear and progression choices | Distinct weapons, armor/protection, consumables, crafting upgrades, recipes and techniques. Add a few meaningful alternatives before vast rarity tables or universal build composition.        |
| 4    | Clear challenge and reward pacing   | Attainable goals, understandable risk, useful loot, new options and satisfying stopping points. A stronger enemy should not be merely an inflated health bar.                                  |
| 5    | Substantial survival activities     | Fishing, fuller hunting/foraging, farming, animal care or another worthwhile production activity. Choose coherent activities rather than requiring all of them for every meal.                 |
| 6    | Better building and useful defenses | Staged home/workshop improvements, functional layouts, a defensible entrance or barrier when threats warrant it, and meaningful storage capacity. Avoid compulsory maintenance tax.            |
| 7    | Simple trade and services           | Buy, sell, barter, commission useful equipment or seek a service. Fixed or authored prices can work before a dynamic economic model.                                                           |
| 8    | Useful companions                   | A companion who can travel, fight, guard, help or rescue through real supported behavior. Formal job agreements, completion reports and elaborate domestic negotiations are not prerequisites. |
| 9    | Readable environmental variety      | Darkness/light, weather or terrain that changes a chosen activity and has understandable counterplay. Full climate, moisture, seasons and ecology are not required.                            |
| 10   | Optional creative and social play   | Gardening, decorating, music, a contest, a fair or a small playable game can be worthwhile in their own right. They need not produce a combat buff to deserve selection.                       |
| 11   | Less repetition after success       | Useful shortcuts, learned methods, convenient inventory, or a bounded routine when an actual recurring chore has been demonstrated. Do not build chores merely to justify automation.          |

## What does not become a new feature

“Prepare supplies” uses the existing inventory, bags, equipment and consumables unless a concrete missing interaction is demonstrated. “Agree on a task” and “report completion” are not substitutes for an enjoyable shared activity. “A camp with consequences” is not a higher-priority product milestone than playable opposition, exploration and rewards. A feature's realistic consequence is a correctness requirement for that chosen feature, not a reason to choose it.

No scores in this register close implementation tasks or establish fun empirically. Use the existing owners to audit gaps and qualify the selected playable slice. The [selection guide](selection-and-scale.md) explains how to evaluate the candidates without reviving the old camp-first or nearest-code ordering.
