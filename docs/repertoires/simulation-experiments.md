# Simulation and experimental worlds

**Canonical catalogue.** Descriptions and current priorities live together here. Each domain table is ordered by priority, then stable ID; implementation Level and build effort are not priority tie-breakers. Entry rows own individual priorities; worked examples provide context without a second ranking. These are proposals, not delivered features.

[Library](README.md) · [Reading the labels](README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](selection-and-scale.md) · [Design foundation](design-foundation.md)

**Proposal catalogue · September 28, 2026.** Two hundred and seventy-two selectable experiments in authored worlds: household time, workshops, ecosystems, rivalries, institutions, memory, performances, strange physics and the consequences of changing a world rule. Each row supplies a setup, a meaningful variation and outcomes worth comparing. Some make satisfying puzzles or exhibitions; others are primarily diagnostic models. None has been run as part of writing this catalogue.

**Scope:** Own the experiment, comparison and observation protocol here. [Ecology] owns ecological processes; [Economy] owns exchange and logistics; [Bodies] owns anatomy and bodily conditions; [Psychology] owns authored decision models; [Politics] owns institutions; [Combat] owns fighting and rescue; [Magic] owns supernatural laws. Do not mistake an experiment about a system for a duplicate inventory of that system's content. See [category ownership](README.md#category-ownership-and-cross-references).

Use the README's [labels](README.md#reading-the-labels) and [table format](README.md#catalogue-tables). C composes a trial from assumed supported models, observations and interventions; missing constituents remain explicit gaps. D defines a new reusable model or experimental policy. Lab does not automatically mean Frontier. `—` retains experiments outside the four seed proposals, including time loops and impossible constitutions that cannot be silently assigned to Borrowed Dawn or Veyra Reach.

**Interpretation boundary:** results would describe the implemented model under its declared assumptions, not establish real psychology, ecology, economics, medicine, engineering or public policy. Generated dialogue is not representative human evidence. Preserve starting states, units where applicable, model revisions, randomness, observation limits and exclusions. Separate real execution from narration, configured motives from observed behavior, and counterfactual copies from the live world. A creator's privileged view never becomes an inhabitant's knowledge by accident. Real accessibility evaluation requires affected people's participation; simulated profiles alone do not validate a design.

All entries are **creative synthesis**. Inspiration identifies a source relationship or tension, not proof that the proposed experiment was performed or that its result is known in advance.

## A rumor transmission laboratory

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: reproducible message networks, interventions and observable outcomes.

Vary who hears a bridge warning, how sources are credited and when corrections arrive. Compare reach, delay and distortion under a fixed authored communication model. This can teach causal reasoning about that model. Keep hidden-state access separate from participant knowledge, record random seeds and rules, and never infer real population behavior from a handful of generated personalities.

Related entries: [SIM-091–100](#signals-rumor-and-public-information).

Seeds and references: [Among Us](source-atlas.md#g02), [Disco Elysium](source-atlas.md#g66), [The Wire](source-atlas.md#w21), [The Wire](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/21-the-wire.md).

## A household time study

**MO SC · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: explicit activity durations, bounded schedules and measurement exports.

Compare how shared kitchens, childcare and commute layouts change available free time. Individuals can have different priorities and resources. Builders explore the consequences of assumptions rather than optimizing a universal happiness score. Record what is excluded, including invisible labor and unmodeled constraints; the result is not evidence that real households should follow the simulated arrangement.

Observe interruptions and whose labor supplies someone else’s convenience.

Related entries: [SIM-001–010](#household-time-and-ordinary-life).

Seeds and references: [The Sims](source-atlas.md#g24), [Persona 5 Royal](source-atlas.md#g124), [Austen’s social worlds](source-atlas.md#w23), [Austen](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/23-austens-social-worlds.md).

## The resilient food web

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: explicit population equations, conservation, interventions and reproducible runs.

A small island model varies habitat refuges, harvesting and seasonality, measuring collapse and recovery. Players can also use it as an optional teaching garden. Keep populations and individual encounters distinct. A stable numerical run validates neither ecological realism nor real conservation advice; disclose parameters, boundary conditions and whether outcomes are sensitive to small changes.

Related entries: [SIM-051–060](#food-webs-habitats-and-living-systems).

Seeds and references: [Ultima Online](source-atlas.md#g19), [Rain World](source-atlas.md#g123), [Oxygen Not Included](source-atlas.md#g129).

## Energy cascades in miniature

**MO SC · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: bounded network flows, failure propagation and instrumented accounting.

Model a workshop, greenhouse and clinic sharing heat and power, then remove one connection or add storage. Compare recovery and unmet demand. The visible model can be an engaging puzzle, while lab use needs precise units and declared simplifications. Do not present fictional efficiency values as real engineering results or let numerical loops manufacture energy.

Related entries: [SIM-061–070](#water-weather-and-energy-networks).

Seeds and references: [Factorio](source-atlas.md#g27), [Satisfactory](source-atlas.md#g28), [Oxygen Not Included](source-atlas.md#g129).

## Institutional handoff experiment

**MO SC · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: auditable workflows, limited actor information and controlled variations.

A request passes through intake, review and delivery; vary deadlines, authority and feedback to see where work stalls. Different workers can be conscientious yet produce a bad collective outcome; another variation can explicitly model favoritism, career incentives or deliberate obstruction. Measure the defined process, not moral worth. Generated dialogue is illustrative, and a convenient outcome cannot establish that a real institution would behave the same way.

Related entries: [SIM-121–130](#institutions-authority-and-service).

Seeds and references: [Dragon Age: Inquisition](source-atlas.md#g53), [Mass Effect: Andromeda](source-atlas.md#g104), [The Wire](source-atlas.md#w21), [The Wire](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/21-the-wire.md).

## Plural motivations without mind reading

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: explicit bounded agent models and separation of internal state from observations.

Compare decisions under competing needs, commitments and beliefs using a small transparent model. Participants can observe actions and statements while a lab operator inspects configured variables. This supports model comparison, not diagnosis. Report the authored decision rules and uncertainty; fluent explanations must not be treated as proof of true internal causes or human psychological validity.

Related entries: [SIM-111–120](#motives-relationships-and-chosen-boundaries).

Seeds and references: [Disco Elysium](source-atlas.md#g66), [Crusader Kings III](source-atlas.md#g126), [The Sopranos](source-atlas.md#w22), [The Sopranos](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/22-the-sopranos.md).

## A market with known assumptions

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: controlled agents, price formation, conserved balances and reproducible shocks.

Vary storage costs, transport delays and concentrated ownership in a three-town economy, then observe shortages, profit and recovery. Add a declared embargo or predatory pricing rule to explore conflict within the model rather than assuming every merchant pursues cooperative trade. The same model can power a trading puzzle. Distinguish model results from real forecasts, and check sensitivity before interpreting an apparent policy benefit. Generated merchants cannot stand in for representative human samples merely because their negotiations sound plausible.

Conserve balances and disclose the model’s price formation.

Related entries: [SIM-031–040](#markets-reserves-and-unequal-access).

Seeds and references: [Ultima Online](source-atlas.md#g19), [Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord](source-atlas.md#g115), [Starsector](source-atlas.md#g120).

## Memory and disagreement over time

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: controlled retention and retrieval policies with provenance-aware observations.

Give fictional witnesses different views of one event, then compare how reminders and retellings affect later accounts. The experiment can reveal weaknesses in an implemented memory system. Keep original observations, retrieval outputs and generated interpretations separate. It does not establish how human memory works, and privileged experiment logs must never leak into ordinary participant decisions.

Related entries: [SIM-101–110](#memory-records-and-interpretation).

Seeds and references: [Hades I](source-atlas.md#g22), [Middle-earth: Shadow of War](source-atlas.md#g116), [Macondo](source-atlas.md#w24), [Macondo](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/24-macondo.md).

## Embodiment and accessible design

**ALL · Blend · Large/New.** Gap: explicit body profiles, perception constraints and route/task instrumentation.

Try a workshop with different reach, mobility and sensory profiles, measuring which tasks the model permits and where assistance is required. Designers can discover obvious layout exclusions. Real accessibility still requires affected people's evaluation and accurate task modeling. A simulated body should not be presented as reproducing a person's lived experience or validating a design on its own.

Related entries: [SIM-071–080](#bodies-access-and-different-capabilities).

Seeds and references: [Caves of Qud](source-atlas.md#g33), [Prey (2017)](source-atlas.md#g118), [Star Trek](source-atlas.md#w04), [Star Trek](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/worlds/04-star-trek.md).

## The compound-effect observatory

**ALL · Lab · Large/New.** Gap: bounded dependency traces, counterfactual runs and sensitivity summaries.

A delayed delivery changes meals, work attendance and a council deadline. Compare one-factor and combined interventions to find interactions the simple story missed. The appeal is seeing a causal chain unfold. Bound run length and event count, report approximations, and keep counterfactual copies separate; visual plausibility alone is not causal identification outside the configured model.

Related entries: [SIM-191–200](#comparative-protocols-and-compound-worlds).

Seeds and references: [RimWorld](source-atlas.md#g21), [Crusader Kings III](source-atlas.md#g126), [Oxygen Not Included](source-atlas.md#g129).

## Selection guidance

An experiment about combat is not an enemy, an experiment about a workshop is not useful crafting, and a convincing simulated evening is not evidence that a player has something enjoyable to do. No standalone experimental-world product is required before the current game becomes playable. **This does not defer required verification of a selected Core feature.** Use a suitable small comparison during that feature's ordinary design or verification when it answers a concrete question; do not first build a general laboratory, comparative-world platform or all the experiments in this catalogue.

Depth includes comparisons that can directly refine a playable experience: threat warnings and remaining response opportunities (SIM-133/249), actionable clues and attainable endings (152/158), learning and useful contribution rather than token repetition (089/243), actual inventory decisions versus repeated clicks (219), newcomers entering an advanced world (220), and meaningful player purpose rather than merely working subsystems (239). SIM-240 distinguishes enjoying creation from enjoying the finished experience as a visitor. Those comparisons can inform a small playtest without becoming additional runtime systems.

SIM-268 compares food as an optional benefit with recurring maintenance; SIM-266 distinguishes skipping encounters from removing repetitive input; SIM-267 checks whether victory actually ends a threat. None prescribes perpetual hardship as the price of a meaningful game. SIM-141/142 retain quiet pleasure and cosmetic expression as legitimate activities. Actual enjoyment, accessibility and comprehension require appropriate human observation; generated praise or configured preferences are not a substitute.

Household reserves, chore allocation, guest-room utilization, repair policies, service acknowledgments, routine notifications and collector emptying are Detail. A Small estimate does not make these priorities for the next playable milestone. A technical comparison of pumping height or spare stock must earn its place through a selected activity, not precede interesting places, opponents or rewards.

Specialist covers dedicated psychological, institutional, ecological and comparative-model investigations; alternate worlds and bodies; cross-world publishing and continuity experiments; and technical systems outside this survival-adventure's present scope. The Lab label is not itself the rule: several practical diagnosis and experience comparisons are Depth, while a Blend label does not make a hypothetical time-loop experiment a game foundation. Permission checks, privacy, accounting and accurate outcomes remain required whenever their associated feature is selected, regardless of where a proposed laboratory experiment ranks.

SIM-180 and other revision, import or restoration experiments do not override the [development save policy](../../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy). This catalogue authorizes neither legacy compatibility nor live-data replacement. Fictional institutional comparisons are model explorations, not rankings or recommendations about real politics. The source interpretation boundary continues to apply to psychology, medicine, ecology and engineering as well.
