# Designing worlds people want to inhabit and change

[Library and labels](README.md) · [Research atlas](source-atlas.md) · [Selection and scale](selection-and-scale.md) · [Worked situations](combinations.md)

**Proposal foundation, September 27, 2026.** This document owns the repertoire's coverage of human motives, playable systems and world dynamics. It guides the existing category patterns and four world proposals; it does not expand those categories into their eventual hundreds of entries or adopt them as engine requirements. The [README](README.md#reading-the-labels) owns classification and table conventions. The [research atlas](source-atlas.md#research-basis-for-the-foundation-revision) records this revision's evidence and limits.

A living world contains people who want things, means of pursuing them, conditions that resist them, and consequences that others must live with. Its inhabitants can be loving, brave, funny, selfish, ambitious, cruel, mistaken, loyal or treacherous. A person may embody several of these without becoming morally interchangeable with everyone else. Places contain pleasures worth seeking, powers worth acquiring, mysteries worth understanding and things worth defending, taking or destroying. The player can enjoy competence, danger, competition and victory as well as creation, affection and peace.

The library supports heroic, domestic, tragic, comic, horrific, competitive and experimental experiences. A selected world needs a coherent emphasis, not an equal quota of each. Retain genuine goodness and genuine evil, mixed motives and ordinary indifference. Explaining why an antagonist acts does not require excusing the act, making the victim equally responsible or providing a guaranteed reconciliation. A courageous knight can want to fight and win; a loving family can include an abusive member; a peaceful settlement can be enjoyable without a hidden atrocity. Those are different authored possibilities.

## Adult scope and world-specific tone

**The creative target is an adult game with an R-rated tone.** The repertoire foundation must make room for raw, disturbing, transgressive and pleasurable aspects of life. Sex, drugs, alcohol, abuse, torture, sexual violence, terror, demons, black magic and deadly catastrophe belong in its account of possible worlds. An idea is not excluded merely because it is immoral, unsavory, frightening or unsuitable for a PG audience. A lighter or PG world is a deliberate world choice, not the foundation's universal ceiling.

Alcohol and drugs may draw on recognizable real-world substances or inventions of a particular world; their modeled effects remain authored assumptions.

Adult scope applies to systems and consequences as well as vocabulary. It must be possible to describe the underlying relationships and changes: intimacy and reproductive choices; intoxication and dependence; a controlling household; a tyrant's prisoners; a predatory cult; a town facing an undead outbreak. A mature label pasted onto universally harmless mechanics would not satisfy this direction. These are proposal capabilities and authored dynamics, not claims that the current engine already implements them.

Keep the distinctions that make these systems intelligible. Desire, affection, obedience and consent are different facts. Coercion, incapacity or a magical override does not turn sexual violence into consensual intimacy. Abuse is not simply a low relationship score. Torture does not produce infallible knowledge. A terrifying power can remain genuinely dangerous after its motive is understood. These are causal and narrative distinctions, not requirements that every inhabitant be kind or every harm receive a comforting resolution.

A particular world selects its subject matter, presentation, player roles and consequence rules. Those choices can produce adult domestic drama, a brutal frontier, criminal nightlife, supernatural horror or a gentler world. They do not require every possible harm in every setting, and they do not require an unseen atrocity behind every happy moment. Keep sex, music, friendship, humor and beauty valuable in themselves; avoid treating pleasure as an automatic prelude to punishment. Victims, perpetrators and witnesses have lives and interests beyond one event, and affection or understanding does not guarantee forgiveness, reform or justice.

Research connections include [Disco Elysium’s substance systems](source-atlas.md#g66), [The Witcher](source-atlas.md#w07) and [Westeros](source-atlas.md#w10) on sexual violence and its treatment, [Wrath of the Righteous](source-atlas.md#g59) on demonic and undead identities, [Project Zomboid](source-atlas.md#g37) on apocalypse survival, [The Forest](source-atlas.md#g137) on horror, and [Warhammer 40,000](source-atlas.md#w26) on supernatural danger and religious cruelty. Their dossiers include disagreements about presentation and effects; this coverage does not assert that every adult enjoys every subject.

This update establishes the foundation and its coverage. Individual scene writing and the later specific inventories remain separate work.

## Motives and experiences to make possible

These are design dimensions, not personality diagnoses, universal needs or a scoring model. Select motives for particular people, factions and player opportunities; let them collide rather than putting every motive into every actor.

| Motive or experience          | What someone might pursue                                                      | Decisions and consequences that make it substantial                                                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Survival and security         | Food, shelter, bodily integrity, escape, a dependable livelihood               | Risk an expedition, submit to protection, fortify a home, sacrifice supplies or abandon a place                  |
| Competence and mastery        | Win a duel, execute a spell, build a reliable machine, perform beautifully     | Practice through meaningful challenges; choose a specialization, learn a counter, outperform a rival             |
| Power and autonomy            | Command, wealth, freedom, territory, influence over one's future               | Acquire allies or subjects, seize an asset, refuse dependence, govern the result of a victory                    |
| Status and recognition        | Glory, rank, respect, reputation, fame, being remembered                       | Compete publicly, boast, prove a claim, endure humiliation, take credit or give it away                          |
| Curiosity and wonder          | An unknown place, an explanation, a forbidden text, a new form of life         | Follow incomplete evidence, enter danger, revise a belief, keep or disclose a discovery                          |
| Possession and creation       | A collection, estate, invention, business, garden, work of art                 | Obtain materials, choose quality over scale, acquire a rare thing, display taste and defend ownership            |
| Love, attraction and intimacy | Courtship, sexual desire, partnership, trust, being known                      | Seek an adult relationship, accept or refuse commitment, face jealousy, infidelity, separation or reconciliation |
| Kinship and belonging         | Family, comrades, faith, a home, a chosen community                            | Join, inherit obligations, shelter an outsider, leave, betray or defend a group                                  |
| Care and responsibility       | Raise someone, nurse a wound, teach, protect a dependent                       | Spend scarce time, delegate, endure unequal burdens, accept help, draw a limit or fail someone                   |
| Justice and conviction        | Protect the innocent, punish an offender, uphold an oath, overthrow a tyrant   | Investigate, judge competing claims, fight, pardon, enforce a sentence or refuse a corrupt order                 |
| Revenge and resentment        | Answer a betrayal, humiliation, dispossession or death                         | Choose a target and cost, prolong a feud, harm an ally, obtain satisfaction or discover it is incomplete         |
| Greed and appetite            | More money, luxury, pleasure, access or advantage                              | Bargain, hoard, exploit, indulge, gamble or accept a profitable compromise                                       |
| Domination and cruelty        | Submission, fear, revenge on the vulnerable, enjoyment of another's suffering  | Threaten, imprison, terrorize or abuse power; resistance, concealment, complicity and lasting harm follow        |
| Faith and transcendence       | Salvation, communion, sacred duty, meaning beyond oneself                      | Worship, doubt, convert, sacrifice, pursue revelation, manipulate belief or oppose a false authority             |
| Play, humor and delight       | Sport, mischief, festivals, beautiful sights, companionship                    | Compete, perform, flirt, joke, explore or linger without needing an economic justification                       |
| Identity and transformation   | Become a knight, parent, monster, ruler, artist or someone free of an old role | Train, swear an oath, change a body, reject an inheritance, keep some commitments and lose others                |
| Grief, endurance and legacy   | Remember a person, survive a loss, pass something on                           | Mourn, rebuild, refuse closure, create a memorial, found a lineage or end an old obligation                      |

A benevolent act can require sacrifice and produce pride. A cruel act can bring profit or victory while leaving victims, enemies and complicity. Consequences need not be immediate, symmetrical or guaranteed moral repayment. A world can contain secure happiness and unpunished wrongdoing at the same time. Give those facts concrete people, institutions and future possibilities rather than using a universal morality meter.

## How systems produce a situation

A base-world proposal should answer the following questions for its important opportunities and threats. These are authoring prompts, not a new runtime schema.

| Question                               | What a useful answer establishes                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is wanted, and by whom?           | Specific goals: obtain the pass, marry the heir, survive winter, take the fortress, protect a friend, become champion                                                 |
| Why now?                               | An opportunity, deadline, discovery, rival move or accumulated pressure; quiet ambitions can also proceed without an emergency                                        |
| What exists before the player arrives? | People, assets, loyalties, plans, history and ongoing work that do not exist solely to accommodate a protagonist                                                      |
| What is known or suspected?            | Perceptible clues, rumors, secrets, mistaken beliefs and evidence that can actually change a decision                                                                 |
| What resists the attempt?              | An enemy, competitor, incompatible commitment, physical condition, law, unknown rule or limited resource                                                              |
| Which methods are available?           | Fight, persuade, deceive, steal, investigate, trade, create, cooperate, endure, retreat or abandon; not every method works in every situation                         |
| What does each method cost or risk?    | Time, material, bodily harm, trust, exposure, an opportunity, a principle or a relationship; another actor may bear the cost                                          |
| What can be won or lost?               | A real objective, resource, position, capability, place, life or bond; victory must not be only a congratulatory line                                                 |
| Who acts afterward?                    | Survivors, beneficiaries, witnesses, enemies, inheritors, institutions and people whose plans were interrupted                                                        |
| What can remain settled?               | A conquered fort, reconciled friendship, functioning mill or enjoyed festival can stay achieved; the world need not invalidate every success to generate another task |

The player can change allegiance or pursue an unanticipated objective. An enemy can refuse negotiation, a friend can sell information, and a battle can be worth winning for its challenge and reward. Uncertainty and authored limits should create choices; a model must not guarantee success because it finds the proposed action sympathetic.

## Combat, danger and power as full parts of play

Combat requires an objective, participants with capabilities, a space that matters, readable opportunities and actual outcomes. Its pleasures can include anticipation, timing, tactical judgment, build expression, teamwork, spectacle and decisive superiority earned through preparation. A fight does not need to be reframed as rescue to deserve inclusion. A player may seek a championship, defeat a monster for treasure, conquer a stronghold or serve an ambitious ruler.

Develop enemy behavior and differentiation alongside player options: a disciplined spear line, opportunistic raiders, an ambush predator, a rival duelist and a curse-bearing creature should pose different decisions. Know what they protect, desire, fear, perceive and do when circumstances change. Some fight to the death; some rout, bargain, surrender or hunt a retreating party. These are authored differences, not a universal promise of mercy.

Weapons and abilities need useful strengths, commitments, vulnerabilities and counters. The same bridge, fire, door, weather or cargo can matter in travel, work and battle. Preparation can earn a decisive advantage; improvisation can rescue a failing plan. Resource and loadout decisions, allied roles, morale, deception and positional control create depth without requiring every possible combat subsystem at once.

Distinguish an immediate win from its aftermath. Killing a tyrant can liberate people and leave a succession struggle. Capturing an enemy can produce ransom, an escape, information or a burden. A raid can yield loot and a feud. Failure can mean death, injury, retreat, captivity, lost territory or the end of an intended run. Some worlds offer recovery, successors or retries; others deliberately make a particular loss final. Technical errors and hidden interface commitments are not valid substitutes for those authored stakes.

## People and factions have their own purposes

A character needs a desired future, a history that helps explain it, useful abilities, relationships, available means and a view of what they will accept. Include admirable, malicious, compromised and ordinary people. Positive experiences can form a hero or a confident exploiter; tragedy can deepen care or grievance without determining either. Do not require every warrior to be reluctant, every ruler to be corrupt, every antagonist to be misunderstood or every civilian to be kind.

A faction needs a purpose, constituency, resources, territory or access, means of enforcement, dependencies, internal disagreements and a response to success or loss. A military order can keep travelers safe while enforcing an unjust tax. A criminal patron can love a family and deliberately terrorize a neighborhood. A religious movement can offer genuine consolation, wield political power and contain both sincere reformers and fanatics. Those tensions become play when particular actors must decide what to do.

Track relationships that matter, rather than one universal approval score. Affection, trust, fear, respect, obligation, desire, dependence and ideological agreement can diverge. A companion may love someone they oppose politically. A rival may respect an enemy's competence and still intend to kill them. A victim does not owe forgiveness after an explanation. An ally can accept a costly compromise, refuse it or remember it differently.

These premises may be changed by a creator. Each proposal should connect home, opportunity, opposition, advancement and aftermath through named people and places. A modern city does not need a permanent war to contain real stakes. A fantasy adventure does not need to apologize for combat. A harsh survival world can include joy without promising that its inhabitants are safe. Peace can be an achievement or a satisfying starting condition, not merely downtime between mandatory disasters.

## Completeness, scope and evidence

A complete base accounts for the major dimensions above and shows how they connect in playable situations. It is not an exhaustive encyclopedia, a promise that every possible mechanic is supported, or a demand to enable all systems in the first scene. The category coverage tables identify the breadth for later inventories; the retained pattern cards preserve useful ideas; the worlds and combinations show selected systems operating together.

For a proposed slice, review what a player wants, what they can do, why an approach is interesting, what can resist it, what success changes and what failure means. Check pleasure, pace, readability, discovery, meaningful opposition and lasting consequences. Include a test of competence, a personal stake, a desirable reward and a reason to return where the premise calls for them. No universal template requires all of those in every quiet scene.

Use the [selection guide](selection-and-scale.md) to choose bounded experiences and evidence of value. Criticality orders candidates for an intended game; it must not assign higher priority merely because an activity is benevolent or lower priority merely because it involves violence. Build scope is an engineering estimate, not a moral judgment or a ranking of artistic worth.

Fictional authority and actual platform authority remain separate under [engine/world boundaries](../engine-and-world-boundaries.md#4-what-must-remain-protected). An admitted world may contain theft, assassination, slavery, coercive magic, corrupt institutions and disobedient NPCs. Those fictional actions do not grant access to real accounts, ungranted private information, billing, executable code or another user's control of the application. This integrity boundary does not require the fictional characters to be helpful, honest or fair.

These are proposals informed by stored research and critical interpretation. No playtest, new population study, current external-source audit or empirical validation of a psychological or social model is claimed. Implementation owners and acceptance criteria remain in [the maintainer index](../maintainers/README.md); this revision closes none of them.
