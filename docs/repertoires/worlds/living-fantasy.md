# FA proposal: The Borrowed Dawn

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Design foundation](../design-foundation.md) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical lore, approved magic rules or implemented content.** The Borrowed Dawn is a fantasy of useful magic, dangerous ruins, ambitious people and a home worth returning to. Learn a craft or a fighting style, gather a party, pursue treasure, fall in love, challenge a predatory order or seek power within it. A feast, a beautiful lamp and a quiet friendship are genuine pleasures; so are a well-aimed arrow, a hard-won duel and the discovery of a legendary weapon. The region contains kindness, oppression and people who knowingly do evil. Not every conflict can be reconciled, and not every happy life must conceal a tragedy.

**Estimate:** Play, High, **3/New** for selected magical effects, combat dependencies beyond supported actions, transformations and unusual creatures. Most names, customs and biographies are **1/Compose**; ordinary relationships, equipment, work and exploration are **2/Extend** candidates. These are design estimates, not proof of implemented behavior. The first arc uses one town, one expedition site, a small cast subset, a limited combat kit and one magical family. Wider religions, dragons and other traditions give the setting room to grow without becoming prerequisites for every scene.

## A valley with room for several kinds of life

Peoples and cultures overlap. Humans, long-lived grove residents, transformed couriers and dragons need not each have one nation, faith or moral outlook. Species does not supply every legendary power or determine whether a person is heroic, ordinary or cruel. Predatory monsters, hostile soldiers and malicious individuals still exist; understanding them need not make them harmless.

## Three possible magical foundations

**Select the Art of Borrowing for this proposed first arc.** The other two are alternative expansions, not additional mandatory systems. The selected version moves warmth between supported targets; light transfer can be a later addition. It serves cooking, warmth, preservation, healing support and combat through the same bounded law.

| Foundation                | What it enables                                                                                                              | Necessary limit                                                                                                                               | Potential / gap                                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Art of Borrowing**  | Transfer stored warmth to a basin, person, tool or weapon; cool a hot surface, charge a blade or discharge a dangerous burst | Known source and recipient, finite capacity, short visible range, transfer loss and interruption; no free creation or recursive amplification | High, 3/New: admitted transfer and thermal effects        |
| **The Craft of Echoes**   | Restore a temporary doorway, bridge outline or observed sound; exploration, performance, deception and tactical routes       | Surviving anchor, imperfect evidence, limited load and lifetime; no perfect unobserved past                                                   | Try, 3/New: anchored geometry and defined expiry outcomes |
| **The Courtesy of Names** | Invite a particular spirit or place into a voluntary relationship and ask for a bounded service                              | This tradition permits refusal and grants no ownership; separate coercive traditions would need separate rules                                | Try, 3/New: scoped agents and service effects             |

Borrowing is powerful enough to be worth mastering and dangerous enough to fear. A beginner transfers warmth from a kettle into a basin. A fighter charges a spearhead; a mage draws from a visible reservoir to burn an exposed target. Distance, cover, an interrupted channel, insulation and an exhausted source create counterplay. Coarse authored thermal states can express these decisions without a full physical simulation. Enemies use the same limits. Heating a person does not automatically heal wounds, resurrect the dead or authorize any effect a speaker can describe.

The Dawnheart is a lost, unusually capacious warmstone core, not a generator or ownership claim over the sun. Its remaining charge can protect households through a cold spell, power an expedition or arm a faction; spending it on one use leaves less for another. Recharging requires the old reservoir at the vault and time to restore its feed. A first arc fixes those quantities and opportunities rather than inventing a new source whenever a scene needs one.

Later charms can use finite targets, triggers, effects, durations and costs. Curses, hostile enchantment or true-name binding are valid fictional possibilities if explicitly authored with signs, resistance and consequences. They are not secretly part of voluntary courtesy magic. No fictional compulsion, prophecy or divine command changes platform permissions, grants unprovided private information or incurs real spending.

| **Promise cord**                           | Displays a voluntarily accepted reminder; useful to lovers, traders and expeditions without judging moral truth                                                | 3/New agreement effects; optional                                     |
| **Stormglass**                             | Holds one regional weather signature for a limited ritual or sensor                                                                                            | 3/New charge and discharge law; optional                              |

A breakthrough might be a heat exchanger, an insulated shield, a better bow or a charm that retires repetitive maintenance. Craft rewards are both useful and expressive. Knowledge, workmanship, traded supplies, dangerous salvage, hunting and negotiated help offer different acquisition paths. Not every recipe requires killing a creature, and not every resource has to be obtainable without danger. Treasure should include money, distinctive equipment and beautiful keepsakes, not only obligations to repair another service.

## History, religion and conflicting inheritance

**A century ago, the Long Dimming** followed the collapse of a great light-and-heat network during a succession war. Its engineers diverted power to defend one capital, knowingly exposing outer towns to winter. The decision and its casualties are fixed facts in this proposal; the surviving records disagree over the alternatives and who gave the final order. Some communities preserved lives through courage and cooperation, while others prospered through hoarding. An admirable rescue and a deliberate atrocity can both be true parts of the history.

**Thirty years ago, the Road Compact** joined towns through guarded shelters and maintained reservoirs. Teachers made travel safer, merchants earned profits, and the House of Returning Light blessed its protections. Exclusive contracts now let a few owners decide whose homes receive warmth. Some teachers sincerely defend standards; Sorell exploits that argument to obtain coercive power. Different factions have material interests, not interchangeable misunderstandings.

**Now, Sorell’s charter guards have seized the town’s winter reserve.** They claim a lawful debt and arrest residents who interfere. An early frost and a washed-out timber road make the stored warmth valuable now; firewood, emergency shelter and rationing can help, but each costs time or comfort. Ilyr Voss searches the Hollow Bell Vault for the Dawnheart, promising Sorell a monopoly while planning his own ascendance. A recovered ledger and the vault’s machinery offer evidence and leverage, but neither a speech nor a repaired bath can settle every claim.

The **House of Returning Light** teaches that borrowed power should leave something useful behind. Its healers, celebrants and knights provide real care; a militant chapter claims the Dawnheart must belong to the temple because secular rulers misused it before. The **Listeners at the Ridge** honor old places and dragons, but disagree over whether reverence demands submission to Vaul. Skepticism, sincere faith, exploitation and real wonder can coexist. The first arc need not decide whether gods exist; doctrine does not automatically prove a miracle or grant moral certainty.

| Active interest                                                         | Wants and means                                                                                      | Incompatible claim and lasting choice                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Veyrbridge Compact circle**: Calen, Elin and willing residents        | Restore household reserves and distribute teaching; local trust, a workshop, defenders and evidence  | Public access threatens Sorell’s exclusive income. Saving the town can still leave a costly rebuilding project or disputed leadership                                       |
| **Kest charter company**: Sorell, guards and dependent workshops        | Own the relay, repair rights and toll revenue; contracts, stored supplies, bribery and armed seizure | Will not accept free public use while retaining its monopoly. Defeat, negotiated divestment or victory changes who controls the road                                        |
| **Returning Light chapter**: Aren and his superior                      | Recover a sacred relic and protect worshippers; disciplined fighters, sanctuary and legitimacy       | Temple custody conflicts with town ownership. Aren must choose what to obey; devotion does not make every institutional order right                                         |
| **Ilyr’s inner circle**: the mage and a few paid or persuaded followers | Control the Dawnheart and force recognition; transfer expertise, dungeon access and hostages         | He intends to rule, not merely receive fair credit. Stop, depose, join or flee him; bargaining can buy time without making his goal compatible with everyone else’s freedom |

The Reed Courts and Cinder Seats remain wider relationships until a later arc selects them. Tharn and Vaul hold incompatible views of neighboring peoples: negotiated coexistence versus prey and tribute. A predator’s intelligence can make it a better antagonist without requiring a peaceful resolution that saves all interests.

## Archetypes as training paths

These are optional, recognizable play styles rather than fixed personalities. Each needs suitable equipment and training; magical or unusual bodily powers add their own admitted family. A tradition’s ethical code can be honored, broken or rejected.

| Tradition             | Fighting and adventure identity                                                                                                            | Other life and combinations                                                                                           |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Waykeepers**        | Knights, guards and paladins using sword, spear, shield, armor and formation discipline; protect an ally, hold a bridge or defeat a raider | Mediation, road care and oath-bound service; a knight can seek glory or become an oppressor                           |
| **Threshold runners** | Rogues, thieves and scouts using bows, blades, stealth, locks and ambush; steal treasure, infiltrate a fort or rescue a captive            | Investigation, courier work and stagecraft; competence does not determine ethics                                      |
| **Storm wrestlers**   | Powerful melee fighters who brace, grapple, break a line and enter a costly battle state                                                   | Heavy work and physical contests; a fierce fighter can be affectionate and disciplined outside battle                 |
| **Hearth scholars**   | Mages who manage charged tools, heat bursts and battlefield denial under Borrowing’s limits                                                | Cooking, materials, healing support and household comfort; a useful art can be turned cruel                           |
| **Echowrights**       | If Echoes is selected, ruin explorers and battlefield engineers change temporary routes and supports                                       | Archaeology, construction and performance; knowledge of a structure can help preserve or destroy it                   |
| **Green companions**  | Hunters and rangers using tracking, bows, traps and knowledge of dangerous creatures                                                       | Animal care, habitats and relationships with particular beings; nature contains affection, indifference and predation |

Start with a small selection: shield-and-spear defense, bow-and-knife scouting and a heat-channeling staff. Give each a useful opening technique, a meaningful upgrade and an enemy or terrain problem it answers. Learned competence, equipment, resources and actual execution remain distinct. Cross-training and a supported change of build let players experiment; some specialized expeditions can still require preparation or an ally.

## Names and voices

Valley names favor liquids and clear stress: **Lethren, Veyra, Senn, Calen**. Marsh names use open endings and rhythmic compounds: **Oriva, Nemi, Reedwake**. Older ridge names preserve compact clusters and long pauses in formal speech: **Tharn, Keld, Varr**. Dragons may use a short everyday name alongside a chosen epithet describing a memorable deed. An epithet may advertise real strength, vanity or a disputed reputation.

A proposed root **leth** can refer to sheltering light in an old language, while modern speakers use it in place names without knowing the origin. Scripts can differ from languages: a practical knot notation may record quantities and promises without encoding ordinary speech. These patterns borrow the principle of linguistic history, not franchise vocabulary. Keep names pronounceable, provide aliases and let individuals rename themselves. Speech should express occupation, education, humor and allegiance as well as regional origin.

## Eight people to meet

**Cast defaults: Play, High, 1/Compose** biographies. Ordinary behavioral consequences need **2/Extend**; transformation, dragon embodiment and magical work need their admitted **3/New** families. Neither profession nor species fixes a person's moral arc.

### Calen Rook, 36 — the hearth scholar who prefers a working bath

Calen grew up above a public bath and first learned magic to stop pipes freezing. He dislikes academic contests that reward spectacular power while apprentices perform endless maintenance. He wants a heat-sharing system that leaves everyone more free time. He is gentle with beginners and unexpectedly competitive at board games. His flaw is dismissing symbolic concerns as impractical. Oriva can show him why a ceremony matters even after the engineering works; his former teacher Veyra respects his skill and worries he understates the cost of broad access.

Calen can also fight: he vents a charged reservoir through a short iron staff, heats an iron grate to deny a passage and recognizes an enemy transfer before it completes. He wants to prove broad training can defend the town, but saving his workshop must not matter more to him than saving a person. Ilyr once helped him win a school contest; defeating that former friend will hurt.

### Oriva Senn, 44 — the innkeeper who remembers people's preferred silence

Oriva was raised in a noisy traveling household and discovered that good hospitality sometimes means leaving a guest alone. She runs a modest hostel in the Unfinished Cloister and wants to preserve its quiet garden as the archive attracts visitors. She loves dramatic stories but dislikes being asked for gossip. She can become territorial about changes others would welcome. Calen is a close friend; reopening a passage through her kitchen gives their disagreement a concrete test. If Echoes is later selected, a vanished doorway can make that question magical. She may support research while insisting on different access.

Oriva hides fugitives in the cloister when the charter guards begin arrests. She knows the kitchens, drains and keys, can use a crossbow from a defended doorway, and refuses to let either faction turn her guests into expendable bait. Her hospitality becomes an act with political risk, while a feast in her garden remains a possible happy ending.

### Nemi Reedwake, 23 — the courier with two forms and one careful ledger

Nemi belongs to a family of voluntary night-form runners. Childhood outings taught her route knowledge and the pleasure of moving through rain, not shame about a curse. She wants a reliable courier cooperative where members can choose human or running form according to the job. She is meticulous about parcels and cheerfully untidy at home. She dislikes being treated as representative of all transformed people. Tharn once sheltered her during a storm; Pellan wants to hire her for fragile deliveries, giving her a practical design challenge.

Nemi carries evidence through a blockade and enjoys racing the guards more than she admits. Her courier knife, thrown distractions and route knowledge help with scouting, escape and a fight she cannot avoid. She will not automatically risk her cooperative for the player; prove the mission serves something beyond another patron’s ambition.

### Pellan Vey, 58 — the potter who keeps making beginner mistakes on purpose

Pellan earned a good living through dependable ordinary pottery, then began trying deliberately awkward shapes to recover the pleasure of discovery. He teaches children and late beginners with patience but can be vain about his own supposed humility. He wants a public prototype fair where failed work is welcome. His stable family life gives him freedom to experiment; he knows others cannot take the same risks. Nemi's delivery needs inspire a new packing design. Veyra might sponsor the fair if he takes safety and attribution seriously.

Pellan makes insulated handles, ceramic heat vessels and protective tiles for an expedition as willingly as festival cups. His fair becomes a place to test equipment and discover a personal style, with useful rewards and joyful failure. Sorell offers enough money to save a struggling apprentice’s shop in exchange for exclusive designs, making Pellan’s principles costly.

### Veyra Darn, 67 — the teacher defending a system she knows must change

Veyra helped make magical training safer after an early workshop accident and built a school that has educated generations. Tuition and maintenance contracts keep it running, but those same arrangements exclude capable learners. She wants to open the curriculum without abandoning staff or standards. She enjoys gardening, terrible puns and watching students become independent. Calen's criticism hurts because she partly agrees. She is a possible reformer, obstacle or collaborator; the player need not humiliate her to change an institution.

Veyra trained Ilyr Voss and once praised his hunger for exceptional power. She can identify his transfer technique, teach an interruption and take responsibility for a reckless recommendation without assuming blame for every choice he now makes. She will defend students fiercely; affection for a former pupil does not require protecting him from defeat.

### Tharn of the Quiet Rain — the dragon with a small embarrassing collection

Tharn lives in a warm ridge cavern and can redirect fog within a limited basin under the chosen weather rule. They have known several generations of valley residents but remember imperfectly and resent being treated as an archive on demand. They collect handmade cups too small to use and enjoy hearing why people chose them. They want a protected nesting ledge and fewer ceremonial visits. Their long view can become condescension. Nemi treats them as a particular neighbor, while Calen wants a practical agreement about the bathhouse's damp laundry.

Tharn is formidable: teeth, wingbeats, a protected ledge and a limited weather gift make attacking them dangerous. They oppose Vaul’s predatory tribute regime but will not abandon their eggs to become a convenient party weapon. An alliance must address that material conflict. A dragon can be a cherished neighbor and an independently powerful actor.

### Elin Moss, 30 — the waykeeper learning to accept help

Elin joined the road service because a well-run shelter made her family's trading life possible. She is proud of preventing trouble through preparation and has never wanted to be famous for battle. She wants to train local hosts so the road does not depend on a few exhausted specialists. She enjoys embroidery and pretends her uneven stitches are intentional. An old injury makes heavy carrying difficult, but she resists delegating. Nemi offers a cooperative route; Pellan's foot-operated tools suggest ways to keep working comfortably without returning to an imagined perfect body.

Elin is an active fighter with a spear, shield and practiced command of a retreat. Her injury limits prolonged heavy carrying, not courage or competence. She can hold a doorway, teach formation discipline and stop a guard who harms a civilian; her wish to prevent battles is credible because she knows how to fight them. Aren’s pursuit of a glorious victory sometimes endangers her careful plans.

### Sorell Kest, 41 — the patron whose generosity has a lock on it

Sorell funds shelters and commissions beautiful public lamps, partly from real civic pride and partly to secure exclusive repair rights. He grew up watching an inventive parent lose credit and now treats control as protection against being forgotten. He can be funny, attentive and sincerely helpful while proposing terms that concentrate power. He wants the next Road Compact to depend on his workshop. Veyra shares some concerns about standards; Calen and Elin can propose a distributed alternative. In the proposed opening he has already chosen to seize public stores and hire armed enforcers, knowing households will go cold. His generosity is real, but it does not cancel this cruelty. He may surrender control to survive exposure; an offer that preserves his title while removing his control will face deliberate resistance.

Sorell has money, storehouse keys, a legal charter and hired fighters rather than unexplained omnipotence. His former apprentice Nalia knows his accounts. He offers the player an excellent weapon and a paid place in the new order; accepting brings real benefits, enemies and complicity, not a secret route to the same benevolent outcome.

## Four adventurers and adversaries

These additions give the existing residents people to fight beside, compete with, love, fear or oppose. Attraction and future relationships can develop through play; the proposal does not prescribe that everyone becomes a friend or romance option.

### Aren Varr, 32 — the paladin who must choose what his oath protects

Aren is a practicing knight of the House of Returning Light, trained in sword, shield, mounted travel and holding a line under attack. His parents still run a prosperous orchard; he loves harvest dances and wants to become the knight whose name children sing. He swore to protect the helpless and recover the lost Dawnheart, but his superior now orders him to deliver it to the temple even if Veyrbridge loses its winter reserve. Aren can defy the order, persuade a narrow faction within the order or enforce it. He respects Elin’s competence and envies her effortless local trust; Nalia’s thefts offend him while her courage attracts his admiration. He is earnest, proud and capable of a bad choice for a glorious cause.

### Nalia Senn, 27 — the rogue who wants the treasure and a home

Oriva’s niece learned accounts in Sorell’s countinghouse, discovered his falsified shortages and stole enough money to flee. She enjoyed the theft as well as the escape. Nalia now works with locks, climbing gear, knives and a short bow; she scouts routes, steals keys and strikes exposed opponents. She wants a share of the Dawnheart bounty to buy a place beyond any patron’s control, yet risks that freedom to protect Oriva. Sorell has proof of her earlier thefts, including one that harmed an innocent trader. Her ledger can expose him, but it also implicates her. Aren’s faith in public confession frustrates her, and she may take the treasure if the party treats her solely as a disposable lockpick.

### Ilyr Voss, 39 — the mage who would rather rule than remain brilliant and poor

Ilyr trained beside Calen under Veyra, then watched a modest safety invention earn his school more money than years of spectacular research earned him. He loves music, precise workmanship and being the most capable person in a room. He now wants control of the Dawnheart’s stored heat to dominate the valley’s roads and prove that power belongs to whoever can wield it. His techniques draw heat from exposed vessels, overload metal weapons and create short, costly bursts; a broken line of sight, a severed conduit or an emptied source defeats the method. Sorell funds him, but Ilyr plans to seize the charter after the relic is charged. He knowingly orders hostages left in the cold and burns a defecting guard’s supplies as a warning. He can bargain or flee when beaten; friendship and a sad memory alone will not make him abandon his ambition.

### Dessa Keld, 29 — the hunter who wants to be worthy of her own legend

Dessa grew up in a loving family of ridge shepherds, learned a hunting bow and spear, and left because she wanted a larger life. She tracks monsters, reads wind, prepares traps and enjoys difficult shots. Her current quarry is Vaul, a dragon who has killed travelers and stolen winter stores. She wants the bounty, the glory and an end to the killings. She can help protect Tharn’s nesting ledge, but first mistakes their refusal to join the hunt for cowardice. Her rivalry with Aren is cheerful until a contest for the finishing blow endangers someone. Nemi is an old racing friend who knows when Dessa is exaggerating. A trophy and a song can be honest rewards; she does not have to retire from hunting to grow as a person.

## A first arc with useful magic

**Arc: The Winter Reserve.** Complete a coherent local adventure before adding the whole region. Its active cast is Calen, Oriva, Elin, Sorell, Nalia, Aren and Ilyr; other residents supply a few authored scenes or services. The first version needs Veyrbridge’s hub, the road to the cloister and a three-part vault. It does not require dragon combat, weather simulation, transformation, Echoes, political simulation across every town or unlimited procedural quests.

Killing a guard, accepting a bribe or refusing the conflict changes relationships and available evidence                                                                      |
| **3. Prepare and enter**         | Nalia offers a ledger and a route into the cloister; Aren wants the relic. Purchase or craft gear, choose one companion and enter by the guarded stair, a narrow drainage route or negotiated access                                                                                                                   | Preparation changes risk and approach. The ledger proves Sorell falsified the debt; it does not compel a hostile faction to surrender                                                                                                   |

His source and timing were taught earlier; no new power appears solely to force the ending                                                                         |
| **6. Decide who keeps the dawn** | If you control the core, give it to the town or temple, sell it, negotiate an allocation or keep it. If another faction kept it, face that outcome and choose recovery, resistance or departure                                                                                                                        | Control of warmth, prices, available teaching, faction hostility and named relationships change. Saving the convoy protects the town’s existing reserve; that victory remains real. Ilyr is still seeking the Dawnheart, so entering the vault can prevent his next move, fulfill Aren’s mission or pursue Nalia’s bounty rather than repeat the same rescue. If the player walks away, conclude the local episode with the threat and consequences it actually leaves.

The pressure advances through explicit story stages and announced commitments, not an invisible timer while the player decorates a room or learns controls. Once a pursuit or assault begins, its bounded danger is real. Returning home before the climax may cost an opportunity, but does not secretly simulate an entire regional war. Offer clear stopping points and a finite ending; later adventures can follow the chosen aftermath.

The ordinary bath problem still matters. Restoring its warmth after the seizure gives Calen’s work a tangible payoff, and Oriva’s garden offers space for music, flirtation, food and reflection. [The dragon and the laundry](../combinations.md#the-dragon-and-the-laundry), [the borrowed doorway](../combinations.md#the-borrowed-doorway), [the werewolf’s first commission](../combinations.md#the-werewolfs-first-commission) and [the spell everyone stops casting](../combinations.md#the-spell-everyone-stops-casting) remain useful domestic or later expansion seeds. They do not replace the dungeon, the dangerous opponent, the treasure or the decisive choice.

## What to preserve and what to defer

- **Keep danger legible:** readable attacks, finite charge, useful armor and retreat routes; defeating a tough opponent should demonstrate learning. Some enemies refuse surrender or intend murder, and the fiction should say so plainly.
- **Keep power rewarding:** money, weapons, training, access, allies and beautiful objects provide different rewards. A basic hunting bow matters; a rare relic should feel exceptional without making ordinary equipment worthless.
- **Keep people particular:** friends disagree, opponents can possess admirable skills, love can survive incompatible loyalties, and deliberate cruelty remains cruelty. Do not turn every confrontation into a misunderstanding or every companion into unconditional support.
- **Keep pleasure real:** festivals, craft, family, intimacy and spectacular scenery need not hide a sinister twist. A good ending can genuinely improve life, while a bad or compromised ending can persist.
- **Bound the first implementation:** one transfer family, three initial combat styles, a handful of enemy roles and a finite arc. Retire repetitive maintenance once solved. Add dragon battles, curses, transformations or new spell operators only when the selected next adventure needs them.
- **Make loss and recovery a declared policy:** decide mortality, downed-state rescue, capture, healing and save/retry behavior before adoption. Do not improvise universal resurrection to undo a consequence or surprise the player with permanent loss inconsistent with prior rules.

Open choices before adoption: exact transfer costs and target states; encounter tempo and control scheme; which characters can join a party; injury, death and recovery; whether the initial three combat styles are feasible together; later transformation and dragon body support; and whether divinities eventually appear as bounded actors. These are design decisions and dependencies, not accepted engine contracts. The [shared design foundation](../design-foundation.md) owns the broader scope and tone principles.
