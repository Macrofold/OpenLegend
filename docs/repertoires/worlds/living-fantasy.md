# FA proposal: The Borrowed Dawn

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Design foundation](../design-foundation.md) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical lore, approved magic rules or implemented content.** The Borrowed Dawn is a valley of useful magic, busy towns, dangerous ruins and people making lives of their own. Learn a craft or fighting style, gather a party, pursue treasure, fall in love or seek influence over the roads. Public baths, harvest dances, a well-aimed arrow and a hard-won discovery offer different pleasures; the proposed opening brings those lives into a conflict over the town’s winter reserve.

**Estimate:** Play, High, **3/New** for selected magical effects, combat dependencies beyond supported actions, transformations and unusual creatures. Most names, customs and biographies are **1/Compose**; ordinary relationships, equipment, work and exploration are **2/Extend** candidates. These are design estimates, not proof of implemented behavior. The first arc uses one town, one expedition site, a small cast subset, a limited combat kit and one magical family. Wider religions, dragons and other traditions give the setting room to grow without becoming prerequisites for every scene.

Peoples and cultures overlap. Humans, long-lived grove residents, transformed couriers and dragons have different bodies, affiliations and individual temperaments. Their actual capabilities and histories determine how they take part in the valley.

Veyrbridge’s market sells orchard cider, practical tools, painted pottery and rare expedition finds. People race along the towpath, play board games after work, arrange marriages, quarrel over recipes and visit the bath for conversation or quiet. Adult relationships include courtship, sex, lasting partnerships and private affairs; households make their own arrangements about privacy, money and care. The hostel garden hosts music and dinners as well as traveling guests. These activities have regular participants and occasions, so they can continue between major adventures.

## Three possible magical foundations

**Select the Art of Borrowing for this proposed first arc.** The other two are alternative expansions, not additional mandatory systems. The selected version moves warmth between supported targets; light transfer can be a later addition. It serves cooking, warmth, preservation, healing support and combat through the same bounded law.

| Foundation                | What it enables                                                                                                              | Necessary limit                                                                                                                               | Potential / gap                                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Art of Borrowing**  | Transfer stored warmth to a basin, person, tool or weapon; cool a hot surface, charge a blade or discharge a dangerous burst | Known source and recipient, finite capacity, short visible range, transfer loss and interruption; no free creation or recursive amplification | High, 3/New: admitted transfer and thermal effects        |
| **The Craft of Echoes**   | Restore a temporary doorway, bridge outline or observed sound; exploration, performance, deception and tactical routes       | Surviving anchor, imperfect evidence, limited load and lifetime; no perfect unobserved past                                                   | Try, 3/New: anchored geometry and defined expiry outcomes |
| **The Courtesy of Names** | Invite a particular spirit or place into a voluntary relationship and ask for a bounded service                              | This tradition permits refusal and grants no ownership; separate coercive traditions would need separate rules                                | Try, 3/New: scoped agents and service effects             |

A beginner learns Borrowing by transferring warmth from a kettle into a basin. Cooks preserve food, potters regulate a kiln and travelers warm a shelter. A fighter charges a spearhead; a mage draws from a visible reservoir to burn an exposed target. Distance, cover, an interrupted channel, insulation and an exhausted source create counterplay. Coarse authored thermal states can express these decisions without a full physical simulation. Enemies use the same limits. Heating a person does not automatically heal wounds, resurrect the dead or authorize any effect a speaker can describe.

The Dawnheart is a lost, unusually capacious warmstone core that stores rather than generates heat. Its remaining charge can protect households through a cold spell, power an expedition or arm a faction; spending it on one use leaves less for another. Recharging requires the old reservoir at the vault and time to restore its feed. A first arc fixes those quantities and opportunities rather than inventing a new source whenever a scene needs one.

Later traditions can add charms, curses or true-name binding, each with defined targets, signs, effects, resistance, duration and costs. The Courtesy of Names remains a distinct practice of voluntary invocation. The [design foundation](../design-foundation.md) owns the boundary between fictional powers and platform authority.

A breakthrough might be a heat exchanger, an insulated shield, a better bow or a charm that retires repetitive maintenance. Makers earn income, recognition and the pleasure of a finished piece. Materials come through trade, gathering, salvage, hunting and negotiated help. Treasure includes money, distinctive equipment and beautiful keepsakes, giving an expedition several kinds of worthwhile return.

## History, religion and conflicting inheritance

**A century ago, the Long Dimming** followed the collapse of a great light-and-heat network during a succession war. Its engineers diverted power to defend one capital, knowingly exposing outer towns to winter. The decision and its casualties are fixed facts in this proposal; the surviving records disagree over the alternatives and who gave the final order. Some communities preserved lives through courage and cooperation, while others prospered through hoarding. Later generations rebuilt smaller networks and developed the bathhouses, traveling schools and lamp festivals now associated with the valley.

**Thirty years ago, the Road Compact** joined towns through guarded shelters and maintained reservoirs. Teachers made travel safer, merchants earned profits, and the House of Returning Light blessed its protections. Exclusive contracts now let a few owners decide whose homes receive warmth. Some teachers sincerely defend standards; Sorell exploits that argument to obtain coercive power. The schools still produce useful work and close friendships alongside those disputes. Veyra helped establish their shared curriculum; Tharn witnessed the earlier ledge treaty that the Compact carried forward.

**Nineteen years ago, Rovan took the Three Lantern Crown.** His conquest joined three towns under one ruler without making him king of the whole valley. Aren fought in his later campaigns, and Elin led the retreat at the Glass Stair. Elsewhere, Mereth's marsh-flood warning and organized evacuation established a following that survived a later failed prediction. Dessa's hunts, Nalia's robberies and Ilyr's siege contracts have likewise left witnesses, possessions and enemies in the present. Nine Ash, where Ilyr deliberately killed prisoners, remains a crime protected by influential clients rather than a forgotten rumor.

**Now, Sorell’s charter guards have seized the town’s winter reserve.** They claim a lawful debt and arrest residents who interfere. An early frost and a washed-out timber road make the stored warmth valuable now; firewood, emergency shelter and rationing can help, but each costs time or comfort. Ilyr Voss searches the Hollow Bell Vault for the Dawnheart, promising Sorell a monopoly while planning his own ascendance. A recovered ledger and the vault’s machinery offer evidence and leverage; armed supporters, supplies and competing claims determine what each faction can enforce.

The **House of Returning Light** teaches that borrowed power should leave something useful behind. Its healers, celebrants and knights provide real care; a militant chapter claims the Dawnheart must belong to the temple because secular rulers misused it before. The **Listeners at the Ridge** honor old places and dragons, but disagree over whether reverence demands submission to Vaul. Mereth's independent pilgrims maintain shelters and follow her Return of Morning teaching; neither her followers nor the established temple can demonstrate that her dreams reveal a god's intentions. Skepticism, sincere faith, exploitation and real wonder can coexist. The first arc leaves the existence of gods open while giving belief visible effects in ceremony, service and allegiance.

Rovan’s court, Mereth’s pilgrimage network, the Reed Courts and the Cinder Seats remain wider relationships until a later arc selects them. Rovan has real soldiers and money, but Veyrbridge is outside his jurisdiction; dispatching a royal force would be a political intervention, not an automatic response to a stranger’s request. Tharn negotiates with neighbors and tends a household; Vaul hunts travelers and demands tribute. Their different aims give a later dragon expedition specific allies, dangers and stakes.

## Archetypes as training paths

These are optional, recognizable play styles rather than fixed personalities. Each needs suitable equipment and training; magical or unusual bodily powers add their own admitted family. A tradition’s ethical code can be honored, broken or rejected.

| Tradition             | Fighting and adventure identity                                                                                                            | Other life and combinations                                                       |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Waykeepers**        | Knights, guards and paladins using sword, spear, shield, armor and formation discipline; protect an ally, hold a bridge or defeat a raider | Mediation, road care, tournaments and oath-bound service                          |
| **Threshold runners** | Rogues, thieves and scouts using bows, blades, stealth, locks and ambush; steal treasure, infiltrate a fort or rescue a captive            | Investigation, courier work, games of concealment and stagecraft                  |
| **Storm wrestlers**   | Powerful melee fighters who brace, grapple, break a line and enter a costly battle state                                                   | Heavy work, wrestling contests and demonstrations of strength                     |
| **Hearth scholars**   | Mages who manage charged tools, heat bursts and battlefield denial under Borrowing’s limits                                                | Cooking, materials, healing support, performance and household comfort            |
| **Echowrights**       | If Echoes is selected, ruin explorers and battlefield engineers change temporary routes and supports                                       | Archaeology, construction, preservation and performance                           |
| **Green companions**  | Hunters and rangers using tracking, bows, traps and knowledge of dangerous creatures                                                       | Animal care, habitats, wilderness travel and relationships with particular beings |

Start with a small selection: shield-and-spear defense, bow-and-knife scouting and a heat-channeling staff. Give each a useful opening technique, a meaningful upgrade and an enemy or terrain problem it answers. Learned competence, equipment, resources and actual execution remain distinct. Cross-training and a supported change of build let players experiment; some specialized expeditions can still require preparation or an ally.

## Names and voices

Valley names favor liquids and clear stress: **Lethren, Veyra, Senn, Calen**. Marsh names use open endings and rhythmic compounds: **Oriva, Nemi, Reedwake**. Older ridge names preserve compact clusters and long pauses in formal speech: **Tharn, Keld, Varr**. Dragons may use a short everyday name alongside a chosen epithet describing a memorable deed. An epithet may advertise real strength, vanity or a disputed reputation.

A proposed root **leth** can refer to sheltering light in an old language, while modern speakers use it in place names without knowing the origin. Scripts can differ from languages: a practical knot notation may record quantities and promises without encoding ordinary speech. These patterns borrow the principle of linguistic history, not franchise vocabulary. Keep names pronounceable, provide aliases and let individuals rename themselves. Speech should express occupation, education, humor and allegiance as well as regional origin.

### King Rovan Leth, 56 — the king whose crown has dents

Rovan took the Three Lantern Crown nineteen years ago after six seasons commanding hired cavalry in the salt country. He broke the siege of Lethcourt, accepted its council's invitation to rule, then conquered the two neighbors who disputed the invitation. He later executed surrendered mutineers who killed his chosen governor. The orders bear his seal; he defends them without pretending he was absent. His reign also reopened the eastern road, ended a toll war and paid for a theater that stages splendidly rude plays about him. He attends them.

His dented crown really was hammered from the breastplate of the commander he defeated at Lethcourt; Pellan's father helped fire the forge. His assets are three fortified towns, a solvent treasury, household cavalry and loyal veterans including Aren. He cannot command independent Veyrbridge, and his council must consent to a new war tax. Sorell's company supplies his forts, which makes intervention in the seizure inconvenient rather than impossible.

Rovan visits the valley for the lamp festival and the baths his late husband loved. Meet him deciding pensions, inspecting horses or demanding another verse of a filthy marching song. He wants a dependable eastern alliance and to choose his successor while still alive. He can buy intelligence, commission escorts or grant access to old campaign records; persuading him matters because he can actually honor an offer.

### Tharn of the Quiet Rain — the dragon who remembers the first festival

Tharn was already grown when the Long Dimming struck. They carried families across the broken ridge road, then destroyed the fortified storehouse that had refused them warmth. The relief convoy and the soldiers killed inside are recorded by different towns. Fifty years later, Tharn negotiated the nesting-ledge boundary still marked on Elin's route maps. Festival singers now claim the dragon swallowed winter itself. Tharn usually lets the song finish; their actual body, weather gift and memory have limits.

Their warm cavern contains eggs, treaty gifts, an alarmingly neat arrangement of enemy helmets and handmade cups too small to drink from. Pellan supplies new cups without solemn ceremony. Nemi has slept beneath their folded wing during a storm; Dessa knows they once lifted a collapsed wagon off her expedition. They oppose Vaul's tribute regime and remember him as a hungry, boastful young dragon before he became a practiced killer.

Tharn wants the ledge protected, an old treaty honored and fewer strangers reciting their own history at them. An audience can become a meal, an argument about a stolen heirloom or negotiations for one specific intervention. They cannot safely abandon the eggs for a distant campaign. Teeth, flight and a narrowly bounded basin-fog gift would need their supported later rules; neither legend nor age grants unrestricted magic. Their recollection of a lost road may also be honestly wrong.

### Aren Varr, 47 — the royal knight with two banners

Aren first fought as an orchard boy in a borrowed helmet. He has since escorted an exiled court through three winters, won the White Ford tournament twice and commanded Rovan's rear guard at the Glass Stair. His twelve surviving household knights still take his orders. He returned through a burning gate three times at the Glass Stair, carrying the trapped standard-bearer on the last trip. Thirty people escaped through the gap his company held. The song names Aren; Elin remembers the soldiers who stood with him.

He became a Returning Light paladin after the campaign, keeping his royal commission with both institutions' consent. This gives him two legitimate superiors, not a magical guarantee that their orders agree. The chapter now demands the Dawnheart; Rovan wants the road secure and his supply contracts intact. Aren can hold a bridge, organize a disciplined withdrawal and defeat accomplished fighters. He cannot feed his knights indefinitely without their pay or order townsfolk to obey the crown.

He loves orchard dances, extravagant clothes and tournament acclaim. His old marriage ended amicably; Nalia's nerve currently fascinates him. Encounter him sparring, settling a veteran's quarrel or singing a disastrously sentimental duet with Rovan. He offers serious training, a paid expedition place or a formal duel. His present ambition is command of the royal household, and surrendering the relic against orders could cost a career he has already built.

### Veyra Darn, 67 — the teacher whose pupils run the valley

At thirty, Veyra designed the relay that kept the flooded town of Hesh warm for nine days. She then spent seven years traveling between hostile guilds until they accepted a common safety curriculum. The school she founded has educated bath keepers, military engineers, wealthy patrons and notorious criminals. Its graduates send her winter gifts from towns she has never visited. The phrase “Veyra's hands” now means reliable work; she dislikes finding it on shoddy equipment.

Her reach is practical: laboratories, scholarships, an archive, a certification seal and former pupils who answer letters. Her own field technique remains exceptional but depends on visible charged sources, finite storage and uninterrupted transfers. She cannot conjure a reservoir. An old workshop accident damaged her hearing, so she places visitors where she can see their faces rather than performing a mysterious sage's silence.

Veyra trained both Calen and Ilyr, recommended Ilyr's first siege commission and withdrew that endorsement after Nine Ash. She now wants to open the curriculum while keeping her staff paid; Sorell owns part of the school's maintenance debt. Encounter her pruning roses while two important graduates wait to argue. She can authenticate machinery, teach a counter-technique or call in a favor. She will also interrupt high politics to hear Pellan's worst pun and shamelessly steal it for her next lecture.

### Mereth Venn, 64 — the prophet who keeps the failed prediction

Mereth worked marsh ferries as a young woman, sang at funerals for a decade and has spent thirty years preaching the Return of Morning. She warned the Reed Courts to move their granaries before the great flood and organized the boats that actually moved them. Her warning and the rescue are documented. Whether the dream she describes came from a god remains unresolved. Her prediction that a dead prince would return at midsummer failed publicly; she keeps its painted placard beside her preaching stool.

Three pilgrim shelters, regular donations and listeners in seven towns make her influential regardless of revelation. She persuaded two feuding abbots to reopen a road and once sheltered a murderer whom Aren later arrested. They still disagree about that decision and still eat together. She condemns Vaul's tribute as worship bought through fear, which puts some Ridge Listeners against her. Tharn calls her an excellent ferryman and an exhausting guest.

Mereth wants a winter pilgrimage and the independence to criticize temple custody of the Dawnheart. Meet her serving breakfast, hearing an embarrassing marital complaint or arguing loudly with a hymn writer over a rhyme. She can arrange lodging, witnesses or a crowd willing to hear a claim, but followers can refuse. A petition does not yield certain prophecy, mind-reading or a new spell. She loves her authority, her listeners and very sweet pears; none requires a hidden fraud or a future sainthood.

### Dessa Keld, 38 — the monster hunter who has already earned the song

Dessa has crossed the salt deserts, served two mercenary companies and returned from eleven paid hunts. She killed the antlered ravager of Gannet Pass, brought its poisoned survivors home and sold the skull for enough to buy a ridge lodge. Three companions share the lodge and the next expedition's profits. She also helped sack a border fortress for a duke who never paid; the ducal silver in her cupboard came from the subsequent looting. She will tell either story when the audience asks a question worth answering.

The song “Dessa and the Hundred Teeth” has reached towns she has not. She likes it enormously. The trophy has precisely one hundred teeth, and Nemi's uncle, who made the shot that stopped it running, sometimes performs the song with her. Her actual advantages are field experience, maps, prepared equipment, a practiced bow and spear, and partners who know when to trust her. She cannot kill an armored dragon with an ordinary arrow.

Vaul is her next chosen quarry: a dangerous bounty, a magnificent trophy and a challenge worthy of her reputation. Tharn has supplied observations, not a promise to fight. Meet Dessa auctioning trophies, training dogs or discussing a disastrous romance over supper. She buys credible tracks, hires competent scouts and can lead a bounded expedition. Aren is an old tournament rival; she wants to beat him at something again before either becomes too respectable.

### Nalia Senn, 35 — the thief who pays her crew first

Nalia learned accounts under Sorell, stole his falsified shortage ledger and spent the next fourteen years moving between countinghouses, roofs and prisons. She burgled the governor's silver room at Lethcourt during Rovan's coronation anniversary, escaped the salt-country prison of Karr and bought the contracts of four people who now form the Ladder crew with her. These were different jobs, with different victims. She once sold a partner's hiding place to clear her own debt; the partner survived imprisonment and will not accept an apology as payment.

She owns a discreet warehouse, pays a fence, keeps two reliable locksmiths working and divides successful takings before buying luxuries. Her crew follows a profitable leader, not an inevitable queen of every thief. Nalia likes velvet coats, difficult locks and the moment a powerful mark realizes they have been beaten. She is genuinely devoted to Oriva, her aunt, whose hostel is off limits to the crew's thefts.

Last month she returned to Sorell's countinghouse and stole his current winter-reserve ledger: its original transfers and later alterations show how he manufactured the town's debt. His surviving records can expose her as well as him. She wants the Dawnheart bounty and a secure foothold in Veyrbridge without surrendering her independence to a king or guild. Meet her pricing stolen relics, paying wages or negotiating over property she has already taken. She can supply access, tools, a competent companion or a well-paid burglary. Aren's attraction is welcome; his imagined right to reform her is not.

### Ilyr Voss, 39 — the siege mage who sold Nine Ash

Before Sorell hired him, Ilyr had already broken two fortress sieges and made a small fortune selling thermal weapons to rival employers. At Nine Ash he ordered guards to lock fourteen prisoners inside an unheated test barracks, then used their deaths to argue for a larger equipment contract. This was deliberate killing, documented by a surviving clerk; patrons protected him from prosecution. Later employers bought his expertise with knowledge of that record. His cruelty does not need a misunderstanding to explain it.

He also designed a remarkably economical travel stove and gave the design to Calen after their first successful expedition. They once crossed the chalk wastes together, played music in inns and divided a treasure honestly. Calen remembers a whole person; those memories do not undo Nine Ash. Ilyr now keeps a workshop, three practiced assistants, paid guards and letters from clients who want his next weapon. He wants lordship rather than another commission, with the Dawnheart as the force behind it.

He is exacting, lavish with competent subordinates and amused by audacity. Meet him testing an exquisite heat blade or offering an unusually good price for equipment. His techniques still need finite charged sources and sightlines. Empty his source, interrupt his transfer or fracture his coalition and he can lose. Joining him can bring actual wealth and authority within an order he intends to control.

### Oriva Senn, 52 — the innkeeper who remembers people's preferred silence

Oriva spent nineteen years running kitchens along the festival road, first in her parents' wagon, then with a puppeteer husband and later a wife who bred horses. The first marriage ended in friendship, the second in an amicable division of an inconveniently large stable; her third spouse, a mason, died after they bought the cloister. She has cooked for armies, hosted two royal weddings and refused a duke's demand to evict paying guests. She can reproduce the duke's speech so precisely that Rovan pays to hear it again.

The hostel, its garden and its keys belong to her. Former travelers send introductions, debts and gossip; she accepts the introductions and declines to trade in every confidence. Nalia has a room and a standing prohibition on stealing from guests. Mereth's pilgrims receive a negotiated rate. Calen's proposed research passage still cuts through her kitchen, however impressive its scholarly purpose.

She wants a successful dinner season, a repaired west roof and the freedom to keep a quiet room quiet. Encounter her matching visitors to rooms, teaching an old marching song or expelling a drunk she has known for twenty years. During the seizures she shelters fugitives and defends a doorway with a crossbow. She can offer lodgings, introductions and access beneath the cloister; none makes her hostel an unlimited barracks for the player's faction.

### Calen Rook, 36 — the hearth scholar who prefers a working bath

Calen learned Borrowing above his parents' bath, studied under Veyra and spent six years repairing reservoirs on the eastern road. He restored the Hesh relay after a landslide, crossed the chalk wastes with Ilyr and returned with enough expedition money to buy the bath outright. His most profitable invention is a valve that stops apprentices scalding themselves. He considers this an excellent achievement, even when visitors want to hear about the ruin instead.

He now employs three people, takes two apprentices and hosts a ferociously competitive board-game evening. A good bath has paid wages, warm towels and regulars whose preferred seat he knows. He wants a shared town reservoir because he has already seen exclusive contracts make competent work impossible. His former teacher respects the design and challenges its finances; Sorell wants to buy it. Oriva refuses to let its shortest inspection route run through her kitchen.

Calen can vent a finite reservoir through a staff, heat a grate and interrupt an exposed channel. He has used those skills under attack, though he would rather finish a successful working day than become famous for them. Meet him repairing a stubborn pump, celebrating an apprentice's first sale or beating Nalia at a game she is trying to cheat. He can teach, supply, introduce and organize. The school-contest prize cup he won with Ilyr remains beside the good crockery, an uncomfortable possession from an actual friendship.

### Elin Moss, 42 — the waykeeper who has brought people home

Elin rode her first escort at seventeen, then worked the salt roads, marsh crossings and Lantern passes. She commanded the retreat from the Glass Stair while Aren held its upper gate; the two remember different parts of the same victory. Later she ransomed a captured company with a wagon of counterfeit temple plate and escorted its furious creditor to a real payment. Mereth still laughs about the creditor, while Aren considers the fraud a disgracefully successful maneuver.

A falling beam damaged Elin's hip eleven years ago. She now captains Veyrbridge's watch and fights with spear and shield where footing and a short advance favor her. She can train defenders, plan an escort and command a retreat; prolonged running or heavy carrying remains costly. Her authority rests on paid service and residents who have seen her perform it, not on universal affection. Several smugglers hate her. One former enemy buys her breakfast because she kept a ransom agreement.

She wants reliable relief captains and a summer journey with her wife that work cannot cancel again. Meet her drilling rookies, negotiating a convoy or embroidering an aggressively inaccurate horse. Nemi's cooperative gives her routes; Pellan makes tools she can use comfortably. She will argue with Aren over command, refuse a suicidal plan and still volunteer for a difficult fight worth winning.

### Sorell Kest, 41 — the patron whose generosity has a lock on it

Sorell inherited a failing lamp shop and turned it into the Kest charter company. He bought a rival's debts during the bad harvest, took three workshops through court and paid enforcers to break a strike at the fourth. Two workers died. He compensated one family whose testimony mattered and threatened the other. Those facts coexist with shelters that have saved travelers, public lamps people love and apprentices he trained well. Civic admiration is not wholly fabricated, nor are his victims.

His present assets are repair franchises, storehouse keys, ledgers, six armed retainers and credit extended to better-born people. Rovan buys his supplies; Veyra's school owes him money; Nalia knows how his false shortages worked. He enjoys beautiful workmanship, expensive dinners and making a talented visitor feel uniquely understood. He intends the next Road Compact to run through his company and regards the winter-reserve seizure as a profitable demonstration of that future.

Meet him unveiling a splendid commission, inspecting a weapon or conducting business while a debtor waits outside. He offers real pay, excellent equipment and influence. He can be outbid, deprived of his charter, deserted by employees or defeated by force; he cannot order the king or conjure an endless garrison. Even after losing the reserve he will try to preserve a business he has spent twenty years building.

### Nemi Reedwake, 28 — the courier with two forms and one careful ledger

Nemi has worked the Reed Courts since fifteen, completed two years on the coastal packet route and helped found the six-member Reedwake courier cooperative. She has delivered wedding agreements, battlefield medicine, ransom money and a singer's spectacularly rude final letter to an ex-lover. During the marsh flood she ferried messages for Mereth, then spent the relief pay on a racing boat that promptly sank. Her cousins still reenact the launch at family dinners.

She belongs to a family of voluntary night-form runners; the proposed later transformation needs its own bounded capabilities and costs. Her useful initial competence does not depend on it: routes, climbing, a courier's knife, careful accounts and people who accept her sealed deliveries. Tharn once sheltered her during a storm, and Dessa pays promptly for reliable tracks. Elin trusts her timetable more than most official notices.

Nemi wants another profitable route and one uninterrupted holiday with her partner. She is cheerfully untidy at home and exacting about parcels. Encounter her settling a delivery dispute, challenging Iven to a towpath race or carrying evidence through the blockade. She can scout, introduce, transport and sometimes fight; hiring her includes an actual schedule and a cooperative's existing commitments. She enjoys outrunning guards, but will charge more when a client's exciting idea risks everybody's livelihood.

### Pellan Vey, 58 — the potter who keeps making beginner mistakes on purpose

Pellan apprenticed in a coastal tile yard, spent four years making kiln linings for the road builders and returned to Veyrbridge to marry his childhood sweetheart. Together they raised two children and paid off the workshop. His tiles line Rovan's theater; his sturdy cups appear in kitchens across the vale. Tharn owns seventeen of his least practical pieces. He likes being recognized in another town because somebody owns a bowl he made.

Now that dependable commissions cover the bills, Pellan tries gloriously awkward shapes. He has built three failed walking teapots without expecting any to become magical. He can be vain about his humility, but apprentices keep their own successful designs and earn their share of sales. Nemi's fragile parcels inspired his best packing system; Elin uses his foot-operated tools. Iven, his grandson, sells maps from a corner of the shop and occasionally includes Pellan's confidential glaze notes as “ancient writing.”

He wants a prototype fair, a repaired kiln and a month by the sea with his wife. Meet him teaching, bargaining or inviting spectators to a spectacular failure. He supplies insulated handles, ceramic heat vessels and protective tiles as readily as festival cups. Sorell's exclusive commission would rescue a former apprentice's struggling shop at a price Pellan understands; his shop also welcomes an uncomplicated order or an afternoon spent learning a useful skill.

### Iven Vey, 11 — the child who has mapped the wrong treasure

Iven grew up between his mother's hives, his father's ferry landing and Pellan's pottery shop. He has visited the coast twice, spent one spectacularly unpleasant week learning to swim and organized three friends into the Bridge Explorers. Their greatest discoveries are a fox den, a lost wedding ring and the old school punishment book. He sells treasure maps based on the last of these, mistaking confiscated marbles for royal jewels. His directions are good; his interpretation is not.

He wants to win the kite contest, keep his best friend from joining a rival club and spend a night in a real ruin. His parents permit the first two and refuse the third. He sometimes steals honey, returns borrowed things late and tells elaborate lies badly. He is neither secretly chosen nor an adult criminal in a small body. Nemi races him with an enormous handicap; Oriva lets the club use a garden table; Mereth answers his questions about dreams without recruiting him.

Meet Iven selling maps, arguing over club rules or asking why Aren's song credits one knight with everybody's work. He can trade an ordinary find, point out a familiar path or show a drawing of a convoy he actually saw. Adults may learn something useful from him. His knowledge follows places he has actually visited, and a conversation or a game can be a complete encounter.

## A first arc with useful magic

**Arc: The Winter Reserve.** Complete a coherent local adventure before adding the whole region. Its active cast is Calen, Oriva, Elin, Sorell, Nalia, Aren and Ilyr; other residents supply a few authored scenes or services. Aren has arrived ahead of his household knights, who are escorting Rovan on a separate road; Nalia’s other crew members have existing jobs away from the vault. Their wider connections remain real without supplying an instant second army for this local expedition. The first version needs Veyrbridge’s hub, the road to the cloister and a three-part vault. It does not require dragon combat, weather simulation, transformation, Echoes, political simulation across every town or unlimited procedural quests.

Saving the convoy protects the town’s existing reserve; that victory remains real. Ilyr is still seeking the Dawnheart, so entering the vault can prevent his next move, fulfill Aren’s mission or pursue Nalia’s bounty rather than repeat the same rescue. If the player walks away, conclude the local episode with the threat and consequences it actually leaves.

The pressure advances through explicit story stages and announced commitments, not an invisible timer while the player decorates a room or learns controls. Once a pursuit or assault begins, its bounded danger is real. Returning home before the climax may cost an opportunity, but does not secretly simulate an entire regional war. Offer clear stopping points and a finite ending; later adventures can follow the chosen aftermath.

Restoring the bath’s warmth gives Calen’s work a tangible payoff. Between stages, players can work toward a better bow, join Pellan’s fair, share dinner in Oriva’s garden or pursue a relationship. A completed project or a good evening can remain a satisfying event of its own. ## What to preserve and what to defer

The first design question is whether Borrowing, three initial fighting styles and the vault’s layout produce several satisfying approaches to the same situation. Each style needs a useful opening technique, a recognizable strength and a worthwhile upgrade. Home activities should also produce a visible result: a crafted object, a more comfortable room, a successful gathering or a changed relationship.

Later adventures can justify new capabilities. A hunt for Vaul needs a supported dragon body and its tactics; an Echoes expedition needs temporary geometry; a courier story may need transformation. Select the next experience before adding its systems. Solved maintenance can become a dependable routine, leaving attention for new projects, relationships and expeditions.

Open choices before adoption: exact transfer costs and target states; encounter tempo and control scheme; which characters can join a party; injury, death, downed-state rescue, capture, healing and save/retry behavior; whether the initial three combat styles are feasible together; later transformation and dragon body support; and whether divinities eventually appear as bounded actors. Declare loss and recovery rules before play so that permanence and available recovery match the stakes presented. These are design decisions and dependencies, not accepted engine contracts.
