# FA proposal: The Borrowed Dawn

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical lore, approved magic rules or implemented content.** The Borrowed Dawn imagines a region where magic can change ordinary life as much as heroic adventure. Its question is: _what responsibilities follow when a person can move a little warmth, restore a vanished doorway or ask a mountain's oldest neighbor for help?_ The title is a metaphor for shared opportunity, not an assertion that someone literally owns the sun.

**Estimate:** Play, High, **3/New** for the magical effect families, transformations and unusual creatures. Most names, customs and biographies are **1/Compose**; ordinary relationships, work and exploration are **2/Extend** candidates. Begin with one admitted magical law and several mundane activities. This proposal does not require implementing every spell below.

## A valley with room for several kinds of life

| Place or polity                   | Proposed character and tension                                                                                                                 | Build path                                             |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Lethren Vale**                  | Orchards, mills and small towns beneath misty limestone ridges; practical magic belongs to trades as well as academies                         | 2/Extend ordinary life; 3/New selected spells          |
| **Veyrbridge**, the starting town | A bridge, public bath, market and small teaching hall; apprentices spend too much time maintaining old enchantments                            | 2/Extend shared work; 3/New bounded charms             |
| **The Cinder Seats**              | Several dragon households occupy warm cliff caverns and negotiate with nearby communities; they are people with differing aims, not one empire | 3/New large bodies and bounded environmental abilities |
| **The Reed Courts**               | Marsh settlements elect seasonal speakers to negotiate channels and grazing; some citizens transform voluntarily into night-running forms      | 2/Extend institutions; 3/New transformation            |
| **The Lantern Principalities**    | Neighboring towns share road wards but disagree about tuition, access and who may repair them                                                  | 2/Extend local policy; 3/New wards                     |
| **The Unfinished Cloister**       | A ruined school now houses gardens, a hostel and an archive; old magical traces intersect present tenants                                      | 2/Extend places; 3/New echo geometry                   |

Peoples and cultures can overlap. Humans, long-lived grove residents, transformed couriers and dragons need not each have one nation, faith or moral outlook. Creature families are authored body and capability choices, not decorative species labels that silently grant every legendary power.

## Three possible magical foundations

Choose one as the main rule family. The other two can be absent, regional traditions or later additions after composition is understood. These are competing design options, not three mandatory engines.

| Foundation                | What it enables                                                                                      | Necessary limit                                                                                                  | Potential / gap                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Art of Borrowing**  | Move warmth, light or a narrowly specified property between compatible sources and recipients        | Explicit source, destination, capacity, range, loss and duration; no free creation or recursive amplification    | High, 3/New: finite transfer operators and bounded processes     |
| **The Craft of Echoes**   | Temporarily restore an observed trace of a doorway, tool shape or sound pattern                      | Needs a surviving anchor and permitted evidence; limited load/lifetime; cannot reveal an unobserved perfect past | Try, 3/New: temporary geometry, evidence and safe expiry         |
| **The Courtesy of Names** | Form voluntary relationships with particular spirits, places or objects and request bounded services | Names invite contact, not ownership; participants can refuse; no universal mind reading or wish fulfillment      | Try, 3/New: scoped fictional agents and admitted service effects |

The recommended starting option is **Borrowing**, because one visible transfer can support cooking, comfort, rescue, preservation and tactics. A cool basin and warm kettle provide a clearer first lesson than a menu of twenty elements. This is a design hypothesis to test, not a claim that it is inherently the best magic system.

Additional techniques can use a small grammar: target, trigger, effect, duration and cost. A rain-stiffening cloth and an ember-warning line are distinct compositions with inspectable rules. Unknown operators remain unsupported until trusted engineering supplies them. Magic cannot override platform authority, private-data boundaries, resource accounting or actual paid operations.

## Materials, technology and magical labor

| Resource                                   | Possible uses and social meaning                                                                 | Broad gap                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Warmstone**                              | Stores heat for baths, seedlings, travel and healing support; households can share charging time | 2/Extend or 3/New thermal storage and magical transfer              |
| **Witness ink**                            | Marks inspectable thresholds and records one supported crossing condition                        | 3/New finite sensing and event-triggered wards                      |
| **Echo lime**                              | Holds a temporary structural trace near surviving material; useful for surveying old buildings   | 3/New anchored geometry with load and expiry                        |
| **Promise cord**                           | Displays a voluntarily accepted, objectively specified reminder                                  | 3/New bounded agreement effects; no automatic moral judgment        |
| **Stormglass**                             | Stores one regional weather signature for a sensor or ritual                                     | 3/New explicit charge law and discharge limit                       |
| **Ordinary timber, iron, cloth and grain** | Remain essential to making, feeding and housing people                                           | 2/Extend supported material work; magic does not make them obsolete |

A technological breakthrough might be a bathhouse heat exchanger, a safer apprentice practice board, or a charm that ends repetitive maintenance. The interesting social consequence is who gains time, access or independence. Avoid making every invention dependent on dragon parts or lethal harvesting; knowledge, workmanship and negotiated help can be scarce resources too.

## History, religion and conflicting inheritance

**A century ago, the Long Dimming** followed a failed attempt to sustain an enormous light-transfer network. Some towns adapted together; others hoarded stored light. The event is remembered through repaired lamps and differing accounts of who warned whom. Its cause should be fixed in the authored scenario if players investigate it, even when characters remain uncertain. It need not have involved an evil god or a single foolish mage.

**Thirty years ago, the Road Compact** linked several towns through warded shelters. It made travel easier but placed maintenance under a small set of teaching halls. A current movement wants repair knowledge shared more widely. The argument is about safety, livelihood and access, not a simple choice between enlightened rebels and malicious teachers.

**Now, the oldest public bath is losing warmth.** This modest problem can introduce material transfers, apprenticeship, archives and a dragon's weather agreement. Solving it should improve daily life. Larger mysteries can remain available without invalidating that accomplishment.

The **House of Returning Light** teaches that borrowed power should leave something useful behind: repaired tools, taught skills or hospitality. Members disagree over whether this is sacred law, ethical metaphor or practical wisdom. The **Listeners at the Ridge** attend to old places and dragons, sometimes learning and sometimes overinterpreting silence. A skeptic can participate in care and ceremony without being secretly wrong about every supernatural claim.

Optional alternative: no historically catastrophic magic at all. The same region can have a history of gradual civic invention, rival schools and changing customs. Keep wonder without requiring every beautiful place to conceal an ancient atrocity.

## Archetypes as training paths

These are optional traditions, not personality templates or compulsory classes. Each is **Play, Try, 2/Extend** for training and equipment; magical or bodily powers add their specific **3/New** gap.

- **Waykeepers** combine protective training, mediation and road care. They can support a paladin-like fantasy without universal alignment rules or divinely certified moral certainty.
- **Threshold runners** practice observation, stealth, locks and escape. A rogue-like character may be a rescue worker, investigator, thief or theater technician; skill does not determine ethics.
- **Storm wrestlers** train intense exertion, bracing and chosen battle states. A berserker-like style need not require losing consent or becoming violent outside conflict.
- **Hearth scholars** learn transfers through cooking, materials and care, with combat as one application rather than the defining purpose of magic.
- **Echowrights** study structures, records and temporary restoration. Their craft connects archaeology, building and performance.
- **Green companions** learn animal care, habitats and negotiated relationships with unusual creatures. Nature is neither automatically benevolent nor a free army.

A person can learn across traditions. Acquired knowledge, current equipment, available resources and admitted capability remain separate. No town should require one exact party composition to solve an ordinary civic problem.

## Names and voices

Valley names favor liquids and clear stress: **Lethren, Veyra, Senn, Calen**. Marsh names use open endings and rhythmic compounds: **Oriva, Nemi, Reedwake**. Older ridge names preserve compact clusters and long pauses in formal speech: **Tharn, Keld, Varr**. Dragons may use a short everyday name alongside a chosen epithet describing a memorable deed; epithets can change and need not announce combat strength.

A proposed root **leth** can refer to sheltering light in an old language, while modern speakers use it in place names without knowing the origin. Scripts can differ from languages: a practical knot notation may record quantities and promises without encoding ordinary speech. These patterns borrow the worldbuilding principle of linguistic history, not franchise vocabulary. Keep names pronounceable, provide aliases and let individuals rename themselves.

## Eight people to meet

**Cast defaults: Play, High, 1/Compose** biographies. Ordinary behavioral consequences need **2/Extend**; transformation, dragon embodiment and magical work need their admitted **3/New** families. Neither profession nor species fixes a person's moral arc.

### Calen Rook, 36 — the hearth scholar who prefers a working bath

Calen grew up above a public bath and first learned magic to stop pipes freezing. He dislikes academic contests that reward spectacular power while apprentices perform endless maintenance. He wants a heat-sharing system that leaves everyone more free time. He is gentle with beginners and unexpectedly competitive at board games. His flaw is dismissing symbolic concerns as impractical. Oriva can show him why a ceremony matters even after the engineering works; his former teacher Veyra respects his skill and worries he understates the cost of broad access.

### Oriva Senn, 44 — the innkeeper who remembers people's preferred silence

Oriva was raised in a noisy traveling household and discovered that good hospitality sometimes means leaving a guest alone. She runs a modest hostel in the Unfinished Cloister and wants to preserve its quiet garden as the archive attracts visitors. She loves dramatic stories but dislikes being asked for gossip. She can become territorial about changes others would welcome. Calen is a close friend; an echo doorway through her kitchen gives their disagreement a concrete test. She may support research while insisting on different access.

### Nemi Reedwake, 23 — the courier with two forms and one careful ledger

Nemi belongs to a family of voluntary night-form runners. Childhood outings taught her route knowledge and the pleasure of moving through rain, not shame about a curse. She wants a reliable courier cooperative where members can choose human or running form according to the job. She is meticulous about parcels and cheerfully untidy at home. She dislikes being treated as representative of all transformed people. Tharn once sheltered her during a storm; Pellan wants to hire her for fragile deliveries, giving her a practical design challenge.

### Pellan Vey, 58 — the potter who keeps making beginner mistakes on purpose

Pellan earned a good living through dependable ordinary pottery, then began trying deliberately awkward shapes to recover the pleasure of discovery. He teaches children and late beginners with patience but can be vain about his own supposed humility. He wants a public prototype fair where failed work is welcome. His stable family life gives him freedom to experiment; he knows others cannot take the same risks. Nemi's delivery needs inspire a new packing design. Veyra might sponsor the fair if he takes safety and attribution seriously.

### Veyra Darn, 67 — the teacher defending a system she knows must change

Veyra helped make magical training safer after an early workshop accident and built a school that has educated generations. Tuition and maintenance contracts keep it running, but those same arrangements exclude capable learners. She wants to open the curriculum without abandoning staff or standards. She enjoys gardening, terrible puns and watching students become independent. Calen's criticism hurts because she partly agrees. She is a possible reformer, obstacle or collaborator; the player need not humiliate her to change an institution.

### Tharn of the Quiet Rain — the dragon with a small embarrassing collection

Tharn lives in a warm ridge cavern and can redirect fog within a limited basin under the chosen weather rule. They have known several generations of valley residents but remember imperfectly and resent being treated as an archive on demand. They collect handmade cups too small to use and enjoy hearing why people chose them. They want a protected nesting ledge and fewer ceremonial visits. Their long view can become condescension. Nemi treats them as a particular neighbor, while Calen wants a practical agreement about the bathhouse's damp laundry.

### Elin Moss, 30 — the waykeeper learning to accept help

Elin joined the road service because a well-run shelter made her family's trading life possible. She is proud of preventing trouble through preparation and has never wanted to be famous for battle. She wants to train local hosts so the road does not depend on a few exhausted specialists. She enjoys embroidery and pretends her uneven stitches are intentional. An old injury makes heavy carrying difficult, but she resists delegating. Nemi offers a cooperative route; Pellan's foot-operated tools suggest ways to keep working comfortably without returning to an imagined perfect body.

### Sorell Kest, 41 — the patron whose generosity has a lock on it

Sorell funds shelters and commissions beautiful public lamps, partly from real civic pride and partly to secure exclusive repair rights. He grew up watching an inventive parent lose credit and now treats control as protection against being forgotten. He can be funny, attentive and sincerely helpful while proposing terms that concentrate power. He wants the next Road Compact to depend on his workshop. Veyra shares some concerns about standards; Calen and Elin can propose a distributed alternative. He becomes a villain only through choices and consequences, not because his name appears in an antagonist slot.

## A first arc with useful magic

Begin with the bathhouse, hostel, market, teaching hall and a ridge path. Demonstrate one transfer using a kettle and basin. Let players improve a comfort, solve a material problem and meet a person who disagrees about how the power should be used. Only then introduce a larger composition or a dragon negotiation.

Use [the dragon and the laundry](../combinations.md#the-dragon-and-the-laundry), [the borrowed doorway](../combinations.md#the-borrowed-doorway), [the werewolf's first commission](../combinations.md#the-werewolfs-first-commission) and [the spell everyone stops casting](../combinations.md#the-spell-everyone-stops-casting). Players can be adventurers, innkeepers, teachers, makers or curious travelers. A warrior's skill remains useful without making violence the source of every resource or social opportunity.

**Anti-boredom and resilience:** temporary effects have warning and safe expiry; magic has bounded sources and targets; automation can retire maintenance; ordinary materials and routes remain useful. Preserve alternatives when a spell is unavailable. Avoid infinite amplification, coercive “true names,” involuntary mind access, universal resurrection loops, secret class requirements and a spell vocabulary so large that novices need a wiki before making tea.

Open choices before adoption: which magical foundation exists, exact transformation rules, mortality and healing limits, dragon size and body support, how public magical knowledge becomes, and whether gods exist as bounded actors or only as beliefs. No answer is fixed by this proposal.
