# SC proposal: Veyra Reach

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical world history or implemented content.** Veyra Reach is a network of inhabited planetary places whose people can reach one another but still have to learn how to live together. Its question is: _what becomes possible when unfamiliar bodies, local knowledge and repairable technology meet?_ Ground expeditions, settlements, diplomacy, work and crew relationships are the primary play.

**No playable spaceflight or navigation.** Ships are inhabited maps with rooms, crew, cargo and capabilities. Players choose destinations and preparations through a route interface; journeys resolve as abstract transitions. Optional external encounters concern crew assignments, communications, repairs and discrete station actions. There is no piloting, orbital maneuvering, dogfighting, navigable starfield or manual landing. A map of routes is a choice of connections, not a vehicle-control game.

**Estimate:** Play/Blend, High, **3/New** for alien bodies, environmental compatibility, route transitions and crew encounters. A small shared port using ordinary human-compatible bodies could be a narrower **2/Extend** composition. Lore and cast are **1/Compose** candidates. The bounded first setting below is not a promise that arbitrary planetary physics or synthetic citizens are supported.

## Four places, not an infinite interchangeable galaxy

| Planet or inhabited location                      | Distinctive opportunity                                                                                                              | Resources and limits                                                                              | Build path                                                       |

| **Pelune**, tidal terraces                        | A coastal port and farms adjust to long but predictable tide cycles; visitors can explore exposed ruins and living shore communities | Flexible salt-fiber, food cultures and tidal power; equipment corrodes without thoughtful design  | 3/New: one bounded tide/exposure model                           |

| **Sere**, forested twilight valleys               | Luminous symbiotic groves guide local travelers; unfamiliar senses and stewardship practices matter                                  | Living sealant cultures and light-reactive resins; harvesting changes habitat relationships       | 3/New: bounded ecology and nonvisual cues                        |
| **Hinge Port**, a shared ground station on Pelune | Arrival halls, mixed-atmosphere workshops, apartments and route services form the starting neighborhood                              | Repair expertise, recovered parts and trustworthy schedules are its main wealth                   | 2/Extend for ordinary port life; 3/New for atmosphere interfaces |

These are proposed authored places with a few coherent environmental laws. A new planet should enable different methods and social questions, not only provide a rarer colored ore. Regions can be added as bounded locations when there is something worth doing there.

## Peoples and cultures are different layers

**Humans** arrive from several communities with different histories, institutions and tastes. They are neither the default owners of every place nor the sole discoverers of knowledge.

**The Teral** are a proposed four-limbed, broad-bodied people with fine touch sensitivity along their forearms. Many tools can be operated by touch, but individuals differ in vision, training and preferences. Their bodies do not confer telepathy. Hinge Port's Teral builders come from both urban manufacturing towns and mobile pastoral communities; their disagreements can concern ordinary matters such as contracts and education. **Build: 3/New**, requiring explicit body interfaces and tactile evidence.

**The Nemei** are a proposed people adapted to a lower-oxygen, high-moisture environment. A visible mantle changes pattern with deliberate expression and some bodily states, but interpreting it requires familiarity and never yields a perfect emotion readout. Some use artificial displays for cross-species communication. Their cultures include coastal cooperatives and private scholarly households. **Build: 3/New**, requiring body-specific atmosphere and expressive signals; no default claim that they are universally peaceful, mystical or deceptive.

**Fabricated citizens** include synthetic bodies and software entities in admitted hosts. Some identify strongly with their makers, some with a profession or city, and some have no interest in origin stories. Replacing a limb or moving to a compatible host need not erase identity; duplication, consent, memory transfer and legal personhood require explicit selected rules. Begin with one embodied citizen rather than assuming arbitrary copying. **Build: 3/New**, with identity-preserving capability changes and tightly bounded host transitions.

A species is a body family; a people can be a cultural or political community; citizenship is an institutional relationship. These need not coincide. No species grants a moral alignment or one automatic faction.

## Institutions and a usable history

**The Landing Compact** maintains route safety and public arrival facilities. Its strength is dependable standards; its weakness is a tendency to treat local customs as paperwork exceptions. It has limited authority and can be challenged.

**The Basin Assembly** coordinates Kareth's cooling and water infrastructure. Some members favor cooperative repair rights, others want exclusive contracts to finance expansion. **The Grove Concords** on Sere are several stewardship agreements, not a single planetary government. A traveler must learn whose land and knowledge they are using.

Three generations ago, the first stable routes made regular exchange possible. Early records celebrate technical pioneers while giving too little credit to local crews who maintained arriving machinery. Forty years ago, **the Closed Winter** interrupted several routes after a calibration failure. Communities survived through local adaptation, creating a lasting debate about dependence on centralized expertise. Ten years ago, the **Repair Accord** allowed independent workshops to maintain selected gate components, lowering costs and unsettling old suppliers.

The present tension is productive: routes work, but the people who maintain them want recognition and control over their labor. This is a setting capable of progress. An alternative darker version can make the Compact coercive; a more hopeful version can emphasize reform within competent institutions. Neither requires eternal interstellar war.

Political detail is **1/Compose** as context and **2/Extend** for a few local agreements; modeled interplanetary markets, elections or conflict would be additional **3/New** scope.

## Technologies with a specific job

| Proposed name              | What it does and why someone cares                                                                               | Constraint and build gap                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wayseed lattice**        | Stabilizes an admitted transit connection between known stations; calibration creates skilled work and diplomacy | 3/New: finite route capabilities, capacity and atomic transitions; no arbitrary destination conjuring |
| **Phase kiln**             | Changes a supported feedstock into specified component structures for repairs                                    | 3/New: bounded fictional transformation, inputs, catalyst wear and outputs                            |
| **Hushmesh**               | Reduces particular sound bands in workshops and mixed households                                                 | 3/New: bounded acoustic effects; it does not hide all evidence                                        |
| **Seamscribe**             | A diagnostic projector marks measured stress or leakage on accessible surfaces                                   | 3/New: evidence-producing sensing; readings can be incomplete and require interpretation              |
| **Relay orchard**          | A small communication network crosses difficult terrain using maintainable local nodes                           | 3/New: scoped messages, latency and failure domains                                                   |
| **Borrowlight cells**      | Store illumination from a particular source for later work or art                                                | 3/New: fictional finite storage and output; no perpetual charge loop                                  |
| **Modular service frames** | Carry tools, cargo or sensors, with visible tradeoffs between weight, power and access                           | 2/Extend or 3/New depending on supported bodies and attachment interfaces                             |

The science-fiction equivalent of a “magic resource” is deliberately narrow: wayseed enables stable route technology, not every impossible effect. A creator may replace it with entirely engineered gate components, making expertise and industrial capacity the constraint. No resource implies that a hyperdrive, time machine or unrestricted matter replicator exists.

## An inhabited ship: the Common Measure

The **Common Measure** is an optional small service vessel with a galley, workshop, clinic nook, cabins, cargo room and route room. Its crew takes passengers, repairs outlying stations and transports delicate samples. It has useful imperfections: the workshop and galley compete for power during certain operations, and one cabin needs adaptation for a Nemei guest.

A journey has three playable phases: choose commitments and prepare at a place; resolve the abstract route, optionally with one bounded crew incident; arrive and act at the destination. Routine uneventful journeys can be summarized. If the ship acts externally, a communications officer negotiates, an engineer allocates power and a medic prepares shelter while a finite encounter state resolves. The player commands people and capabilities, never spatial ship motion.

The vessel can also remain permanently grounded as a community workshop. That variant preserves much of its social value with less route-system work. Ship interiors should use ordinary supported spatial behavior; the ship's route state is a separate capability, not permission to teleport any object at will.

## Names, languages and belief

Port language mixes practical compounds—**Hinge Port, Common Measure, Borrowlight**—with older place names. One Teral language proposal favors firm consonants and short repeated syllables: **Tarek, Ketta, Rulen**. One Nemei naming tradition favors open syllables and vowel transitions: **Aveli, Nemea, Seori**. These are small invented phonotactic palettes, not copies of Klingon, Elvish or a real sacred language. Families, professions and migration can mix patterns.

Teral tactile marks and Nemei mantle signs are writing or expression systems with specific affordances; they are not automatically different spoken languages. Translation can convey a technical sentence while missing a hospitality assumption. Provide diagrams, interpreters and patient questions rather than language grind.

The **Witness of Arrival** is an optional tradition in which newcomers and hosts each name something they hope the meeting will change. Some regard it as sacred, some as civic courtesy and some decline it. The **Keepers of Continuity** care for old records and repaired bodies, disagreeing over whether continuity rests in material, memory, relationships or voluntary self-identification. Their beliefs do not settle the engine's identity rules.

## Eight people to meet

**Cast: Play, High, 1/Compose** as biography. Follow-through needs **2/Extend** agency and memory; alien embodiment and environmental behavior use the **3/New** gaps above. Species descriptions never predetermine these personalities.

### Aveli Soren — the Nemei host who dislikes elaborate welcomes

Aveli grew up in a bustling harbor household where guests came and went without ceremony. Trained as an atmosphere technician, they now manage Hinge Port's arrival rooms and want hospitality to feel ordinary. Officials keep appointing them to ceremonial delegations, which they find exhausting. They enjoy dry human percussion recordings through a tactile speaker. They are warm, direct and occasionally dismissive of customs others need. Marae's dining experiments delight them when practical; Tarek helps them design a quiet shared room without turning it into an exhibit about difference.

### Tarek Voss — the Teral builder who trusts a beautiful joint

Tarek learned construction in a mobile community where buildings had to come apart without waste. At the port, he is praised for tactile precision but wants recognition as a designer, not merely a specialized pair of hands. He collects badly translated jokes and tells them with perfect timing. He believes good construction should be inspectable and can be stubborn about hidden fasteners. His former apprentice Ketta favors faster factory methods. Their disagreement could produce a better standard if neither treats the other's history as obsolete.

### Marae Linn — the human cook making a table for everyone

Marae trained in a prestigious route hotel, then left because its hospitality meant keeping differences invisible. She wants a dining room where people can share an occasion without eating or breathing alike. Her parents ran a small pleasant café; invention, not escape, brought her here. She sketches seating arrangements on every spare receipt and sometimes overdesigns an evening. Aveli reminds her that conversation can be enough. Her friendship with Seori grows through exchanging descriptions of flavors neither can safely taste.

### Ketta Rul — the Teral engineer with a debt to a method

Ketta's family workshop survived the Closed Winter aftermath by manufacturing standardized replacement parts. She values interchangeability because it once meant survival, and fears bespoke craft will make maintenance depend on irreplaceable masters. She is an excellent teacher and an impatient meeting participant. She wants independent workshops included in the next gate certification standard. Tarek taught her structural judgment; she resents his assumption that speed means carelessness. Her private hobby is making tiny useless sculptures, which quietly contradicts her public rhetoric about pure function.

### Ivo Pell — the human route clerk who wants to be corrected early

Ivo once approved a departure with incomplete local weather information. Nobody died, but a stranded crew lost weeks and their trust in him. He now asks careful questions and sometimes overcompensates with paperwork. He wants a route desk where local maintainers can flag uncertainty directly. He loves amateur theater and is surprisingly good at comic roles. Ressa sees him as an ally but worries his caution can delay urgent aid. A player can help create a better handoff instead of repeatedly reassuring him that mistakes do not matter.

### Seori Anen — the Nemei field naturalist whose family wants postcards

Seori grew up in a secure scholarly household and travels because unfamiliar ecosystems fascinate them. They are brave about fieldwork, anxious about disappointing family and delighted by ordinary market stalls. Their goal is to study Sere's living sealant with local stewards, leaving useful knowledge and fair credit. They sometimes mistake enthusiasm for permission. Marae is a friend who asks the practical questions their colleagues miss. Aveli can challenge Seori when a research visit becomes a burden on hosts, without treating curiosity itself as wrong.

### Ressa Vale — the human captain who would rather fix the schedule

Ressa inherited no ship or grand destiny; she became a captain after years coordinating ground repairs. She likes a crew that can explain its decisions and dislikes stories portraying captains as solitary geniuses. She wants the Common Measure to have predictable leave and a sustainable service route. She sings while checking stores and pretends not to notice others joining in. Her weak point is taking on one more rescue contract. Ivo can help her see the accumulated commitments; the crew can refuse without becoming disloyal.

### Morrow-in-Copper — the fabricated citizen who grows flowers badly

Morrow began as an embodied industrial inspector under a world rule that later recognized their independent citizenship. They remember their early work with mixed pride and discomfort, and choose a new name associated with a favorite repaired casing. They want to run a small nursery despite poor horticultural instincts. Precise measurement does not make them socially detached or infallible; they enjoy gossip they are actually told and dislike being asked to settle philosophical debates at dinner. Ketta maintains a compatible hand module, while Seori helps with plants without treating Morrow as a research specimen.

## A bounded first route

Start in Hinge Port with one shared workshop, one apartment cluster, one alien environmental interface and one reachable ground field site. Add the Common Measure as a familiar home only when route transitions are supported. The first expedition can repair a relay and host a meal; neither requires external combat.

Use [the alien welcome meal](../combinations.md#the-alien-welcome-meal), [the colony's unwanted miracle](../combinations.md#the-colonys-unwanted-miracle) and [the mineral nobody needs more of](../combinations.md#the-mineral-nobody-needs-more-of). The [grounded rescue ship](../combinations.md#the-grounded-rescue-ship) is an optional later crew scenario.

**Anti-boredom and resilience:** summarize routine transit; solve atmosphere access once and maintain it dependably; make repair techniques reduce material grind; limit each planet to a few consequential environmental differences. Route disruptions have alternate destinations and local activities. No endless scanning for percentage completion, universal rare-mineral ladder, species-wide personality or hidden requirement to master flight controls. Bound external encounters and keep their effects separate from real service/account operations.

Open choices before adoption: gate versus ship-transition fiction, exact alien body support, synthetic identity/copying policy, whether conflict is present at all, severity of environmental mistakes, and how much trade runs beyond visited settlements. Keep those alternatives explicit.
