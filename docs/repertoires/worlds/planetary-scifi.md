# SC proposal: Veyra Reach

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical world history or implemented content.** Veyra Reach is an inhabited frontier connected by finite, reliable routes: beautiful shores and alien forests, dangerous ruins, ambitious settlements, armed expeditions and competing claims to the next discovery. People come to discover unfamiliar life, make a home or a fortune, master a craft, find company or build an empire. Its question is: _what will you discover, build and become on a frontier whose possibilities are still taking shape?_ Exploration, fighting, invention, settlement life, diplomacy and crew relationships are primary play. A cabin of your own, a scientific discovery, a friendship and a hard-won prize offer different reasons to stay; rivalry, predation and ruthless ambition can put them at stake. See the [design foundation](../design-foundation.md).

**No playable spaceflight or navigation.** Ships are inhabited maps with rooms, crew, cargo and capabilities. Players choose destinations and preparations through a route interface; journeys resolve as abstract transitions. Optional external encounters concern crew assignments, communications, repairs and discrete station actions. There is no piloting, orbital maneuvering, dogfighting, navigable starfield or manual landing. A map of routes is a choice of connections, not a vehicle-control game.

**Estimate:** Play/Blend, High, **3/New** for alien bodies, environmental compatibility, route transitions and crew encounters. A small shared port using ordinary human-compatible bodies could be a narrower **2/Extend** composition. Lore and cast are **1/Compose** candidates. The bounded first setting below is not a promise that arbitrary planetary physics or synthetic citizens are supported.

These are authored places with a few coherent environmental laws, not interchangeable ore tiers. A sailmaw can feed on a tidal shoal, defend its clutch and retreat from injury; it does not exist solely to attack the player. Learning its behavior supports hunting, avoidance and protecting a worksite. Creatures, ruins and local people offer different kinds of uncertainty, and a beautiful grove can simply remain beautiful after its dangers become familiar.

Build one shore site before a planet-wide ecology. Begin Sere's first-contact thread with a bounded pattern of apparently deliberate grove signals: the player can observe and test it while rivals demand a quick commercial claim. The answer may reveal an unfamiliar agent, a mistaken interpretation or several collaborating organisms; the selected explanation needs actual supported behavior.

## Peoples and cultures are different layers

**Humans** arrive from several communities with different histories, institutions and tastes. They are neither the default owners of every place nor the sole discoverers of knowledge.

**The Teral** are a proposed four-limbed, broad-bodied people with fine touch sensitivity along their forearms. Many tools can be operated by touch, but individuals differ in vision, training and preferences. Their bodies do not confer telepathy. Hinge Port's Teral builders come from both urban manufacturing towns and mobile pastoral communities; their disagreements can concern ordinary matters such as contracts and education. **Build: 3/New**, requiring explicit body interfaces and tactile evidence.

**The Nemei** are a proposed people adapted to a lower-oxygen, high-moisture environment. A visible mantle changes pattern with deliberate expression and some bodily states, but interpreting it requires familiarity and never yields a perfect emotion readout. Some use artificial displays for cross-species communication. Their cultures include coastal cooperatives and private scholarly households. **Build: 3/New**, requiring body-specific atmosphere and expressive signals; no default claim that they are universally peaceful, mystical or deceptive.

**Fabricated citizens** include synthetic bodies and software entities in admitted hosts. Some identify strongly with their makers, some with a profession or city, and some have no interest in origin stories. Replacing a limb or moving to a compatible host need not erase identity; duplication, consent, memory transfer and legal personhood require explicit selected rules. Begin with one embodied citizen rather than assuming arbitrary copying. **Build: 3/New**, with identity-preserving capability changes and tightly bounded host transitions.

A species is a body family; a people can be a cultural or political community; citizenship is an institutional relationship. These need not coincide. No species grants a moral alignment or one automatic faction.

## Institutions and a usable history

Three generations ago, stable routes made regular exchange possible. Records celebrate technical pioneers while undercrediting local crews. Forty years ago, **the Closed Winter** interrupted several routes after a calibration failure. Communities survived through adaptation, mutual aid, hoarding and seizures of supply depots; surviving families remember different heroes. Ien Orali's relief journeys and later Open Door houses grew from that emergency. Twenty-three years ago, Nevia Aren's recovery of a missing calibration series reopened the unreliable Sere connection and established Vant's frontier power. Twelve years ago, Dara Venn's defense of Hinge against a concession-holders' army during the **Nine-Day Siege** made her its first elected Convenor after reconstruction. Ten years ago, the **Repair Accord**, backed by Ketta Rul's workshop cooperative, let independent shops maintain selected gate components, lowering costs and unsettling old suppliers. Some suppliers now finance new concessions, private security and campaigns to narrow the Accord.

A separate struggle secured independent citizenship for formerly company-owned fabricated workers; Morrow-in-Copper's refusal of a recall order became its defining public scene. These events have living participants, contested recollections and material remains. They are selected context for encounters, not a requirement to simulate every historical event.

The present frontier works well enough to be worth fighting over. Discovery changes bargaining power; competent institutions can protect a port while discriminating against outsiders or serving a patron. No faction has unlimited resources, a unanimous membership or a monopoly on kindness or cruelty.

A player can work for, rise within, defy or play these powers against one another. Supplying a port is different from controlling it; winning a firefight does not settle ownership, local legitimacy or a contract. Friendship can cross affiliations, and an enemy may still pay honestly or save someone they love. Local victories should endure: a won workshop, protected grove or defeated raiding crew changes the next session instead of resetting automatically.

Political detail is **1/Compose** as authored context and **2/Extend** for a few local roles and agreements. Persistent faction operations, changing territory, broader markets and armed expeditions require additional **3/New** behavior; none is claimed to exist merely because this table names it.

Ordinary expedition gear matters too: a dependable rifle, cutting tool, climbing line, field shelter, protective suit, medical kit and enough compatible supplies. Early success should come from understandable equipment and judgment before exotic devices. A player might win a fight by striking an exposed joint, draw a predator toward a rival camp, or bypass both with a climbing line; these are distinct methods with consequences, not a requirement to offer a morally clean optimum in every encounter.

Wayseed enables one specific class of route technology, not every impossible effect. A creator may replace it with engineered components, making expertise and industrial capacity the constraint. It does not imply a hyperdrive, time machine or unrestricted replicator. New mechanisms still require admitted capabilities and costs.

## An inhabited ship: the Common Measure

The **Common Measure** is an optional small working vessel with a galley, workshop, clinic nook, cabins, cargo room and route room. Its crew carries passengers, repairs outlying stations and takes survey, salvage and armed escort contracts. Ressa wants predictable leave and a sustainable business, but a valuable recovery could buy the crew its own workshop and a rival's threat can make refusing a job costly. The workshop, weapon station and galley cannot all draw maximum power at once; one cabin still needs adapting for a Nemei guest. Crew members have their own wages, loyalties, skill limits and reasons for staying.

A journey has three phases: choose commitments and prepare at a place; resolve the abstract route, optionally with one bounded crew incident; arrive and act at the destination. Routine travel is summarized. During a discrete external encounter, a communications officer can bargain or threaten, an engineer can allocate power, and a gunner can resolve a supported shot against a declared target. Other stations can shield a compartment, recover cargo or prepare evacuation. The decision may be whether to spend the rescue charge saving a stranded rival, use it to secure the prize, or withdraw with a damaged ship. Crew competence and willingness are separate; affection does not make someone a qualified gunner.

An external encounter uses finite targets, phases and outcomes. Capturing a cargo pod or forcing a hostile vessel to withdraw can be an offensive objective without simulated pursuit, manual aiming of ship motion or orbital maneuvering. Any boarding scene begins through an admitted abstract transfer into a bounded interior. The player commands people and capabilities, never spatial ship motion. Opponents can surrender, bluff, resist or disengage when the authored encounter supports those outcomes.

Between contracts, the ship is home: shared meals, music, arguments over cabin space, flirting, sexual intimacy, quiet companionship and keepsakes from places visited. A successful voyage can end in satisfaction rather than an immediate new emergency. The vessel can also remain grounded as a community workshop or contested outpost, preserving much of its social value with less route-system work. Interior movement uses ordinary supported spatial behavior; route state never grants unrestricted object teleportation.

## Names, languages and belief

Port language mixes practical compounds—**Hinge Port, Common Measure, Borrowlight**—with older place names. One Teral language proposal favors firm consonants and short repeated syllables: **Tarek, Ketta, Rulen**. One Nemei naming tradition favors open syllables and vowel transitions: **Aveli, Nemea, Seori**. These are small invented phonotactic palettes, not copies of Klingon, Elvish or a real sacred language. Families, professions and migration can mix patterns.

Teral tactile marks and Nemei mantle signs are writing or expression systems with specific affordances; they are not automatically different spoken languages. Translation can convey a technical sentence while missing a hospitality assumption. Provide diagrams, interpreters and patient questions rather than language grind.

The **Witness of Arrival** is an optional tradition in which newcomers and hosts each name something they hope the meeting will change. Some regard it as sacred, some as civic courtesy and some decline it. Ien Orali's Open Door movement practices one religious interpretation; its houses offer shelter, and its stewards disagree over his authority. The older tradition also has participants outside his following. The **Keepers of Continuity** care for old records and repaired bodies, disagreeing over whether continuity rests in material, memory, relationships or voluntary self-identification. Some challenge company ownership claims; others accept company patronage to keep their sanctuaries open. Their archive can shelter a vulnerable citizen or expose an uncomfortable past. Belief can motivate courage, exclusion, comfort or ambition; it does not settle the engine’s identity rules.

## Fifteen people to meet

**Cast: Play, High, 1/Compose** as biography. Follow-through needs **2/Extend** agency and memory; alien embodiment and environmental behavior use the **3/New** gaps above. These are encounterable inhabitants with existing lives and commitments, not fifteen prospective player protagonists. Species descriptions never predetermine their personalities. Stated deeds are proposed historical facts; attributed stories, accusations and religious interpretations are beliefs people hold about them. Knowing a reputation does not reveal its subject's private history.

Dara, Orek, Nevia, Morrow and Ien already occupy unusually large places in the Reach's political, military, industrial or spiritual life. Their reach differs: office, soldiers, capital, public standing and devotion are not interchangeable powers. Other people below can be accomplished, formidable or unforgettable without secretly governing the frontier. Introduce the cast across repeated visits and selected locations; a first arrival need not convene all fifteen.

### Dara Venn — the elected marshal who held the arrival hall

Dara's portrait hangs in the arrival hall because she held it during the Nine-Day Siege twelve years ago. The human commander brought scattered ground crews behind the barriers, personally led the counterattack that broke the fuel-yard occupation and persuaded the Basin Assembly to send supplies. Survivors elected her First Convenor; she has won two further terms while retaining the marshal's commission. The port's protection is real. So is the South Locks detention order she signed afterward: seventy people imprisoned without hearing, including rivals whose property financed reconstruction. Some remain dispossessed.

She intends to make Hinge the Compact's regional capital and secure a successor who will preserve her work. Her grown son moved to Kareth rather than serve in her administration; their affectionate, furious correspondence is not public. She plays cards badly, funds a youth percussion group and remembers the names of soldiers she lost. Aveli helped her during the siege and now opposes her passenger registry. Ivo's records can expose the limits of her legal claim to salvage.

Meet her opening a seized crate herself, impatient with both a smuggler's excuses and an officer's careless inventory. She can grant a license, command a raid or make a formidable enemy. Her sixty constables, council votes and finite budget cannot occupy every shore or overrule the Basin Assembly.

### Aveli Soren — the host with five ports behind them

Aveli, a Nemei atmosphere technician, has received arrivals in five ports, survived two failed businesses and been married twice. Their first boarding house on Pelune prospered until a wealthy guest bought the building and tripled the rent. They spent the next seven years managing mixed-atmosphere workers' accommodation on Kareth, where a dry room could be an inconvenience to one resident and a medical emergency to another. During Hinge's siege, Aveli kept the arrival rooms open to families from both sides and threw out a Compact recruiter trying to enlist people from the queue. Dara never forgot either act.

Now they manage the same rooms, know which promises visiting officials have broken before, and want to buy the adjoining bathhouse with three coworkers. They enjoy tactile percussion, affectionate gossip and a recent, deeply undramatic courtship. Elaborate public welcomes exhaust them; refusing a ceremony can nevertheless hurt someone whose arrival deserves recognition. They have made that mistake before.

A decorated visitor arrives with a leaking atmosphere seal; Aveli repairs it while recounting the last time this person promised to pay. They can find the player a room, introduce a previous guest or insist that Orek wait his turn. They know names and histories learned over the counter, and quite a few guests have fooled them.

### Orek Tal — the mercenary victor of the Red Stair

Across the Reach, recruits repeat the story that Orek held the Red Stair alone. The broad-bodied Teral actually held it with thirty-one exhausted fighters, Tarek's folding defenses and a Basin water crew who refused to abandon the position. That victory ended a six-month ground campaign and made Red Wake expensive to hire. The songs omit his next contract: he ordered eight surrendered depot guards killed because transporting prisoners threatened the timetable. He has never claimed the order came from someone else. Some veterans left; the company he now leads kept the victory and the money.

He has fought on all three inhabited worlds, lost a spouse to an ordinary illness, trained two generations of soldiers and failed spectacularly at running a restaurant. He still cooks enormous meals. His ambition is a permanent hall and a recognized seat at the Compact's negotiating table, where he could stop depending on patrons like Nevia. He pays his soldiers promptly and enjoys winning battles; neither makes him safe to trust without terms.

Orek can recruit, teach, protect or attack the player. In a planning room he welcomes a sound objection and humiliates a bluff. His company needs wages, compatible ammunition and rest; he cannot promise every detachment's loyalty. Ressa respects his competence and will not place her crew under his command.

### Marae Linn — the cook who fed a traveling theater

Before the dining room, Marae, a human, fed hotel guests, a touring theater company, a Kareth road crew and three successive wedding parties for the same spectacularly optimistic couple. She met her closest friends hauling kitchens between settlements. A prestigious route hotel promoted her for inventing meals that made differences in diet invisible; she left after discovering she preferred tables where guests could ask about them. Her first independent restaurant failed because she made wonderful dinners and terrible purchasing decisions. The present one pays its bills.

She wants a permanent evening salon where workers, visiting artists and neighbors can linger without buying another course. An old lover now tours with a dockside rock band; their reunions are warm, unreliable and inconvenient for breakfast service. She likes extravagance, plays favorites and sometimes needs Aveli to remind her that nobody requested an educational banquet. Seori sends descriptions of flavors neither can safely share.

The band arrives with twice its promised guests. Marae finds more chairs, puts a willing player to work and saves her favorite song for after service. There is cooking, flirting, argument and an excellent view of the tide. Her expertise ends with the kitchen and the people she knows; the evening belongs to whoever sits down.

### Ien Orali — the prophet who walked through the Closed Winter

Forty years ago, Ien was a thirty-two-year-old human route calibrator stranded during the Closed Winter. He crossed three Pelune shore settlements on foot, carrying copied repair instructions and persuading feuding storekeepers to pool supplies. Later he established the **Open Door houses**, religious refuges associated with the Witness of Arrival. They now shelter travelers on all three worlds. Pilgrims say he heard the routes call him home. The authored history establishes repairs, journeys and remarkable persuasion; it does not establish supernatural hearing.

His second great prediction failed. He promised that the reopened Sere connection would endure, encouraged a congregation to settle beyond it and watched another interruption strand them for a year. Some still call him a prophet; others call him the man who spent their children's childhood on his certainty. He returned repeatedly to help rebuild, without winning everyone back.

At seventy-two, he wants the houses to survive his death without becoming hereditary possessions or a Compact instrument. Several stewards want him to name a successor; he fears becoming irrelevant almost as much as choosing badly. Nevia financed two houses and expects his endorsement. Morrow is an old argumentative friend. Meet Ien protesting a shrine painted around a perfectly useful door. He offers hospitality, influence and forceful interpretation, not infallible predictions or orders every believer obeys.

### Ketta Rul — the engineer who united fourteen workshops

Ketta's Teral family workshop survived the Closed Winter's aftermath by making interchangeable parts. She apprenticed under Tarek, then spent eight years in Kareth factories before returning with a method independent workshops could reproduce. During the Repair Accord negotiations she organized fourteen shops into a buying cooperative. Their first shared production run made them solvent; their second exposed a defect in a seal she had approved. No one died, but one settlement lost a season's stored crop. She published the fault under her own name and spent the cooperative's reserve replacing the run. Some partners respect that decision; others never reinvested.

Now she wants independent shops included in the next gate-certification standard. Vant offers the capacity to prove her method at scale, with an exclusivity clause that would shut out former partners. She is a compelling teacher, an impatient negotiator and a maker of exquisite, useless little sculptures that she gives away rather than explain. Tarek still praises work in ways that make her feel like his apprentice.

Encounter her dismantling an award-winning component to show an apprentice its concealed weakness. She can improve equipment, outfit an expedition or turn recovered wayseed into a working shop. She needs measured evidence and compatible feedstock; talent does not let her diagnose an inaccessible gate from a rumor.

### Brisk-in-Glass — the broker who built the Glasshouse crew

Brisk began as an embodied fabricated inspector in the same industrial network as Morrow. After gaining citizenship, they stole their employer's impounded cargo and used the proceeds to buy a warehouse. A decade later, they run the Lantern Exchange's Hinge brokerage and the small armed **Glasshouse crew**. Their career includes a daring theft from a Vant auction, two prison terms and the sale of a rival's meeting place to an ambush team. Four people died in that meeting. Brisk knew what the buyers intended and charged extra for certainty.

They also kept a costly promise to supply an unlicensed neighborhood clinic through a shortage, and they adore the elderly gambler who first taught them to bluff. Neither is a concealed alibi. Brisk wants the warehouse legalized before a rival crew takes it; legitimacy would protect their assets, not automatically end their crimes. Morrow remains a friend who will eat with them and testify against them. Siv once escaped with Brisk's fee and is welcome back only with interest.

Meet Brisk auctioning a stolen trophy while arguing with its former owner about its artistic merits. They can buy salvage, commission a robbery or ruin a local enemy. Their information comes from brokers and witnesses, can be wrong, and covers far less than they imply.

### Ressa Vale — the expedition captain with a crew-owned ship

Ressa, a human, began coordinating ground repairs, bought into a survey crew, and eventually earned command of the Common Measure. She has led expeditions through Pelune tidal ruins, negotiated return rights for a crew expelled from Sere and recovered a stolen medical shipment by boarding its grounded carrier. At Kareth's Split Kiln she withdrew when a partner demanded a second assault. Six contractors were left behind and later ransomed; one still calls her a coward. Her surviving crew bought out the last outside shareholder after that contract. The ship is theirs, with shares and disagreements to prove it.

She wants a sustainable service route, predictable leave and enough profitable adventure to avoid becoming a freight clerk in a better cabin. She sings while checking stores, remembers excellent meals with embarrassing precision and is competitive about expedition prizes. She refused Orek's offer to merge crews because he expected authority without a crew vote. Ivo helps track commitments she habitually underestimates.

An employer makes an excellent offer over supper. Ressa asks three quiet questions, returns the advance and carries on eating. A player who wants a berth will have to learn what her crew can actually undertake: which sites she knows, which compartments are usable, and which people have already earned their leave.

### Pella Nesh — the twelve-year-old who has lived in three homes

Pella is a twelve-year-old human child. Her mother works the ground cargo routes; her father repairs cooling equipment on Kareth and calls when the relay schedule allows. Pella has lived above a kiln, beside a Pelune fish market and now in Hinge's apartment cluster. The last move followed a rent rise. She misses a friend who promised to write and never did, and claims that she cannot remember his name.

She builds toy salvage rigs, tells outrageous versions of Red Wake battles and recently won the school percussion contest by changing an arrangement halfway through. Dara's visiting photograph ruined her favorite part of the performance. She wants a place on the dockside junior band, her own workbench and a summer visit to her father. Morrow lets her help in the nursery; she is better with two of the plants and insufferable about it.

Meet her demanding compensation after the player's baggage crushes a toy. She can show a familiar neighborhood shortcut or recognize a regular visitor. Her knowledge ends where her actual experience does. School, family visits, band practice, neighborhood games and wrong guesses occupy much more of her life than adult intrigues.

### Seori Anen — the naturalist who found a path through hostile signals

Seori, a Nemei, has spent fourteen field seasons between Sere and Pelune, slept in living ruins and helped excavate a shore observatory submerged for most of a generation. They earned their reputation by showing that a supposedly hostile grove signal was a territorial warning with a predictable boundary. That let a steward community reopen its path without burning the grove. They also shot an ambush hunter that killed a colleague; afterward, their published account understated how far the expedition had strayed beyond its permit. The surviving guide refuses to work with them.

They come from a secure scholarly household, still send affectionate, absurdly detailed postcards, and enjoy markets as much as remote habitats. Their present ambition is to explain a new pattern of apparently deliberate grove signals before Nevia's sponsored team does. Nevia funded their early education and still expects first refusal on discoveries. Seori likes fair credit until being first becomes personally important; Marae knows how quickly their generous explanations turn possessive.

Meet them perfectly still while a luminous grazer examines their glove, then hear an exuberant account of a terrible field breakfast. They are a capable guide and field shot, not a universal interpreter of alien life. Their atmosphere equipment, incomplete records and particular relationships determine where expertise is useful.

### Nevia Aren — the magnate who reopened the Sere connection

Nevia, a Nemei, is already the name on three research halls. Twenty-three years ago she led the ground survey that recovered the missing calibration series for the Sere connection, then turned a small team into Vant's dominant frontier concession. The breakthrough lowered travel costs and made several settlements viable. Its first commercial contracts also excluded the steward guides who found the survey route. Later, during the Kareth lockout, she deliberately withheld company transport until striking workers accepted her terms; two died awaiting treatment. She settled the claims without admitting the decision was wrong.

Now she wants the next recovered wayseed to secure a route independent of the Compact. She offers fine laboratories, generous expedition pay and the intoxicating experience of being taken seriously by someone who can fund a dream. She is an accomplished singer, a patron of unfashionable composers and a devoted aunt whose family does not agree about her reputation. Seori's independence irritates her because she once imagined them as her successor. Marae likes her company and refuses to turn dinner into a private reception.

Meet her bidding fiercely for a cheap recording made before she became important. Her reach rests on patents, concessions and a board that can remove her. Orek's contracted force is not hers by birthright, and neither money nor a famous discovery makes an uncertain signal intelligible.

### Tarek Voss — the caravan builder who knows what holds

Tarek grew up in a mobile Teral pastoral community, worked ground caravans through Kareth's dry basins and built temporary settlements along Pelune's terraces before taking a Hinge workshop. He has raised homes for people who later quarreled, dismantled a chapel abandoned by its congregation and rebuilt his own marriage after four years apart. He and his husband now prefer neighboring rooms and a common breakfast. He trusts arrangements that can be adjusted without tearing everything down.

His folding barricades helped hold the Red Stair. Orek still owes the last design payment, which Tarek mentions with enough theatrical venom that strangers assume a feud. He is angrier about a beautiful footbridge that failed under loads he had underestimated; the injured worker recovered, but never returned to the trade. Tarek can name every joint he changed afterward. Ketta learned structural judgment from him and rightly objects when he treats factory production as inferior craft.

He wants to finish a covered neighborhood courtyard and take one last long caravan visit to his siblings. He invites the player to help fit a bed, tells a magnificently filthy mistranslated joke and makes them check the joinery by touch. His siblings say he invents new courtyard improvements whenever the next caravan date approaches. They may be right; at the port, he can see the people living inside what he made.

### Siv Ren — the human thief who stole her own funeral

For nineteen years Siv, a human, stole from collectors, expedition stores and people who trusted an attractive stranger with a convincing story. She crossed Kareth as a troupe accountant, Pelune as a salvage assessor and Sere under a botanist's borrowed permit. Her finest theft was the stormglass table from a guarded magistrate's dining room. Her meanest was a fellow thief's escape money; he went to prison while she enjoyed two comfortable years. She tells the first story readily and the second only when someone already knows it.

To shake a persistent warrant, she arranged a reported death and a memorial auction, then stole back a keepsake from her own estate. It worked locally, not everywhere. Brisk recognized a forged appraisal and now wants a share of her next job. She has returned to Hinge because the same buyer kept the accompanying letters: the last correspondence from a sister she loved. Recovering it is her priority; she may also rob the buyer because she dislikes wasting an opportunity.

Encounter her buying tools openly under her real name, delighted that somebody remembers her alleged funeral. She can teach observation, scout a bounded theft or become a treacherous partner. Her disguises require preparation and credible records; no conversation makes a guarded stranger automatically believe her.

### Morrow-in-Copper — the Copper Witness with soil in their joints

Morrow worked forty-six years as an embodied fabricated industrial inspector before the law recognized their citizenship. They had signed clearance orders that dispossessed unauthorized residents and later used those same retained records to help bring a successful case against the network. During the campaign they refused an employer's recall order in an open court, then organized a prolonged work stoppage. That confrontation secured independent status for hundreds of embodied workers. Posters call them the **Copper Witness**; some Keepers of Continuity treat their repaired casing as a relic. Morrow finds both admiration and requests to touch it tiresome.

Citizenship did not restore the homes they had cleared. Some former residents will never forgive them; others now visit the nursery. Their immediate ambitions are to keep six stubborn plants alive and preserve a defense fund before younger organizers spend it on another symbolic monument. Brisk's criminal career strains a friendship older than either person's current name. Ien brings cuttings and terrible theological arguments. Pella has quietly become the nursery's best propagator.

Meet Morrow covered in potting grit while visitors expect a historic speech. They can lend public standing, testify or decline a cause. Their retained memories are incomplete, their hand module needs Ketta's maintenance, and their authority grants no access to other citizens' private records or automatic body copying.

### Ivo Pell — the route clerk and very serious comic actor

Ivo, a human, has kept route desks in a Kareth freight yard, a Pelune fishing settlement and Hinge's busiest arrival hall. He raised a daughter through alternating shifts with his former wife, performed comic roles in six amateur productions and spent one rainy season trying to sell insurance. Nobody who bought it lets him forget. His consequential professional mistake was approving a departure on incomplete local weather information. No one died, but the stranded crew lost weeks of pay and a workshop missed the shipment that might have kept it open.

He now wants local maintainers to flag uncertainty directly at the route desk. That practical reform has made him useful to Ressa and irritating to Dara, whose preferred schedules sometimes depend on unquestioned estimates. He declined her request to backdate one seizure notice; he also once quietly corrected his own brother's late filing. He knows the distinction and does not enjoy having it pointed out.

He firmly refuses an influential passenger, then asks the player to help rehearse a ridiculous entrance line. He can find a permitted record, correct a departure plan or explain whose word carries weight at another desk. At seven he leaves for the theater. Even an important request may have to wait until morning.

## A bounded first route

Start with Hinge Port's shared workshop, apartment cluster and arrival hall, plus one Pelune shore site reachable by an abstract ground journey. Offer a usable room, a basic expedition kit and a clear short contract before adding broader faction simulation. The Common Measure can become a familiar home once route transitions are supported.

The port also offers things to do on an ordinary evening. Marae is trying a new shared-table service: arrange seating for different bodies, choose dishes with the cooks, invite someone or simply enjoy the meal. Tarek can help furnish the player’s room; Ivo’s comic performance and a dockside rock band offer a night out, with drinks, dancing and people to meet. These activities can succeed on their own, without becoming a trap or a prerequisite for the expedition.

**Opening: the bell below the tide.** An unusually low tide exposes a service vault containing an intact wayseed core and a failed relay. Ketta can use the core to reopen an independent workshop. Nevia offers a much larger payment for exclusive recovery rights; Dara claims the component must enter Compact custody. An old registry suggests the site belongs to a small shore cooperative, while Brisk offers evidence that one of the current claims is forged. These people can argue over a bounded valuable object without needing a simulated interplanetary economy.

**Failure also changes the next session.** A rising tide can force abandonment of the core, leave a cache for a later expedition or injure an unprepared companion. Losing a fight can cost cargo, equipment or freedom under the selected consequence policy; deaths, when enabled, are persistent rather than quietly reversed. Rivals may return with the prize and use it, leaving another route to better gear, a different contract, recovery, revenge or settlement. The world does not reset because the player missed the most profitable ending. Tide warnings, enemy capabilities and escape opportunities must be readable before a commitment becomes irreversible.

A successful return can mean a better rifle, a secure room, an equipped workshop, a shared feast or the beginning of a romance. Let the player enjoy it. Use [the alien welcome meal](../combinations.md#the-alien-welcome-meal), [the colony's unwanted miracle](../combinations.md#the-colonys-unwanted-miracle) and [the mineral nobody needs more of](../combinations.md#the-mineral-nobody-needs-more-of) as compatible situations, rather than a requirement to turn every contract into repair work. The [grounded rescue ship](../combinations.md#the-grounded-rescue-ship) can become a later crew scenario.

### Alternative focused premises

Choose one main promise for an initial campaign; the whole Reach need not be active at once.

| Premise                             | Central play and desired payoff                                                                                     | Pressure that keeps it moving                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Independent expedition company**  | Hunt dangerous creatures, recover artifacts, invent field equipment and make a name with a small recurring crew.    | Rivals, injuries, limited supplies and decisions about whom to trust with a discovery.                                      |
| **A frontier home worth defending** | Build a workshop and comfortable settlement, form relationships, host festivals and improve local services.         | Predators, a contested water right and a company seeking exclusive ownership; peace and successful defenses can last.       |
| **First contact on Sere**           | Learn signals, explore strange habitats and form a relationship with an unfamiliar intelligence.                    | Competing researchers, predation, uncertain evidence and incompatible ideas about who may negotiate or claim a finding.     |
| **Contract war on the ground**      | Lead a small mercenary unit, fight for limited objectives, recruit specialists and decide what victory is worth.    | Wages, rival commanders, civilian interests, shifting contracts and a patron whose ambitions exceed the agreement.          |
| **The port's second economy**       | Smuggle, steal, investigate, bargain and build a profitable network while keeping a place and people to care about. | Inspectors, criminal competition, unreliable partners and friendships damaged by profitable choices.                        |
| **A working ship and its people**   | Take varied contracts, improve shared quarters, divide responsibilities and grow attached to a crew.                | Discrete external encounters, incompatible promises and the temptation to risk a secure livelihood for a spectacular prize. |

**Pacing and persistence:** summarize routine transit, make established atmosphere access dependable, and let better tools or delegated work retire solved chores. Keep a few consequential environmental differences per site; avoid endless scanning and interchangeable mineral tiers. Hostile areas can become safer through actual success, while a new region offers fresh uncertainty. Shared pleasures need not always conceal a threat. No species-wide personality, mandatory whole-galaxy war or hidden flight controls are needed for a dangerous and lively frontier.

Before adoption, select gate versus ship-transition fiction, exact alien body support, synthetic identity/copying policy, the campaign's main premise, severity of injury/death/capture, and how much activity persists beyond visited places. Fictional conflict is part of the repertoire; its frequency and consequence rules depend on that chosen experience. These are authored content proposals and capability gaps, not changes to live worlds or implemented engine contracts.
