# SC proposal: Veyra Reach

**Current-game selection: 5 Specialist, as a separate primary experience.** Retain planetary exploration and crew technology for a separately selected world. Its existing no-piloting/no-space-navigation/no-dogfighting boundary remains unchanged.

[Library](../README.md) · [Labels](../README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](../selection-and-scale.md)

**Optional setting proposal, not canonical world history or implemented content.** Veyra Reach is an inhabited frontier connected by finite, reliable routes: beautiful shores and alien forests, dangerous ruins, ambitious settlements, armed expeditions and competing claims to the next discovery. People come to discover unfamiliar life, make a home or a fortune, master a craft, find company or build an empire. Its question is: _what will you discover, build and become on a frontier whose possibilities are still taking shape?_ Exploration, fighting, invention, settlement life, diplomacy and crew relationships are primary play. A cabin of your own, a scientific discovery, a friendship and a hard-won prize offer different reasons to stay; rivalry, predation and ruthless ambition can put them at stake. See the [design foundation](../design-foundation.md).

**No playable spaceflight or navigation.** Ships are inhabited maps with rooms, crew, cargo and capabilities. Players choose destinations and preparations through a route interface; journeys resolve as abstract transitions. Optional external encounters concern crew assignments, communications, repairs and discrete station actions. There is no piloting, orbital maneuvering, dogfighting, navigable starfield or manual landing. A map of routes is a choice of connections, not a vehicle-control game.

**Estimate:** Play/Blend, **Large/New** for alien bodies, environmental compatibility, route transitions and crew encounters. A small shared port using ordinary human-compatible bodies could be a narrower **Moderate/Extend** composition. Lore and cast are **Small/Compose** candidates. The bounded first setting below is not a promise that arbitrary planetary physics or synthetic citizens are supported.

These are authored places with a few coherent environmental laws, not interchangeable ore tiers. A sailmaw can feed on a tidal shoal, defend its clutch and retreat from injury; it does not exist solely to attack the player. Learning its behavior supports hunting, avoidance and protecting a worksite. Creatures, ruins and local people offer different kinds of uncertainty, and a beautiful grove can simply remain beautiful after its dangers become familiar.

Build one shore site before a planet-wide ecology. Begin Sere's first-contact thread with a bounded pattern of apparently deliberate grove signals: the player can observe and test it while rivals demand a quick commercial claim. The answer may reveal an unfamiliar agent, a mistaken interpretation or several collaborating organisms; the selected explanation needs actual supported behavior.

## Peoples and cultures are different layers

**Humans** arrive from several communities with different histories, institutions and tastes. They are neither the default owners of every place nor the sole discoverers of knowledge.

**The Teral** are a proposed four-limbed, broad-bodied people with fine touch sensitivity along their forearms. Many tools can be operated by touch, but individuals differ in vision, training and preferences. Their bodies do not confer telepathy. Hinge Port's Teral builders come from both urban manufacturing towns and mobile pastoral communities; their disagreements can concern ordinary matters such as contracts and education. **Build: Large/New**, requiring explicit body interfaces and tactile evidence.

**The Nemei** are a proposed people adapted to a lower-oxygen, high-moisture environment. A visible mantle changes pattern with deliberate expression and some bodily states, but interpreting it requires familiarity and never yields a perfect emotion readout. Some use artificial displays for cross-species communication. Their cultures include coastal cooperatives and private scholarly households. **Build: Large/New**, requiring body-specific atmosphere and expressive signals; no default claim that they are universally peaceful, mystical or deceptive.

**Fabricated citizens** include synthetic bodies and software entities in admitted hosts. Some identify strongly with their makers, some with a profession or city, and some have no interest in origin stories. Replacing a limb or moving to a compatible host need not erase identity; duplication, consent, memory transfer and legal personhood require explicit selected rules. Begin with one embodied citizen rather than assuming arbitrary copying. **Build: Large/New**, with identity-preserving capability changes and tightly bounded host transitions.

A species is a body family; a people can be a cultural or political community; citizenship is an institutional relationship. These need not coincide. No species grants a moral alignment or one automatic faction.

## Institutions and a usable history

Three generations ago, stable routes made regular exchange possible. Records celebrate technical pioneers while undercrediting local crews. Forty years ago, **the Closed Winter** interrupted several routes after a calibration failure. Communities survived through adaptation, mutual aid, hoarding and seizures of supply depots; surviving families remember different heroes. Ten years ago, the **Repair Accord** let independent workshops maintain selected gate components, lowering costs and unsettling old suppliers. Some suppliers now finance new concessions, private security and campaigns to narrow the Accord.

The present frontier works well enough to be worth fighting over. Discovery changes bargaining power; competent institutions can protect a port while discriminating against outsiders or serving a patron. No faction has unlimited resources, a unanimous membership or a monopoly on kindness or cruelty.

A player can work for, rise within, defy or play these powers against one another. Supplying a port is different from controlling it; winning a firefight does not settle ownership, local legitimacy or a contract. Friendship can cross affiliations, and an enemy may still pay honestly or save someone they love. Local victories should endure: a won workshop, protected grove or defeated raiding crew changes the next session instead of resetting automatically.

Political detail is **Small/Compose** as authored context and **Moderate/Extend** for a few local roles and agreements. Persistent faction operations, changing territory, broader markets and armed expeditions require additional **Large/New** behavior; none is claimed to exist merely because this table names it.

Ordinary expedition gear matters too: a dependable rifle, cutting tool, climbing line, field shelter, protective suit, medical kit and enough compatible supplies. Early success should come from understandable equipment and judgment before exotic devices. A player might win a fight by striking an exposed joint, draw a predator toward a rival camp, or bypass both with a climbing line; these are distinct methods with consequences, not a requirement to offer a morally clean optimum in every encounter.

Wayseed enables one specific class of route technology, not every impossible effect. A creator may replace it with engineered components, making expertise and industrial capacity the constraint. It does not imply a hyperdrive, time machine or unrestricted replicator. New mechanisms still require admitted capabilities and costs.

## An inhabited ship: the Common Measure

The **Common Measure** is an optional small working vessel with a galley, workshop, clinic nook, cabins, cargo room and route room. Its crew carries passengers, repairs outlying stations and takes survey, salvage and armed escort contracts. Ressa wants predictable leave and a sustainable business, but a valuable recovery could buy the crew its own workshop and a rival's threat can make refusing a job costly. The workshop, weapon station and galley cannot all draw maximum power at once; one cabin still needs adapting for a Nemei guest. Crew members have their own wages, loyalties, skill limits and reasons for staying.

A journey has three phases: choose commitments and prepare at a place; resolve the abstract route, optionally with one bounded crew incident; arrive and act at the destination. Routine travel is summarized. During a discrete external encounter, a communications officer can bargain or threaten, an engineer can allocate power, and a gunner can resolve a supported shot against a declared target. Other stations can shield a compartment, recover cargo or prepare evacuation. The decision may be whether to spend the rescue charge saving a stranded rival, use it to secure the prize, or withdraw with a damaged ship. Crew competence and willingness are separate; affection does not make someone a qualified gunner.

An external encounter uses finite targets, phases and outcomes. Capturing a cargo pod or forcing a hostile vessel to withdraw can be an offensive objective without simulated pursuit, manual aiming of ship motion or orbital maneuvering. Any boarding scene begins through an admitted abstract transfer into a bounded interior. The player commands people and capabilities, never spatial ship motion. Opponents can surrender, bluff, resist or disengage when the authored encounter supports those outcomes.

Between contracts, the ship is home: shared meals, music, arguments over cabin space, flirting, sexual intimacy, quiet companionship and keepsakes from places visited. A successful voyage can end in satisfaction rather than an immediate new emergency. The vessel can also remain grounded as a community workshop or contested outpost, preserving much of its social value with less route-system work. Interior movement uses ordinary supported spatial behavior; route state never grants unrestricted object teleportation.

## Names, languages and belief

Port language mixes practical compounds—**Hinge Port, Common Measure, Borrowlight**—with older place names. One Teral language proposal favors firm consonants and short repeated syllables: **Tarek, Ketta, Rulen**. One Nemei naming tradition favors open syllables and vowel transitions: **Aveli, Nemea, Seori**. These are small invented phonotactic palettes, not copies of Klingon, Elvish or a real sacred language. Families, professions and migration can mix patterns.

Teral tactile marks and Nemei mantle signs are writing or expression systems with specific affordances; they are not automatically different spoken languages. Translation can convey a technical sentence while missing a hospitality assumption. Provide diagrams, interpreters and patient questions rather than language grind.

The **Witness of Arrival** is an optional tradition in which newcomers and hosts each name something they hope the meeting will change. Some regard it as sacred, some as civic courtesy and some decline it. The **Keepers of Continuity** care for old records and repaired bodies, disagreeing over whether continuity rests in material, memory, relationships or voluntary self-identification. Some challenge company ownership claims; others accept company patronage to keep their sanctuaries open. Their archive can shelter a vulnerable citizen or expose an uncomfortable past. Belief can motivate courage, exclusion, comfort or ambition; it does not settle the engine’s identity rules.

## Eight people to meet

**Cast: Play, High, Small/Compose** as biography. Follow-through needs **Moderate/Extend** agency and memory; alien embodiment and environmental behavior use the **Large/New** gaps above. Species descriptions never predetermine these personalities.

### Aveli Soren — the Nemei host who dislikes elaborate welcomes

Aveli grew up in a bustling harbor household where guests came and went without ceremony. Trained as an atmosphere technician, they now manage Hinge Port's arrival rooms and want hospitality to feel ordinary. Officials keep appointing them to ceremonial delegations, which they find exhausting. They enjoy dry human percussion recordings through a tactile speaker. They are warm, direct and occasionally dismissive of customs others need. Marae's dining experiments delight them when practical; Tarek helps them design a quiet shared room without turning it into an exhibit about difference.

### Tarek Voss — the Teral builder who trusts a beautiful joint

Tarek learned construction in a mobile community where buildings had to come apart without waste. At the port, he is praised for tactile precision but wants recognition as a designer, not merely a specialized pair of hands. He collects badly translated jokes and tells them with perfect timing. He believes good construction should be inspectable and can be stubborn about hidden fasteners. His former apprentice Ketta favors faster factory methods. Their disagreement could produce a better standard if neither treats the other's history as obsolete.

### Marae Linn — the human cook making a table for everyone

Marae trained in a prestigious route hotel, then left because its hospitality meant keeping differences invisible. She wants a dining room where people can share an occasion without eating or breathing alike. Her parents ran a small pleasant café; invention, not escape, brought her here. She sketches seating arrangements on every spare receipt and sometimes overdesigns an evening. Aveli reminds her that conversation can be enough. Her friendship with Seori grows through exchanging descriptions of flavors neither can safely taste.

### Ketta Rul — the Teral engineer with a debt to a method

Ketta's family workshop survived the Closed Winter aftermath by manufacturing standardized replacement parts. She values interchangeability because it once meant survival, and fears bespoke craft will make maintenance depend on irreplaceable masters. She is an excellent teacher and an impatient meeting participant. She wants independent workshops included in the next gate certification standard. Tarek taught her structural judgment; she resents his assumption that speed means carelessness. Her private hobby is making tiny useless sculptures, which quietly contradicts her public rhetoric about pure function. Vant offers her the manufacturing capacity to prove her method at scale, but its exclusive contract would exclude workshops she has publicly defended.

### Ivo Pell — the human route clerk who wants to be corrected early

Ivo once approved a departure with incomplete local weather information. Nobody died, but a stranded crew lost weeks and their trust in him. He now asks careful questions and sometimes overcompensates with paperwork. He wants a route desk where local maintainers can flag uncertainty directly. He loves amateur theater and is surprisingly good at comic roles. Ressa sees him as an ally but worries his caution can delay urgent aid. A player can help create a better handoff instead of repeatedly reassuring him that mistakes do not matter.

### Seori Anen — the Nemei field naturalist whose family wants postcards

Seori grew up in a secure scholarly household and travels because unfamiliar ecosystems fascinate them. They are brave about fieldwork, anxious about disappointing family and delighted by ordinary market stalls. Their goal is to study Sere's living sealant with local stewards, leaving useful knowledge and fair credit. They sometimes mistake enthusiasm for permission. Marae is a friend who asks the practical questions their colleagues miss. Aveli can challenge Seori when a research visit becomes a burden on hosts, without treating curiosity itself as wrong. Seori wants to be first to explain the grove signals and may conceal an uncertain result to keep a rival sponsor from taking over. They can handle a field weapon but would rather understand a creature than kill it; danger does not erase their delight in discovery.

### Ressa Vale — the human captain who would rather fix the schedule

Ressa inherited no ship or grand destiny; she became a captain after years coordinating ground repairs. She likes a crew that can explain its decisions and dislikes stories portraying captains as solitary geniuses. She wants the Common Measure to have predictable leave and a sustainable service route. She sings while checking stores and pretends not to notice others joining in. Her weak point is taking on one more rescue contract. Ivo can help her see the accumulated commitments; the crew can refuse without becoming disloyal. Ressa also wants to win: she will take an armed recovery contract, set a firm boarding plan and demand payment. She is capable of a ruthless withdrawal when staying would sacrifice the crew, even if someone she likes calls it betrayal.

### Morrow-in-Copper — the fabricated citizen who grows flowers badly

Morrow began as an embodied industrial inspector under a world rule that later recognized their independent citizenship. They remember their early work with mixed pride and discomfort, and choose a new name associated with a favorite repaired casing. They want to run a small nursery despite poor horticultural instincts. Precise measurement does not make them socially detached or infallible; they enjoy gossip they are actually told and dislike being asked to settle philosophical debates at dinner. Ketta maintains a compatible hand module, while Seori helps with plants without treating Morrow as a research specimen.

## Four people whose ambitions change the frontier

### Dara Venn — the human marshal who intends to hold the port

Dara commands the Compact's local constabulary and wants Hinge Port to become a secure regional capital under her authority. She is a capable field commander who drills with her people, pays attention to ammunition and enjoys winning a difficult operation. She funds a youth percussion group and is an incorrigible, usually unsuccessful card player. Her protection is real, but so is her willingness to seize unlicensed wayseed and hold suspected brokers without a quick hearing. Aveli values her defense of the arrival halls and opposes her proposed passenger registry. Ivo can provide evidence that changes an operation; if he merely delays it, she may remove him from the briefing. She can become a reliable ally, a superior to challenge or a dangerous opponent.

### Orek Tal — the Teral mercenary who wants a banner of his own

Orek leads Red Wake's Hinge detachment and wants enough successful contracts to buy a permanent hall and become a recognized political power. He likes battle, precise plans, extravagant meals and the respect earned by doing a hard job well. He will raid a guarded depot for pay and accepts that some opponents will die; he refuses contracts framed as deliberate massacres and may still misjudge what an employer intends. Tarek built his first portable barricades and resents an unpaid design fee; Ketta could equip his company at a scale her workshop has never managed. Orek admires Ressa's decisiveness, competes for her best crew and can offer the player training, profitable violence or a feud that survives one encounter.

### Nevia Aren — the Nemei director who wants the discovery to bear her name

Nevia directs Vant's frontier concessions. She is a serious researcher, an accomplished singer and a talented negotiator who wants to be remembered for opening the next inhabited route. She finances comfortable laboratories and risky expeditions, pays well and treats results as proof that her authority is deserved. She also buys exclusivity, buries embarrassing failures and employs Red Wake to enforce claims. Seori once benefited from her scholarship; she considers their independence a promising investment until it threatens her concession. Marae enjoys her company but will not reserve the whole dining room merely because she can afford it. Nevia can reward a discovery generously, become a genuine friend and still betray a partner whose success threatens her legacy.

### Brisk-in-Glass — the fabricated broker who finds honesty expensive

Brisk began as an inspector in the same industrial network as Morrow and now runs a Lantern Exchange brokerage with a small armed salvage crew. They want a legitimate warehouse and enough leverage that no patron can repossess their body or business. They love ostentatious lighting, competitive board games and winning auctions with an audacious bluff. Brisk steals cargo, trades forged claims and has knowingly sold information that enabled an ambush; they also keep a costly promise to supply an unlicensed neighborhood clinic. Morrow remembers their kindness and refuses to excuse their present choices. Brisk can obtain rare gear, hire the player for a robbery, betray a temporary partner or risk a fortune to protect a chosen friend. Their synthetic origin explains neither their criminality nor their capacity for affection.

## A bounded first route

Start with Hinge Port's shared workshop, apartment cluster and arrival hall, plus one Pelune shore site reachable by an abstract ground journey. Offer a usable room, a basic expedition kit and a clear short contract before adding broader faction simulation. The Common Measure can become a familiar home once route transitions are supported.

The port also offers things to do on an ordinary evening. Marae is trying a new shared-table service: arrange seating for different bodies, choose dishes with the cooks, invite someone or simply enjoy the meal. Tarek can help furnish the player’s room; Ivo’s comic performance and a dockside rock band offer a night out, with drinks, dancing and people to meet. These activities can succeed on their own, without becoming a trap or a prerequisite for the expedition.

**Opening: the bell below the tide.** An unusually low tide exposes a service vault containing an intact wayseed core and a failed relay. Ketta can use the core to reopen an independent workshop. Nevia offers a much larger payment for exclusive recovery rights; Dara claims the component must enter Compact custody. An old registry suggests the site belongs to a small shore cooperative, while Brisk offers evidence that one of the current claims is forged. These people can argue over a bounded valuable object without needing a simulated interplanetary economy.

**Failure also changes the next session.** A rising tide can force abandonment of the core, leave a cache for a later expedition or injure an unprepared companion. Losing a fight can cost cargo, equipment or freedom under the selected consequence policy; deaths, when enabled, are persistent rather than quietly reversed. Rivals may return with the prize and use it, leaving another route to better gear, a different contract, recovery, revenge or settlement. The world does not reset because the player missed the most profitable ending. Tide warnings, enemy capabilities and escape opportunities must be readable before a commitment becomes irreversible.

A successful return can mean a better rifle, a secure room, an equipped workshop, a shared feast or the beginning of a romance. Let the player enjoy it. Use [the alien welcome meal](../combinations.md#the-alien-welcome-meal), [the colony's unwanted miracle](../combinations.md#the-colonys-unwanted-miracle) and [the mineral nobody needs more of](../combinations.md#the-mineral-nobody-needs-more-of) as compatible situations, rather than a requirement to turn every contract into repair work. The [grounded rescue ship](../combinations.md#the-grounded-rescue-ship) can become a later crew scenario.

### Alternative focused premises

Choose one main promise for an initial campaign; the whole Reach need not be active at once.

| Premise                             | Central play and desired payoff                                                                                     | Pressure that keeps it moving                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Independent expedition company**  | Hunt dangerous creatures, recover artifacts, invent field equipment and make a name with a small recurring crew.    | Rivals, injuries, limited supplies and decisions about whom to trust with a discovery.                                      |
| **A frontier home worth defending** | Build a workshop and comfortable settlement, form relationships, host festivals and improve local services.         | Predators, a contested water right and a company seeking exclusive ownership; peace and successful defenses can last.       |
| **First contact on Sere**           | Learn signals, explore strange habitats and form a relationship with an unfamiliar intelligence.                    | Competing researchers, predation, uncertain evidence and incompatible ideas about who may negotiate or claim a finding.     |
| **Contract war on the ground**      | Lead a small mercenary unit, fight for limited objectives, recruit specialists and decide what victory is worth.    | Wages, rival commanders, civilian interests, shifting contracts and a patron whose ambitions exceed the agreement.          |
| **The port's second economy**       | Smuggle, steal, investigate, bargain and build a profitable network while keeping a place and people to care about. | Inspectors, criminal competition, unreliable partners and friendships damaged by profitable choices.                        |
| **A working ship and its people**   | Take varied contracts, improve shared quarters, divide responsibilities and grow attached to a crew.                | Discrete external encounters, incompatible promises and the temptation to risk a secure livelihood for a spectacular prize. |

**Pacing and persistence:** summarize routine transit, make established atmosphere access dependable, and let better tools or delegated work retire solved chores. Keep a few consequential environmental differences per site; avoid endless scanning and interchangeable mineral tiers. Hostile areas can become safer through actual success, while a new region offers fresh uncertainty. Shared pleasures need not always conceal a threat. No species-wide personality, mandatory whole-galaxy war or hidden flight controls are needed for a dangerous and lively frontier.

Before adoption, select gate versus ship-transition fiction, exact alien body support, synthetic identity/copying policy, the campaign's main premise, severity of injury/death/capture, and how much activity persists beyond visited places. Fictional conflict is part of the repertoire; its frequency and consequence rules depend on that chosen experience. These are authored content proposals and capability gaps, not changes to live worlds or implemented engine contracts.
