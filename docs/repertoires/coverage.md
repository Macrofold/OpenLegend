# Whole-game coverage

[Library](README.md) · [Priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md) · [270 patterns](README.md#catalogue-map) · [385 actions](actions.md) · [Worked situations](combinations.md)

**Current survival-adventure priorities · October 4, 2026.** Coverage tables describe broader families than the illustrative pattern cards. This guide decomposes those families into the basic player capabilities and later elaborations they contain. It prevents a catalogue full of interesting refinements from hiding an absent weapon, enemy, objective or reward. It is selection policy, not a second delivery tracker, implementation audit or approval to adopt an optional world's lore.

The scores are **1 Core, 2 Complete, 3 Depth, 4 Detail, 5 Specialist**, defined in [the policy](gameplay-priorities.md#priority). Rank the smallest stated scope, not the whole domain by its most important member. A missing Core capability outranks another refinement to a working one. A representative weapon does not mean every weapon; a functional home does not mean a city simulator. Existing adequate capabilities need no reimplementation merely because they appear here.

## 1 Core: close the playable loop

These are interconnected capabilities, not a serial delivery queue. Deliver a thin playable combination rather than finishing one isolated subsystem exhaustively. The source links identify repertoire owners, not claims that their full proposed mechanics exist.

The [ordinary melee attempt CBT-13](actions.md#cbt-conflict-defense-and-tactical-action) names an existing category of action, not a newly delivered capability. The [action tracker](../maintainers/action-capabilities.md#ac096--equipped-contact-strikes-implemented-acceptance-partial) retains the narrow implemented contact-strike slice and incomplete acceptance. An implemented attack alone does not establish a satisfying encounter, rewards or progression.

**The distinctive part is not postponed:** a small useful non-canned invention, one person’s independent purpose, and a witnessed consequence that matters again belong in this first playable experience. These can use a single obstacle and character; they do not require a general physics or society simulator. The [priority policy’s first proof](gameplay-priorities.md#the-game-we-need-to-be-able-to-play) owns the connected evaluation target.

## 2 Complete: make the game worth continuing

| Rank | Addition                            | Scope boundary                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Enemy and encounter variety         | Different attack patterns, melee/ranged roles, a threatening elite or boss-like objective, and more than one encounter goal. Do not require adaptive personalities or regional war.            |
| 2    | More interesting destinations       | Ruins, caves, camps, valuable resource locations, alternate approaches, shortcuts, secrets and useful discoveries. New places should change decisions, not just scenery.                       |
| 3    | Useful gear and progression choices | Distinct weapons, armor/protection, consumables, crafting upgrades, recipes and techniques. Add a few meaningful alternatives before vast rarity tables or universal build composition.        |
| 4    | Clear challenge and reward pacing   | Attainable goals, understandable risk, useful loot, new options and satisfying stopping points. A stronger enemy should not be merely an inflated health bar.                                  |
| 5    | Substantial survival activities     | Fishing, fuller hunting/foraging, farming, animal care or another worthwhile production activity. Choose coherent activities rather than requiring all of them for every meal.                 |
| 6    | Better building and useful defenses | Staged home/workshop improvements, functional layouts, a defensible entrance or barrier when threats warrant it, and meaningful storage capacity. Avoid compulsory maintenance tax.            |
| 7    | Simple trade and services           | Buy, sell, barter, commission useful equipment or seek a service. Fixed or authored prices can work before a dynamic economic model.                                                           |
| 8    | Useful companions                   | A companion who can travel, fight, guard, help or rescue through real supported behavior. Formal job agreements, completion reports and elaborate domestic negotiations are not prerequisites. |
| 9    | Readable environmental variety      | Darkness/light, weather or terrain that changes a chosen activity and has understandable counterplay. Full climate, moisture, seasons and ecology are not required.                            |
| 10   | Optional creative and social play   | Gardening, decorating, music, a contest, a fair or a small playable game can be worthwhile in their own right. They need not produce a combat buff to deserve selection.                       |
| 11   | Less repetition after success       | Useful shortcuts, learned methods, convenient inventory, or a bounded routine when an actual recurring chore has been demonstrated. Do not build chores merely to justify automation.          |

## All 25 worked situations

[Combinations](combinations.md) contains all 25 complete scenes with their current priority and selection boundary beside each description. They combine owned entries; they are not another inventory of required systems. A simpler adaptation can deliver an opening experience without adopting a scene’s entire politics, psychology or physics.

The [toll tower](combinations.md#the-toll-tower-at-dawn), [recognizable quarry](combinations.md#the-quarry-that-learns), [wedding crossing](combinations.md#the-wedding-bridge), [local predator mystery](combinations.md#the-wolf-at-the-school-gate) and [companion allegiance](combinations.md#the-companion-who-changes-sides) offer different combinations of challenge, invention, knowledge and personal consequence. Pick a coherent small experience rather than implementing this list as a quest queue.

## Optional world families

The [four world proposals](README.md#four-possible-world-families) each own their current selection note. They are sources of connected examples, not simultaneous release commitments. Draw bounded content from medieval survival or living fantasy when it makes this game better; modern life and planetary science fiction remain separate primary experiences. Selecting a magical tool does not adopt a whole magical constitution.

A future explicit product choice can rerank an alternate family for its own intended experience. Family scores are not universal genre judgments, and a name or biography is not a new runtime-system requirement. The planetary proposal’s no-piloting, no-space-navigation and no-dogfighting boundary remains unchanged.

## What does not become a new feature

“Prepare supplies” uses the existing inventory, bags, equipment and consumables unless a concrete missing interaction is demonstrated. “Agree on a task” and “report completion” are not substitutes for an enjoyable shared activity. “A camp with consequences” is not a higher-priority product milestone than playable opposition, exploration and rewards. A feature's realistic consequence is a correctness requirement for that chosen feature, not a reason to choose it.

No scores in this guide close implementation tasks or establish fun empirically. Use the existing owners to audit gaps and qualify the selected playable slice. The [selection guide](selection-and-scale.md) explains how to evaluate the candidates without reviving the old camp-first or nearest-code ordering.
