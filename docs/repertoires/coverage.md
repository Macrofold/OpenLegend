# Whole-game coverage

[Library](README.md) · [Priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md) · [270 patterns](README.md#catalogue-map) · [385 actions](actions.md) · [Worked situations](combinations.md)

**Current survival-adventure priorities · October 17, 2026.** Coverage tables describe broader families than the illustrative pattern cards. This guide decomposes those families into the basic player capabilities and later elaborations they contain. It prevents a catalogue full of interesting refinements from hiding an absent weapon, enemy, objective, reward or usable camp. It is selection policy, not a second delivery tracker, implementation audit or approval to adopt an optional world's lore. The [existing opening specification](../worlds/base/playable-openings.md#first-playable-game-target) owns the required survival-first scenario; [FPV](../maintainers/first-playable-validation.md) connects it to actual design, delivery and player evidence.

The scores are **1 Core, 2 Complete, 3 Depth, 4 Detail, 5 Specialist**, defined in [the policy](gameplay-priorities.md#priority). Rank the smallest stated scope, not the whole domain by its most important member. A missing Core capability outranks another refinement to a working one. A representative weapon does not mean every weapon; a defensible first camp does not mean a city simulator. Existing adequate capabilities need no reimplementation merely because they appear here.

These additions preserve other Complete candidates such as enemy variety, richer rescue, magical capabilities, useful services and companion activities. Selecting a method that travels or a person whose experience matters does not require every creator-platform or personality-simulation extension. Likewise, completing the local expedition does not turn the broader campaign suite into an early dependency.

## 1 Core: close the playable loop

These are interconnected capabilities, not a serial delivery queue. Deliver a thin playable combination rather than finishing one isolated subsystem exhaustively. The source links identify repertoire owners, not claims that their full proposed mechanics exist. The empty-handed opening, basic camp walls/entrance, required character cast and cooperation, and independent roaming conflict are first-playable coverage; broader family-level expansion below cannot demote those selected requirements.

The [ordinary melee attempt CBT-13](actions.md#cbt-conflict-defense-and-tactical-action) names an existing category of action, not a newly delivered capability. The [action tracker](../maintainers/action-capabilities.md#ac096--equipped-contact-strikes-implemented-acceptance-partial) retains the narrow implemented contact-strike slice and incomplete acceptance. An implemented attack alone does not establish a satisfying encounter, rewards or progression.

**The distinctive part is not postponed:** useful non-canned invention, independent purposes and witnessed consequences belong in the first survival/camp/adventure. A focused early test can use one obstacle or person, but does not reduce full first-playable cast, building and conflict coverage. The [priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md#the-game-we-need-to-be-able-to-play) and world target own that scope. [The playtest plan](../projects/first-playable-playtest-plan.md) distinguishes partial learning from whole-game acceptance.

## 2 Complete: make the game worth continuing

| Rank | Addition                            | Scope boundary                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ---- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Enemy and encounter variety         | Beyond the selected first predator/rival encounters, add different attack patterns, melee/ranged roles, an elite or boss-like objective and more encounter goals. Do not require regional war.  |
| 2    | More interesting destinations       | More ruins, caves, camps, resource locations, alternate approaches, shortcuts, secrets and discoveries. New places should change decisions, not just scenery.                                   |
| 3    | Useful gear and progression choices | More distinct weapons, protection, consumables, crafting upgrades, recipes and techniques. Add meaningful alternatives before vast rarity tables or universal build composition.                |
| 4    | Clear challenge and reward pacing   | Broader attainable goals, understandable risk, useful loot, new options and satisfying stops. A stronger enemy should not be merely an inflated health bar.                                     |
| 5    | Substantial survival activities     | Fuller fishing/hunting/foraging, farming, animal care or another worthwhile production activity beyond the required bootstrap and collaborative hunt. Do not require every activity for a meal. |
| 6    | Better building and useful defenses | Additional home/workshop structures, richer layouts, larger defenses and more expressive improvements beyond the required camp walls and entrance. Avoid compulsory maintenance tax.            |
| 7    | Simple trade and services           | Broader buying/selling, commissions or services beyond required sharing/basic trade. Authored prices can work before a dynamic economic model.                                                  |
| 8    | More companion activities           | Additional travel/fight/guard/rescue opportunities beyond required selected cooperation. Formal job agreements and elaborate domestic negotiations are not prerequisites.                       |
| 9    | Readable environmental variety      | Further darkness/light, weather or terrain interactions with understandable counterplay. The selected first predator/night experience remains Core; full climate/seasons/ecology do not.        |
| 10   | Optional creative and social play   | Gardening, decorating, music, a contest, a fair or a small playable game can be worthwhile in their own right without a combat buff.                                                            |
| 11   | Less repetition after success       | Useful shortcuts, learned methods, convenient inventory or a bounded routine for an actual recurring chore. Do not build chores merely to justify automation.                                   |

## All 26 worked situations

Pick a coherent small experience rather than implementing this list as a quest queue.

## Optional world families

The [four world proposals](README.md#four-possible-world-families) each own their current selection note. They are sources of connected examples, not simultaneous release commitments. Draw bounded content from medieval survival or living fantasy when it makes this game better; modern life and planetary science fiction remain separate primary experiences. Selecting a magical tool does not adopt a whole magical constitution.

A future explicit product choice can rerank an alternate family for its own intended experience. Family scores are not universal genre judgments, and a name or biography is not a new runtime-system requirement. The planetary proposal's no-piloting, no-space-navigation and no-dogfighting boundary remains unchanged.

## What does not become a new feature

“Prepare supplies” uses the existing inventory, bags, equipment and consumables unless a concrete missing interaction is demonstrated. “Agree on a task” and “report completion” are not substitutes for enjoyable help or hunting. The required player-built defensible camp belongs alongside opposition, exploration, invention and rewards; it is not permission to put domestic administration ahead of that whole experience. A realistic consequence is a correctness requirement for a chosen feature, not by itself a reason to choose more detail.

No scores in this guide close implementation tasks or establish fun empirically. Use existing owners to audit gaps and qualify the selected slice while preserving the complete required scenario. The [selection guide](selection-and-scale.md) explains how to evaluate candidates without treating old domestic-first or nearest-code ordering as current priority.
