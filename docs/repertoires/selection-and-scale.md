# Selecting ideas and paying for their complexity

[Library and labels](README.md) · [Current priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md) · [Ranked coverage](ranked-coverage.md) · [Worked combinations](combinations.md) · [Source atlas](source-atlas.md) · [Design foundation](design-foundation.md)

**Selection revision — October 3, 2026.** Build a complete, playable, enjoyable survival-adventure before elaborating its simulation. The library is a set of options, not a command to build whichever option is closest to the existing code. Choose desired rewards, meaningful resistance, usable player capabilities and an attainable outcome. [Category ownership](README.md#category-ownership-and-cross-references) locates the equipment, rules, skills and situations that deliver that experience. The [foundation](design-foundation.md) covers constructive and destructive possibilities; a world need not enable every category.

The [priority policy](gameplay-priorities.md) and its registers replace the former High/Try recommendations and within-domain ordering. Scores below are current product judgments, not a player survey, measured fun ranking, validated forecast, runtime audit or implementation approval. Engineering simplicity and novelty are separate from gameplay value.

## The strongest candidates to explore first

**Find the highest-priority missing capability first.** These are the same twenty retained candidate experiences, now explicitly scored and ordered for the current game. A proof's advanced layers must not become prerequisites for its basic playable portion. Detailed source patterns retain their own scores in [ranked patterns](ranked-patterns.md); all 25 combined scenarios are scored in [ranked coverage](ranked-coverage.md#all-25-worked-situations-reranked).

| Priority | Candidate | Player payoff and research tension | Smallest worthwhile proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 Core** | [A first evening](automation-creators.md#a-creation-with-a-first-evening) | A creation is useful when someone can actually play it; Dreams/Roblox/Spark distinguish creation power from a discoverable experience. | Without author coaching, a visitor understands a goal, makes a choice, faces resistance and reaches a real outcome. General pack distribution is not required. |
| **1 Core** | A duel with distinct weapons | Timing, reach, commitment and reading an opponent make competence enjoyable; [Hades](source-atlas.md#g22), [Elden Ring](source-atlas.md#g75) and [Kingdom Come II](source-atlas.md#g114) motivate checking feedback rather than relying on stat inflation. | A readable enemy, a useful weapon, an alternative defensive/escape method, actual damage and clear recovery. Spectator memory and political obligations are later depth. |
| **1 Core** | A treasure beyond a hostile boundary | Fear and curiosity make discovery rewarding; [Subnautica](source-atlas.md#g139), [Grounded](source-atlas.md#g142) and [Qud](source-atlas.md#g33) connect environment and access. | Reach a guarded or dangerous destination, obtain something useful and use it to open a new possibility. Inventory and bags are sufficient preparation unless a specific gap is demonstrated. |
| **1 Core** | A threat defeated for good | Earned relief makes a victory matter; [Middle-earth](source-atlas.md#w01) and [Avatar](source-atlas.md#w13) motivate preserving meaningful success. | Defeat or remove a bounded local threat and use the changed route or reward. Do not require a regional aftermath simulator or prohibit repeatable encounters elsewhere. |
| **2 Complete** | A dangerous quarry | Pursuit, preparation and victory make a rewarding arc; [The Witcher 3](source-atlas.md#g72), [V Rising](source-atlas.md#g146) and [Terraria](source-atlas.md#g29) connect targets to advancement, with grind as a risk. | Learn one creature's behavior, choose a counter and obtain useful loot, material or capability. Broader ecology and adaptive biographies are not required. |
| **2 Complete** | [Return expedition](adventure-discovery.md#the-return-expedition) | New knowledge or gear changes an interesting place; Outer Wilds/Elden Ring also warn against pointless repeated traversal. | Revisit a blocked location using a newly obtained tool, route or technique and gain a worthwhile result. |
| **2 Complete** | [Tool learning](mechanics.md#tools-teach-their-users) | Gear can teach lasting competence; FFV/FFIX and FFII motivate avoiding perverse practice incentives. | Learn one useful technique through meaningful use, then apply it beyond the original item. |
| **2 Complete** | A fortress worth taking | Victory offers equipment, access and leverage; [Bannerlord](source-atlas.md#g115), [Shadow of War](source-atlas.md#g116) and [Rust](source-atlas.md#g131) suggest checking that capture is not empty repetition. | Overcome a bounded defended site and gain useful access or loot. Garrison economics and the displaced claimant's full campaign are later layers. |
| **2 Complete** | A competition with a real loser | Recognition and mastery give familiar activities stakes; [Austen](source-atlas.md#w23), [The Sims](source-atlas.md#g24) and [The Culture](source-atlas.md#w17) motivate real competitors rather than universal wins. | A sporting, crafting or other bounded contest with understandable performance criteria, a prize and a worthwhile result for an imperfect attempt. |
| **2 Complete** | [Win by getting home](combat-rescue.md#win-by-getting-everyone-home) | An evacuation objective expands encounters beyond killing; PEAK/Kenshi/XCOM motivate checking dependence without a punishing loss spiral. | Help one person escape using actual supported movement, protection or care. Formal role administration is not the attraction. |
| **2 Complete** | [Retire a solution](mechanics.md#retire-a-successful-solution) | Solved repetition should free attention; factory-game inspiration also warns about opaque automation. | Remove or automate one demonstrated recurring burden with clear stop conditions. Do not build reserve-accounting machinery merely to create the demonstration. |
| **3 Depth** | A build with a decisive combination | Compositional power rewards understanding; [Path of Exile](source-atlas.md#g10), [Divinity: Original Sin II](source-atlas.md#g57) and [Slay the Spire](source-atlas.md#g43) suggest costs and counters. | Combine a few supported effects against an enemy that rewards a different approach. Basic equipment choices and useful upgrades come first. |
| **3 Depth** | [Borrowed purpose](mechanics.md#borrowed-purpose) | Cleverness can emerge from consistent affordances; Minecraft/immersive sims/Zelda and Scribblenauts motivate avoiding a universal answer. | Repurpose one supported tool in two worthwhile activities. A rack/sieve/load demo alone does not close an absent combat or exploration loop. |
| **3 Depth** | [A dependable meal](needs.md#a-dependable-meal) with a [public kitchen](settlements-architecture.md#the-public-kitchen) | Shared service life can create attachment but also chores and schedule anxiety. The combined institution is not basic food access. | After food and meaningful activities work, let a small shared kitchen reduce repetition. Negotiation over every meal is not a Core dependency. |
| **3 Depth** | [Useful refusal](mechanics.md#a-useful-refusal) | Principled companions can remain useful; Dragon Age/BG3 motivate avoiding opaque approval gates. | A companion declines one consequential activity and offers an alternative they can perform. First supply worthwhile activities and useful companionship. |
| **3 Depth** | A costly companion allegiance | Affection, trust and duty can conflict; [Dragon Age](source-atlas.md#g51) and [Mass Effect 2](source-atlas.md#g102) motivate neither universal obedience nor arbitrary gating. | A useful former enemy and an established ally create a concrete choice with discoverable reasons. |
| **3 Depth** | [Reliable disagreement](relationships.md#the-reliable-disagreement) | Relationships need not become unanimous; DAII and Austen suggest limits of one affection score. | Two people disagree about a consequential project while retaining a functional relationship. Chore negotiations are not the project's purpose. |
| **3 Depth** | Betrayal with evidence and opportunity | Trust changes what people reveal; [Among Us](source-atlas.md#g02), [DayZ](source-atlas.md#g132) and [Westeros](source-atlas.md#w10) motivate discoverable evidence rather than omniscient deception. | Two escorts have different aims for valuable cargo; observations allow suspicion and real choices. |
| **3 Depth** | Authority that can be used well or badly | Leadership changes access and other lives; [Crusader Kings III](source-atlas.md#g126), [Kingmaker](source-atlas.md#g58) and [Dune](source-atlas.md#w09) connect power to dependencies. | Allocate a captured asset and observe concrete benefit, loss or opposition. Do not need a complete political economy first. |
| **4 Detail** | [History in an object](history-myth.md#history-changes-an-object) | A particular possession can gain emotional value; Dwarf Fortress/Wildermyth/RDR2 also motivate avoiding lore noise. | Recognize a meaningful tool's history after a real adventure. Repair, display and witness appraisal do not replace useful gear or rewards. |

Pleasure, beauty and belonging can be the payoff. Gardens, music, a contest and familiar places remain legitimate Complete or Depth selections when they add something a player enjoys. **Fun-first does not mean combat-only, productivity-only or every pleasure must grant a numerical bonus.** It means not mistaking technical sophistication, simulated realism or routine administration for an enjoyable game.

## Different people want different returns

These retained perspectives help evaluate a chosen activity. They do not authorize fifteen simultaneous product roadmaps or elevate every specialist preference to a current-game foundation.

| Perspective | What may be worth their time | Cost to avoid | Good evidence of value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New player with a short evening | An understandable goal and satisfying stopping point | Terminology, sorting and obligatory lectures | Chooses and completes a small goal without being told the answer |
| Domestic-life player | Decorating, familiar faces and evolving households | Ever-declining meters and punishment for absence | Voluntarily returns to a settled place and chooses what to change |
| Explorer or investigator | Useful clues, alternate routes and consequential knowledge | Map-marker sweeps, exact phrase gates and pointless backtracking | Explains a discovery that changed the next action |
| Tactical or survival player | Capable opponents, skill, useful builds, dangerous rewards and meaningful victory/loss | Arbitrary death, invisible collateral and repetitive training | Understands an encounter and wants another worthwhile challenge |
| Competitive player | Outperforming a responsive rival and gaining a prize | Predetermined results or an advantage that erases all contest | Understands a win/loss and wants a rematch or stronger opponent |
| Heroic adventurer | Exploration, powers, genuine adversaries and people worth protecting | Every villain is a misunderstanding or victory changes nothing | Uses an improvement to overcome a threat and sees the result persist |
| Roleplayer or story creator | Motives, loyalties, romance, rivalry, ambition, betrayal and historical specificity | Predetermined emotion and generic assistant-like inhabitants | Plausible choices create distinct coherent continuations |
| Romance and social-drama player | Attraction, wit, intimacy, reputation and conflicting commitments | Universal availability or gifts replacing relationships | Recalls a particular shared experience and chooses its continuation |
| Builder and decorator | Functional layouts, reusable parts and beauty | Constant repairs and one numerical optimum | Another person understands and enjoys the place |
| Systems power user | Small grammars, useful operators, diagnostics and reusable methods | Hidden ordering, ignored effects and fragile dependencies | Explains, changes and reuses a creation |
| Conqueror, ruler or ambitious operator | Power, policy, command and actual opposition | Cosmetic power, universal obedience and automatic absolution | Identifies whose opportunities changed through a decision |
| Horror and harsh-survival player | Apprehension, vulnerability, resource judgment and earned safety | Repetitive jump scares, unknowable rule changes and constant interruption | Anticipates danger and recognizes earned safety |
| Community host | Stable projects, clear participation and bounded operating attention | Always-on model costs, grief-driven churn and constant presence | A group enjoys a session within explicit attention and operating budgets |
| Educator or researcher | Declared assumptions, instrumentation and reproducible comparisons | Fluent dialogue mistaken for empirical validity | Attributes results to a stated model and demonstrates limitations |
| Archivist, spectator or occasional visitor | Chronicles, performances and visible change | Requiring a full-time operator to appreciate anything | Understands an interesting event and chooses whether to participate |

Use [world tone and scope](design-foundation.md#world-tone-and-scope) for subjects and presentation. Pleasant, disturbing or explicit subject matter alone determines neither priority nor quality. These perspectives overlap: a builder can want a dangerous expedition and a quiet garden in the same game. Use optional activities and explicit world choices, not one fixed player stereotype.

## Complexity budgets that protect the experience

The figures here remain **illustrative prototype bounds**, not measured capacities or accepted product limits. A first scene might use 3–8 named people, 3–5 interacting families, one locality and a finite objective. Do not treat those counts as requirements for every encounter. Expand when added scope creates an observable, worthwhile decision.

| Kind of complexity | Pay for it when | Contain it by | Warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material properties | A property creates a useful distinction or tradeoff | Few named properties and bounded local processes | Hundreds of substances differ only in price and color |
| Relationship detail | It changes a concrete decision, interpretation or commitment | Selected consequential events; no all-to-all appraisal each tick | Every encounter adds mood updates and lengthy monologues |
| Autonomous actors | Behavior creates opposition, useful help or worthwhile continuity | Due work, native execution and inference at meaningful decisions | Cost grows with simulated seconds despite nothing interesting happening |
| Environmental simulation | Interactions are part of the chosen activity | Region graphs, windows, declared approximations and conservation | One fire or fluid change wakes the whole world |
| Institutions and economics | Services, claims or exchange offer real alternatives | Named stakeholders and bounded flows | Most play reconciles accounts with no meaningful choice |
| Creator combinations | Operators are inspectable and useful in distinct situations | Finite trusted families, composition caps and local failure domains | Description installs arbitrary behavior or creates unbounded effects |
| Historical persistence | A consequence can be revisited, recognized or used | Identity for meaningful things, selected records and attributed summaries | Every incidental object needs a generated biography |
| Lab accuracy | A separately selected experiment has a clear model and question | Controlled parameters, seeds, units, limits and sensitivity checks | “Looks lifelike” substitutes for validation |

A distant village can remain aggregate until visited, but established identities and commitments must stay consistent. A later detailed scene cannot retroactively invent witnesses; implementations must explain how aggregate quantities and individuals reconcile. These are constraints on a selected future slice, not a reason to build a universal simulation framework before the game.

Novel interpretation and consequential conversation may use models within permitted context. Movement, known production, accounting, repeated conditions and scheduled effects should not require fresh prose generation. This document establishes no measured cost, concurrency or real-time capacity.

## Combinations worth limiting initially

**Full ecology, metabolism and population cognition:** deliver a readable local predator and useful resource loop first. A garden is a legitimate selected activity, not the mandatory proof for a game missing enemies. Add ecological precision only when it earns a gameplay or explicit lab role.

**Every institution, dynamic markets and city-wide politics:** start with a bounded opponent, trader, objective or useful service that the current game actually needs. A neighborhood budget simulation is not automatically the next milestone merely because it is smaller than a civilization model.

**Freeform spells, mutable bodies and arbitrary topology:** choose one coherent magical capability only when selected, with real costs and counterplay. A few useful powers can precede a universal grammar.

**Persistent wars, lethal needs and severe injury:** choose an understandable loss/recovery contract, then add severity deliberately. Do not accidentally stack harsh systems until no interesting choices remain. Basic enemy combat does not need continental war or detailed lifelong injury.

**Infinite generation, marketplaces and versioned packs:** first prove that another person can enjoy the authored game. General distribution, world compilation and portability are separate platform scope.

## What would change our prioritization

Observe an **uncoached first encounter, a repeat encounter and an intentional variation**. Ask what the player wanted, what they expected, what happened, why it mattered and what they want to do next. Check meaningful failure and an unavailable capability. Unsupported attempts remain honest limitations, not narrated successes.

Promote a feature when its player payoff survives ordinary use: learned judgment wins a fight; loot changes equipment or access; a discovery enables a method; a companion makes a shared activity better; a creative activity is voluntarily chosen; or a routine removes demonstrated repetition. A reproducible experiment can justify its own specialist mode, not leapfrog a missing playable foundation.

Before advancing a candidate, identify the actual gap. Existing inventory and bags may already support supply preparation; existing rest may need usable presentation rather than a bedding subsystem. A small repair can stay low-priority even if easy to implement. Necessary integration and correctness work is part of the chosen slice, not a reason to expand every adjacent mechanic family.

When two options offer comparable player value, cost and dependency readiness can guide execution. They must not be used to avoid a higher-priority missing game capability. When only the author can make an activity interesting, improve the activity or narrow it before adding depth. This document records no completed playtests, runtime acceptance or implementation schedule.
