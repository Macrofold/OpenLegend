# Character formation and backstory seeds

[Library](README.md) · [Reading the labels](README.md#reading-the-labels) · [Selection and scale](selection-and-scale.md) · [Design foundation](design-foundation.md)

**Proposal catalogue · September 27, 2026.** These are seeds for people encountered in worlds that already have histories. A ruler has ruled, a thief has stolen, a mercenary has served employers, a parent has raised a household, and a child has experiences particular to their age. Past deeds, places, relationships and consequences give present skills and desires substance. Include established powers, celebrated or notorious figures, traveled practitioners, ordinary residents and some people whose larger lives are only beginning.

## Coverage of the playable foundation

These domains guide later inventories; the ten cards illustrate selected patterns. Seed-world labels mark fit, with individual examples depending on the chosen world’s rules.

| Domain                                      | Seed worlds | Lives and encounters to develop                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Power, office and public legend             | ALL         | Kings, gang leaders, prophets, famous explorers and founders have already won authority or recognition. Name the deeds, constituency, assets and limits that make a reputation matter, and distinguish the public story from established facts. |
| Journeys, campaigns and practiced trades    | ALL         | A mercenary has employers and campaigns behind them; a thief knows particular houses and prisons; a maker has completed works and customers in several places. Their competence appears in how they act and what others expect of them.         |
| Crime, complicity and chosen violence       | ALL         | A bandit has victims, a collaborator has benefited, a torturer has obeyed or issued particular orders. Let culpable acts remain part of a life whether the person regrets, justifies, repeats or profits from them.                             |
| Built lives and enduring relationships      | ALL         | People have raised families, made fortunes, lost businesses, sustained friendships and enjoyed good years. Adult love, sex and substance-use choices can shape those lives alongside work, art, faith and everyday pleasure.                    |
| Faith, allegiance and fractured reputations | ALL         | A royal knight has served a ruler, a prophet has followers who remember predictions, and a spy has protected some confidences while betraying others. Their loyalties, claims and witnessed conduct need not match neatly.                      |
| Loss, displacement and survival             | ALL         | War, exile, bereavement or adult experiences of abuse, rape and sexual assault can leave material and personal consequences. Survivors also have skills, joys, relationships, achievements and goals beyond those events.                       |
| Childhood, age and lives beginning again    | ALL         | Actual children have friends, games, school, responsibilities and limited knowledge; young adults may be starting out. Older people can begin a new pursuit while retaining the marriages, work, journeys and habits of earlier lives.          |
| Belonging, outsiders and self-invention     | ALL         | Migrants, transformed people, adopted children and returnees have particular communities, languages and histories. A new identity can change opportunities while old acquaintances, obligations and pleasures continue to exist.                |

### Casting an already inhabited world

The four [initial world proposals](README.md#four-possible-world-families) each select **15 people**, including **at least three figures of established public importance or near-legendary reputation** alongside ordinary residents and people whose influence is more local. This is a commitment for these casts, not a requirement that every small settlement contain several rulers or that every world use the same social hierarchy. A modern city's mayor, criminal magnate or acclaimed artist can have the reach a king, prophet or renowned adventurer has elsewhere. Meetability may mean a public appearance, a working visit, a commission, a confrontation or access earned through an existing relationship.

Most adults need a history of things already done, not only an origin and an ambition. Give past events actual places, participants and outcomes: the company they commanded, the household they built, the person they sold out, the city where they prospered, the discovery other people still use. Ordinary lives do not need concealed royal ancestry or a legendary exploit to earn their place. Some people have remained rooted and accomplished a great deal locally; a few are inexperienced, and children have shorter, age-appropriate histories.

| Question                                            | What belongs in a useful biography                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What has this person already done?                  | Specific completed work, adventures, relationships, decisions or wrongdoing, with effects that survive into the present.                                                                                |
| Why have other people heard of them?                | A title or reputation with a recognizable audience and basis. Mark exaggeration, disputed accounts and supernatural claims separately from authored facts.                                              |
| What power and competence do they actually possess? | Skills, offices, property, followers, debts, access and dependencies; a famous name need not make someone the best fighter or give them universal knowledge.                                            |
| What is it like to meet them?                       | An action, voice, situation or social response that reveals the person before the player reads a biography. Make something possible: bargain, compete, learn, joke, work, investigate, refuse or fight. |
| Where does their past remain visible?               | Other people remember, buildings and routes remain, obligations continue and successes can still be enjoyed. Named cast ties should have their own histories.                                           |
| What are they doing now?                            | Independent purposes and pleasures that continue without the player; an encounter can change an existing life rather than initiate it.                                                                  |

These questions support judgment rather than an identical biography template. A title, a traumatic event, a hobby and a request do not by themselves make a person memorable. Give the cast different scales, rhythms and kinds of experience, then let their existing lives meet on the playable map.

## The child of a successful compromise

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: authored biography and present commitments.

A character grew up in a town saved by an unpopular treaty and learned that adults can be both right and disliked. Now a mediator, they overvalue agreement and hesitate when refusal is necessary. Give them a playful hobby and someone who remembers the treaty differently. Their story offers diplomacy without requiring a secret royal bloodline or a murdered family.

Seeds: [Dragon Age: Origins](source-atlas.md#g51), [Crusader Kings III](source-atlas.md#g126), [Austen’s social worlds](source-atlas.md#w23).

## The late beginner

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: authored career history and teachable goals.

A retired warehouse clerk starts learning instrument making, bringing excellent inventory habits and terrible patience with mistakes. They may fund younger apprentices while struggling to ask them for help. Players can teach, collaborate or commission a flawed first instrument. Competence in one life should matter without granting instant mastery in another; age is not a uniform personality modifier.

Seeds: [Fire Emblem: Three Houses](source-atlas.md#g64), [Final Fantasy IX](source-atlas.md#g86), [Kingdom Come: Deliverance II](source-atlas.md#g114).

## The person who stayed

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: remembered local changes and relationships.

While friends left to seek fortune, one resident kept the clinic open, learned everyone's routes and quietly changed the neighborhood. They are proud, curious and sometimes resent being treated as provincial. Returning travelers need their knowledge. Avoid revealing that staying was merely failure; rootedness can be a chosen adventure with its own growth and influence.

Seeds: [Dragon Age II](source-atlas.md#g52), [Assassin's Creed III](source-atlas.md#g99), [The Wire](source-atlas.md#w21).

## Two apprentices one accident

**ALL · Play · Try · 2/Extend.** Gap: distinct witnessed histories and enduring but revisable interpretations.

A workshop accident taught one apprentice to document everything and the other to improvise before machinery fails. Neither conclusion is entirely wrong. Years later, a shared repair tests both approaches. Keep their lives larger than the accident: friends, jokes, ambitions and ordinary successes. The same event should produce different people rather than one standardized trauma trait.

Seeds: [Wildermyth](source-atlas.md#g25), [XCOM 2](source-atlas.md#g125), [The Broken Earth](source-atlas.md#w18).

## Raised between calendars

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: authored overlapping traditions and contextual expectations.

A child of two trading communities learned different feast days, greetings and assumptions about punctuality. As an adult they translate practical expectations, sometimes enjoying their freedom and sometimes exhausting themselves as everyone's mediator. Let them choose when to explain. Mixed heritage must not automatically confer perfect diplomacy or make the character an encyclopedia for the player.

Seeds: [Fire Emblem: Path of Radiance](source-atlas.md#g61), [Octopath Traveler II](source-atlas.md#g108), [Avatar: The Last Airbender](source-atlas.md#w13).

## The former beneficiary

**ALL · Play · Try · 2/Extend.** Gap: material entanglements and choice-driven restitution.

A surveyor discovers their comfortable education was funded by an unfair water concession. They want reform but still depend on the institution's pension and friendships. Players can help inspect records, improve service or organize negotiations. The interesting choice is what to change now, not a binary confession that instantly purifies them or makes them irredeemable.

Seeds: [Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire](source-atlas.md#g55), [Fallout: New Vegas](source-atlas.md#g112), [Fallout](source-atlas.md#w28).

## An ordinary happy childhood

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: authored sources of security and curiosity.

A cook grew up with patient caregivers and expects conflicts to be discussable. That confidence helps strangers feel welcome but sometimes underestimates real hostility. They leave home because they want to taste unfamiliar food, not because home was destroyed. Stable love can generate courage, humor and obligations every bit as useful for stories as suffering.

Seeds: [Animal Crossing: New Horizons](source-atlas.md#g03), [The Sims](source-atlas.md#g24), [Pokémon](source-atlas.md#w08).

## The competent understudy

**ALL · Play · High · 1/Compose.** Gap: authored expertise, recognition and succession pressures.

An assistant has quietly run the expedition's logistics for years while its charismatic leader receives credit. When leadership becomes vacant, they must decide whether they want the public role at all. Players can recognize expertise without forcing promotion. Their competence should have visible evidence; ambition may mean better working conditions, rightful command or a ruthless bid to surpass the old leader.

Seeds: [Fire Emblem: The Blazing Blade (Fire Emblem, GBA)](source-atlas.md#g60), [Final Fantasy VI](source-atlas.md#g83), [Mass Effect 2](source-atlas.md#g102).

## A return to a changed home

**ALL · Play · Try · 2/Extend.** Gap: persistent places and independently changing relationships.

A traveler returns with money and stories to find their old friend has built the life they once planned together. They can become a guest, contribute to new work or leave again. Nostalgia becomes a negotiable relationship with the present. The world must not freeze absent characters or punish them for growing beyond the player's remembered version.

Seeds: [Dragon Age II](source-atlas.md#g52), [Final Fantasy VI](source-atlas.md#g83), [Narnia](source-atlas.md#w19).

## The maker of an inconvenient truth

**ALL · Play · Try · 2/Extend.** Gap: evidence-bearing discoveries and social consequences.

A young restorer uncovers names beneath a celebrated mural and cannot tell whether they record an injustice or an old repair. Their first goal is accuracy; public pressure demands a verdict. Players investigate, preserve, explain or postpone. Give the character reasons to care about the mural's beauty too, and let incomplete evidence remain incomplete.

Seeds: [Outer Wilds](source-atlas.md#g34), [The Elder Scrolls III: Morrowind](source-atlas.md#g113), [Macondo](source-atlas.md#w24).
