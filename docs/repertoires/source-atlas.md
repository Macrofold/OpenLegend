# Source atlas and research-to-idea map

[Library](README.md) · [Selection and scale](selection-and-scale.md)

## Method and limits

This atlas gives an individual design takeaway, caution and destination for **the original 130 game subjects and all 28 fictional worlds**. It synthesizes dossier interaction cases, mechanics, reception/criticism and transfer discussions, together with the related thematic research. It is not a claim that every citation in every dossier was independently reopened, that every inherited research gate is complete, or that these new ideas have been tested with players.

The [research roster](../../archive/02-research/game-inspiration/research-roster.md) now contains 148 subjects, including 18 planned survival additions (G131–G148); those additions are outside this atlas. Their inclusion in the roster does not establish completed research or synthesis.

The takeaways are interpretations. The cautions include both source-reported friction and our design implications; they do not assert that all reviewers agreed, that a complaint remains true in every version, or that a mechanic caused commercial success. Consult the linked dossier for editions, named reviews, access limitations, examples and primary evidence. Broad reception comparisons cannot rank the popularity of individual mechanics from these purposive samples. Historical, proposed and shipped systems retain their distinctions.

Source codes are navigation anchors inherited from the research roster (G) and this ordered world list (W), not opaque gameplay categories. Catalogue cards display source titles. Each catalogue destination shows where the lesson informs the new library; it does not claim every card in that catalogue directly derives from that one source.

## Additional worldbuilding reading

These primary-author or estate-published sources were consulted on September 27, 2026. They inform a craft approach, not empirical guarantees of player enjoyment:

- [Carl Hostetter on Tolkien's invented languages](https://www.tolkienestate.com/scholarship/carl-hostetter-tolkiens-invented-languages/): linguistic development and invented history can shape one another. Our naming palettes use recurring sounds, older place-name layers and social variation, without borrowing Tolkien's vocabulary.
- [Arden Smith on writing systems](https://www.tolkienestate.com/scholarship/arden-smith-writing-systems/): an invented script and the language written in it are distinct. Tactile signs, knot records and spoken forms therefore receive separate purposes in the proposals.
- [Sanderson's Second Law](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-second-law): limitations can make a power dramatically useful. This motivates finite sources, range and consequences rather than a generic unlimited magic resource.
- [Sanderson's Third Law](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-third-law-of-magic): develop implications of existing powers before continually adding new ones. This motivates one magical transfer supporting cooking, shelter, diplomacy and rescue. These are an author's heuristics, not universal laws of fiction or game design.
