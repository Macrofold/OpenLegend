## Direction and feedback retained

The [creative brief](../../../art-direction/final-board/creative-brief.md) remains the overall target. For the earlier study, Mike rejected a painterly model merely displayed at low resolution, rigid cape movement, a visible front-cloth gap and distracting shimmer during rotation. The study explores purposeful pixel detail and material colors, readable adult proportions, fitted cloth and dimensional lighting. These requests are recorded in its design/refinement files; they do not establish that every visual issue is resolved.

The isolated viewer's target is about 192 vertical pixels with integer display scaling. The earlier game pilot samples the model into a fixed 128×192 color/depth image while the world renders at full screen resolution. Those are historical implementation choices in their respective contexts, not an approved universal resolution for all artwork.

On October 14, Mike reported that the game mercenary looked too small, gained apparent detail when zoomed in, lost its eyes and face, and showed moving pixels or underlying body parts during rotation. He requested a new design whose colored blocks belong permanently to the body and clothing, preserving the old model. That feedback authorizes this iteration; it is not final aesthetic acceptance. [Current rendering behavior](../../world-presentation.md#animated-character-pixels) and the [iteration plan](../../projects/completed/mercenary-voxel-iteration.md) own the resulting contract and delivery scope.

## Visible height and whole-character pixels

MER12 is the current scoped correction. It restores original body/cape diffuse maps inside a substantially coarser image of the complete animated character, rather than reducing the face twice or leaving the silhouette at screen resolution. A separate vertical display correction normalizes the upright body extent to about 10% above the nearby people's opaque artwork and preserves exact projected feet; horizontal width and the fixed 1.1 physical rig/cloth scale remain separate. Selection, authorized read-through and outlines share the resulting pixels/depth, while anchors and inverse pointer rays use the same displayed transform. [Current presentation](../../world-presentation.md#animated-character-pixels), [A3D18](../../limits/3d-pixel-art.md#a3d18--restored-original-animated-mercenary) and the [completed plan](../../projects/completed/mercenary-visible-size-and-pixels.md) own behavior, bounds and the scoped delivery.

Actual standing captures retain **94 opaque image rows versus 86** for the nearby person (about **9.3% taller**) across the tested low/default/high-pitch, normal/close and perspective views. Same-frame camera changes preserve displayed anchors and actual mouse picking; natural Walk/Idle/pause, sampled edge coverage, selection/read-through/fade, resize/DPR and image-resource lifecycle checks passed without page errors. [Detailed evidence](../../verification/mercenary-default.md#visible-height-and-whole-character-pixels--october-15-2026) distinguishes those scoped results from broader art/device acceptance. This choice returns to camera-sampled live 3D art following Mike's preference for the original model; rotation and animation can change its pixels. It does not deliver his earlier requested permanent 3D-cell body, and final creator acceptance of the sampling tradeoff remains open. Preserve every original/block source and failed-attempt observation; no production-rights or general art-pipeline acceptance follows.
