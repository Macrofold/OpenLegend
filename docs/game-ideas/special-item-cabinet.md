# Special-item cabinet: digital time capsules

**Status:** Endorsed creative direction; product details and implementation are future work. [Follow-up: ND30](../maintainers/needs-design.md#nd30--personal-journal-extensions-and-exported-or-printed-editions).

## The player promise

Let the player designate an item as special to them and keep it visible in a cabinet on their account. Clicking it opens a beautiful, tactile card together with their notes and special moments from its history. The object becomes a **digital time capsule**: something to revisit across characters and worlds because of what happened with it.

The player chooses what matters. A commonplace lantern, a friend's gift, a first successful craft or a much-repaired tool can deserve a place beside a rare relic. Neither rarity nor a system's judgment of significance is required. Personal attachment can grow through play without the item gaining more power.

## Visiting the cabinet

The player marks an item as special during play, then returns to it from their account cabinet. They can inspect or turn its card, read or add a note, and revisit selected moments associated with that particular object.

| Part               | What makes it personal                                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The card           | A recognizable representation of this object, with its known origin, description and meaningful wear, repairs or inscriptions   |
| Personal notes     | The player's own writing, or an optional generated draft they can edit, about why it matters to them                            |
| Remembered moments | How they acquired it; a memorable use, discovery, rescue, repair, gift or parting; the relevant character and world where known |
| Connections        | A way to open the permitted account of a moment and understand how it relates to the item                                       |

Use the [tactile-card idea](tactile-trait-cards.md) for material, depth, light and inspection, with the same readable controls and reduced-motion treatment. The cabinet should feel satisfying to return to even when there is no new reward. It need not replay a full scene to make a moment vivid; a faithful short account can carry the memory.

For example, an ordinary lantern might recall buying it before a first expedition, using it during a rescue, repairing its cracked glass and eventually giving it to a friend. Its card can retain the repair mark, but opening that mark also reveals the remembered episode and the player's words about it. The mark is an entrance into a story.

## Keep the time capsule when the adventure moves on

The intended experience is lasting account access even after the object is lost, destroyed, gifted or traded, after the character dies or retires, and when the original world is no longer available. Designing that durability is part of this idea; it must not quietly depend on the item remaining in current inventory or the source world staying active.

The cabinet preserves a personal representation and recollection. It should distinguish remembered possession from current possession and should not imply a known current location when none is known. Keeping the card does not restore the physical item, create a playable duplicate or make an ordinary object transferable between worlds. The player should also be able to remove a keepsake or their own notes deliberately.

## Notes and moments with an honest history

Generated recollections use actual retained events and lore available to this player. They can help tell the story, but should not invent an acquisition, a successful use or someone else's thoughts to fill a gap. Keep unknown details unknown. Let the player choose and annotate the moments that matter; do not require a complete log of every use before an item can be treasured.

Personal notes can express affection, uncertainty or a different interpretation of events. Make their authorship clear and keep them distinct from the underlying record. Editing a note does not rewrite what happened. Reuse the existing [knowledge](../knowledge.md) and [history](../memory-architecture.md) boundaries: the human's account recollection does not automatically teach a new character, and the cabinet does not expose hidden events or another person's private material.

Personal notes and memories remain private unless the player chooses a supported sharing action. Trading the object does not automatically hand over its former owner's private time capsule. Any history offered with a trade needs its own permitted, understandable presentation.

## How this connects to the other ideas

[Official-world relics](official-world-relics.md) concern collectibles that can actually be assigned to characters in other official worlds. A relic can also be a personal keepsake, but the cabinet is useful for ordinary objects too, including meaningful objects from custom worlds. Marking one as special grants no official collectible eligibility or gameplay advantage.

The [personal Journal proposal](../projects/personal-journal-feature-spec.md) provides relevant foundations for selected moments, personal authorship and source permissions. This cabinet adds a distinct account collection and durable recollection goal; the existing narrow export/draft proposal does not already deliver it. Neither relic transfer nor Journal export needs to be finished before this idea can be explored on its own.

## A useful first design comparison

Compare an ordinary treasured object with a rare official relic. For each, author a hypothetical journey through acquisition, a memorable use, a player note and a later revisit after giving up possession. Show the card, the selected moment and what the player can still understand when the original item or world is unavailable. The ordinary object tests whether personal history can carry the appeal; the relic tests how that history coexists with authored lore and collection identity.

Ask whether a player recognizes the object, remembers why it mattered and wants to revisit it without receiving another reward. Let them choose which moments matter and leave without completing a collection. The point of the comparison is to judge attachment and the pleasure of recollection, not assume that attractive rendering or greater rarity proves enjoyment.

[ND30](../maintainers/needs-design.md#nd30--personal-journal-extensions-and-exported-or-printed-editions) owns the remaining design work, including moment selection and notes, durable retention when a world is unavailable, and correction, deletion and sharing permissions. This note selects no storage architecture, automatic capture policy or delivery priority.
