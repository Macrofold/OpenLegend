## October 31, 2026 — One look-neutral art workflow with tool defaults and a 3D route

Mike asked how the claude.ai-synced `openlegend-art-pipeline` skill differs from the repository's art skill. The synced skill is a runbook for an `artgen` tool that never existed in this repository. The real workflow was sound but abstract. Mike then asked for the workflow to be fully redesigned:

- no built-in artistic direction;
- platforms and models as request options with current defaults;
- explicit artistic latitude;
- enough detail for a junior to follow;
- priority on what makes the game fun and distinctive rather than parity with other games;
- the simplest system that works, avoiding over-engineering at this zero-player stage.

The [art workflow](../../production-workflows/art-flows/README.md) is now one procedure in seven stages (Brief, Explore, Prepare, Make, Finish, Check, Preserve), with Mike deciding only the direction, the candidate and acceptance.

- **Settings:** requests can set tools per stage (presets `standard`, `higgsfield`, `both`) and **fidelity** (`exact`, `faithful`, `interpretive`, with named locks and free areas). Interpretations are recorded rather than hidden.
- **Defaults:** a dated [tool roster](../../production-workflows/art-flows/tools.md) names each stage's default, with commands, cost estimates and job recovery.
- **3D route:** [3D models](../../production-workflows/art-flows/3d-models.md) takes a concept or character sheet to a checked GLB through clean A-pose reference views, three candidates, Blender cleanup, rig routes, clips and an in-game look check.
- **Play checks:** [play checks](../../production-workflows/art-flows/profiles.md#play-checks) (name, same thing, state, threat, construction, scale) judge art by what helps play: recognisable people, readable threats, and visible states the simulation actually records.

**Look decoupled.** The workflow lost its style wording. A stable [art-direction entry point](../../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-direction-at-a-glance) now names the current owners, and its "how the current look changes production" notes collect the look-specific rules from the old pages, world presentation and research: the renderer's final pixel pass, surface preparation per the existing recipes, modelling heights, review conditions and animation holds. Changing the look now means editing only the art direction.

**Adopted from artgen:** the reference-views step, concrete defaults and candidate counts, outside library models as candidates, and real heights. **Not adopted:** its automatic model-wide pixelation step (surface treatment belongs to the look), models stored in Git, its style bible and its fixed model list. Mike's Higgsfield account already offers Tripo besides Meshy, so the default 3D step mixes two models without a new account.

**Consolidated:** the Standard and Higgsfield pages merged into the shared procedure and tools page, and `/ol-design` became an alias.

The helper scripts, a local model preview in the study viewer and a first proof run are designed but not built ([AF05–AF09](../../maintainers/art-direction.md#art-flow-follow-through)); open decisions are listed in the [feature specification](../../projects/art-workflow-feature-spec.md#10-open-decisions-and-questions).

**Same day, follow-up decisions.**

- **Streamlined path:** a supplied character sheet skips Explore. The brief is filled automatically, and clean views on the sheet are cropped rather than regenerated. Requests can delegate picks, ask for one candidate, skip the critique, or defer archiving. Check keeps the technical file check and one look in the game's renderer; Preserve keeps files outside Git, recorded.
- **Proof run:** it waits for Mike's explicit go.
- **Skeleton:** MPFB is the provisional body family, with the Mixamo or GameEngine rig preferred over the bone-heavy Default rig.
- **Training on uploads:** Higgsfield's terms allow it to train on uploads, while the OpenAI API does not by default. OD1 and OD8 now record that tradeoff.
- **Character creator:** a character creator and varied NPCs would be built on that parametric family, with values sampled per NPC, not on generated one-off meshes. The design is registered as [DG37](../../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg37--character-appearance-a-creator-and-varied-populations) and not selected.

**Later the same day, Mike decided:**

- **Upload as you go:** every meaningful art output goes to S3 as soon as its batch finishes, and only the exact references are kept locally, except very likely temporary scratch.
- **Higgsfield for all generation (OD1).**

- **People:** the MPFB study people are not good enough, and the mercenary sets the bar. - **Legs follow travel:** Walk, Run and Sprint stride clips blend by displayed speed at the rate that keeps planted feet in place. A planted foot now drifts a tenth to a fifth of body speed instead of all of it.
- **Working look change:** smooth poses and the simulated cape replace the held 12-poses-per-second cadence and the rigidly carried cape selected on October 16. At running speed, held poses slid planted feet with the body, and the carried cape tilted like a board with the run's lean. - **Strikes** play the mercenary's own Attack and Punch clips in step with his drawn position, every frame from start to contact, instead of switching to the sprite.
- **Capture:** his pixel capture now covers the current pose, so strides, strikes and the cape are no longer cut off.
- **Frozen after leaving the screen:** since October 17, his first off-screen sleep zeroed his animation speed for good; it no longer does.
- **Tradeoff:** the steadier playback runs drawn bodies, the controlled one included, about 0.25 s behind the newest snapshot instead of 0.08 s. Health bars and effects still appear on arrival, so a struck body can react just before the drawn blow lands.
- **Five people:** the proof-run people now have the same eight clips, including Dodge and Wave, which have no game event yet and play in the art study. `art-tool.py clips` gained world-space retargeting and in-place strides.

## November 1, 2026 — Wide views at half the cost: a closer zoom limit, daylight without fire shadows, thinner woods

Wide camera views of the plain-3D world were GPU-bound at 30 fps on Mike's Mac ([SW18.18](../../maintainers/spatial-world.md#sw18--world-presentation-delivery)). Measurement found the time going to the firs' needle geometry, the sun's and fire's shadow maps redrawn every frame, and broadleaf leaf cards. Smaller 8 m instancing cells removed some of that work without changing the picture. Mike then chose three visible remedies. The zoom-out limit halves from 30 to 15. Firelight shadows fade out as the sun rises and back in as it sets, so they never appear at once. The perimeter woodland, which he found a little dense, leaves three in ten tree positions empty. At the same zoom the two content changes cut GPU time by about 30%, and the widest view costs about half what the old widest did ([evidence](../../verification/wide-view-frame-rate.md)). The tradeoffs:

- No view shows all of the 128×112 m Main at once.
- By day a fire's light passes through walls and floors within its 8 m reach, which matters only once fires burn indoors ([VP01](../../limits/interface.md#vp01)).
- Existing saves keep their trees.

Measured later that day in a 1466×661 view in a visible browser window on Mike's Mac, every view from zoom 4 to 15 held 60 fps by day, at sunset with both kinds of shadow and at dusk, even before counting the thinner woods ([evidence](../../verification/wide-view-frame-rate.md#real-display-check)). Lighter firs and simpler needle blades were not needed at that size.
