# Week of November 2, 2026

## November 4, 2026 — Shared instructions instead of private memory, and people built from parts

Mike works across many AI platforms, so project knowledge, rules and lessons live only in this repository. [AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md#documentation-is-a-maintained-source-of-truth) forbids creating or updating an AI tool's private memory, such as Claude Code's, because it silos knowledge from the other platforms.

The same day Mike rejected the MakeHuman library bodies as the game's people ("They are horrible"). He had never asked for them: they stayed the game's people because the game already used them, his November 3 rejection was read as a crouch to fix, and his yes to moving the character fixes into the game was taken to cover the bodies under them. Root guidance now treats his rejections as decisions about the thing itself. Name what work builds on and check it against recorded rejections; offer replacing as well as fixing; a yes covers only what its question stated ([task scope](../../../AGENTS.md#task-scope-and-authorization), [developer questions](../../../AGENTS.md#resolve-developer-questions-before-dependent-work)).

The [art direction](../../../art-direction/decisions.md#how-the-current-look-changes-production) records the rejection. Generating whole people is no answer either: image to 3D makes a dressed person as one shell, with the clothes fused in and no body underneath. Mike therefore requires every person to be built from parts, each selected or generated from their character sheet: a body, the closest match from a canonical set covering every body type (or a report that none fits); an adjustable face and hairstyle; and clothing they wear and can take off. The steps are designed before the art workflow adopts them ([people built from parts](../../maintainers/art/people.md), [DG37](../../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg37--character-appearance-a-creator-and-varied-populations) ND39–ND42), and the [3D route](../../production-workflows/art-flows/3d-models.md#step-1--choose-the-build-route) asks Mike before making a person until then. Until the cast is rebuilt from parts, Ada and Mara Reed move onto the models generated from their sheets, whose clothes are still fused into the body, and the other people keep their shipped bodies. Review links open the art study's comparison of every version ([comparison sheets](../../production-workflows/art-flows/README.md#comparison-sheets)).

## November 3, 2026 — The cast at their own heights

Mike noticed that every person shared one height. Every prepared library person and every proof-run model stood 1.85 m, the look's single adult height; only the mercenary differed. The [character heights study](../../../art-direction/studies/2026-11-03-character-heights/README.md) gives the nine people their own heights, read from their stories since none states one: from Mara Reed at 1.58 m to Tavin Vale at 1.82 m, beside the unchanged 2.035 m mercenary. Mike identified the proof run's fifth sheet, first taken for the base world's Orin, as Maer Fen of the medieval-survival proposal (now 1.68 m, compact and wiry as his design notes have him), and Sela Tern, first set at an ordinary 1.67 m, now stands a commanding 1.76 m; the proof-run and motion-study records now name Maer Fen. Each of its 35 models (every outfit, sheet model and candidate) is a published or proof-run file with one uniform scale on its root, so clips and textures are unchanged.

The art study gains **Character lineup · own heights** and a **Characters ·** subject per person comparing all their versions, from catalog folders named `study-*` that never enter the game's runtime catalogs; its version picker now offers one option per version. The [art direction](../../../art-direction/decisions.md#how-the-current-look-changes-production) gives each person their own height, and the [3D page](../../production-workflows/art-flows/3d-models.md#step-2--add-the-3d-facts-to-the-brief) asks for it in the brief. In play every person still stands on the world's single 1.75 m body. The study page could not show more than four models at once from a private collection, because its download queue counted only public files; it now counts every file in the selected release. With Mike's permission the study is in the public studies release, and the hosted art and scene studies (the game's art-inspector link) are rebuilt from it, replacing their October 18 build, so they also show the November 1 motion files.
