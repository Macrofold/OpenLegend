# Make a person from a selected concept

**Accepted for the first family pilot.** Mike accepted the [character and wardrobe design](../../projects/people-from-parts-feature-spec.md) and [technical design](../../projects/people-from-parts-tech-design.md), and authorized the [first three-body proof](../../../art-direction/studies/2026-11-05-adjustable-people-proof/README.md). Its concept selection precedes 3D production. Routine production needs a qualified body, rig and parts library through the [people build gate](../../maintainers/art/people.md). A concept study can end at the selected drawing.

The repeatable output is a person's appearance recipe, separate editable parts and a default outfit assembled from those parts. A second character can use the same coat on different measurements. Changing the coat later leaves the person's face, body and gait source intact.

For implementation use the [family authoring contract](../../projects/people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md): exact source roles, input resolution, independent shape/age evaluation, skin production, build outputs and isolated experiments. The [bundled control definitions](../../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#control-definitions) own the human endpoints and age samples. Finish and approve one covered person's anatomy and materials before expanding the control library. Shared topology alone is not art acceptance; the failed scripted anatomy comparison is not the normal native image-to-3D route, and SAM's plain inferred body is a foundation rather than finished skin. Produce the selected trio first; then qualify the full family before claiming every slider or shared wardrobe combination works. The skill invokes this one procedure; do not copy its control table or add a competing recipe evaluator to the skill.

## Prepare the family once

1. Start with the selected world's art direction, accepted concepts and body profile. For the [bundled adult family](../../worlds/base/people-appearance.md), follow its starting frames, measurement ranges, presets and [source decisions](../../../art-direction/decisions.md#how-the-current-look-changes-production). Use the same arrangement and numbering of vertices for the frames, including the neck seam, eyes and body landmarks.
2. In Blender, leave space under the arms and between the legs in the neutral rest pose. Sculpt each included control on a copy of the neutral mesh with identical vertex order and save its change as a relative shape key. Place joints and attachment points on the shaped landmarks. Apply the stature adjustment once. Save the control ranges, landmark measurements and qualified combinations with the family.
3. Prove the complete body first. Keep the shaped torso, pelvis and limbs beneath separate [world-selected coverage pieces](../../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#baseline-coverage). Fit the head and hair to the contrasting bodies and intermediate shape. Inspect the body and coverage separately in the authoring tool, then show front/profile/back and clay-face comparisons against the selected construction sheet. Mike judges the body and face before the wardrobe is built. Record an unsupported shape or failed likeness as a gap.
4. Build templates as the selected outfit needs them. A shirt benefits from a torso shell with sleeve openings, a skirt from a surface bridging the legs, and studs from ear anchors. Preserve the fitting data, openings and reviewed ranges with each reusable part. Qualify the assembled outfit on different builds before admitting those parts to the library.

## From a description to separate parts

1. **Brief and concepts.** Extract authored appearance facts, height, age presentation, distinguishing features, garment function, closures, layers and requested motions. Follow the world's look and the existing tool roster. When Mike asks to choose from concepts, offer three distinct directions on a matched-scale sheet and wait for his selection before spending on 3D. Start at the construction sheet if he supplies a selected concept. Record what the description states and what the artist inferred. [Tools and defaults](tools.md#default-roster)
2. **Construction sheet.** Show the person in the selected world’s fitted baseline undergarments from front, profile and back, feet on one line with a scale bar. Add the selected dressed silhouette, neutral front/profile/three-quarter face views, the scalp and back of the hair, and the actual left/right side of asymmetric details. Give each removable item an ID, colour regions, layer order and separate views, including the inside or fastening where necessary. Send matching views of **one piece** to each image-to-3D job. Where padding obscures a body measurement, record the uncertainty and resolve it with a covered construction view or an explicit interpretation.
3. **Body and face first.** Start with the closest family preset, then adjust continuous proportions against the sheet. Use measured height when given. Convert the height control through the family's range and record rest stature from bare soles to the anatomical crown. Keep dressed bounds, including hair, shoes and headwear, for framing and clearance. The ordinary model report measures the whole asset. Compare the covered body to the sheet at matching scale. Fit the face using a face preset and local controls. If the concept needs a distinctive sculpt, transfer the canonical head surface onto a sculpted or isolated generated head. Save the resulting shape with the shared neck seam, eyelid loops and expression topology. Check the actual untextured jaw, cheeks, eye openings, eyeball fit and profile. Set iris colour, skin and hair colour separately. If the body or locked likeness falls outside the supported range, save the nearest candidate with its measured differences and report the uncovered landmarks to Mike. A new body type is his decision.
4. **Hair and facial details.** Select or model sculpted hair on the reference scalp, fitting the roots and hairline while preserving volume at the tips. Provide the style's supported adjustment ranges and a compressed crown for hats where needed. Preserve the exposed fringe/ends and the original hat-off shape. Use the family's reserved hair bones for braids and ponytails. Fit beards and brows against their facial landmarks. Check the same person bareheaded and in the proposed headgear, including both ears. Save the hair piece separately from the person's chosen style controls and colour.
5. **Wardrobe.** For each item, compare available parts and colour variants with the sheet. Reuse a matching item, adapt a template for predictable construction, or generate/sculpt a distinctive piece on its own and then fit it. Preserve cuffs, lining, hem thickness and openings where visible. Inspect the inside and thickness around each open neck, cuff and hem, then check the separate piece over the intact body. If a generator fills boots with feet or joins a shirt to the mannequin, rebuild that geometry or reject the result before rigging. Record one editable source per item with its fitting method, equipment ports, covered regions, layer, material masks and compatible family revision. Straps and plates may be separate meshes of one equippable item.
6. **Assembly and handoff.** Apply the saved body, face, hair and materials. Resolve variants and named supports in the family’s fit order, fit the chosen pieces, apply target-specific coverage masks, then pose the dressed character. The [binding and mask contract](../../projects/people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md#external-family-package) owns those rules. First bake only the combinations needed for delivery into animated GLBs. Preserve the covered base, removable components and assembly recipe. A later runtime assembly or supported outfit variant needs a successful in-game change of clothes before an artist can report that the player can swap the item.

Respect the requested stop. Compare whole-character design directions and review the uncertain generated parts. Use a catalogued boot directly when its exact release fits the design. Before submission, estimate the missing pieces and review rounds against the one task budget. A full wardrobe can involve several separate jobs. ## Fit one new garment in Blender

For example, a shirt should get wider when its wearer gains torso girth, keep a little air between fabric and skin, and bend at the shoulder when the wearer reaches. The proposed offline fitting method draws on documented source-to-target resizing, surface bindings and garment weight transfer. [Fitting research and sources](../../../art-direction/research/3d-pixel-worlds/adjustable-people-and-wardrobe.md)

1. **Construct the neutral garment.** Fit its outside and openings to the approved reference body or template at rest. Model the intended ease, the space between cloth and body. Give outer clothes a reference surface that also allows for their supported inner layers. In Blender, duplicate the torso template into a separate object, cut the neckline and arm openings, and shape the sleeves and hem. Set thickness, finish topology and UVs before creating shape targets, so their vertex indices remain stable. Keep the body and shirt in the same units, axes and bind pose. Build an inspectable underside where the collar or loose hem can reveal it.
2. **Bind the garment to the body shape.** For close-fitting cloth, associate each garment vertex with a point on a triangle of the appropriate body region plus an offset for thickness and ease. The three corner weights specify where the point lies within the triangle. As the corners move, reconstruct the point on the new triangle and carry its offset in the local surface directions. Limit matches by region and side. Otherwise an armpit can bind to the chest instead of the sleeve or a left trouser leg to the right leg. For a flared skirt or coat, bind to a smooth helper surface spanning the legs that follows the pelvis and body dimensions. A vest over padding uses an envelope around that padding. Blender’s Surface Deform modifier can bind the neutral garment to its helper and evaluate it again on the shaped helper.
3. **Bake the requested fit.** For each fixed export, evaluate the bound surface on the requested body and bake the resulting garment positions. Inspect openings and ease, correct a demonstrated miss, and repeat on the other proof body. Keep the editable binding. If a later batch or live preview warrants precomputed garment targets, compare them with direct fitting on the same item at endpoints and mixed values for both cost and visible error. Use an additional fitted cut when one shape cannot maintain the required clearance. An unresolved fit stays out of the common wardrobe.
4. **Bind motion.** For snug sections author motion weights from the final reference fit, including evaluated lining, with Blender's Data Transfer nearest-face interpolation constrained by body region and side. A corrected cut or projection can change the supporting triangle: re-author its canonical binding instead of retaining stale weights. Ordinary recipe evaluation reuses those authored bindings; per-body nearest-surface experiments diagnose a needed rebind or qualified corrective, not a new per-frame search. Clean them around elbows, cuffs, fingers, hips and knees while keeping each side distinct. For hems, capes and braids use deliberate hip/shoulder falloff and a small number of approved secondary bones where needed. Articulate a rigid piece on its appropriate bone or attachment point. Keep the family's joint budget across the whole outfit. Test with the skeleton at the wearer's fitted joint positions, applying both the character and garment shape **before** posing. The October 31 proof run caught skirts splitting down the middle during a stride. [Skinning checks](3d-models.md#skinning-checks)
5. **Mark layer coverage.** After the complete body and fitted garment pass review, specify which underlying faces stay completely covered through the garment's tested motions. Cull only those regions, with margins at openings. A scoop-neck shirt keeps the neck and upper chest visible. A coat may cover portions of that shirt. Build the union on the stable source topology from the current outfit and apply it before triangulation/export. Removing the coat and rebaking must restore the shirt and exposed arms. A later live mask needs its own mapping to each exported primitive because vertex splits, materials and LOD can change the indices. Use shaped clearance or a tucked garment variant at visible seams. Correct two intersecting sleeves on their visible garment surfaces.

Publish common wrist and ankle guides with the templates. Keep ordinary sleeves and trouser ends inside the shaped guides. Give glove cuffs and boot shafts enough clearance and overlap to cover the joins in motion. One new pair of boots can then be checked against the guide and representative trousers. Use a tucked variant for a deliberately bulky hem. [Production precedent](https://docs.baldursgate3.game/Creating_Armour).

Keep the fitting bindings and corrections in the editable sources. Bake vertex positions, the fitted rest skeleton and its matching bind matrices, weights, materials and clips for a fixed character export. Shared clip preparation occurs once per compatible source and target profile. During motion, the skeleton drives the fitted shapes already produced by the authoring pass.

## Worked wardrobe

Consider an illustrative adult scout wearing a linen shirt and trousers, with a padded vest, a breastplate, gloves, boots, a cap, ear studs and a pendant. Produce the underlayer first, followed by the visible inner garments, rigid outer pieces and accessories. The following breakdown describes the art. Which items can be equipped together follows the selected world's [equipment design](../../projects/character-and-map-additions-tech-design.md#equipment-slots).

| Separate source                         | How to make it wearable                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Check on the dressed person                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| World-selected baseline undergarments   | Fit each separate coverage piece on the same body controls. Preserve the intact torso and pelvis beneath it in the master. Treat coverage as a presentation minimum independent of equipment or protection.                                                                          | Remove all gear and inspect the front, back, underneath, a crouch and a run. Verify complete modesty coverage and clean, continuous edges throughout.                                     |
| Shirt, trousers and gloves              | Shape the sleeve from shoulder to cuff, each trouser tube to its leg and each glove to the corresponding fingers. Fit them independently on the reference body. Author a tucked sleeve/pant end when worn inside gloves/boots.                                                       | Bend elbows and knees, close a hand, grip a tool. Check wrist, crotch, ankle and finger spacing.                                                                                          |
| Padded inner vest and outer breastplate | Fit the padding over the supported shirt thickness. Form solid breastplate sections to the torso measurements at rest. Keep plates rigid during motion and skin the flexible straps separately. Use panel overlap or another size where scaling would crush engraving or bend metal. | Breathe, turn, reach and crouch with both layers. Remove the plate, then the vest. Each removal reveals the newly exposed areas while still covering skin beneath any remaining garments. |
| Boots                                   | Fit the upper shaft to calf and ankle, then fit sole, heel and toe to foot length and width. Keep the sole structure and assign flex at the forefoot where the design permits. A pair can be one source with two fitted pieces.                                                      | Compare barefoot/booted foot placement, seated shin flex and the planted sole during travel.                                                                                              |
| Cap, hair and ear studs                 | Fit cap clearance on the shaped head and switch the covered crown to its hat-compatible shape. Place each stud rigidly at the fitted ear-lobe point. Declare each attachment side and whether the pair represents one item.                                                          | Change jaw/ear/head width, turn the head, take the cap off and restore the original style.                                                                                                |
| Necklace and pendant                    | Fit the loop to the shaped neck, clavicles and chest. Select an inside/outside presentation for the supported collar. Preserve its rigid pendant as a separate rigid component of the same item.                                                                                     | Check short and long necks, a turned head, a forward bend and the high collar. Opaque armour may hide a necklace worn underneath without removing the item.                               |

Draw the assembly only from the permitted equipped state. An equipment port describes where a world item attaches. A coverage region determines what it hides, and a fitting surface determines its geometry. Use the [bundled wardrobe profile](../../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#what-a-person-wears) for the scout's proposed placements. It can be reviewed as an art assembly before those equipment ports exist in the game. Other worlds supply their own combinations.

## Motion, acceptance and preservation

- Use existing compatible family clips for the body and outfit proofs. Verify upright posture, planted feet and grip on the final wearer after a proportion change. For a genuinely missing move, follow the [animation procedure](animation.md) with the actual rig revision, gait contacts and native action timing. New or replacement human clips follow the selected Kimodo route; source/target qualification remains required before production use. Keep the garment fitted to the character's rig while adapting the motion. Add corrective poses or limited secondary-bone motion to a troublesome piece before considering an independently qualified cloth simulation.
- Check the rough exported outfit before painting final textures. Exercise the two starting frames, affected slider endpoints, deliberately difficult combined values **and intermediate values between them**, plus the exact selected person. Record which recipes passed, with a garment alone and over each supported neighbouring layer class. Watch the assembled result in the game camera and close-up, moving through upright idle, a complete stride, turning, reaching and sitting/crouching as applicable. Include a head turn under a helmet and the worst clearance case. Feet stay planted at the rate used for actual travel. Compare the consumer's [export budgets and look checks](3d-models.md#step-10--export-and-check-in-the-games-look).
- Save a covered and a default-outfit view from the same scale and camera as the selected concept. Reopen the exported GLB to check the assembled result. In the editable sources remove, swap and restore each piece, rebuild from the recipe and inspect the resulting GLBs. Once a runtime wardrobe is available, repeat the changes through that consumer, including a second wearer of different build. Check what appears immediately after removal and after the replacement finishes loading. Mark independently what passed artist inspection, file validation, viewer playback and actual gameplay.
- Attach the signed-off or rejected candidate IDs, remaining deviations, achieved body measurements, exact family/part/clip revisions, corrections, source rights, provider/model/mode, actual cost and the consumer evidence to the existing [study record](study-record.md). Store every requested iteration and editable source outside Git through the [asset workflow](../../asset-workflow.md). Release the shared parts separately from any dressed export. Preserve original source terms and asset visibility. Check publication rights for images, meshes, clips and raw library sources separately.
