# Perceived World Events

## Status and ownership

Accepted player-facing event-history design; [Architecture](architecture.md#hearing-captions-and-perceived-events) records the implemented read route, finite filter catalogue and remaining qualification. The existing [narration contract](narration-and-conversations.md#5-external-world-events-and-awareness) retains ownership of world occurrences, event-time awareness, and retention. This document owns the comprehensive player-facing viewer, filtering, and read projection. [Hearing and speech](hearing-and-speech.md) owns speech detail; [memory](memory-architecture.md) owns recall and forgetting. Updated 2026-10-16 for the accepted world-first player interface: Events replaces the separate player conversation log and Journal event feed. It introduces no new write pipeline; personal story, promises and places retain their separate reading homes.

## 1. Navigation and scope

Open **Events** in normal player navigation. Reuse the current panel/pullout system and narrow-screen sheet behavior. This is distinct from the god-only World Events editor: the normal panel is read-only, actor-scoped, and never has raw JSON/edit/administrative access merely because the app also supports god mode.

The header offers **All**, **Speech**, **Actions**, **Conflict**, **Body** and **Observations**. The selected world's event-presentation policy assigns event types and icons to those categories; unfamiliar types use its explicit fallback. **All** shows only event types whose authored **Display to player** flag (`displayToPlayer`) is true. The default is false, including the bundled world's fallback for new/unmapped event types. Speech, selected action results such as attacks and crafting, and meaningful bodily consequences are explicitly included. Routine movement, inspection, sightings, presence departures/returns and animal movement are retained but omitted. God mode offers **Show all events** to include those otherwise hidden types; it still shows only this character's permitted history, never unheard speech or an omniscient world log. These are display choices, never retention limits or grounds to discard character evidence. No component ticks are fabricated as occurrences.

Include already-recorded permitted sensed body outcomes through their existing scope, but not a dump of private thoughts, plans, LLM diagnostics, technical status, or Narrator prose. Another actor's private experiences remain excluded. A heard but unintelligible utterance is a Speech row with no words, not an invisible row. A completely undetected and otherwise unperceived utterance does not appear. A supported visual-only observation of speaking must be labeled Seen, not Heard; no speech content is revealed.

One-off numeric changes to a character's sparse installed meters are recorded sensed-body outcomes. Their authoritative writer retains the actual before/after values, definition ID/version, units and cause after admission; owner-private outcomes require the character to be able to notice them, and public outcomes use the installed observation/disclosure policy. Live and retained public projections carry the permitted numeric facts, without raw execution metadata. Passive rates, ordinary drain, material exposure and continuous transfer accounting create no per-interval outcome events. The meter's projected state-owner capability selects recorded-effect overhead feedback, so the client does not reconstruct these effects by subtracting snapshots. Current visibility and definition version govern transient notices; history retains event-time permitted evidence. [Bundled survival feedback](worlds/base/survival.md#injury-feedback) supplies the player examples.

A discrete health outcome names its affected person and reads as an amount rather than a before/after dump: for example, the owner reads “I took 3 damage from hunger” when that cause was recorded and permitted. A native periodic consequence records its precise authored status cause separately from the lifecycle cause: starvation and exhaustion no longer both become an unexplained `needs` event. The affected character may read that cause and its authored explanation; a bystander seeing public health loss does not thereby learn a private bodily cause. Before/after values remain in Details. Displayed quantities use at most two decimal places; health and needs-bar values use whole numbers. Exact stored measurements remain unchanged. Missing historical causes are not invented, and raw prose or quoted speech is not rewritten by searching for numbers.

Names and links are captured only for event-time admitted participants. Links open separately authorized inspection. Native speech attribution can link its identified speaker, but names inside quoted words are testimony, not identity bindings. Descriptive freeform Act prose likewise does not infer identity links by matching names; its separately recorded participant references remain available in Details. Native causal action text retains actor/target links. Shared mechanic explanations consume the recorded authored meter/status wording, and repeated labels with ambiguous identities are not converted into misleading inline links.

The **Speech** filter is the conversation log: it includes self-speech and overheard utterances from all perceived conversations. Speaking uses a compact world text bar; there is no separate player conversation transcript. Selected personal story, promises and places remain available through Character and Places instead of masquerading as event-type filters.

## 2. Rows and interaction

Rows show a small semantic icon and the listener’s permitted text. A full-width day divider separates game days. A small time appears above the first entry, at each day boundary, and when at least ten game minutes separate it from the preceding displayed event. A continuous series of events less than ten minutes apart does not add repeated timestamps. Individual rows reveal their exact day/time on hover and in keyboard-accessible Details. Speech uses quotes only for heard fragments, with an explicit Partly heard or Unintelligible qualifier when appropriate. A missing identity displays Someone or an appropriate permitted description, not the raw source name. Capture attribution as perceived at event time; do not silently update old unknown names from the current entity registry.

An expandable row may expose permitted modality, delivery, partialness and coarse event-time direction, plus recorded observer-permitted inspection facts (such as visible place, support or carrier as seen at inspection time, exact item/definition identity and inspected readable text). It must not expose hidden target IDs, full original speech, exact hidden origins, internal audience lists, technical acoustic measurements or JSON metadata simply because the row has an event handle.

Expanded Details provide a read-only **Compare** control (`Compare as Source A` / `Compare as Source B`) that pins up to two selected entries in a comparison tray inside **World Events**. Comparing two sources displays their recorded time, source category (`Witness statement`, `Read document`, `Direct observation` or `Other record`), speaker or subject as known at event time, recorded location/holder and full permitted text side by side on wide panels (`520px` desktop rail) or stacked on narrow/short viewports. Each slot includes a **Back to source** control that jumps to the source row when loaded on the current page or reports that the row is on an unloaded page without losing scroll position, search text or active **Speak** drafts. Closing the comparison tray (via **Close** or `Escape`) restores focus to the triggering row control. Comparison is strictly read-only: it creates no new sensory evidence, memory record, model call or hidden contradiction verdict.

A speaker link opens current inspection only when that inspection is separately permitted now. An old log row cannot target, follow or focus the exact current position of a hidden actor. Reading history, comparing sources and changing filters create no new sensory evidence, cognition opportunity, or overhead caption.

Display chronological order within each loaded page, opening at the latest row. **Jump to time** keeps labeled **Day** and **Time** controls visible with a **Jump** action that seeks to retained events at or before that game time. Search, **Latest** and category filters remain visible without filling the reading area. **Latest** retains its label and returns to the current tail; a separate new-events notice reports incoming records. Filters use selectable chips, distinct from action buttons; aligned shared controls do not change height or move when a read begins. **Load older events** and **Load newer events** navigate both directions from that point; **Latest** returns to the present. Times use the installed world clock offset, not wall-clock time. Retain older pages while the reader scrolls upward; new events show an unobtrusive “New events” affordance instead of stealing scroll. Changing filter resets the page cursor and isolates in-flight results from the old filter while retaining pinned comparison slots within the same actor/world/save-timeline scope. Failed loads show a retryable error and keep valid already-loaded content where scope is unchanged.

The UI may initially load 50 rows per page, with a server maximum of 100. These are page sizes, not history-retention limits. “Load older” must reach all authorized retained history, rather than the last 60 hot events or last 512 awareness entries. Show truthful empty, loading, unavailable-history and end-of-history states; do not call an unavailable archive empty or claim complete history while a page failed.

## 3. Read API and common projection

The browser uses the shared `getScoped` and `post` request helpers, carrying its tab identity and current authority scope. Pending requests can be aborted, and responses from an obsolete access generation are rejected before entering the panel. This matches the other private-history consumers.

The actor-scoped read surface is `GET /api/world-events?category=speech&showAll=false&limit=50&cursor=...`. Its batched detail route, `POST /api/world-events/sources`, accepts `{ references: WorldEventSourceReference[] }` with one or two observer-bound references. The application-issued token binds the observer/world/timeline scope, source family and source ID. The separate revision identifies the view from which the source was selected; rereading that retained selection returns its current permitted revision and correction qualification rather than pinning obsolete text. Forged, cross-actor or stale-timeline references are refused without exposing another observer's records. A valid reference whose retained evidence is unavailable returns an explicit unavailable result. Results preserve request order, including unavailable entries. Both endpoints use the existing history and experience repositories; they are not a new event system.

Both endpoints bind authenticated account/observer scope and recheck current timeline, evidence and access after asynchronous reads; they do not create speech or grant a second event authority.

World, controlled actor and authorization are bound on the server; the client cannot request another actor's experience by choosing an actor ID. The current route accepts a syntactically valid exact event type, binds it in SQL and returns an empty page for an unmatched type; the ordinary UI uses semantic `category` instead. The installed world maps event types to categories and an opt-in `displayToPlayer` flag, including its explicit fallback for unmapped types. Category and display exclusions apply before paging and text search. An omitted or false `showAll` returns only explicitly displayed types. `showAll=true` requires server-authorized God inspection both before and after the asynchronous read and removes only the display exclusion; observer scope, source permissions and the chosen category still apply. `at` is a nonnegative simulation-second time seek. Omit type/category to browse all otherwise admitted types.

The current wire contract is `PerceivedEventsPage` in `packages/protocol/src/index.ts`: an `events` array of `PublicEvent`, an optional older `nextCursor`, an optional `newerCursor` for forward historical navigation, and an optional `scanLimited` flag for bounded text-search work. An absent cursor means no further page in that direction in the read snapshot. Scope is carried by the authenticated request and bound cursor, not a second client-supplied result envelope. The implemented `PublicEvent` includes an observer-bound `source` where comparison is supported. `WorldEventSourceDetail` returned by the detail route is either `available` or `unavailable`. Available detail carries its current reference, recorded time/order, acquisition category/label, source label, permitted text, optional observed location, and `corrected`/`correctionNote`. Unavailable detail carries only the requested source ID and an explanation. Corrected evidence is presented as a qualified source; forgotten evidence is unavailable. These fields describe the existing wire schema: [the protocol](../packages/protocol/src/index.ts) owns the exact types. Preserve the existing occurrence identity across captions, personal story references and Events; never serialize a raw `WorldEvent` as personal evidence.

Use one audited evidence-to-public-view projector for hot events, paginated history and batched source details. It starts from the reader's retained event-time awareness/perspective, not from raw world text after an audience-membership check. Domain/model formatting shares the same underlying permitted content, without requiring a server UI module in the domain.

## 4. Query and storage behavior

Events and personal-story reading reuse actor-scoped history rather than scanning global event order for a small page. Watermarks come from the actor’s perspectives, not the global archive. The time seek uses an actor-scoped expression index over the already stored perspective time, then resumes ordinary order/identity keyset paging. Search remains bounded and matches only permitted perspective text in either direction.

Reuse the existing durable event and actor-perspective/awareness records. A retained `history_perspectives` row grants access and carries the complete permitted evidence; it replaces the separate audience-membership row. Typed order/type and permitted source/target/dialogue-participant columns are written in the same transaction and indexed for scoped pages. Batched source lookups (`readPerceivedEventSources`) resolve up to two requested event IDs through direct primary-key joins against `(world_id, actor_id, event_id)` and `(world_id, id)` in one round-trip, never scanning the event archive. Speech masks, inspection place/holder/text facts and identity attribution remain self-contained after recall consolidation. No per-player copied event table or speech-specific event store is required. Persist a listener's projected partial text/identity at emission under the existing transaction owner; both hot and cold reads use those same facts. Normal memory consolidation cannot make old permitted transcript rows disappear simply because they left a hot/context window.

Apply actor authorization, forgetting/revocation, external/permitted-occurrence scope and type/category/display filters **before pagination**. A post-filtered global page can leak counts, miss rows or produce misleading empty pages. Use stable keyset pagination over the existing event order plus unique identity, not timestamps alone or ever-growing OFFSET scans. A cursor binds the authorized world/actor, timeline, history epoch, filter, position and initial high-watermark. It is validated/opaque, not a grant of authority.

Newer events arriving during older-page navigation must neither duplicate nor skip rows in that snapshot. A separate refresh supplies the new tail. Use an appropriate existing actor-perspective/order index; add a measured type-query index if plans require it. Do not scan or serialize the entire event archive to open a panel. Avoid expensive total counts; any displayed count must be actor-scoped and clearly describe its filter/retention window.

An optional text search (`q`) filters the same authorized, type-filtered rows before paging. It matches only each row's permitted perspective text, the text the reader is shown, so a search adds no exposure beyond that text and can never confirm unheard words or raw payload content. The [BW22 target-name repair](verification/camp-life.md#observer-safe-target-names-bw22) keeps targeted expression, teaching and strike/hunt shared text neutral and records each observer's own known/unknown label. Its PostgreSQL evidence covers Talk, Journal and World Events without retroactively naming earlier unknown targets; it is not full browser or provider qualification, and existing development history was not backfilled. Every word must match a word start. One request examines a bounded window of rows; when that window holds no more matches the response says so and the cursor continues into older history, so every retained row stays reachable without unbounded work per request ([HR06](limits/hearing-and-speech.md#hr06--history-paging-and-growth)).

Cold-history read failure never falls back to unredacted raw events. A missing expected perspective is an explicit integrity/unavailable condition, not an excuse to show full speech. Preserve current history backpressure and storage-failure boundaries.

## 5. Invalidation and privacy

The list uses existing scoped history revision notifications rather than periodic polling or movement-triggered full-history reads. Extend those notifications to include every row family eligible for this view, including families excluded by personal-story selection; otherwise a movement event could be stored but never appear live. Use the smallest appropriate separate view revision if reusing a personal-story revision would cause needless reads.

Incremental new entries preserve scroll. Correction or forgetting changes the history revision; open comparison slots keep their selected references and navigation origins while their permitted details refresh. The browser binds displayed bodies to the exact viewer scope, evidence revision and complete source references. It hides old bodies during the render that receives an invalidation, before starting replacement reads. Loading, failure or a late obsolete response cannot restore the old text; the existing cards retain retry and source navigation. An available correction shows its qualification/note; forgotten evidence becomes unavailable. Different private owners and changed save timelines clear retained pages and comparison slots during the same render; correction/forgetting epochs clear the old pages while the retained comparison references request newly permitted details. A control-token renewal for the same private owner retains the already admitted prose while refreshing the selected view; paging/source tokens expire immediately, comparison closes, and late reads from the previous control scope cannot replace the visible records. The renewed response supplies fresh paging/source tokens. A save rewind is not permission to merge old-future rows into the new timeline. Reconnect refresh is history recovery, not a request to replay overhead speech.

The normal log is durable player history, not the actor's bounded current recall window. However, authoritative forgetting, privacy erasure and source revocation still remove or suppress affected evidence and derived projections. A player-facing history cache is not an escape from those boundaries. No UI can guarantee that a human forgets text they previously saw; the enforceable requirement is that the system no longer serves the revoked records.

Private Narrator prose remains in the personal-story reader, linked to permitted evidence. It must not be re-emitted as an external world occurrence or count as a second speech event. Refreshing the latest passage waits for its existing indexed read before publishing the view, rather than publishing a temporary empty passage and restoring the same text. Narration completion invalidates that read; unrelated telemetry does not. Final scope, world-generation and evidence checks reject a read crossed by control loss, restore or forgetting. An actual removed passage remains absent. This corrects presentation flicker without deduplicating distinct authored occurrences or inventing new narration.
