## Social policy data

The following is the selected `people.yaml` cooperation subsection. The complete schema is in the [technical design](../../projects/first-playable-cooperation-tech-design.md#authored-data-contract). The installed shared social policy owns the pending lifetime once and is also consumed by gifts/barter, existing outings and combat-help; do not copy it into individual family implementations.

```yaml
cooperation:
  id: base:first-playable-cooperation
  eligibleParticipantCapability: personal-memory
  pendingLifetimeRef: base:first-playable-social-offers
  physicalEpisodeSeconds: 14400
  ceasefireSeconds: 7200
  pendingOutgoing: 3
  pendingIncoming: 6
  invitationReception: mutually-perceived-speaking-reach
  families:
    - guide
    - help
    - hunt
    - alliance
    - ceasefire
```

The referenced pending-offer definition is **7,200 game seconds**. With the selected 60 game seconds per real second this is two real minutes at normal speed, replacing the current 1,800-game-second/30-real-second default for the first-playable profile. Four real minutes is the default maximum purposeful help/guide/hunt episode; a ceasefire lasts two real minutes at normal speed. These are first-pass tuning selections, not measured patience or balance. Faster simulation shortens real opportunity; show the current exact game-time deadline and its current-speed estimate. Offer creation/acceptance does not secretly pause the world. Renegotiation or a new invitation is required to extend an episode, and stopping is always available under its authority rules.

The current outgoing limit of three is retained; six incoming pending invitations prevents many senders creating unbounded decision preparation for one person. Count pending native offers at the shared social-offer admission owner across the participating families, rather than giving every new family another allowance. An inbox refusal says the person cannot receive another offer now; it does not disclose their other partners, private work or counts. No queued invitation or silent replacement follows refusal.

One actor has at most one accepted purposeful physical cooperation episode, retaining the existing bounded pairing model. Alliance and ceasefire are independent of this physical slot. Different pairs can hunt the same animal without a party controller; a third independent hunter may also choose to act under ordinary rules. A group roster, collective commands and generalized joint lifting are outside this selected slice. This limit does not cap the world's population or the number of meaningful acquaintances.

The `helpActions` reference list is compiled from the selected elementary survival recipe set and supported camp contribution action definitions. It does not infer capability from displayed labels. Known-method availability, body/geometry, free inputs, current permissions and chosen work still govern each offered contribution.

## Relationship and after-conflict boundaries

A mutual alliance is useful shared intent and later evidence, not a claim that both feel the same way. Ordinary directional understanding can change through lived experience. No permanent favorable modifier, universal friendship threshold, favor currency or automatic public reputation is selected. Ordinary refusal does not itself break an alliance; an explicit withdrawal or selected lifecycle ending does. Exact initial stock, useful objects and company remain meaningful without a reward for repeatedly saying the right words.

A ceasefire stops future chosen hostile work only while each actor retains assent; it does not enforce moral compliance through body control. Each attack still needs ordinary authority. A person may decide to betray or renew it, with actual perceived actions informing later understanding. No hidden retaliation script, forced forgiveness or automatic camp-wide hostility results. Returning goods, changing position, taking shelter or sharing food can matter as personally perceived evidence, not points on a prescribed repair ladder.

Kest and Orin retain their existing distinct identities, supplies and relationship. Their ranging and threat choices use the danger owner; cooperation can arise if individually accepted, without resetting them to friendly templates or replacing their established motives. A ceased fight can remain unresolved. The player can leave or relocate without completing a conversation.
