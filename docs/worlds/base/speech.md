## The bundled partial-word choice

A clear listener and the speaker's self account receive the accepted words. A linguistic listener in the partial tier hears one lexical unit from each consecutive pair, selected by the fixed observer-local deterministic bit. An unmatched unit waits while the utterance is open; at closure that same bit reveals or hides the terminal singleton. Choices depend on the pinned policy and original occurrence/observer binding, never provider chunks, later movement or a new draw from the shared simulation random sequence. Atomic and streamed admission of the same final text therefore yield the same final fragments under the same initial facts.

Retain chosen units in their original order and spelling. Preserve punctuation and spacing only where the prepared spans join adjacent heard units; do not bridge an unheard word or reconstruct missing grammar. Adjacent hidden units form one opaque gap. Physical partial clarity remains partial even when no readable unit emerges, and an utterance without lexical units supplies no invented quotation. A sound-only receiver uses its acoustic evidence and never enters this linguistic policy.

The approximately-half rule is a deliberate bundled-world approximation. Another supported world may choose different disclosure through the same installed boundary. NST's [mask contract](../../projects/npc-speech-streaming-tech-design.md#stream-stable-partial-mask) owns Unicode sealing, deterministic key construction, shared preparation, continuation, source bounds and current-format validation; those engine mechanics are not repeated here.

## Wording from permitted evidence

Use `[…]` for each unintelligible run in a partial quotation and indicate that the listener only **partly heard** it outside the quoted words. Where no readable words are available, use the shared rendering of **indistinct speech** or **the words are unintelligible**, with only the actually permitted speaker description and delivery. Do not create an empty quotation, guess a missing ending or turn a partially heard claim into a verified promise, threat or instruction. The shared participant grammar supplies the appropriate observer perspective, name and verb form.

Neutral blurred placeholders may express an unintelligible run where the existing presentation permits them; the hidden original text must never enter a display, tooltip, accessible label or client cache. Placeholder size and reading time cannot disclose exact hidden length. These templates describe the listener's evidence; they never rewrite literal heard words. Operational open/interrupted indicators and accessible announcement timing remain presentation concerns under NST, without invented fictional apologies or sound.

Recognition and spoken-name learning remain with [observer knowledge](knowledge.md); native reactions to the fixed acoustic cue remain with [animal sound responses](animal-sound-responses.md). Neither is derived from a guessed interpretation of missing words.
