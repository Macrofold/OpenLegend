# Vision and gaze — selected world policy

**Selected design target; not installed or qualified.** This document owns the bundled world's authored values for [visual sensing and physical orientation](../../projects/vision-and-gaze-feature-spec.md). The [technical design](../../projects/vision-and-gaze-tech-design.md) owns physical preparation, evidence adapters, temporal certification and integration. The [limit inventory](../../limits/vision-and-gaze.md) owns restrictions and qualification; the [maintainer tracker](../../maintainers/vision-and-gaze.md) owns implementation. These are game rules selected for readable, useful differences between bodies, light and vantage. They are not biological measurements or universal engine constants.

## Ownership and units

The spatial/action owner admits body heading, motion and supported head/eye movement. Vision reads their actual transforms. Attention chooses whether to glance, watch, inspect or investigate. A camera changes none of them. Head-only looking does not turn a shield, change a guard cone or rotate the feet. Body turning during another action must satisfy that action's actual compatibility rules. A separately owned recognition/knowledge policy records what the observer acquired; this policy never remembers a hidden person's equipment or decides surprise.

Angles below are degrees; implementation converts once to radians. Distances and represented areas are metres and square metres. Angular velocity and turn speeds use **normal-speed seconds**. Convert admitted durations once using the installed world clock: the current base clock has 60 simulation seconds per normal-speed second. Changing runtime speed to 3× or 8× changes wall time, not these laws. Pause freezes admitted progress. `[a,b)` includes its lower boundary and excludes its upper; hard range and field boundaries include equality, except an exactly zero-duration contact supplies neither weak accumulation nor an immediately obvious opportunity.

Read Perception through the settled attribute owner. For the selected capable people its authored range is 0–100 and default is 50, as designed by module A. The visual extension selects `g(P) = 0.75 + 0.005 P`; a physical quality of zero stays zero. Apply this gain to the detection and fine-detail qualities below after actual geometric, receptor, material and illumination support. A separately applies its recognition law to acquired features. This is an explicit extension of the upstream recognition-only applicability, requiring reconciliation when installing the combined world. Missing a required attribute is invalid preparation, not a hidden zero. Bodies without this attribute can explicitly select `g = 1`; do not infer a human statistic for animals or machines.

### Goal allocation and tracking control

The selected exact half-turn tie is **positive yaw**, from world +Z toward +X. Positive pitch points upward. A yaw/pitch rotation is `R(y,p)=Ry(y)Rx(−p)` in the Y-up frame: perform local pitch then parent yaw. Compose head and eye transforms normally; their angles are not added as if rotations commuted. A fixed eye/head has a zero-width interval at its actual admitted angle; an action-held joint uses its held interval.

Every gaze request names an installed receptor/gaze group, or asks the installed group to choose a lead receptor. For an inspection goal, enumerate enabled receptors in authored order, solve each actual rest mount, and choose the achievable result with shortest required completion duration; stable authored receptor ID breaks an exact duration tie. Duration is the maximum remaining turn duration of its simultaneously admitted joints/body. A grazer's head +Z is not either eye's central direction. A literal world point first supplies its direction from the actual current receptor; the control cadence below updates that direction when the receptor subsequently moves. Human eyes use one selected head goal, with secondary-eye angles automatically computed toward the same literal point using each actual eye offset and its allowed joint range. They are not separately chosen attention targets; an unachievable secondary eye remains within its bounds and need not be central.

For the selected forward-eye two-joint group, allocate the captured desired unit direction as follows. Its selected rest rotation is identity; all final verification still includes the actual authored rest transform.

1. Try the current body yaw first. For an explicitly body-assisted request only, also prepare a residual body-yaw candidate `desiredYaw−clamp(desiredYaw,headYawMin+eyeYawMin,headYawMax+eyeYawMax)` relative to the current body, then a body yaw aligned with the desired horizontal direction. Remove duplicates, preserve this order and reject any candidate incompatible with the actual action/clearance. Pitch never requests torso assistance.
2. Transform the captured direction into each candidate final body frame. Let its desired yaw be `a=atan2(x,z)` and pitch use `atan2(y,hypot(x,z))`. At an exactly vertical direction retain current yaw; no arbitrary azimuth is invented.
3. Try an eyes-first head candidate: `hy=clamp(a−clamp(a,eyeYawBounds),headYawBounds)`. Transform the direction by `Ry(−hy)` to get `u`. Let `p=atan2(u.y,u.z)` on the supported forward branch, and choose `hp=clamp(p−clamp(p,eyePitchBounds),headPitchBounds)`. Next try a closest-head fallback with `hy=clamp(a,headYawBounds)` and `hp=clamp(atan2(u.y,u.z),headPitchBounds)` after recomputing `u`. An undefined or backward pitch branch does not create a fabricated solution; proceed to the next body candidate.
4. For each candidate final head pose, compute `v=R_rest⁻¹R(hy,hp)⁻¹d`. Required eye angles are exactly `ey=atan2(v.x,v.z)` and `ep=atan2(v.y,hypot(v.x,v.z))`. Select the first candidate whose angles fit the actual eye bounds and whose composed achieved direction matches the captured goal under the shared certified numerical rules. Clamping an excessive eye angle cannot pass this exact-goal check. A numerically unresolved check remains unresolved. If no candidate passes, refuse before replacing work, or select the closest offered achieved direction only when the request explicitly accepts partial alignment. This finite allocation policy does not assert that an arbitrary future rig has been exhaustively solved.

For the selected fixed-eye lateral group, solve the rest mount directly rather than pretending its axis is +Z. Let `r=R_rest(0,0,1)`, `A=hypot(r.y,r.z)` and `φ=atan2(r.y,r.z)`. The desired body-local vertical component satisfies `d.y=A sin(hp+φ)`. Enumerate both inverse-sine branches and their angle-period equivalents inside the finite head-pitch bounds; if `abs(d.y)>A`, exact central alignment is unavailable. For each admitted `hp`, compute `z' = −r.y sin(hp)+r.z cos(hp)` and required total yaw `t=atan2(d.x,d.z)−atan2(r.x,z')`. First try head yaw `t` with the body unchanged; if explicitly body-assisted, also try head yaw clamped to its range and compatible body residual `t−headYaw`. Verify `R_body R(hy,hp)R_rest(0,0,1)` against the goal and choose the shortest feasible completion, then authored branch order. A rigid machine uses the same verification with fixed head/eye bounds and a compatible body yaw only. Nonzero arbitrary rest pitch/roll with independently steerable eyes needs its own admitted allocation family; it is not silently solved by this forward-eye special case.

All admitted joints start together at their own rate limit, stopping at their selected goal. Eye axes pivot at their actual receiver mount; turning the eyes does not move the pupils around the head. Head rotation moves the head volume, face landmarks and eye mounts together. Relative neutral roll is fixed; this slice adds no roll or general skeleton solver. Capabilities/offers expose this installed allocation family, including a refusal that distinguishes its unsupported precise goal from an absent physical receptor.

A fixed relative goal needs only its admitted start, completion and limit boundaries. Tracking a moving A-permitted target or maintaining a literal world-point direction while the observer moves selects **30 orientation-control ticks per normal-speed second**, anchored to the directive's original admission time; under the current clock each tick spans **2 simulation seconds**. At each due tick, resolve the currently permitted target point or literal world point, allocate the bounded joint goals, then execute rate-limited piecewise-linear turns until the next tick or earlier completion/limit. These are native control boundaries, not render updates. Do not sample a hidden target. Loss ends tracking or retains the last actually observed literal point according to the admitted request; it never follows an unseen target's new coordinates. Keep checks on the original tick phase while relevant receiver/goal motion continues, even when currently aligned; skip unnecessary goal recomputation rather than the next check. Suspend ticks only when both the selected goal and all relevant receptor transforms are fixed; an admitted dependency change wakes the next tick on that same phase. Save the anchor, tick ordinal, accepted goals and residual turn so pause, speed changes or restore do not phase-shift the servo. The controller's finite cadence is deliberate; temporal sensing still evaluates the complete resulting admitted turns.

For a literal world point or currently permitted tracked point, report **reached** at the first certified entry into an angle of at most **0.5°** between the actual receptor axis and the directive's currently admitted point goal. That entry is a native completion boundary under the technical temporal rules, not whichever control tick or display query first notices it. The goal is the literal point or permitted estimate admitted for the current segment; a later control tick may admit a new estimate, and hidden current coordinates never determine completion. This is an alignment/completion tolerance only; it changes no field, light, occlusion, detail or actual pose. On orient-once completion, stop its remaining joint segment atomically at the accepted pose. A fixed relative-angle directive instead finishes its admitted joint goals exactly. World-point/tracking durations are estimates until actually aligned. Orient-once point pursuit ends at its explicitly admitted earlier deadline or, by default, **2 normal-speed seconds** after admission (**120 simulation seconds** at the selected clock); expiry reports alignment unreached and retains the actual pose. This handles a moving or near-origin goal that cannot converge. Maintained action/deadline directives keep their own finite extent. Reached alignment never satisfies a separate view-required work constraint by itself.

Attachments have their own placement, shape and facet membership. Pose moves these patches through the admitted body transforms; it cannot replace standing height with a generic crouch multiplier. A low wall may hide hands and hips while exposing a head; foliage may admit a weak torso silhouette; the results differ by facet.

The selected optical occluder family supports boxes through native convex-plane intersections and spheres through analytic ray/sphere intersections. A head sphere is a narrow optical primitive to implement, not permission for arbitrary skeletal physics. The coarse collision cylinder remains a movement envelope; **do not insert that envelope as a second opaque solid over the optical parts**. That would bury the face and eyes. Optical body parts are opaque and self-occlude one another. Ignore only the exact source/receiver skin at the endpoint; a ray that enters another part or penetrates its own head has a real blocker. C/contact retain physical movement/attack shapes, using the actual admitted part/attachment envelope wherever those operations require it.

## World illumination and optical materials

Light uses normalized game units, **not lux**. Source position and output are world state. For local clock hour `h` modulo 24, daylight strength is `D=max(0,sin(π(h−6)/12))` on `[6,18]` and zero outside it. The admitted sun direction has azimuth `90°+15°(h−6)` and elevation `60°sin(π(h−6)/12)` during that interval. Direct sun intensity is `0.8D`; diffuse sky intensity is `0.5D + 0.002(1−D)`. The selected diffuse level and brightness thresholds permit ordinary shaded faces to retain useful landmark quality despite the lower face receiving only some sky directions; walls and genuinely blocked sky still matter. The sun, HUD day/night indicator and renderer read this same installed policy: daylight is **06:00 inclusive to 18:00 exclusive**; the existing HUD's 19:00 endpoint must be removed during implementation. Dawn/dusk names remain owned by the installed clock. At dawn/dusk the low sky term can exist while direct sunlight is zero.

Approximate sky access using exactly nine world-up hemisphere rays: zenith weight **0.2**, four cardinal azimuths at elevation **45°** with weight **0.1** each, and four diagonal azimuths at elevation **15°** with weight **0.1** each. Use the material transmittance on each ray to the local world's admitted sky boundary. Sky access `K` is their weighted transmission sum. An opaque roof and enclosing walls block these paths; there is no indoor global ambient minimum. This nine-ray approximation deliberately makes covered spaces legible without a full radiative-transfer simulation.

The installed environment supplies a finite authoritative **optical-domain axis-aligned bounding box**, containing all admitted physical optical geometry and source/receiver extents. It is not the displayed map extent or camera draw distance. Trace sun/sky/background rays through complete required geometry until they exit this box. Outside it is the declared sky only for a direction with `directionY>0`, whichever box face the ray exits; horizontal/downward exits have no sky background. Adding or moving geometry outside requires expanding the domain through its owner and changing its revision **before admission**, or refusing the unsupported transition. Unknown/unloaded required geometry cannot count as clear sky. The finite domain is a declared world's boundary convention, not a short shadow ray that makes a tall wall transparent. For the bundled installation, derive the initial box from the union of all admitted optical geometry/body/attachment bounds and emitter/receptor positions, expanded outward to the existing spatial index cell boundaries. Keep the resulting bounds in the existing environment/spatial owner; grow by the same cell rule before an admitted swept extent exits it, and do not shrink merely because a body moves inward. Construction/explicit domain replacement can rebuild from complete indexed content. This chooses an executable initial rule without inventing a second map size or a renderer-derived horizon.

Surface illumination at patch `i` is `E_i = skyIntensity×K_i + sunIntensity×T_sun×max(0,n_i·sunDirection) + Σ sourceContribution`. A point source contributes `I×max(0,1−d/R)²×T×max(0,n_i·sourceDirection)` inside its finite radius `R`, zero outside. A spot source multiplies that result by 1 inside an authored inner cone, then a linear falloff to zero at its authored outer cone. Cone angles are half-angles from its axis and must satisfy `0≤inner≤outer≤180°`; equal inner/outer angles mean a hard cone boundary. All eligible sources sum; no nearest-two/eight rule changes physical light. A fully occluded source contributes no direct light through the blocker. A doorway can transmit its light to a visible floor without exposing the lamp itself; the observer gets the illuminated floor, not hidden emitter coordinates.

The base fire fixture selects intensity **2.8**, radius **8 m**, and emission origin **0.8 m above its support anchor** when that is the actual authored flame location. This origin lies inside its authored **non-occluding flame volume**; the solid fuel/support geometry is separate and can block appropriate directions. A lamp emits from its actual emissive boundary/open aperture, and its own solid housing continues to block backward rays. A carried light attaches to its real hand/tool transform; do not reuse a ground-fire offset on it or skip the whole carrier assembly during light queries. The visibly luminous flame patch has intrinsic luminance **2.8**. Seeing that patch is possible within ordinary 28 m sight range even beyond the 8 m radius in which it illuminates other objects. Fuel, extinguishing, admission and attachment remain owned by their existing object/fire actions. No delayed remembered fire continues to emit world light.

Its six axial surface samples have normals ±X/±Y/±Z and represented area `4π(0.12)²/6` each. They count toward the attached assembly's sample envelope.
