# Vision and gaze — selected world policy

**Selected design target; not installed or qualified.** This document owns the bundled world's authored values for [visual sensing and physical orientation](../../projects/vision-and-gaze-feature-spec.md). The [technical design](../../projects/vision-and-gaze-tech-design.md) owns physical preparation, evidence adapters, temporal certification and integration. The [limit inventory](../../limits/vision-and-gaze.md) owns restrictions and qualification; the [maintainer tracker](../../maintainers/vision-and-gaze.md) owns implementation. These are game rules selected for readable, useful differences between bodies, light and vantage. They are not biological measurements or universal engine constants.

## Ownership and units

The spatial/action owner admits body heading, motion and supported head/eye movement. Vision reads their actual transforms. Attention chooses whether to glance, watch, inspect or investigate. A camera changes none of them. Head-only looking does not turn a shield, change a guard cone or rotate the feet. Body turning during another action must satisfy that action's actual compatibility rules. A separately owned recognition/knowledge policy records what the observer acquired; this policy never remembers a hidden person's equipment or decides surprise.

Angles below are degrees; implementation converts once to radians. Distances and represented areas are metres and square metres. Angular velocity and turn speeds use **normal-speed seconds**. Convert admitted durations once using the installed world clock: the current base clock has 60 simulation seconds per normal-speed second. Changing runtime speed to 3× or 8× changes wall time, not these laws. Pause freezes admitted progress. `[a,b)` includes its lower boundary and excludes its upper; hard range and field boundaries include equality, except an exactly zero-duration contact supplies no accumulated weak evidence.

Read Perception through the settled attribute owner. For the selected capable people its authored range is 0–100 and default is 50, as designed by module A. The visual extension selects `g(P) = 0.75 + 0.005 P`; a physical quality of zero stays zero. Apply this gain to the detection and fine-detail qualities below after actual geometric, receptor, material and illumination support. A separately applies its recognition law to acquired features. This is an explicit extension of the upstream recognition-only applicability, requiring reconciliation when installing the combined world. Missing a required attribute is invalid preparation, not a hidden zero. Bodies without this attribute can explicitly select `g = 1`; do not infer a human statistic for animals or machines.

### Goal allocation and tracking control

The selected exact half-turn tie is **positive yaw**, from world +Z toward +X. Positive pitch points upward. A yaw/pitch rotation is `R(y,p)=Ry(y)Rx(−p)` in the Y-up frame: perform local pitch then parent yaw. Compose head and eye transforms normally; their angles are not added as if rotations commuted. A fixed eye/head has a zero-width interval at its actual admitted angle; an action-held joint uses its held interval.

For the selected two-joint gaze group, allocate a captured desired unit direction as follows. A literal world point first supplies its direction from the actual current receptor; the control cadence below updates that direction when the receptor subsequently moves.

1. Try the current body yaw first. For an explicitly body-assisted request only, also prepare a residual body-yaw candidate `desiredYaw−clamp(desiredYaw,headYawMin+eyeYawMin,headYawMax+eyeYawMax)` relative to the current body, then a body yaw aligned with the desired horizontal direction. Remove duplicates, preserve this order and reject any candidate incompatible with the actual action/clearance. Pitch never requests torso assistance.
2. Transform the captured direction into each candidate final body frame. Let its desired yaw be `a=atan2(x,z)` and pitch use `atan2(y,hypot(x,z))`. At an exactly vertical direction retain current yaw; no arbitrary azimuth is invented.
3. Try an eyes-first head candidate: `hy=clamp(a−clamp(a,eyeYawBounds),headYawBounds)`. Transform the direction by `Ry(−hy)` to get `u`. Let `p=atan2(u.y,u.z)` on the supported forward branch, and choose `hp=clamp(p−clamp(p,eyePitchBounds),headPitchBounds)`. Next try a closest-head fallback with `hy=clamp(a,headYawBounds)` and `hp=clamp(atan2(u.y,u.z),headPitchBounds)` after recomputing `u`. An undefined or backward pitch branch does not create a fabricated solution; proceed to the next body candidate.
4. For each candidate final head pose, compute `v=R(hy,hp)⁻¹d`. Required eye angles are exactly `ey=atan2(v.x,v.z)` and `ep=atan2(v.y,hypot(v.x,v.z))`. Select the first candidate whose angles fit the actual eye bounds and whose composed achieved direction matches the captured goal under the shared certified numerical rules. Clamping an excessive eye angle cannot pass this exact-goal check. A numerically unresolved check remains unresolved. If no candidate passes, refuse before replacing work, or select the closest offered achieved direction only when the request explicitly accepts partial alignment. This finite allocation policy does not assert that an arbitrary future rig has been exhaustively solved.

All admitted joints start together at their own rate limit, stopping at their selected goal. Eye axes pivot at their actual receiver mount; turning the eyes does not move the pupils around the head. Head rotation moves the head volume, face landmarks and eye mounts together. Relative neutral roll is fixed; this slice adds no roll or general skeleton solver. Capabilities/offers expose this installed allocation family, including a refusal that distinguishes its unsupported precise goal from an absent physical receptor.

A fixed relative goal needs only its admitted start, completion and limit boundaries. Tracking a moving A-permitted target or maintaining a literal world-point direction while the observer moves selects **30 orientation-control ticks per normal-speed second**, anchored to the directive's original admission time; under the current clock each tick spans **2 simulation seconds**. At each due tick, resolve the currently permitted target point or literal world point, allocate the bounded joint goals, then execute rate-limited piecewise-linear turns until the next tick or earlier completion/limit. These are native control boundaries, not render updates. Do not sample a hidden target. Loss ends tracking or retains the last actually observed literal point according to the admitted request; it never follows an unseen target's new coordinates. Suspend unnecessary ticks when both the selected goal and receptor frame are fixed. Save the anchor, tick ordinal, accepted goals and residual turn so pause, speed changes or restore do not phase-shift the servo. The controller's finite cadence is deliberate; temporal sensing still evaluates the complete resulting admitted turns.

For person locomotion compatible with independent body heading, select maximum speed factors by the absolute horizontal angle between motion and body forward: **1** through 60°, **0.75** above 60° through 120°, and **0.55** above 120°. A head/eye-only turn has **no additional effort tax**. These are the selected B-to-C integration values; locomotion/posture owner C must admit and charge the actual movement, footing and body capabilities. They do not independently grant strafing or backward travel to an unsupported controller. An action that requires forward travel or fixes the torso may refuse that composition. Movement and whole-body turn rates use the accepted motion/action policy rather than silently slowing or redirecting another action.

## World illumination and optical materials

Light uses normalized game units, **not lux**. Source position and output are world state. For local clock hour `h` modulo 24, daylight strength is `D=max(0,sin(π(h−6)/12))` on `[6,18]` and zero outside it. The admitted sun direction has azimuth `90°+15°(h−6)` and elevation `60°sin(π(h−6)/12)` during that interval. Direct sun intensity is `0.8D`; diffuse sky intensity is `0.2D + 0.002(1−D)`. The sun, HUD day/night indicator and renderer read this same installed policy: daylight is **06:00 inclusive to 18:00 exclusive**; the existing HUD's 19:00 endpoint must be removed during implementation. Dawn/dusk names remain owned by the installed clock. At dawn/dusk the low sky term can exist while direct sunlight is zero.

Approximate sky access using exactly nine world-up hemisphere rays: zenith weight **0.2**, four cardinal azimuths at elevation **45°** with weight **0.1** each, and four diagonal azimuths at elevation **15°** with weight **0.1** each. Use the material transmittance on each ray to the local world's admitted sky boundary. Sky access `K` is their weighted transmission sum. An opaque roof and enclosing walls block these paths; there is no indoor global ambient minimum. This nine-ray approximation deliberately makes covered spaces legible without a full radiative-transfer simulation.

Surface illumination at patch `i` is `E_i = skyIntensity×K_i + sunIntensity×T_sun×max(0,n_i·sunDirection) + Σ sourceContribution`. A point source contributes `I×max(0,1−d/R)²×T×max(0,n_i·sourceDirection)` inside its finite radius `R`, zero outside. A spot source multiplies that result by 1 inside an authored inner cone, then a linear falloff to zero at its authored outer cone. Cone angles are half-angles from its axis and must satisfy `0≤inner≤outer≤180°`; equal inner/outer angles mean a hard cone boundary. All eligible sources sum; no nearest-two/eight rule changes physical light. A fully occluded source contributes no direct light through the blocker. A doorway can transmit its light to a visible floor without exposing the lamp itself; the observer gets the illuminated floor, not hidden emitter coordinates.

The base fire fixture selects intensity **2.8**, radius **8 m**, and emission origin **0.8 m above its support anchor** when that is the actual authored flame location. A carried light attaches to its real hand/tool transform; do not reuse a ground-fire offset on it. The visibly luminous flame patch has intrinsic luminance **2.8**. Seeing that patch is possible within ordinary 28 m sight range even beyond the 8 m radius in which it illuminates other objects. Fuel, extinguishing, admission and attachment remain owned by their existing object/fire actions. No delayed remembered fire continues to emit world light.

| Material family | Optical rule | Separate physical rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid wall, closed opaque door, solid terrain/slope | `T=0` for positive interior penetration | Existing shape governs movement and attacks |
| Clear glass fixture | `T=0.85` per pane crossing | Can block traversal/weapon passage despite admitting sight |
| Leafy volume | `T=exp(−κℓ)` with authored density coefficient `κ` and actual path length `ℓ`; base sparse/dense foliage **0.8 / 2.0 m⁻¹** | Leaves may admit movement; branches/trunks keep their own collision and attack geometry |
| Open air/open gap | `T=1` | Clearance still uses the moving body/tool shape |
| Opaque cloth/mask | `T=0` through its actual covered volume | Does not become a wall merely because it covers a face |

Moving through foliage changes the actual path and possibly authored geometry through its owner; it does not automatically toggle an invisibility state. A tiny peep gap may admit a ray but refuse a hand or arrow shaft. Sloping terrain uses actual volumes and heights, without a special rule that all uphill targets are hidden. This first family has direct sun/sky and finite point/spot emitters; it has **no reflected bounce light, mirrors, refraction, volumetric smoke, diffraction or global dark adaptation**. A world asking for one must install and qualify its physical family before claiming corresponding evidence.

## Detection, motion and silhouettes

For each supported patch, surface brightness is `B_i=ρ_i E_i + emission_i`, with authored diffuse reflectance `ρ_i∈[0,1]`, default **0.6** for ordinary body/clothing patches. Emissive patches carry explicit nonnegative emission; names such as “glowing” do not install it. Ordinary detection light quality is `L_i=min(1,B_i/detectionLightFull)` using the profile's brightness threshold above. For a silhouette sample, continue the admitted sample direction behind the target to the nearest admitted opaque physical background surface or the sky boundary, excluding that target's own volume only and transmitting through optical volumes on that continuation. Obtain background brightness using the same light law, multiplied by that continuation's transmission; call the received result `B_bg`. For sky, use the installed sky intensity in that direction. No qualifying background yields no silhouette term. Define contrast `C=abs(B_i−B_bg)/max(B_i,B_bg,0.001)` and silhouette quality `H=min(1,B_bg/0.15)×clamp((C−0.2)/0.6,0,1)`. This makes a dark figure against a bright doorway detectable without making its unlit face readable. It also avoids manufacturing contrast from two black surfaces. Background sampling and its finite geometric support belong to the technical family, not a view into hidden renderer buffers.

Let `W_i` be the patch's detection angular weight and let `Q_signal = Σ(a_i T_i W_i max(L_i,H_i))/V`, zero when `V=0`. Relative target/receptor angular speed `ω` is measured from their actual trajectories after gaze tracking. Detection motion gain is `M=1+0.2min(|ω|/90°s⁻¹,1)`: movement can make a supported cue easier to notice but cannot create light or a line of sight. The per-receptor physical quality is `q_raw = sqrt(x)×S×Q_signal×M`; use `q=clamp(g(P)×q_raw,0,1)`. Take the maximum supported receptor quality for one physical opportunity, never sum duplicate eye observations. Illumination, field and occlusion remain per actual patch throughout.

## Fine detail and visual identity

Detection of a body is not disclosure of its face, hands, weapon, colour or name. Each fact requires its own visible patches and detail quality. The surface-light term for detail is `F_i=min(1,B_i/detailLightFull)`; silhouette contrast supplies **no** identity or fine colour support. Multiply by the detail angular weight, the actual material transmission and motion clarity `J=1/(1+(|ω|/45°s⁻¹)²)`. A supported body can therefore remain detected while quick relative motion, turned head or shadow withholds fine information. Evaluate a face feature only if all its required landmark rays are supported, its actual outward face normal is within **65°** of the receiver direction, and its numerator span subtends at least **0.10°**. Its size quality is `min(1,spanAngularSize/0.50°)`. Let physical feature quality be that factor times the cosine of face obliquity times the minimum landmark `F_i×detailAngularWeight_i×T_i×J`; apply the Perception gain last and clamp to `[0,1]`. The intrinsic-proportion descriptor is the installed family's deliberate approximation; foreshortening and unavailable landmarks reduce what it can claim rather than revealing an exact profile from a distant side view.

For ordinary nonidentity detail on an installed facet, use `q_detail=clamp(g(P)×sqrt(x)×S×Σ(a_iT_iF_i detailAngularWeight_i J)/V,0,1)`, zero for `V=0`; the same actual part gates apply and there is no silhouette term. Below **0.25**, supply no coarse physical category; at **0.25**, an actually supported part-shape descriptor may be available. At **0.45**, admit supported coarse material/colour/marking or item-family descriptors; these describe the exposed appearance, not hidden chemical composition, contents or equipment metadata. At **0.70**, admit counts of separately visible supported parts and dimensions rounded to **0.05 m**, with at least **0.025 m** rounding uncertainty plus the authored patch family's measurement uncertainty. A visible-part count is not a hidden inventory total. An item family requires its authored distinguishing patches; a shaft alone cannot reveal an unseen spearhead. A held/carried relation requires the visible hand/tool or attachment bridge and enough corresponding detail, not truth read from an inventory relation. The technical family defines the finite eligible fields and their required patch sets. These thresholds select availability, not names, recognition or independent evidence storage.
