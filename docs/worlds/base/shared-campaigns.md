# The Ash Road: a finite local campaign

**Status: proposed optional authored profile, 2026-10-09.** This is a product design for two small communities in one supported world. It is not installed content, adopted event service, a new default primitive start or evidence of shared-campaign capacity. The [feature specification](../../projects/shared-campaigns-feature-spec.md) owns complete player behaviour, scenarios, research and qualification. [PS07.1–PS07.7](../../maintainers/product-scalability.md#dg34--linked-local-campaign-opportunities) owns delivery; [PS-L42–PS-L49](../../limits/product-scalability.md#ps-l42--first-finite-campaign) is the single inventory of proposed values and restrictions.

## 1. People and a useful road

Reedbank and Stonebend are two actual authored places in one continuing world, connected by an ordinary long path and an older shorter road. The two communities need no population threshold or government. Nessa wants to carry supplies along the shorter route. Orrin likes the long path and its lookout but wants neighbours to have another choice. They are particular independent adult residents, not quest dispensers or automatic work crews. Their age, ancestry, existing relationships and knowledge must be authored truthfully rather than inferred from placeholder dates or assigned to existing people without a source.

The player can explore, open a route, disconnect a source, help a willing resident, use a tool they already have, carry their own supplies, enjoy the lookout or leave. Opening the road does not require an account reward, an association, a conversation, a campaign rank or attendance at a later operation. Ordinary walking, food, rest, carrying, speech and knowledge retain their current owners. The shorter road must be meaningfully useful in the actual layout; a decorative shortcut with no wanted destination does not establish the experience.

## 2. Exact fixtures and finite work

The profile contains two actual road gates, two relay braziers, one released coupling per relay, one compatible free-use crank and one Cinder Warden. These are finite authored objects. Names do not establish a generic metal value or manufacture permission. The crank must have a real installed use, and a recovered coupling without a supported use is honestly a keepsake. Neither success nor later arrival replenishes an object.

Each gate begins closed and can be opened through its actual winding fixture while its relay remains connected. This gives immediate use with a known possibility of later closure. Disconnecting that relay prevents this Warden from closing the corresponding gate; the player still needs to open the physical gate. Neither action makes an obstruction disappear. Gate and shutter sweeps require actual clearance and may not move another person's body, bag or protected possessions by narration.

At the western relay, the player can follow a visible connection and walk around a ruined wall to an accessible rear disconnect. At the eastern relay, a winding socket raises an access shutter. The compatible crank is physically reachable in the nearby store, while an already held compatible tool can save the detour. Supported capability and lawful possession determine compatibility. A generated description does not authorize an effect; an invention still needs ordinary admission and actual materials. The prepared crank keeps the first outing useful when generation is unavailable.

The proposed winding, opening and disconnect steps each take **120 game seconds**, or **two normal real seconds** at the current 60:1 world rate. The short work supports interruption, not a repeated-input challenge. Required reach, tool possession, clearance and permission must still hold when the action commits. A cancelled incomplete step does not sever a source or produce salvage. Concurrent attempts can have only one committed disconnection and one released coupling.

The completing actor may take the exact coupling released at the fixture under the openly authored access rule, or leave it there. It does not appear automatically in their inventory. The crank is retained by use; the resident's request to put it back is not a secretly created debt. Any actual agreement retains its normal owner. Removal, loss, blockage and lack of a replacement are real conditions; the campaign neither spawns another crank nor identifies a private possessor.

## 3. One shared adversary

The Warden is an explicitly nonliving authored construct with one body and one finite purpose: close an eligible road gate. Dormancy has no physiology, unattended projects, personal thoughts or recurring paid deliberation. This says nothing about the costs or rights of an ordinary NPC or a later intelligent adversary.

With both relays connected, the Warden's shutdown access is warded. Disconnecting either exposes that access on the same actual body and removes the machine's future power over that relay's gate. The other community can therefore gain a real shutdown option from work it did not witness. Disconnecting both before launch prevents the operation. A remaining relay can support one physical attempt to close an actually open corresponding gate. If no such destination exists, there is no useful operation to launch.

The first Warden does not injure humans or NPCs, damage unrelated goods, invade homes, push through bodies or teleport past a blocked route. Its physical movement and fixture work need their own real qualification. It cannot be turned into a lethal siege by changing difficulty. Permanent shutdown, power loss, physical obstruction and the finite operational ending retain their precise behaviour in the feature; no restart creates a second attempt or resurrects a dismantled source.

The proposed operation spends one finite stored charge and at most **3,600 game seconds** of active time, including work. Its candidate movement speed is **0.011 world units per game second**; shutdown and one attempted gate closure each use the same **120-game-second** work rule. These correspond to at most one normal real minute, 0.66 units per normal real second and two normal real seconds of work. The actual route must fit the selected budget if unobstructed; a longer or blocked route cannot silently increase the allowance. The target is an actually open connected gate on a supported route, with the shorter route preferred and the western route breaking an exact tie. A stopped or spent body remains at its actual place, with a genuinely usable bypass established before launch. Moving or dismantling it is optional later work only where an actual supported use justifies that capability; the first campaign needs no general heavy-object hauling system.

The destination becomes fixed at launch. If that gate's relay is disconnected or somebody closes that target gate first, the current attempt ends spent at the body's actual position, even if the other relay remains connected. There is no surprise retargeting or second operation. A person who deliberately closes the gate to stop the machine may subsequently reopen it through ordinary supported work. Effective harmless counterplay is not repaired with an invented attack or refilled charge.

This is a deliberately nonlethal route problem. Its success is a usable road and a changed shared situation; the fiction may not claim that players saved lives from a law that could never threaten them. Later damaging campaigns must independently reconcile actual harm, protection, recovery, property and participation.

## 4. Timing, ordinary life and participation

Local preparation and complete prevention are available immediately through the supported world. The optional advertised operation consumes the feature's explicit real notice and bounded service offer. A wall-clock opportunity is different from a physical arrival estimate. Current world speed, pause, admitted presence, route conditions and actual player work determine what mechanics can occur. Saving, an expired window or returning after an outage does not create offline progress or launch a missed operation.

The proposed first offer gives at least **24 real hours' notice** before a **two-real-hour activation window**. One replacement may start at least 24 real hours after its new notice, using a different time of day where feasible; a 12-hour offset, for example 36 hours after the original start when notice is timely, is the selected candidate. There remains only one physical operation. Exhausted offers leave the actual unresolved dormant state and local results, without an automatic later launch. Actual prevention may end the offer earlier. The two-hour window is a chance to start supported play, not a promise of a two-hour battle or an exact body arrival minute.

The profile adds no chapter hours for ordinary play. It does not keep living residents fed, rested or available for a two-hour event by freezing their bodies. At uninterrupted normal speed, two real hours are five game days; the selected world must actually support its living people throughout whatever interval it advances. The dormant machine's inexpensive law cannot be applied to them.

Campaign interest, world invitation, capacity admission, physical arrival and a human's chosen action remain distinct. The campaign supplies no public account registration, exceptional property access, human speech or absentee work. Existing participation and travel owners control readiness and reconnects. The one-world outing needs no cross-world transport, separate calendar, unusual mind or unattended-community service.

An accepted resident **entry reservation** holds its disclosed place until **15 real minutes after the window opens**, unless released sooner. Its review states that the one brief operation may finish before that check-in allowance ends; it does not reserve a personal climax. Once admitted, the person does not need repeated check-ins and this arrival expiry does not remove them. Existing configured multiplayer reconnect grace remains separate. Unclaimed places return to ordinary admission, while a late resident retains their home, history and ordinary eligibility without evicting another admitted person. Actual reserved capacity, normal work and reconnect headroom must be qualified before an advertised offer is made.

## 5. Knowledge, rewards and an honest ending

A person's own accepted contribution, an authorized public record and a resident's acquired knowledge are different. The feature's publication policy may explain the shared result without identifying a hidden scout, private correspondence, unrecognized person or private location. Nessa and Orrin can learn through actual sight, reports or chosen reading. They do not receive a memory merely because a campaign record changed, and their independent reaction is not guaranteed praise or labour.

The first authored bulletin deliberately makes the two fixtures' connection/gate states, the Warden's broad operational state, real service dates and authorized outcomes public. This finite disclosure is selected before participation; it is not a general strategic sense or automatic NPC knowledge. Attribution is separately optional and uses only permitted identity. A future hidden front needs its own narrower publication policy rather than inheriting these public fixture readings.

Public contribution attribution defaults to **no personal name**. A contributor may explicitly choose an identity they are permitted to disclose. The actual public fixture result still appears; withholding a name does not undo the work or remove its causal value. This publication choice does not erase what actual witnesses already learned.

The road, actual retained tools or salvage and changed future choices are the first benefits. No mandatory points, season pass, attendance streak, paid priority, last-hit reward or endless delivery faucet is added. Any separately promised compensation needs a real authorized source and clear acceptance; a failed global outcome does not erase completed local work.

The road can be prevented from reclosing, reopened after the Warden succeeds, or left honestly unresolved. A returning player encounters the actual fixtures and permitted history. Remaining useful work may already have been completed by someone else. The campaign does not create another blockage or unique defeat to give everyone the same personal climax. An optional later retelling must be explicitly noncanonical.

Campaign history, objects and fictional knowledge must join the existing coherent restoration boundary. Current service commitments, permissions, privacy and real accounting are reconciled under their own owners. Once a campaign effect or learned information has escaped into another world, unsupported independent pre-export restoration must refuse. Campaign closure stops new optional work without deleting accepted claims or promising permanent funded simulation.
