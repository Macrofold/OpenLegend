## What the player can find

The Ash Road crosses a flooded cut between Reedbank and Stonebend. Two old gates still obey a single nonliving Cinder Warden. Nessa wants a dependable short journey between the two small settlements. Orrin wants one of the springwood couplings for a useful bow and enjoys the long northern route. He may help, bargain, take an available coupling first, or pursue another concern. He is not the first-playable Watchpost resident **Orin**; identities, biographies and recognition remain separate.

The west relay is reached from the west bank by walking around a broken wall. The east relay and the bow-maker's physical records are in a workshop behind a winding shutter. A real crank lies outside. The player can use it, make a known turning tool, propose a mechanically compatible invention, obtain help, or use admitted structural damage to breach an exposed shutter. The public north path reaches Stonebend even when both road gates are closed. It is a real path available before accepting any undertaking.

Disconnecting a relay releases that relay's one actual springwood coupling. It does not open its road gate. It prevents this Warden from closing that gate and unlocks the Warden's already visible stop control. Disconnecting both before launch prevents the one operation permanently. Gates remain ordinary reusable physical objects after the expedition. A disabled machine does not produce a replacement adversary, renewed source or attendance reward.

## The one local operation

The dormant Warden can be **deliberately started at its reached control** by an admitted participant with the exact public operation grant. The clear review states its one effect and alternatives: it may close one currently open connected road gate; that gate can be reopened; removing both relays prevents the operation. Local start needs neither invitation, operator attendance, calendar announcement, reservation nor paid call. Read-only inspection remains available while manually paused; starting physical work requires ordinary resumption.

At launch choose the shortest complete current Warden route to an actually open connected gate's inside work stance, with west winning an exact distance tie. Its positively declared operation is only close for this exact linked gate family. Actual support, whole-body clearance, reached handle and the target’s current geometry must qualify; it gets no general human hands or permission to operate the driven workshop shutter. Preserve the chosen target. Starting spends the charge once. The machine advances at **0.011 m per game second**, with a **3,600-game-second** total allowance including one **120-game-second** gate closure. The unobstructed route is `sqrt(9² + 2²)`, approximately 9.22 m; plus closure it takes about **958.14 game seconds**, or **15.97 real seconds at normal speed**. Route changes, bodily obstruction, pause and speed settings change that actual result; this is no timed boss battle.

The machine cannot injure or shove anyone, damage unrelated property, choose a new gate, teleport, rearm or call a model. Blocking it physically can consume its finite remaining allowance; removing its selected relay, shutting it down or someone closing its target ends this attempt spent. It never moves to the other gate because the first target became unavailable. A native route-preparation/service failure is an operational hold, not fictional obstruction that spends the allowance while no game time advances.

After either relay is severed, the visible stop control is mechanically unlocked. A reached **120-game-second** shutdown action must remain feasible while the body actually moves; starting it does not freeze the Warden. Completion ends the operation or disables a dormant remaining charge. Losing reach or authority stops the person's work without refunding committed machine motion. Cutting both before launch makes the operation prevented; both routes and the workshop remain playable. Ordinary body placement and the permanent north path provide a physical bypass around every supported spent position. It is not necessary to learn a general hauling system to leave.
