## Starting-world content reconciliation

Before reconciling saved-world content, database/save versions or starting definitions during a merge, or creating/replacing the standard development starting world, apply the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy). The newest storage format determines how the world is represented; it does not determine which useful content survives. Preserve the union of useful starting content from the relevant sources and organize it as one coherent main world.

1. **Compare content before choosing or replacing data.** Compare both sides' authored starting definitions and their scenario documentation. Disposable development progress does not require a whole-database audit; inspect saved-world contents only when Mike requests retention or a known useful authored addition exists only there. Inventory distinct scenarios, characters, encounters, possessions, methods, resources, environments and feature prerequisites. When those retention conditions apply, check for useful content present only in an older save or diagnostic setup; the current initializer alone cannot prove nothing was lost. Inspect incompatible data read-only as evidence, without loading it as current state, changing its version marker or adding a legacy reader/migration.
2. **Reconcile meaning, not database rows.** Deduplicate equivalent people, objects and scenarios while retaining useful distinct additions and variations. Resolve identities, relationships, placements, quantities, prerequisites and supported definitions together. A newer timestamp or format is not sufficient reason to drop content. Respect explicitly superseded or removed authored choices; do not revive an abandoned choice solely because an older save contains it. If a consequential conflict needs the developer's decision, follow the existing question rule and the mandatory all-work stop for uncertain merge/rebase resolutions.
3. **Compose a playable world.** Reconcile overlapping locations and competing uses of space, supplies and characters. Relocate or adapt contrived demonstrations into coherent, accessible places or optional encounters; do not discard useful content merely because it began as a test. Preserve discovery and access to the demonstrated feature without crowding the first scene or triggering every encounter immediately. For example, retained rivals can occupy a discoverable distant site rather than appearing beside the player at arrival. Keep feature prerequisites available and place directions or opportunities consistently with the world's knowledge rules.
4. **Make the combination durable.** Recreate retained content through current supported world-owned definitions and the existing initializer, including useful content authored only in saved data. Update its canonical scenario documentation and related work items. A one-off database patch or retained archive is not sufficient if the next fresh start would lose the content again. Use current integrated engine support; this requirement does not authorize merging unfinished/excluded code. Preserve deliberately selected solo/custom openings and separate incompatible diagnostic laws instead of forcing them into the main world's rules.
5. **Keep reusable content separate from play history and protected records.** The content union is not a row-wise union of arbitrary gameplay progress, duplicate inventories, completed actions, private memories or conflicting outcomes. Preserve useful reusable definitions and starting circumstances without fabricating past events or granting private knowledge. Credentials, permissions, spending and unresolved external operations retain their own exact preservation requirements; a content decision cannot clear a charge or grant access.
6. **Verify the saved result and account for differences.** Check the actual combined world after construction, persistence and reopening against the content inventory, including important scenario prerequisites and any intentionally distant encounters. Run the smallest relevant checks under the existing verification/reconciliation policy; this adds no blanket browser test or complete gameplay replay. Report what was retained, combined, relocated, explicitly superseded or still unresolved, as well as any loss of development progress. Do not claim the union is complete while useful content is missing or unexamined.

## Current history capture boundary

Portable verification copies use the same complete checkpoint capture and restore owners as gameplay. `WorldRecords.load(true)` reconstructs the active working set, with cold history and retired records still in SQL; serializing it is not a complete export. Even a full world-record read does not copy the other repositories. Keep original checkpoints and use their complete payloads when constructing an owned disposable continuation.

Autosaves are server-owned recovery files for the entire shared persistent world, not per-player saves. One scheduler serves the world regardless of which players are connected. Manual save/list/delete/load requires the creator or authorized operator's current save grant; ordinary participants have no save controls or automatic-save request path. Normal gameplay durability comes from database commits. Autosaves accrue only while the world is running; the current presence policy pauses after all admitted players finish any installed simulated vulnerable fade; a lone fading player still receives normal simulation. A future host that runs without connected players uses the same server-owned checkpoint path.

Gameplay capture flushes preceding native progress, pins one consistent database revision and releases the mutation queue once that snapshot is established. The capture slot is reserved before the mutation queue: one manual save may wait for a capture in progress and runs before the next automatic save, and each capture receives a durable per-world sequence that orders the catalog and retention independently of the wall clock. An owned worker streams canonical records, complete cold history, source versions and annotations in stable primary-key pages; it does not hydrate every actor or clone a full world into the worker. PostgreSQL capture uses a read-only repeatable-read connection. One capture runs at a time; repeated identical save requests share completion, a second different manual request while one waits fails busy, and deleting a save that is still waiting for or running its capture answers busy.

The physical `records-jsonl-2` encoding carries exact current table coverage, captured revision and SHA-256 integrity metadata; streamed metadata declares both byte and row counts. Work allowances remain 64 rows per database page, 1 MiB per encoded record, 256 MiB per package, two million records and a two-minute scan deadline, with a 256 MiB worker old-generation heap limit. Oversized owners must split growing collections through the canonical codec; exceeding an allowance fails visibly and retains previous checkpoints. Database format 38 and save format `development-2026-10-30-journal-expiry` identify the integrated current layout, including shared current/temporal payloads paired with personal reception and per-observer perception components, exact source and retention dependencies, wetness/burning body conditions without an aggregate injury field, separate current ranged aim-target life and original review subject, timeline-bound construction receipts, exact body-port equipment attachments, finite guard work, authored-method provenance, invention receipt sources, recipe-record transactions/knowledge, exact recipe-family pins, sparse body meters, the explicit memory search projection and saved mechanical sampling progress, owned character practice records and active coaching episodes, assembly families/parts and finite material exposure, installed food preparations and finite cast definitions. The existing world-settings codec captures the remaining duration and optional exact deadline; rates and geometry rebuild after load. The practice and coaching owners have dedicated current-format tables; there is no older-format reader. The current journal requires exact semantic-expiry metadata alongside canonical row changes, so recovery cannot re-archive expired evidence. A shared format marker alone does not establish complete combined qualification. History perspectives now retain exact family/body-hash references to canonical current or archived personal accounts; capture validates these references against the complete captured records and source versions. Entity records reconstruct one persistent ordered entity collection rather than a plain dictionary. Animals save an absolute due game time while their timer runs, or a remaining wait while paused. Derived lexical documents and durable pending keys are rebuilt from canonical sources after restore; they are not independent gameplay save authority. The physical history encoding and maintained event total remain unchanged. Runtime loading rejects older databases/checkpoints without modifying them. Agents may replace incompatible development state with a fresh current-format world under the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy). The exact preceding-layout reader has been removed. Every current package requires the stream encoding, exact byte/row counts and checksum; inline database worlds and whole-JSON slots are refused before installation. The database recovery row contains only an integrity-checked file pointer. The same stream reader/writer serves manual saves, autosaves, recovery and operational backup.

Normal startup checks hot-plus-archived event coverage against the count committed transactionally with event insertion/deletion. It no longer physically recounts every retained event. This deliberately moves detection of out-of-band deletion/count drift to explicit full recovery (`load(false)`), checkpoint capture and restore validation. Checkpoint capture compares the maintained count with `COUNT(*)` in its read snapshot; decoding/install also checks the captured total against captured event rows. Same-version atomic commits, current validation and missing-hot-source rejection remain required. This scalar check does not prove payload integrity or detect every possible database corruption.

The capture reader holds its database snapshot through stream output, file synchronization and publication. The worker releases the gameplay capture barrier earlier, but slow storage can still retain PostgreSQL row versions. The two-minute scan deadline is checked between records; a separate 125-second whole-capture watchdog terminates a worker stalled in a write, sync or publication call. Failure is reported only after worker exit and private-stage cleanup, so 125 seconds starts cancellation rather than guaranteeing an exact end-to-end deadline. The failed worker is not reused. The command-line operational backup has its own combined snapshot outside this worker limit; [SV07](limits/persistence.md#sv07) and [SB16](limits/persistence.md#sb16) own these limits and measured tradeoffs.

Private staging files and metadata are synchronized before directory rename and parent synchronization. A repeated create that finds an existing matching identity verifies the body and synchronizes its parent again before acknowledging success, including after an ambiguous publication failure. Only published complete slots are listed. Startup reclaims a staging directory only when its recorded local process is demonstrably dead; legacy or ambiguous stages require operator review. Catalog reads inspect small metadata and payload sizes without parsing world bodies; public pages retain at most 101 metadata entries and return 100 saves plus a cursor. Directory enumeration remains linear in the manually retained slot count, with asynchronous per-entry work outside the gameplay mutation queue; it never loads save bodies. Catalog permission is checked before reading and again before disclosure. Loads verify complete streams and reconstruct state while play is explicitly paused. This full candidate/install path still needs memory proportional to the saved world; the capture bounds do not promise constant-memory restoration.

Restore preserves current forgetting/accounting and atomically switches the world and its pointer to an already durable pre-load recovery file. The rotation target of two integrity-checked recovery packages protects the previous cut during interrupted installation. A gameplay load first writes a new full recovery checkpoint pinned to the exact revision it replaces, before any database write; if the current world exceeds capture limits or storage cannot publish that checkpoint, the load is refused, the current world is kept (still paused by the load request) without a storage latch, and the refusal is recorded as a checkpoint failure. There is no supported bypass; [SB13](limits/persistence.md#sb13) gives operator guidance. Existing-world operational restore also writes it before its installation transaction; a failed install removes it, and an uncertain commit keeps and names it ([SB18](limits/persistence.md#sb18)). Integrity-checked means valid checksum/stream counts, not a completed current-format decode/restore rehearsal. Cleanup failure is reported separately from successful installation. A newly installed gameplay load also checks the following participation save before acknowledging healthy completion. ### Background and synchronous world saves

Current evidence storage uses immutable suffix segments, exact changed-owner preparation and compact application inputs through the existing save/journal owners. The [journal contract](projects/performance-overhaul/journal-contract.md#compact-evidence-storage) owns the layout, admission proofs and recovery requirements. [Current-format storage checks](verification/attention-and-memory-core.md#current-format-storage-and-development-readiness--october-29-2026) demonstrate exact retention/restoration and reduced encoded writes; complete-game capacity remains open.

Between checkpoints, the database itself is the save: every change to the world is written to it. All changes to the world take turns in one line (the `WorldService` mutation queue), one at a time. Changes are saved in one of two ways:

- **Background save.** The world's own simulation progress — people and animals moving, needs changing, computed routes — is saved about once per real second. It takes its turn in the line only to prepare the save, then leaves; the database write happens while the game keeps running. This progress is already shown to players before it is saved, as it always was. In code this is `WorldService.saveProgress` and `backgroundSave`.
- **Synchronous save.** A change whose effect must not be seen until it is saved holds the line until its save finishes, and only then is shown or acknowledged: player commands, AI characters' finished actions and replies (including conversation updates), pause/resume, world-editor changes and loads. Simulation progress is also saved synchronously when saving it in the background would drop from memory a record the database does not have yet ([SB20](limits/persistence.md#sb20)). In code this is `WorldService.commit` and the synchronous branch of `saveProgress`.

**Experimental journal integration — October 17, 2026.** Eligible immutable movement/native changes can now confirm after PostgreSQL commits their exact record/event delta and command receipt, before applying the ordinary query tables. The world header distinguishes committed and applied revisions. Existing canonical/history/memory readers wait for coherent applied coverage; authority, logout and retry receipts remain immediate. Unsupported transitions drain the journal and use the existing atomic table transaction, with both heads advancing together. Restart replays exact accepted changes before serving the world, and checkpoint capture requires equal heads. Provisional pending limits are 32 entries/32MiB, with synchronous fallback for more than 512 admission lookups or an oversized change. This does not cap population/history. The [contract](projects/performance-overhaul/journal-contract.md) and [negative first cost comparison](verification/performance/overhaul-storage-implementation.md#first-integrated-journal-cost-comparison-negative-evidence) record incomplete qualification and extra total work; retention and smooth3× remain unproved. The current database and journal checkpoint identities reject incompatible development data under the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy).

An implemented worker thread applies eligible, already accepted journal batches through the existing history, memory and canonical-record writers. Pending entries and worker packets retain changed records, exact personal accounts and revision metadata rather than complete world generations. The main thread publishes completion and wakes searchable-history preparation only after SQL commits. Independent server startup remains excluded while the original primary or any owned application connection can write. A lost completion may be recognized only after the old writer is proved finished and the durable revision proves the full batch committed; otherwise saving fails explicitly without repeating gameplay. Startup recovery keeps its existing local owner. Ordinary application and cold recovery have run; the complete worker-failure matrix, retained-buffer envelope and complete-game capacity remain open under [PF17-J01](limits/persistence.md#pf17-j01--experimental-deferred-saving).

A slow database can still freeze the world during table-backed synchronous saves or journal backpressure ([SB21](limits/persistence.md#sb21)), but not during background saves. A crash loses the simulation progress since the last completed background save: about one second (up to 5 s while [commands are waiting](limits/persistence.md#sv21)), plus the previous write's full duration when storage is slow, plus up to about 4 s of the in-flight write, after which [backpressure](limits/persistence.md#sv19) delays native time. Consumers that must not act on unsaved evidence (for example paid wake-ups) wait on the service's durable mark rather than forcing a save. [Design](projects/ordered-async-saves.md#ordered-persistence-design).

## Progressive public speech — accepted target

The [shared NPC speech feature specification](projects/npc-speech-streaming-feature-spec.md) and [technical design](projects/npc-speech-streaming-tech-design.md) select durable incremental text for all public NPC dialogue. [NST](maintainers/npc-speech-streaming.md) owns pending implementation and qualification, and [streaming limits](limits/npc-speech-streaming.md) owns the new bounds. This section is an accepted target, not evidence that the current finished-reply path already supports it.

Each displayed prefix must finish the existing synchronous `WorldService.commit` boundary with canonical text, complete fixed audience evidence and exact speech-operation progress. The first such transition also commits any enabled native acoustic intake and its ordinary consequences. Only then may authorized post-commit delivery amend the same occurrence. The ordinary background save interval and experimental deferred journal do not authorize exposure of an unsaved prefix. A storage failure leaves the last acknowledged accepted prefix authoritative; privately parsed or screened text has not been spoken.

A consistent capture retains the original occurrence identity/time/order, open/complete/interrupted state, exact accepted body, observer-permitted segments and revisions, frozen hearing/identity policy, shared lexical preparation and observer-mask continuation, operation receipt and any terminal semantic readiness. It retains the shared native order allocator and existing considered cursors needed to consume terminal readiness once. Native speech trimming remains incremental and append-safe; capture never persists an unscreened pending suffix as accepted text. Provider transport/parser state, admission buffers and wall-clock handles are transient. Capturing an open utterance does not wait for the provider or close the original live world's ongoing generation.

The existing operational job holds the sole durable publication claim and exact outstanding-close bindings; a decision group retains only its reference and ordinary disposition. Provider/job terminal status does not discharge closure or permit pruning those bindings while an accepted occurrence remains open. Retained obligations stay charged to existing native/operational work admission without holding an AI slot for a client. The [NST ownership and recovery contract](projects/npc-speech-streaming-tech-design.md#first-accepted-words-reserve-the-existing-response) governs atomic claim updates and current-owner cleanup; neither active indexes nor restored provider state become another progress owner. An immutable save captured while speech is open retains the admitted binding and close-only proof for that captured prefix. Loading it after the live occurrence has completed and its operational claim was pruned validates the saved proof separately, without recreating a runnable job or consumed response entitlement; [NST recovery](projects/npc-speech-streaming-tech-design.md#save-restart-and-gameplay-restore) owns the exact contract.

Restart or installation of a captured cut fences old callbacks and applies current privacy/forgetting authority before serving it. Through the same native owner, it closes each captured open accepted prefix as interrupted, without a second speech event, audience reconstruction, remasking or provider dispatch. This first closure may finalize the retained lexical tail and create its one semantic readiness transition; native acoustic intake and already terminal introduction/semantic consequences are not replayed. Open references and recovery do not renew sensory expiry or recreate suppressed/expired accounts. Absence from hot residency is not retirement: required retained records resolve through exact cold lookup under current authority. Current-format validation checks the related event/account/receipt/lifecycle fields together. Recovery must persist the closure before publication and normal semantic intake; failure follows the existing explicit storage hold.

Operational provider identity, receipts, reservation settlement and uncertain spending remain in their non-rewindable owner. First speech publication is not a whole-response completion receipt. A late provider result may settle the original cost but cannot reopen restored speech or replace its words. Existing caller pause/cancellation and current actor authority remain controlling, without a new speaking clock or interruption by movement or doors.

## Purpose and recovery promises

A player should be able to retain a world at a committed point, leave it, and continue from that point later. Loading a pre-death save restores the coherent world from before the death, including its other consequences; selectively reviving a character is a separate gameplay operation.

Keep four capabilities distinct:

| Capability         | Promise                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crash recovery     | Recover the latest durable committed state under the published durability policy.                                                                             |
| Gameplay save      | Retain an identified point that remains loadable independently of later world progress. Manual saves and rolling autosaves use the same correctness contract. |
| Operational backup | Recover storage and control records after infrastructure failure under the operations policy.                                                                 |
| Replay             | Reconstruct a sequence from retained inputs/outcomes under compatible rules; not a prerequisite for loading a save.                                           |

A compacted recovery journal alone does not provide historical saves. “Ten minutes ago” is recoverable only to a retained point, unless a separate verified point-in-time reconstruction capability exists. Show both real creation time and simulated time so accelerated or paused play is unambiguous. A save is successful only once it is durably published, not when its request is queued.

[Sling practice/coaching qualification](verification/practical-competence.md#continuity-and-private-inspection) verifies one outcome per actual release, duplicate receipts, current SQL reopening, active NPC work and current correction overlays. Completed support survives reconnect/restore. Continuously present NPC preparation and jointly chosen feedback retain their owned work and resolve once. Human work also survives the transport reset during paused restoration pending verified presence; actual departure or resuming without that presence interrupts it before simulation advances, as described in [Continuation and identity](#continuation-and-identity). Saving supplies no consent or exception to presence rules.

## Research and design basis

These primary sources describe useful, established patterns, not a universal implementation or a ranking of games:

- **Continuity:** Wube describes Factorio's requirement that save/exit/load preserve observable behavior when external conditions are unchanged, and the difficulties introduced by content changes and reference ordering. OpenLegend adopts that continuity goal for native simulation under compatible rules. [Factorio save/load overview](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-270).
- **Explicit scope and asynchronous writes:** Epic supports custom save objects, multiple slots and separation of global progression from a playthrough; it recommends asynchronous saving to avoid frame hitches. OpenLegend similarly defines save scope explicitly and separates consistent capture from potentially slow output. [Unreal Engine saving and loading](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/saving-and-loading-your-game-in-unreal-engine).
- **Deliberate reconstruction:** Godot's tutorial selects persistent data explicitly and explains why nested saved objects require staged reconstruction and renewed references. Its example is introductory, not a complete transactional loader. OpenLegend therefore treats graph restoration and validation as an explicit phase. [Godot saving games](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/io/saving_games.html).
- **Versioned evolution:** Factorio tracks applied migrations in each save and orders their execution. This is future release-design context subject to the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy), not a current migration requirement. [Factorio migrations](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/auxiliary/migrations.html).
- **Consistent storage capture:** Use the PostgreSQL snapshot and operational backup owner. Copying checkpoint files alone cannot capture current gameplay, accounting and privacy records. A storage snapshot does not itself determine which records should rewind.

The requirements below are OpenLegend's synthesis of those lessons and its existing authority, privacy and paid-work boundaries. They do not require an engine change or a new serialization dependency.

## Capturing and publishing a save

Capture at a complete authoritative transition boundary. An explicit save must establish a committed cut, including pending native progress intended to precede it. A transition is entirely before or after that cut; its world effects, required history and accepted durable side effects cannot straddle it. Do not wait indefinitely for external generation: classify unfinished work under the external-work rules below.

Treat the save as one logical root identifying a revision and its complete dependency closure. It may physically use one file, several blobs, database rows or a snapshot plus bounded committed changes. All parts must describe the same cut. Independent reads of “latest” rows across stores are insufficient. Use a consistent read view, immutable version references or an equivalent coordinated boundary, and fail capture if completeness cannot be established.

After capture, serialize and write from stable data. Background serialization of a mutable live object is not a consistent snapshot. Bound the capture pause, retained snapshot memory and concurrent saves; prefer the simplest measured solution over unconditional full copying or new coordination infrastructure.

Write a candidate without destroying the last good save. Validate completeness and integrity, then publish it atomically using the chosen storage system's durability guarantees. An interrupted write must leave either the previous complete save or the new complete save discoverable, never a partial replacement. A checksum detects accidental corruption; it does not establish trust. Concurrent completion must not let an older capture replace a newer slot revision accidentally. Return useful failures for full storage, missing dependencies or failed publication.

## Loading as an authority transition

Loading replaces a coherent world timeline through one authoritative operation:

1. Check authorization and compatibility; read and validate a candidate in isolation. Bound input sizes and decompression, reject unsafe paths or executable payloads, and verify required dependencies. A save is data, including generated content.
2. Validate the candidate against the current supported model and its exact dependencies, retaining the source on failure. Restore identities and relationships and prepare required derived state without emitting gameplay events or performing paid work. Compatibility scope follows the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy).
3. Quiesce world admission, establish a recoverable pre-load point, and recheck mutable permissions, privacy restrictions and external ledgers at installation. Fence outstanding work and requests using a fresh authority generation.
4. Install the world and its required projections atomically, or by an equivalent recoverable generation switch. A crash must recover one complete authority generation. Failed preparation leaves the active world untouched; failed installation must never expose a mixture.
5. Rebuild remaining safe caches, issue fresh client baselines, invalidate old cursors/selections and resume paused. Advancing again is an explicit player action.

These are responsibilities, not prescribed services or APIs. An initial loader may stop the world during preparation. A later loader can prepare off the critical path if it revalidates the installation boundary. Restoration must not call ordinary creation hooks that allocate replacement identities, award items, schedule duplicate effects or consume fresh randomness.

Give the load operation a durable identity outside the rewindable timeline. Retrying a request after a lost response must return its recorded outcome, rather than loading the checkpoint again after the world has advanced. A deliberately requested new load remains a separate operation.

The browser consumes a selected load's successful acknowledgment only to reload, adopting no old world data or private text. That acknowledgment intentionally crosses the changed timeline; ordinary obsolete reads and mutation results retain their existing fences. Reload resolves the currently signed-in account and authority afresh, including after sign-out or account replacement during the load. Refused, failed or unreadable acknowledgments do not establish success; retry retains the selected load's request identity.

The abandoned future must not remain visible as current world history, actor knowledge, conversation context or accepted workspace content. Retained audit data is separately scoped and cannot feed restored cognition accidentally. Privacy overlays may remove access to older evidence even when the checkpoint contains it.

Independent database records do not change this requirement. Capture and restore unloaded regions, cold memory/evidence, definitions and pending native work with the same coherent cut as active entities. The current timeline/writer fence is installed outside the rewindable payload; old jobs, cursors, vectors and callbacks must validate it even when restored source IDs/revisions happen to match. The [production model](../archive/07-technical-architecture/production-data-model.md#timeline-and-authority-fencing) owns those storage references.

## External work, privacy and shared authority

The world creator and authorized OpenLegend system administrators may save and load that world through the game service. Ordinary participants have no world-rewind permission. Validate the principal and world scope on both request and installation, and record the administrative operation. This permission does not grant inspection of human-private content or authority to rewind external accounting. Shared restoration uses a coordinated complete world cut; it cannot roll back one player's side of a shared trade alone.

Loading never refunds actual spending, erases uncertain paid attempts, reverses a provider call or restores revoked access. Current forgetting/erasure protections remain effective across older saves and their derived projections. The detailed policies belong to memory, billing and operations owners; save retention and export must respect them. Saves containing private minds and history require private access and must exclude credentials.

The shared restore commit reapplies the complete current forgetting ledger to installed transcript audiences, perspectives and dependent stories. Comparing only against the abandoned world's ledger is insufficient: an older backup may restore permissions for evidence that was already forgotten in that world. This enforcement covers both gameplay loads and the operational restore command.

Saved work intent and an external execution are different things. Work already committed before the cut restores its accepted result. In-flight work from the abandoned generation cannot publish into the restored world, even if IDs recur or cancellation fails. Work known never to have been dispatched may be reconsidered through normal admission after resume; uncertain completion stays uncertain and must not trigger an automatic paid retry. Restoring an accepted character workspace does not restore a live provider session or authorize continuing its abandoned context.

An operational recovery onto a fresh host must recover or reconcile the non-rewindable authority as well. If that authority is missing, block affected external dispatch rather than assuming no spending or revocation occurred. Copying an old whole database over a live account is not a gameplay-load implementation.

The unit of rewind is the world or another explicitly defined, dependency-closed authority boundary. A future shared world cannot rewind one player's possessions while leaving the corresponding shared trades intact. Cross-world transfers and irreversible external effects require explicit reconciliation or branch isolation; distributed transactions are not required before those features exist. A new generation distinguishes old commands and callbacks even if in-world identifiers remain stable.

Creator restore/export permission does not grant access to human-private messages or private character notes. Shared-world save tools must preserve these through authorized protected storage or explicitly exclude non-rewindable private-channel data under its chosen policy, without leaking plaintext through game interfaces. Direct database/host administrators are outside this game-level privacy guarantee. Private-channel timeline treatment and participant synchronization remain D48/D60 feature decisions; save/load roles are settled. Do not silently omit gameplay-critical private state or claim a complete save when it is missing.

## Compatibility and retention

Version save interpretation separately from mutable display names and physical storage layout. Identify required rules/content versions and the current supported reader. Unknown required mechanics, missing dependencies and unsupported versions must produce a clear refusal, not silent deletion or regeneration. Cosmetic substitution is permissible only when it cannot change semantics and is disclosed where relevant.

A future released-save support window and migration pipeline belong to [SL06](maintainers/save-and-load.md#sl06--compatibility-policy-after-real-player-support-begins), after the owner changes the [development save policy](../AGENTS.md#development-save-policy). Any then-authorized conversion must preserve source data, relationship/behavior semantics and atomicity, and be restart-safe.

Retained saves pin their necessary content, history and incremental bases. Garbage collection, history compaction and definition cleanup must account for those references. A save depending on mutable “latest” content or already-deleted journal entries is not retained successfully. Explicit privacy deletion overrides ordinary retention through the privacy policy, including handling affected backups.

Autosave policy is an operator setting stored per world outside gameplay rewind: enabled by default, every five minutes of running wall time and three integrity-checked autosaves retained, separately from manual saves ([bounds](limits/persistence.md#sv23)). Paused time does not accumulate; a waiting manual save goes first; while a capture runs or a manual save waits, the due automatic save is held and starts as soon as the slot frees; an opportunity the busy mutation queue refuses retries after 30 s of running time, and the third consecutive busy refusal is recorded as a failure; other failures wait for the next cadence rather than looping. A settings record that cannot be read keeps automatic saves off (never guessed), is retried every 30 s of running time and is recorded as a failure after three failed reads. Shutdown drains admitted output within its [deadline](limits/persistence.md#sv22). The Game panel shows retained points, the last automatic checkpoint from the catalog, save-folder bytes and the latest checkpoint failure, which survives restart until acknowledged; catalog health is reported separately. There is no arbitrary between-checkpoint rewind. Autosave rotation must not evict a manual save implicitly or delete the last valid checkpoint before publishing its replacement. Engineering selects reasonable cadence/recovery defaults and records retention choices under the [retention ledger](../archive/07-technical-architecture/data-delivery-and-scale.md#retention-decision-ledger). [D60](maintainers/decisions/delivery-and-privacy.md#d60--gameplay-save-and-load-policy) retains conditional compatibility, notification and cloud conflict choices; save/load authorization is fixed above. Do not merge divergent simulations silently.

## Performance and evidence

Follow the [performance design](performance.md). Measure capture pause, peak memory, bytes written, background work interference, durable-save latency and time until a restored world is playable at representative population/history sizes. Moving serialization off-thread does not remove consistency or memory costs. A bounded hot working set does not justify dropping cold authoritative data from a save.

Keep the large-history workloads distinct: [SL09-C](verification/ordered-async-saves.md#slow-output-snapshot-pressure-sl09-c) demonstrated three 100,000-memory captures and restores under concurrent writes, while [PW09](verification/data-foundation.md#pw09--unicode-and-scoped-retrieval) failed all six 100,000-source restores with large synthetic vector indexes during cache reuse under the writer-statement deadline. Capture, full recovery and reopen succeeded in that second workload; rollback after a failed installation did not qualify restoration. The existing PW09/PF08 and D1/D2/SL owners retain this concrete failure. Neither the successful plain-memory workload nor the worker watchdog closes it.

The current complete-record stream and owned capture worker are delivered foundations, not pending prerequisites. Incremental saves, deduplication or compression remain optional improvements selected for a measured need, not a reason to rebuild the working capture path. If incremental chains are used, bound reconstruction work and protect every required base until replacement is durable. Save-frequency tuning must not silently weaken acknowledged-command durability.

Verification should demonstrate native continuation across partial work, death, random choices and simulation deadlines; complete restoration of cold and cross-store dependencies; safe handling of cycles and missing references; and explicit rejection of incompatible or malformed input. Failure injection must cover interrupted publication/installation, corrupt or oversized input, missing content, exhausted storage and repeated load requests. External-work scenarios must prove that stale callbacks, uncertain attempts and old browser commands cannot create duplicate effects or spending, and that current privacy restrictions survive rewind.

These are save/load acceptance principles, not completed tests or an implementation checklist. Concrete delivery tasks belong in focused maintainer trackers, and observed results in [Verification](verification.md).
