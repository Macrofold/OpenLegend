# Unreal Engine 5 for Open Legend

**Unreal is the part of the game a player installs.** It draws the valley, plays the birdsong, shows the player's inventory and handles their mouse and keyboard. Open Legend’s server still runs the shared world. If someone builds a bridge, the server decides whether the bridge exists and who can cross it. Each player’s copy of Unreal draws the bridge from their own side of the river.

That is our destination. Today the game still runs in a browser using PlayCanvas. Rust is the chosen server for the whole game. It already runs a measured opening with a real browser client and can open and resume River Camp and Solo. TypeScript still launches by default while the team completes and checks the full Main experience and the remaining server features. [Current server and evidence](architecture.md#stand-alone-rust-host)

## What you and your players actually run

| On your development machine or remote workstation | On the player’s PC | On the game server |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unreal Editor** is the workshop. Agents import art, set up scenes, build the game and capture views here. You can open it to fly around and judge the result. | **The packaged game** is the finished application. A player installs it, signs in, and connects to the world. They do not install the editor or need an Epic developer account to use our own sign-in. | **Open Legend** owns time, terrain that affects play, movement, private knowledge, NPC decisions and saves. Computer-controlled characters (NPCs) can hear an axe through the trees without rendering an image or running a copy of Unreal. |

The little **Play** button in the editor is a quick preview. A packaged build on a clean PC answers a different question: can someone actually install it, sign in, walk through the forest and return tomorrow? Both checks matter. ## Read a scene like a developer

| Word you’ll hear | Picture it in the village |
| --- | --- |
| **Level**, **actor**, **component** | The level is the scene. A lantern placed in it can be an actor. Its visible frame, light and sound can each be a component. A thousand blades of grass can share one drawing operation. They need no individual game identity. |
| **Mesh**, **texture**, **material** | The mesh gives the roof its shape. A texture supplies a pattern or surface information. The material tells light how the damp thatch responds. A sunbeam painted into the texture will still be there at midnight. |
| **Skeleton**, **skin weights**, **animation** | Bones give a person joints. Weights tell the sleeve how much to follow the arm as it bends. A convincing still model can put an elbow through a shirt when it reaches. |
| **C++**, **Blueprint**, **Python** | C++ is regular text code. A Blueprint is an Unreal asset that can express behavior as connected boxes. Editor Python can have an agent import 100 shirts the same way. Code that must run on a player’s PC needs a runtime-supported route such as C++ or Blueprint. |
| **Cook**, **package**, **patch** | Cooking prepares a model and its materials for a particular platform. Packaging builds the installable game. A patch delivers later changes. The original editable model and the file a player downloads serve different jobs. |

[Epic’s terminology guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-terminology) · [What editor Python can do](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/scripting-the-unreal-editor-using-python)

## Where the beauty comes from

In the rider image, the bright strip of valley leads your eye past the rider to the mountain. In the village, bridges and roofs give the sunlit stream a human scale. In the dark forest, a gap between trunks reveals the people. These effects depend on **where you put things**. Adding detail to every rock cannot create a good route through a valley.

Unreal supplies an unusually good toolbox. A moving sun casts shadows. Fog makes a sunbeam visible where it crosses damp air. **Lumen** lets light bounce as the world changes, so opening a building to daylight can brighten the interior. **Nanite** can draw detailed shapes at different distances efficiently. **World Partition** loads nearby parts of a large authored landscape. The team still chooses the mountain shapes, the clearings and what a player sees rounding the bend. A moving camera naturally gives nearby branches more apparent motion than distant peaks. Play that walk in ordinary daylight as well as at the perfect sunset. [Sky and fog](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/environmental-light-with-fog-clouds-sky-and-atmosphere-in-unreal-engine) · [How Lumen spends frame time](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine) · [How large scenes load](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine)

**The engine sets no minimum level of model detail.** Spend detail where the player can see it. A distant pine needs a convincing outline. A door you can walk through needs the right size and hinge position. A companion whose face you inspect needs good eyes, hair and expressions. Reuse a small family of pines in clusters, give buildings interchangeable pieces, and dress people from a fitted wardrobe. Thousands of placements or combinations are a realistic solo goal. Thousands of individually polished characters, costumes and animations are a very different commitment.

## Your day-to-day job with agents

1. **Give them an observable outcome.** “Walk from the bridge into pine shade at dawn. Show me where I can build, and keep the far ridge visible.” That gives an agent something it can build, record and compare.
2. **Have agents do the repeatable work.** A good handoff includes the original files, a recipe that can regenerate the Unreal assets, a short moving capture from the actual game, and any failures. Two agents can work on different shirts. A level or material can be saved as a binary file that Git cannot reliably merge. Two agents editing it can overwrite each other. Assign one owner to publish shared assets.
3. **Review movement and consequences.** Watch from player height and the normal gameplay camera. Look for sparkling leaves, sliding feet, clothes cutting through skin, or a roof that hides the person you’re talking to. Then build something, leave, and return. A beautiful still frame answers none of those questions.
4. **Ask where the time went.** At 60 frames per second there are about 16.7 milliseconds for a whole frame, including input, animation and drawing. A long frame feels like a hitch even if the average is 60. Ask separately for rendering time, time from click to visible response, and the server’s ability to keep world time. Faster graphics cannot cure a late server response. [Unreal Insights](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/trace-quick-start-guide-in-unreal-engine)

A mostly hands-off editor is credible. Agents can run editor scripts, build from the command line and launch packaged sessions. Someone still has to maintain those jobs and look at the results. Pick a released engine version and pin the tools that produced each approved asset. Epic’s agent interface and 5.9 Preview expand what is possible, but making a preview-only button essential would put your production schedule in the hands of its next release. ## Four decisions worth your attention

- **Who gets to play first?** My recommendation is a downloadable Windows PC build first, with named minimum hardware and a smoother, higher-quality target. A remote Windows machine with a suitable graphics card lets you review from your Mac. Mac support for features such as Nanite varies by chip. An editor workstation, a minimum-spec player machine and a graphics-free Rust server are three different budgets. [Mac feature support](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)
- **What has to work in the browser?** Keep the browser for tools such as the character-thinking lab and administration. A downloadable game changes how friends join. Streaming the game to a browser means paying to draw and encode each independent view remotely. A native web export is another option under development. Decide separately whether the convenience is worth its own costs and visual targets. [Pixel Streaming](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/overview-of-pixel-streaming-in-unreal-engine)
- **How quickly does a new invention need its own art?** A player can immediately see a shelter made of approved posts and roof pieces. Generating an entirely new creature with a moving skeleton during play requires another production and delivery system. Start with an honest, recognizable representation, keep play moving and deliver richer art when ready. The same distinction applies to AI motion. A generated walking clip still needs to fit the body and stop where the server says the person stopped.
- **Who owns and may ship each piece?** Open Legend’s first-party code currently uses AGPL. Epic restricts combinations that would impose another license on its engine. Decide the new client’s license and review what can cross that boundary **before agents copy code or combine components**. A separate program talking to the server is a candidate structure that needs review. Track the rights to art, plugins and voices separately. Under Epic’s standard game terms the usual royalty is 5% on eligible lifetime gross revenue above $1 million per product, with exclusions. [Project licensing](../LICENSING.md) · [Epic’s agreement](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula/unreal) · [Release and royalty guidance](https://www.unrealengine.com/release)

**The first milestone I would ask to see:** install the game on a second PC, join the Rust opening, walk past wildlife, speak to a resident, and reconnect as the same character. Next, build the sunlit village route and show it from the viewpoints of two characters on opposite sides of a wall. Those are new Unreal acceptance checks. What those two people could see, hear and do matters as much as how the village looked. The [migration feature spec](projects/unreal-engine-5-migration-feature-spec.md) gives the next AI the full handoff.
