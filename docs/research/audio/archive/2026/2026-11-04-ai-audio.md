# AI music and sound generation: licensing and production evidence

Research checked **November 4, 2026**. Several current generators permit commercial game use, including Suno, but permission, copyright, usable exports and downstream distribution rights remain separate questions. No audio was generated or auditioned for this research. Product capabilities and training-data claims below are attributed to their publishers; the external listening benchmark is identified separately.

The [Open Legend direction](../../openlegend-direction.md) owns the creative recommendation, scope and bounded audition. This record owns current AI-provider evidence and unresolved terms. The [rights and delivery study](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md) covers composition/master rights, contracting, player videos, creator exports and browser delivery.

## Suno: can we sell a game containing its music?

**Yes, for eligible music under the applicable paid commercial-use conditions.** Suno's [paid-use help][suno-paid], updated September 3, explicitly includes video games, film, distribution platforms and separately sold albums, without a Suno share of earnings. The operational detail matters: current terms require an authorized download, not merely access to a generated song.

### Current terms and the ongoing Content license

The [terms][suno-terms] were revised August 10 and became effective September 3, 2026. Pro/Premier assignments convey Suno's available output rights, without guaranteeing copyright or uniqueness. Commercial exploitation requires an approved download within tier allocations. Commercial-download rights are perpetual and survive cancellation, downgrade, suspension and later pricing/quota changes. Stream-ripping and deliberate concealment of provenance are prohibited.

Inputs remain yours, but Suno receives continuing rights over both inputs and outputs. Its Content license is worldwide, nonexclusive, royalty-free, perpetual, irrevocable, assignable and sublicensable. It permits adaptations, service delivery, monetization, marketing and improvement, including related AI models, and necessary sublicensing to other users. [Content provisions][suno-terms].

**Production implication:** a commissioned demo or proprietary theme should only be uploaded if our agreement covers that onward grant and the composer accepts it. A game-use license alone should not be treated as permission to grant these separate rights. This affects how much control an upload-based workflow preserves even when the original composition remains ours.

### Download entitlements and the creation-time discrepancy

The [download FAQ][suno-downloads] specifies **20 songs/month for Pro and 60 for Premier**. Repeated formats and stems from one song count once; extra downloads can be purchased. Free accounts receive seven lifetime trial downloads for personal, noncommercial use. The FAQ says Studio workflows retain unrestricted export. Limits apply from September 3 to existing libraries too.

There is an unresolved documentation discrepancy. The newer paid-use/download guidance emphasizes songs **downloaded while subscribed**. The [no-retroactivity article][suno-retro] and [ownership article][suno-ownership] still emphasize songs **created while subscribed**; even the [copyright help updated September 4][suno-copyright] retains that distinction. The no-retroactivity article allows only possible, song-specific exceptions. These sources do not establish that a new paid download automatically clears a free-created favorite.

**Recommended operating rule:** create the original and all derivatives while paid, obtain the authorized commercial download while paid, and archive the entitlement. Obtain written, song-specific confirmation before relying on any exception for earlier free-tier work. This avoids needing to resolve the historical edge case for new production.

### Own-song remixes, other users' remixes and extensions

The [Remix FAQ][suno-remix] makes a useful distinction: a remix of another user's song, or another user's remix of ours, is not eligible for commercial use. A remix of **our own paid-created song**, also made while paid, can qualify. The authorized-download rule still applies. Do not interpret the cross-user restriction as a ban on every editorial change to our own material.

The [extension guidance][suno-extension] warns against assuming ownership in someone else's source or monetizing a free-origin extension. Preserve the entire input chain: original recordings, lyrics, collaborators' permissions and source versions. An extension does not remove the source material's restrictions.

### Production capabilities and price snapshot

The September release introduces **v6, v6-wild and v6-mini**. Core v6 and v6-wild require Pro/Premier; the help page lists up to eight minutes for the family. Section editing, combining sources and selective changes are documented capabilities to test, not evidence of successful Open Legend arrangements. [Release notes][suno-v6] · [v6 help][suno-v6-help].

**Studio 2.0 is Premier-only.** Its documented tools include MIDI editing, automation, effects and source separation. Export supports complete mixes, selected ranges, multitracks and individual WAV clips; MIDI extraction from a stem costs ten credits. [Studio overview][suno-studio] · [export guide][suno-export]. Estimated stems from a completed mix must be checked for leakage, reverberation, phase problems and usefulness when parts are muted; an export button does not establish clean composition stems.

The [pricing page][suno-pricing] displayed **$8/month Pro and $24/month Premier as annual-billing equivalents**. These are not month-to-month quotes. Recheck checkout terms before a trial, and count curation, repair and rejected generations as production costs.

### Remaining legal and release distinctions

Suno's permission for a game or album does not settle all creator-pack sublicensing, raw asset exports or player-video claims. Apply the [release-rights questions](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md#specify-the-actual-uses) to the actual distribution, and do not infer exclusive copyright from a paid account.

The [Warner partnership][suno-warner] settled litigation between those parties. Suno's [v6 announcement][suno-v6-blog] names Warner, BMG and Believe as partners; this is not evidence that every rights-holder dispute has ended. [November 2 reporting][suno-litigation] still describes the UMG/Sony action as pending. That reporting does not determine whether a particular customer's output infringes.

### Stable Audio: hosted use and model terms differ

The current [product page][stable-product] advertises **six-minute instrumental music**, **30-second SFX**, 50 monthly free credits and paid plans starting at **$12/month**. These are listed capabilities/prices, not our output measurements or a purchase quote.

The [September 30 service terms][stable-terms] assign Stability's available output rights, acknowledge that other users can receive similar outputs, and retain customer responsibility for inputs. They prohibit misrepresenting AI output as human-generated or circumventing provenance. A referenced privacy mechanism permits opting out of training use.

Stability describes licensed data and publishes provenance information. Treat those statements as the provider's assertions about the specific model, not an independent audit or a guarantee against resemblance to an existing composition. Hosted-plan permission should not be confused with the revenue threshold for self-hosted weights.

### Eleven Music: eligibility and continued shipping

The [October 9 terms][eleven-music-terms] require both **title lifetime revenue below $500,000**, including versions/DLC, and **entity prior-year games revenue below $1 million** for an Indie Game. Affiliate revenue and all monetization count, net of specified fees, refunds and chargebacks. Losing qualification starts a **60-day** upgrade window, with a continuation exception for previously distributed copies. Under the published table, Studio Games require Enterprise Music; Enterprise Music Lite does not suffice.

Starter/Creator/Pro are **individual-only**; Scale permits entities below ten employees and Business below fifty. Lossless/high-quality downloads start at Pro; streaming distribution at Creator. Artist/songwriter names, song/album titles, publishers, labels and distinctive lyrics are prohibited prompts. Music-library redistribution needs written authorization. Cancellation/downgrade retains creation-plan conditions; additional model terms can apply. [Music terms][eleven-music-terms].

Resolve the actual contracting entity and plan before production, and plan for a successful title rather than assuming eligibility remains permanent. The [current documentation][eleven-music-docs] covers structure, section editing and audio references, and points API users to v2.5. Record the model ID; old indexed descriptions can lag a version change.

### Eleven SFX: the useful production boundary

The [SFX documentation][eleven-sfx-docs] supports **0.1–30-second output and looping**. The [applicable terms][eleven-creative] distinguish embedding in a larger work from distributing isolated reusable audio. Do not equate a game's permission to play an effect with permission to give every world author that source file.

Prompt and assess a specific acoustic event, perspective, environment, onset and tail. Review ambience for unwanted recognizable speech or event sounds. These are production acceptance criteria, not observed weaknesses of the model.

## External listening evidence and its limits

Artificial Analysis's **AA-Music-Instrumental v1.1** [leaderboard][aa-leaderboard] provides a dated preference signal, not a completed Open Legend audition. Selected rows from the November 4 snapshot:

| Model                | Elo and reported 95% interval | Count labelled “Samples” |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------: | -----------------------: |
| Suno v6              |               1148; 1126–1170 |                    2,208 |
| Suno v6-mini         |               1114; 1093–1135 |                    2,332 |
| Mureka V9.5          |               1084; 1063–1105 |                    2,297 |
| Lyria 3.5            |               1078; 1057–1099 |                    2,302 |
| Stable Audio 3 Large |                1014; 993–1035 |                    2,291 |
| Eleven Music v2.5    |                1001; 980–1022 |                    2,236 |

Suno v6 leads that snapshot. The count column is reproduced using the site's label; it is **not established here as a count of unique tracks or listeners**. The methodology describes preference votes. Overlapping intervals should not be presented as decisive pairwise differences, and selected rows do not represent the full board.

The [methodology][aa-methodology] uses private evaluators' blind comparisons of tracks from the same prompt/modality; public votes do not enter ratings. It generates one track per prompt, targets approximately three minutes where supported, and normalizes listening volume and delivery format. Voting requires only **ten seconds of listening per track**, so the minimum does not establish full-track assessment. Ratings use Bradley–Terry estimation and report 95% intervals.

**Interpretation:** this supports taking current AI music seriously enough to compare. It does not establish superiority to a commissioned human score: this board compares AI models. Nor does it measure thematic development, long-session fatigue, clean stems, transitions, input rights or fit with game dialogue and effects. Those are reasons to keep the [bounded audition](../../openlegend-direction.md#a-fair-bounded-audition), rather than treating a leaderboard as music direction.

## AI-specific acceptance and rights follow-through

Use the direction's shared brief and listening procedure. For an AI candidate, additionally establish that the accepted version can leave the service in useful files, be revised without losing its identity, and keep shipping under the actual entitlement. Compare the cost of an **accepted cue**, including selection, editing and discarded material, rather than nominal generation minutes.

Preserve a record of provider/model version, generation date, plan and licensee, approved download, source inputs, any human changes and final asset identifier. Keep that record with the established asset workflow. No runtime generation, self-hosted model or procedural music system is required by this research.

Provider assignment and commercial permission do not settle copyright. The U.S. Copyright Office's [2025 report][usco-report] distinguishes human-authored expression from purely generated material; prompting alone generally does not provide enough human control. Do not assume cleanup, format conversion or re-recording makes an entire generated composition exclusive. See the [copyright analysis](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md#ai-output-and-copyright) for the fuller distinction.

The [release-rights matrix](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md#specify-the-actual-uses) and [player-video policy](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md#protect-players-ability-to-share-the-game) also apply. In particular, SOUNDRAW/Mubert fingerprinting restrictions and Eleven's isolated-library restrictions survive the fact that a game use is permitted. Creator-world export and third-party hosting need their own basis; the engine's AGPL license does not supply one.
