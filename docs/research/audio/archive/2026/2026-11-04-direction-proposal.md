# Music and sound direction for Open Legend

**Historical November 4 proposal; superseded by the [selected November 6 direction](../../../../worlds/base/audio-direction.md) and [audio production design](../../../../projects/audio-production-feature-spec.md).** Its composer-first preference, palette and package-size example are retained as research history, not current requirements.

**Proposed creative and production direction**, informed by the [audio sourcing research](../../README.md). The goal is excellent, distinctive music at a practical scale. Music can be authored offline; sound-effect generation is optional. Vendor/budget selection and listening remain production work.

## Recommended approach

Keep one musical lead responsible for recurring themes, coherence and revisions, whether cues are composed, generated or hybrid. My default is a composer-led pilot with a serious licensed AI comparison before expanding. Use good virtual instruments and selective live performance where they improve the result. For effects, use free/paid recordings, skilled editing, a little Foley and a few original signatures.

Select by musical identity, useful revision and lasting control. Neither price nor production method establishes quality. The [industry evidence](2026-11-04-music-production.md) supplies workable small-team methods; listening must establish what we prefer for Open Legend.

The most useful compromise is **small scope with high authorship**: two or three excellent themes developed into a first package, rather than an hour of loosely related music. Spend on musical ideas, arrangement, performance and in-game listening. Additional tracks should answer a demonstrated emotional or repetition problem.

## The musical identity

The [creative brief](../../../../../art-direction/final-board/creative-brief.md) describes a beautiful, tactile living wilderness. People have motives and histories; the player makes things, finds companionship, takes risks and leaves consequences. Sunshine, humor and sincere happiness coexist with cruelty, desperation and loss. The score should make this place worth inhabiting, while leaving enough space to hear and interpret it.

Start with an intimate folk/chamber/electroacoustic palette: resonant plucked strings, an expressive bowed instrument, a breathy woodwind or reed, restrained hand percussion and subtly designed sustained textures. Those are starting colors, not a mandatory instrument list or a claim that every fictional culture shares one tradition. Choose specific instruments with the composer after hearing the pilot. Avoid a miscellaneous collection of culturally unrelated sounds used merely to signify something exotic.

The distinctive part must be the writing: a memorable melodic vocabulary, characteristic phrasing, recurring harmonic relationships, and how those ideas change. A list of fantasy instruments alone will not make the game identifiable. Ask the composer to make a theme compelling in a spare sketch before orchestrating it; the score should still have a voice when its expensive layers are muted.

Three initial thematic families are enough to investigate:

| Family                     | Emotional job                                                       | Useful transformations                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Home and belonging         | Fragile safety, shared effort, affection, the pleasure of returning | Solitary plucked version; warmer small-ensemble version; a restrained damaged/absent version when an actual known loss merits it |
| Wilderness and possibility | Beauty, curiosity, exposure and the attraction of the unknown       | Open exploration; ambiguous unease; discovery with a wider register and space                                                    |
| History and consequence    | Things outlasting the moment, wonder, regret, remembered deeds      | Quiet older tune; tense fragment; intimate aftermath; occasional larger statement                                                |

These editorial families can share musical material; they do not require a separate score for every generated person, activity, item or biome.

Humor should often come from timing and a lightly unexpected musical turn, not a joke sting announcing that a character is ridiculous. Danger needs musical teeth when it earns them, but every rabbit encounter should not summon an orchestra. Grief can be expressed through a familiar idea with something missing; it does not require continuous mournful music. Ordinary joy should be allowed to feel sincere.

## The player experience to aim for

Imagine leaving a modest camp at dawn. The player hears water, clothing and their own movement. Music enters after a little time: a brief recognizable phrase, then room for the landscape. An encounter becomes dangerous; a compact, rhythmically clear arrangement supports the known action without obscuring its signals. Afterwards, the music can finish and leave the player with breath, weather and consequences. Returning to people the player knows allows the earlier phrase to feel warmer.

This is a proposed listening scenario, not a claim about installed gameplay or a script that every outing must follow. Its purpose is to judge the same music through solitude, pressure and return. The soundtrack should support a player's experience without declaring a stranger evil, revealing an unseen enemy, announcing a secret death, or telling the player exactly how to feel.

An emergent game does not need newly generated composition for every emergent event. Authored music gains associations through use. Repetition of a good theme can become meaningful; constant unrelated novelty can prevent that familiarity. Deliberate silence also helps preserve contrast. The Minecraft, Outer Wilds and theme-led production cases in the [studio study](2026-11-04-music-production.md) inform this proposal.

## A deliberately small first music package

After a successful pilot, start around **eight minutes of genuinely new composition**, with only the additional arrangements and edits required by the playable slice. This is a scope/budget example, not a runtime quota or a maximum for the game. If the actual pilot suggests a better scope, revise it before commissioning.

| Asset family               | Initial purpose                                     | Handoff to request                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity statement         | Establish the recognizable musical idea             | A finished short cue, a spare version and a useful ending; a trailer edit only if the current marketing task needs it |
| Exploration                | Ordinary travel with sufficient space for the world | A few developed passages with clean entries/endings; seamless loops only for passages intended to loop                |
| Camp and companionship     | A recognizable human center                         | A restrained related arrangement that tolerates conversation and repeated visits                                      |
| Known tension and conflict | Support the playable threat and its consequence     | One purposeful tension/conflict family, with clean exit and limited alternates rather than a generic track per enemy  |
| Aftermath or discovery     | Let a consequential moment register                 | A short related passage or ending that earns its attention                                                            |

A phrase can serve several jobs through arrangement; the table is not five independent full-length song orders. Count alternate composition, editing and live performance honestly in the quote. A longer **12–18 minute** package becomes useful after the first slice shows what repeats too often or lacks coverage. Do not treat 30–60 minutes as a launch prerequisite.

Use instrumental music for ordinary play. Reserve a sung piece or diegetic performance for a specific, valuable later purpose. Lyrics compete with conversation, create additional authorship/performance/localization work, and need deliberate narrative handling.

The existing [bard direction](../../../../narrative/authored-worlds-and-personal-discovery.md#bards-carry-player-legends-and-world-news) is a promising future connection: a tune heard around home could later carry a story about the player's deeds. That can reuse an authored melody, an instrumental performance and an independently grounded account. This research does not require live sung generation, invent the bard's knowledge or change its unfinished feature scope.

## Where AI should fit

Use AI where it demonstrably improves the result or the production cost. The [current provider comparison](2026-11-04-ai-audio.md) owns eligibility and export limitations; tool labels and prices do not establish musical quality.

There are three valid outcomes:

1. **An AI candidate is strong and suitable enough to ship.** Retain it when listening, continuity, file delivery and the required rights all pass. A human editor may still do endings, loops, balance or a distinct live overdub.
2. **AI is useful for exploration but poor for controlled revisions.** Use the exploration to communicate an abstract brief to a composer. Let them compose independently unless the agreement explicitly covers adapting the generated material.
3. **It consumes more curation and repair time than it saves.** Stop and commission the small custom package. Cheap generation does not oblige us to use it.

For the main identity, my default remains a composer-led pilot because melody ownership, thematic transformation and repeatable revision are unusually valuable. I would change that judgment if an AI-led route produces better accepted results with adequate control. This is an investment judgment, not a measured claim that current AI sounds worse.

Do not upload commissioned demos, third-party samples or reference recordings into a provider merely because we can listen to them. The input license and provider's onward-use terms must permit it. Do not ask for a recognizable imitation of another game's melody or a performer's voice.

### A fair, bounded audition

Use one short brief: quiet exploration and a tense treatment of the same musical idea. Compare production routes when the choice remains unresolved. An AI-led or direct-artist audition can qualify on its own under the same acceptance criteria; a paid comparison is useful evidence, not a mandatory expense after a satisfactory route is found. Hide source labels during musical comparison, then assess rights and maintainability separately.

- For a composer pilot, quote one 60–90 second exploration cue, a short same-theme tension alternate, an ending/loop edit and one revision round. Select from existing portfolios; pay for custom work.
- For an AI comparison, use a few eligible candidates from at most two providers, with the same durations, instrumental restrictions, brief and scenes. Set curation time and spending allowances before generating.
- Match perceived level; listen to full cues and several repetitions, both isolated and under game effects. Include the same scene with effects/ambience alone to judge where music improves the experience and where silence works better.
- Ask Mike and a few appropriate listeners what they remember later, which world the music suggests, whether it distracts, and where it feels emotionally false. These are qualitative judgments, not a statistical study or an artificial weighted quality score.
- Use the agreed revision round to preserve the theme while changing its emotional intensity or repairing an ending. Record whether the route can make the change coherently.
- Count total generation, selection, cleanup, revision and delivery time per **accepted cue/minute**, including discarded candidates. Record cash separately from human time.

Accept both the musical result and a maintainable production path. A capture mix can support the audition; final in-play timing, transitions and fatigue remain with the [whole-scene workflow](../../../../production-workflows/audio-and-feedback.md#5-mix-and-inspect-in-the-whole-scene).

## Sound effects: use recordings as ingredients

Use curated free/commercial source recordings, then spend human effort on families of actions, tactile details and the final mix. The [sound sourcing study](2026-11-04-sound-sourcing.md) identifies exact providers, licenses, extraction restrictions and price anchors.

Start with movement over the actual supported materials; clothing/equipment; handling and placing objects; work, fire and shelter; the current wildlife/threat encounter; and restrained interface feedback. Pick packs for the holes in that list. A library of thousands of files is not evidence of a finished soundscape.

Original recording or synthesis should concentrate on memorable exposed sounds: the way a meaningful object resonates, a distinctive creature's physicality, an unusual material or a particular divine presence. Broader examples remain options until those things exist in the selected world. Editing, timing, variation and mix can make common source recordings feel specific to our game.

Offline generated SFX may help with an unusual source or fast variations. Ordinary sample selection, layering and restrained pitch/level changes can already reduce repetition. Neither route requires per-action paid AI generation or new procedural acoustics.

Ambience needs truthful use. A generic forest bed should not introduce intelligible speech, nearby chopping or an identifiable animal threat that the game has not authorized the player to hear. Separate nonspecific environmental texture from meaningful event sounds; review bird/insect detail against the selected ecology and the player's likely inference. Mechanical hearing continues to be owned by the actual world sound model.

## Keep production and playback separate

Offline composition is a production method. Choosing an authored track, allowing silence, fading at an appropriate point, or using a small set of arranged states is playback. Procedurally composing new music is a third, optional capability. The first two satisfy this recommendation.

Begin with ordinary finished cues and clean transitions. Request synchronized stems only for a selected transition or mixing need. Real composition stems, separated from the writing session, are different from estimated stems extracted from a finished mix. Do not build a generalized music engine just to exploit an export button.

The reusable engine owns playback facilities and lifecycle. The selected world supplies musical content, cue meanings and any tuning. The base-world folk/chamber direction is not a universal law of all Open Legend worlds. Existing [hearing](../../../../hearing-and-speech.md), [physical-sound policy](../../../../worlds/base/physical-sound.md) and [feedback production](../../../../production-workflows/audio-and-feedback.md) keep world facts, permitted perception and rendered sound distinct.

Specific integration expectations to carry into the existing owner: music must not expose unobserved threats or private character intentions; background score is not an in-world sound heard by NPCs; an actual musician's performance must follow its action/hearing semantics; muting playback does not change what a character mechanically perceives; important outcomes remain understandable with audio off. Browser startup, lifetime, decode/transfer costs and rights packaging are covered in the [delivery study](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md).

## What I would spend and when

The [commissioning study](2026-11-04-music-production.md#planning-budgets) shows dated survey evidence and explicit scenario arithmetic. Its **$500–1,500 pilot** and approximately **$3,500–7,500 eight-minute identity package** are planning allowances, not quotes or authorized purchases. A broader 15-minute package with selective performers and production is estimated at roughly **$9,000–18,500**. Sound design and source licenses remain separate; the [SFX study](2026-11-04-sound-sourcing.md#first-production-package-and-planning-allowances) gives its own scoped allowances.

My practical sequence is to risk the pilot amount first, then fund the lean music package and a representative soundscape if they are promising. Keep the score small if the available budget is lower. A high-quality prototype does not need an album, a live orchestra, a permanent employee or a new middleware stack.

If cash is extremely limited, an AI-led route with careful selection and modest professional finishing can be a reasonable temporary or shipping solution, subject to the same acceptance. Its true cost depends on the acceptable-output rate and Mike's time; a cheap subscription is not a researched estimate of a finished score's cost. Protect the ability to replace individual cues without changing game rules.
