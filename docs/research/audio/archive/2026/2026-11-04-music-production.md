# Original music production and commissioning evidence

Research date: 2026-11-04.

This record compares documented studio practices and practical ways to commission original music. The [Open Legend direction](../../openlegend-direction.md) owns the proposed musical identity and selected production approach; [rights and delivery](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md) owns the licensing and release checklist. The methods and budgets below are planning recommendations, with their evidence limits stated separately.

## What other developers actually do

The examples span small independent teams, established RPG studios and major publisher productions. Team-size and “AA” labels are imprecise, so the comparison focuses on documented production choices. These cases are not a representative industry sample or a guarantee of what a new commission will achieve. The transfer column contains our interpretation.

Choose a composer whose strengths and a few sounds fit the world; scale ensemble size after the musical idea works.                                                      |

### Industry-wide context and rates

GameSoundCon's 2025 survey received 654 usable responses, including 631 professionals, during May 1–June 15. Its per-finished-minute composer figures were: [S1]

| Project category in the survey | Median USD/minute | Mean USD/minute |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------: | --------------: |
| Indie                          |               407 |             618 |
| Midcore                        |             1,000 |             998 |
| AAA                            |             1,700 |           1,871 |

These are self-selected respondent data, not quotes, universal tariffs or quality guarantees. The survey's category “midcore” is not a clean synonym for “AA.” Scope, rights, revision work, musicians and implementation affect real offers. The same survey reports 8% using generative AI in some game-audio work and 15% exploring it; this is dated professional-survey evidence, not proof of current market-wide adoption. It also reports 36% of freelance indie composers retaining rights through a license. [S1]

The survey's public landing page reports an increase in audio outsourcing and says the most common AI applications were dialogue and scripting. This supports considering an external specialist without assuming AI-generated score is already the industry norm. [S1a]

## Routes to original music

| Route                                                       | Strength                                                                                                     | Cost / production issue                                                                                                                                     | When useful                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mike or a team member composes                              | Complete creative control and close game knowledge                                                           | Learning, composition, orchestration and mixing time displace game development; tools alone do not supply musical judgment                                  | Viable if the person already enjoys and can sustain music-making; weak default if the aim is mainly to save cash |
| Independent composer/producer                               | A recurring voice, personal collaboration, flexible ensemble, clear revision owner                           | Select for portfolio fit and reliable delivery; include music editing and implementation handoff explicitly                                                 | When a small team needs a coherent score and one creative counterpart                                            |
| Composer plus selective musicians                           | Distinctive phrasing and timbre over an affordable virtual-instrument foundation                             | Performer booking, arrangement preparation, recording rights, editing and mixing need explicit allocation                                                   | When exposed melodies or textures benefit from distinctive performance                                           |
| Boutique game-audio studio                                  | Composition, sound design and technical delivery can be coordinated                                          | Minimum engagements and project management overhead can exceed a tiny first-slice budget                                                                    | Good when a broader audio package and accountability save Mike substantial time                                  |
| Exclusive license to an existing independent artist's music | A distinctive sound already exists and can be auditioned before a commission                                 | Existing releases may already have licenses, co-writers, publishers or Content ID; exclusivity may be narrow; stems may not exist                           | Strong alternative if an exceptional artist is discovered; secure game-specific rights and adaptations           |
| Non-exclusive existing artist catalog                       | High musical quality is possible without commissioning every cue                                             | Less identity ownership, less control of variations, possible familiarity from other media                                                                  | Occasional diegetic or special-use music when the fit and permissions are strong                                 |
| In-house composer/audio director                            | Deep ongoing collaboration, fast iteration, broad consistency                                                | A long-term staffing commitment is hard to justify for a small initial score                                                                                | Reconsider when continuous content work supports the role                                                        |
| AI-first finished score                                     | Fast exploration, low marginal generation cost, many candidates                                              | Acceptance rate, editability, coherent motifs, licensing and final production effort must be demonstrated                                                   | Run a bounded comparison; do not choose solely on subscription price                                             |
| AI concept + human composer/arranger                        | Helps Mike communicate mood and compare directions; human can deliver coherent structure and editable assets | The composer must agree to the workflow. Copying an AI melody is not automatically a clean independent work; rights and substantive authorship still matter | Useful if it improves communication; a written brief and reference listening may already be faster               |

Dynamedion is a concrete example of an external provider offering composition, orchestral/soloist work and audio integration, with an indie program and virtual-orchestra mockup services. These are verified offerings, not a recommendation that it will be the cheapest or available vendor. Obtain a scoped quote. G.A.N.G.'s member directory and the credits/official portfolios of smaller games are useful discovery routes. A portfolio listing is a lead, not a reference check. [S10, S11]

## A practical hiring process

### 1. Write a compact brief before searching

Provide a playable slice or short capture, the world description, expected player feelings, and three scenes: solitary travel, safety with other people, and danger with consequences. Include practical constraints: browser delivery, intelligible sound effects, sparse music, modest first batch, and the existing event/perception contracts.

Use references to explain a property: “the intimacy of a small ensemble,” “space between phrases,” “an identifiable home melody,” or “tension without an unbroken percussion wall.” Ask for the composer's own response. Do not ask them to duplicate a living artist or recreate a copyrighted game's tune.

Clarify what is still exploratory. In particular, a score reference is not automatically a request for full live orchestral recording.

### 2. Shortlist from existing work

Review about five portfolios, then speak with two or three plausible candidates when outreach is authorized. Ask for one example of quiet exploration, one of a recurring motif transformed, and one of delivered game audio. Favor a small number of deeply suitable examples over a cinematic trailer showreel alone.

Ask who wrote, performed, mixed and implemented the samples; what they can do directly; what they subcontract; and what their current schedule allows. Establish their AI/sample practices openly. Request an estimate broken into musical writing/production, live players, revisions and game delivery.

### 3. Pay one composer for a bounded pilot

When selecting a composer, quote the [shared audition package](../../openlegend-direction.md#a-fair-bounded-audition): one 60–90 second exploration cue, a short same-theme tension alternate, an ending/loop edit and one revision round. Set an explicit pilot license and whether the fee is credited toward a larger commission. Pay for custom work after portfolio selection.

The shared procedure owns comparisons, meaningful revision, repeated listening and accepted-cue cost. A paid composer comparison is optional when an AI-led or direct-artist route already satisfies that brief; the recommendation does not require buying redundant evidence.

### 4. Commission a small reusable first package

Scope the first package around the playable scenes and related themes in the [direction brief](../../openlegend-direction.md). The 8-, 15- and 35-minute budget scenarios below illustrate different project sizes; they do not select the amount to commission. Count new composition honestly: exporting three stems is not three new compositions, but additional arrangements and production still require time.

Request music that can end cleanly and allow silence. Most cues should ship as ordinary finished tracks or loopable segments. Add a calm/intense alternate only where the existing presentation system will actually use it. Do not commission dozens of hypothetical cultures or biomes before the first playable world establishes what works.

### 5. Evaluate in play, revise, then expand

Review with footsteps, weather, speech and action sounds present. Listen on modest speakers and headphones. Include long exploration, repeated danger, quiet home activity and returning to a familiar place after a consequential event. Check hearing fatigue, masking and whether an emotional cue feels earned. These are proposed acceptance sessions, not tests performed in this research.

Commission further music after player observation identifies a concrete gap. Maintain one creative lead across additional composers or performers.

## Planning budgets

These are **proposed planning allocations in USD**, not offers, fixed market prices or spending authorization. Per-minute assumptions use the survey as context; production allowances below are budget choices that must be quoted. No individual musician or studio has agreed to them.

| Package                                 | Illustrative arithmetic                                                                                                  |       Exact calculated range | Rounded planning allowance |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------: | -------------------------: |
| One paid pilot                          | One 60–90 second cue, short same-theme alternate, one revision and ending/loop edit; flat fee to negotiate               | Not a per-minute calculation |             **$500–1,500** |
| Lean identity package                   | (8 new minutes × $300–600 + $600–1,600 production/editorial allowance) × 1.15                                            |                 $3,450–7,360 |     **About $3,500–7,500** |
| Substantial first slice                 | (15 new minutes × $400–800 + $1,500–4,000 for selected live performance, editorial/implementation handoff and QA) × 1.15 |                $8,625–18,400 |    **About $9,000–18,500** |
| Broader polished score after validation | (35 new minutes × $600–1,200 + $5,000–10,000 production/integration allowance) × 1.15                                    |               $29,900–59,800 |   **About $30,000–60,000** |

The multiplier adds 15% contingency to the combined composition and production amount; rounded allowances are rounded upward. The pilot is a separate preliminary scope unless the commission credits its fee.

The first package can be smaller still: two excellent themes with several useful arrangements. Scope reduction preserves quality better than demanding an hour of music at a token fee. Live orchestras, famous composers, travel, large revision cycles, outside legal review, taxes and unusual rights can push costs beyond these examples. Put fixed limits on revisions and agree what a changed brief costs.

Composition price is not total audio cost. Budget sound design, licensed source libraries, implementation and acceptance separately. Do not buy both a large library subscription and many overlapping packs before the cue list exposes the actual gaps.

Musicians' Union guidance explicitly asks commissioners and composers to settle responsibility for parts, contracted performers, studios and rehearsals; its media guidance says price depends strongly on the rights package. Its current recording page notes Scale 4 Combined Use applies to computer-game recording. These are useful contracting references, not a universal US or global price schedule. [S12]

## Contract and production handoff

Use the [commissioning checklist](2026-11-04-rights-and-delivery.md#commissioning-control-without-a-needlessly-hostile-deal) to settle composition/master rights, contributor permissions, permitted game and marketing uses, soundtrack releases, exclusivity, payments and creator-video claims. Agree that scope before recording or accepting a final delivery. The production handoff below concerns the usable files and metadata needed to carry out that agreement.

### Minimum useful production handoff

- Final uncompressed masters and agreed distribution renders; agree sample rate/bit depth with the existing asset pipeline before production, with 48 kHz/24-bit WAV a proposed interchange baseline rather than a new engine mandate.
- Musical start/end and loop points, reverb tails, tempo/time signature where needed, and explicit filenames/cue IDs.
- Only the stems or alternate mixes actually requested. Stems should be synchronized exports from the production session when available; machine-separated audio is a different deliverable and may contain bleed/artifacts.
- Dry/clean exposed instruments if future arrangements need them and the agreement permits reuse; agreed MIDI/notation and session files or consolidated audio if continuity requires them. A DAW project can depend on plugins and sample licenses that do not transfer, so require usable renders as well.
- Short audition mixes for scene review and a final reference mix, so implementation can be compared against the intended balance.
- A cue manifest with intended uses, asset locations, authors/contributors, commercial rights, modifications permitted, restrictions, source provenance and licensing evidence. Reuse the game's existing asset ownership system rather than introduce a parallel catalog.

## Evidence limits

The case studies document what their creators say they did. They do not establish that the same method guarantees comparable emotional impact. The evidence comes from public pages and interview accounts; production sessions and source stems were not inspected. Survey rates are dated observational evidence. Provider services and rights must be reconfirmed against the actual quote and contract. The budget allocations and hiring method are recommendations to evaluate, not facts learned from a survey. No comparative audio listening, commissioned pilot or vendor quote is included in this evidence.
