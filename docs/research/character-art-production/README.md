# Character-art production: evidence and OpenLegend implications

**Initial research: October 31, 2026. Focused verification: November 5, 2026.** This is a source-based production study, not a generated-asset benchmark. No generator, rigging application, paid account, game runtime or artist trial was exercised. Additional external spending: $0. The [character-production feature specification](../../projects/character-art-production-feature-spec.md) and [execution guide](../../projects/character-art-production-tech-design.md) own the offline decisions; the existing [3D feature/technical design](../../projects/3d-pixel-art-feature-spec.md) retains runtime ownership. This record owns evidence and its limitations. The [repertoire application review](repertoire-review.md) maps these decisions to all 28 catalogue areas and four proposed world families at its stated production-review depth.

## What the supplied video actually supports

**S01 — [Mike's supplied character-production video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VeOU0FnTjjc).** The evidence inspected is the full transcript supplied in this conversation, not an independent frame-by-frame inspection or recovered production files. The transcript describes a rapid whole-character generation, an approximately one-hour assembled/rigged result, and a longer polished result described as roughly six hours. Its later account of 5.5 hours before another hour in Unreal is approximate and is not a reliable six-hour production estimate.

The creator generates head, body and accessories separately, varies mesh settings, assembles and repairs them in Blender, prepares UVs and materials, improves the eyes, uses automatic rigging as a starting point, attaches rigid accessories and adds selected secondary motion. He chooses direct generated geometry rather than rebuilding a detailed sculpt because that route worked better for this example. He still describes high-detail baking as useful where preserving sculpted detail warrants it. He reports manual geometry, texture, weighting, material and engine work, not a one-click finished character.

The transcript includes a Tripo referral offer and promotion of the creator's course. Those commercial interests do not invalidate the demonstrated method, but the result is a selected practitioner example rather than an independent comparison. The supplied stylized character and Unreal workflow do not establish OpenLegend's grounded proportions, pixel presentation, interchangeable equipment or browser performance. No specific model price, license entitlement, API capability or automatic success rate is inferred from the video.

**The valuable distinction:** separate parts can improve production control without remaining detachable parts in the game. Replacing an unsuccessful head while retaining a good body is different from implementing removable helmets or armor. The former is an artist's technique; the latter requires the existing body/equipment compatibility and custody contracts.

## Primary professional practice

The sources below describe practices worth adapting, not a ranking of the world's best artists or proof that one studio's entire pipeline is appropriate here. Source dates describe publication, not a claim that the tools remain current. The initial review recorded the sources below; the November 5 pass rechecked S02, S03, S05–S12 and S14, added S15, and retained the explicit access limits for S04 and S13.

These sources converge on a useful production principle: the finished game experience determines what art must do, and early editable work makes correction less wasteful. They do not establish that AI generation, physical simulation, maximal realism or maximal modularity is always preferable.

## Decisions this evidence informs

1. **Protect successful work.** Use local modeling, painting or part generation when the defect is local. Keep whole-object regeneration available for fundamentally wrong construction, not as the default answer to a weak eye or sleeve.
2. **Separate by a concrete need.** Authoring control, deformation and runtime equipment are independent reasons for a boundary. Choose the smallest useful separation and plan the final assembly. More parts can mean more fitting, seams, material submissions and difficult revisions.
3. **Choose the shortest route that passes the actual criteria.** Refine a usable direct mesh. Rebuild topology when deformation or editable structure fails. Use high-detail baking when retained detail has visible value. Do not require every source to visit every traditional stage.
4. **Test before polishing dependent work.** A rough fitted body, grip and action in the real viewing conditions can reveal problems before final textures or a large animation library. A prototype and a polished package share correctness requirements but need not share optional finishing. Game integration and player appeal need their own evidence.
5. **Spend detail on recognition and readable use.** Broad form, faces, important clothing/material divisions, useful equipment and acting deserve attention at their actual display size. Extra source resolution alone cannot guarantee readable or stable pixels.
6. **Treat secondary motion as a chosen presentation technique.** Static/settled forms, authored bone motion, baked movement and runtime simulation are alternatives. The actual consumer decides what is usable; Unreal cloth settings are not exported OpenLegend behavior.
7. **Qualify useful revision, not just initial generation.** A second needed outfit, compatible body or equipment change should retain identity and working motion with recorded correction effort. Keep a one-off asset when a reusable family would cost more than it serves.

These are OpenLegend production judgments supported by the sources and existing project goals. They are not quotations, new world laws or measured improvements. They do not reorder the [first-playable priorities](../../repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md).

## Claims deliberately not carried forward

- One impressive character does not prove economical large populations, interchangeable clothing, arbitrary creatures or crowd performance. Earlier conversational optimism about a broad roster is a hypothesis, not a result.
- The creator's elapsed times do not include a repeatable OpenLegend acceptance study; they are not task estimates, service promises or maximum artist time.
- A quad mesh is not automatically a good deforming mesh; a low polygon count is not automatically the rejected low-poly aesthetic. Conversely, pixel rendering does not repair bad anatomy.
- Full PBR maps, separate spherical eyes, cloth simulation, autorigging, a high-detail sculpt and three competing generators are not mandatory stages. The video's per-part polygon example is not a project limit.
- A production tutorial supplies no new character motives, cognition, history, equipment rights, damage or animation-driven game rules.
- No vendor/artist is declared universally best. The comparison concerns useful practices under different constraints, not fame or a promotional gallery.
