# Authoring a history players can discover in different orders

**Authoring method and hypothetical example.** This guide develops the [many-entrances direction](authored-worlds-and-personal-discovery.md#author-a-history-let-people-discover-and-change-the-world). It selects no world history, runtime mechanism or required storyline. Use it within the [characters and play workflow](../production-workflows/characters-and-play.md), which owns rehearsal, supported content and evaluation.

The intended pleasure is gradually understanding a place through a life there. A discovery can offer immediate usefulness, beauty, company or a changed interpretation before the player cares about the larger mystery. Learning more should create possibilities for action and affection as well as explain the past.

## Prepare a small, connected piece of history

Start with one event that still matters to somebody. Record enough to make its traces coherent, then develop what a player can encounter:

| Authoring question               | Useful answer                                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What happened?                   | A concise established past, including what remains genuinely unknown.              |
| Why does it matter now?          | A present concern, useful place, valued object or relationship.                    |
| What survives?                   | Physical traces, records, practices and people with separately authored knowledge. |
| What does each source establish? | The observation or account available, its limits and possible interpretations.     |
| What is worthwhile immediately?  | Something useful, moving, amusing or intriguing without collecting every clue.     |
| What could the player change?    | A supported present opportunity and its understandable aftermath.                  |

These are content-development questions, not a second history database or mandatory clue schema. [World creation](../projects/world-creation-feature-spec.md#52-relations-and-knowledge) owns authored historical setup and initial beliefs. [Knowledge](../knowledge.md) owns learning; [personal experience](../memory-architecture.md#personal-perspective-and-acquisition) preserves how somebody learned something. [Narration](../narration-and-conversations.md#2-three-kinds-of-truth) presents permitted facts without creating the discovery.

## Worked example: the flooded lower district

This expands the earlier hypothetical flood example. Decades ago, a settlement flooded its lower district to contain a dangerous growth. The diversion protected the upper town, but evacuation warnings reached households unevenly. Some families lost possessions and homes. A commemorative song celebrates the diversion; different residents remember different costs.

That is authored setup. The author can settle those facts while leaving some motives or details unknown. Neither the song nor a resident's conviction automatically establishes every claim they make.

Today, an old covered passage could provide shelter and a useful route. A local repairer values the district's surviving craftsmanship and wants usable working space. They also enjoy making absurdly elaborate handles for ordinary tools. Their present interests give the player reasons to know them beyond extracting testimony; their history need not dictate a confession, favor or joke.

### Several encounters, each worth having

| Possible first encounter              | What is available to discover                                                                                                     | Immediate value and an optional next question                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A waterline beneath a sheltered stair | Old flood marks and a dry sleeping shelf show how people used the structure.                                                      | A useful resting place and an interesting construction detail. Why does the waterline stop at the sealed passage? |
| The commemorative song                | Its words attribute the town's survival to the diversion and omit the evacuation's unevenness.                                    | An enjoyable performance and an account worth discussing. Who is absent from its version?                         |
| A damaged maintenance record          | An order identifies the deliberately opened waterway; a missing section leaves the warning arrangements unclear.                  | A concrete connection between two familiar places. What could establish how the warning travelled?                |
| Time with the repairer                | Their own childhood recollection covers one household's evacuation; their craft knowledge concerns the building's surviving work. | Company, practical insight and appreciation of a distinctive person. What else survived nearby?                   |

The opportunities remain candidates until their objects, access and actions are supported. A record describing a waterway does not automatically grant a repair method. A conversation may be refused. Another source can sustain a useful question without supplying an identical speech elsewhere.

Humor can arise from the repairer's fondness for magnificent handles on humble tools. Quiet pleasure can come from shelter, old workmanship or listening to a song. Neither requires the player to solve the historical dispute, and the person remains interesting when no joke occurs.

### Three possible discovery sequences

An **explorer** finds the sheltered stair first, later examines the maintenance record, and eventually recognizes the waterway in a song. The first visit already offered rest; subsequent knowledge changes the meaning of familiar architecture.

A **neighbor** gets to know the repairer, hears the song afterward, and visits the old district together if both choose to. The difference between a personal recollection and a public celebration makes the relationship and place more particular. They may stop with a worthwhile afternoon.

A **historian** hears the song first, seeks the maintenance record, then investigates the physical trace. Evidence refines the account without requiring the historian to reenact another player's introduction or pretend ignorance of what they already know.

These are plausible orders for rehearsal, not prescribed routes or character choices. Sources can overlap enough to orient someone while contributing different detail. Understanding need not converge on a compulsory moral verdict.

## Let discovery change present play

A candidate undertaking is to make the covered passage usable again. Understanding its history could help participants recognize an approach, discuss which remnants to preserve, or decide the work is not for them. The selected world's actual capabilities, materials, permissions and costs determine what they can accomplish. A clever proposal and an evocative description do not complete the work.

If players succeed, later visitors encounter the usable passage and its actual remaining traces. They can appreciate the new shelter, investigate the older history or hear about the recent work. A failed attempt leaves its actual consequences. Nobody must repeat a contradictory version in which the passage is still untouched merely to receive the same authored experience.

A witness might tell a bard about the repair. The bard can choose to retell it, combine it with the old song, misunderstand part of it or pursue another concern. If a performance occurs, it can give participants recognition, inform travelers about the route and invite another listener's curiosity. Keep the deed, testimony and performance distinct under the [bard direction](authored-worlds-and-personal-discovery.md#bards-carry-player-legends-and-world-news).

## Use the existing production route

During a [cheap rehearsal](../production-workflows/characters-and-play.md#3-rehearse-the-uncertain-premise-cheaply), compare the three entry orders, a refused conversation and arrival after somebody else has completed the work. Ask what remains enjoyable without full understanding, what each source adds, and which present choice becomes more meaningful. Preserve unexpected attractions as well as repairing confusing dependencies.

Then use the existing workflow to identify supported content and any concrete missing capability. Keep the chosen facts, individual accounts, actual opportunities and useful aftermath in their existing world/project owners. The [play and revision guidance](../production-workflows/characters-and-play.md#7-play-judge-and-revise-the-right-part) owns assessing the resulting experience; this example is not evidence that the game already delivers it.
