# Curse of Resthaven: making a return carry story

Reference study — October 22, 2026. Mike selected this game for its connection between failure, divine intervention and a changed next attempt. The Open Legend ideas it inspired are recorded in [Gods, death and personal mythology](../gods-death-and-personal-mythology.md); this page records the reference evidence and the distinction between the two games.

## Material inspected

The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3796150/Curse_of_Resthaven/) currently lists two videos: **Developer Game Overview**, Steam movie **257458454**, approximately **87.91 seconds**; and **CoR Demo Trailer**, movie **257347708**, approximately **91.85 seconds**. Both were inspected through one frame per second across their complete runtimes, with a full-resolution close-up of the overview's boon selection at 01:00. The review establishes visible sequences and readable text, not audio performance, hands-on play or the finished game's quality. Timestamps below are approximate.

The boon examples concern several kinds of play. **Architect** improves construction while increasing companion hiring costs; **Relic Hunter** provides starting clue resources while slowing questioning. Divine progression here reaches economic and investigative activity, not only fighting. Earlier footage includes trading, town businesses, conversations and card-based expeditions.

## What the official description supports

The Steam description says the island's guardian returns the protagonist to arrival after a cataclysm, preserving memories and experience. Permanent skill upgrades and temporary goddess boons carry advantages into later loops; the boons also impose consequences. [Hilltop's website](https://www.hilltop.so/) independently describes this return structure. Their precise day-count wording differs, so this study does not select one count.

This is a specific time-loop premise. It does not establish multiple quarrelling gods, unscripted divine cognition or a random reward after every ordinary death. Those are Mike's proposed extensions for Open Legend.

## The useful adaptation

For Open Legend, a return could create anticipation through a god's reaction, a changed relationship, new information or an unusual gift alongside recovery consequences. Gods can recognize exploration, care, promises and creativity as well as combat. Their interventions should reveal authored personalities. The proposed personal Beyond offers somewhere for these encounters while the shared physical world continues independently; it need not reproduce Resthaven's island reset.

The **Hades** comparison is strongest around continuity. [Supergiant's FAQ](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-faq/) connects successive attempts with discovering more story. Its [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1145360/Hades/) describes recurring characters who remember the protagonist and distinguishes god-given boons, relationships and permanent progression. Neither supports treating an ordinary death as an automatic random-boon award. Optional God Mode's increased resilience after deaths is a separate feature.

These games are references for original Open Legend material; their art, characters and mythology are not licensed production assets.
