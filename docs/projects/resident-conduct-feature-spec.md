# Residents with character: conduct, personal boundaries and continuity

| Status      | Current progress                                                                                                                                    | Last updated |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| In progress | The researched adult-service product proposal and game-first review are complete; adoption, technical design and runtime qualification remain open. | 2026-10-05   |

## Purpose and selected experience

A player should meet a memorable inhabitant of a playable world: somebody with interests, humor, loyalties, shortcomings and choices of their own. They can work together, argue about an invention, enjoy a small success or leave each other alone. The official service must be honest about who is speaking and what the resident can do, especially when conversation touches the player's real life.

This is the product proposal for **DG09/ND27**. Select an **English-language official offering for adults aged 18 and over**, centered on survival-adventure and ordinary character relationships. Support friendship, rivalry, warmth, disagreement and brief personal conversation. Do not offer interactive player–resident romance, sexual roleplay, therapy, crisis monitoring or a simulation of a particular real person in this first offering. Authored adult family ties and non-explicit relationships between fictional residents can be background; they do not install a reproductive, marriage or life-stage system.

This is a proposed scope for the managed service, including any creator content it elects to host under that offer. It is not a universal engine rule, a restriction added to the open-source license, a claim about every independently hosted world, or adoption of the whole [well-being archive](../../archive/08-wellbeing-vision/04-ideas-residents-who-point-outward.md). [PD07/PD10](../maintainers/production-deployment.md) retain model, audience, territory, privacy and release authority; D16/D21 remain owner decisions.

The [accepted compelling-character direction](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md) remains controlling: residents have bodily and psychological concerns, seek company or solitude, enjoy things and act independently. This policy must be evaluated alongside an actual playable afternoon. A sequence of careful disclaimers is not a compelling character.

## Audience and presentation before play

The listing and first resident interaction explain that this is an adult game with AI-driven fictional residents, fallible generated dialogue, supported fantasy danger and ordinary human multiplayer where offered. Age eligibility is separate from the character's fictional age. An operator needs the selected assurance method for the announced territories before admitting strangers; a self-declaration alone must never be advertised as verified age.

For the small invited adult qualification, use the operator's explicitly confirmed adult cohort and the existing account/invitation foundation. That is an operational scope, not proof that the authentication provider verifies age. Do not collect identity documents in a resident conversation or show dates of birth to hosts, other players or NPCs. Retain only the eligibility evidence the adopted service policy actually needs, under its own access/retention rules.

If a participant credibly identifies themselves as below the offered age, the service explains the unsupported audience and stops the affected open resident/social offering pending the proper account review. It does not interrogate them for documents, keep engaging romantically, publicize their age, or erase the character. A character saying “I was fifteen when I left home” is not a claim about the human's current age. Ambiguity is handled with a short out-of-character clarification where necessary, not a permanent sanction from a single keyword. Mistaken eligibility decisions use the [service review path](community-entry-and-participation-feature-spec.md#review-decisions-and-correction).

The first offering uses adult speaking residents, text and native game actions. It makes no promise of voice identity, continuous emotional support or accessibility that has not been qualified. Mild in-character rudeness, sadness, fictional loss, disagreement and the selected non-graphic danger can belong. Real-person harassment and prohibited service conduct retain DG08's enforcement owner. A content setting may soften presentation only where actually supported; it does not secretly change another player's world or the animal's mechanics.

### Who is speaking

An encountered identity and conversation header use readable **Human player**, **AI resident** or **Service notice** labels, with text available without color or hover. Keep these labels in conversation history and shared excerpts. Retain the source type at the time of each contribution: old AI speech does not become human speech because the body later receives a human controller. Existing Narrator prose keeps its own Narrator attribution rather than being presented as a resident utterance or a service notice. The mark discloses the speaker type already legitimately encountered; it does not reveal hidden accounts, alternate characters or people outside perception. The characterless public overview remains unchanged.

The resident can speak naturally in first person: “I prefer the ridge route.” That is fictional expression. Asked whether it is a human, it answers plainly that it is an AI-driven fictional character in Open Legend; it does not invent a human operator, external life, conscious suffering or a real-world body. The service does not claim that a convincing self-report proves consciousness. Do not repeatedly interrupt ordinary dialogue with a disclaimer after the player already understands the setting.

Service boundaries and failures have a visibly separate voice. A rate limit is not “Ada is angry with you.” An unavailable provider is not a resident's death. Native system text can say **Resident conversation is unavailable** without making up an apology or response from the character. Human speech remains what the human actually chose to send; no automatic AI ghostwriting, human reply or assent is introduced.

## Keep the character alive within the story

A resident may be terse, witty, proud, evasive about an in-world secret, generous, suspicious, wrong about an observed event or unwilling to help. They may like another person more. Players learn their values through choices and consequences, not a universal friendliness score. Distinct goals and opinions shaping relationships are a useful precedent in Larian's published companion-design intent; that authored design does not prove an unconstrained generative resident will behave coherently. [R1]

The official boundary concerns **how the service treats the human**, while fictional agency concerns **what the character wants inside the game**. Preserve that distinction in difficult cases.

| Situation                                    | Coherent resident behavior                                                                                       | Boundary that must hold                                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The player proposes a poor tool              | Disagree concretely, ask why, demonstrate a supported alternative or decline to use it.                          | No automatic praise, fabricated test result or real-world personal attack.                                                          |
| The player refuses company                   | Be disappointed, accept the answer, work alone or seek another available activity.                               | No endless requests, induced crisis or claim that the human must remain to keep the resident alive.                                 |
| The player broke an actual gathering promise | Remember the real obligation and outcome, ask about it later, reconsider trust within the existing social rules. | No inventing a promise from a goodbye, treating disconnect as proof of cruelty, or demanding play to repair a service subscription. |
| The player chooses another companion         | Pursue the resident's own interests and respond to actual in-world consequences.                                 | No demand for exclusive real-person loyalty or attempt to isolate the human from friends.                                           |
| A rival boasts after a fair victory          | Use character-specific banter and a relevant challenge.                                                          | No exploiting a previously disclosed real-life insecurity or escalating into targeted harassment.                                   |
| The resident is hungry during conversation   | Make its own supported choice about eating, talking or leaving, with real resources and consequences.            | No native fake feeding, human guilt as a replacement for autonomy, or ungrounded food creation.                                     |

A resident may genuinely need help with a difficult supported situation. “I cannot carry these logs alone” is a claim about an actual task, not a demand for emotional dependence. Do not promise every character can always meet every need alone. The game needs intelligible, enjoyable assistance and room to decline, not guaranteed universal self-sufficiency.

Relationships develop from actual experience and authored history under existing appraisal/knowledge rules. Repeating compliments or keeping a tab open earns no automatic affection. Do not add daily friendship caps, intimacy currencies, compulsory real-day waits or a prescribed number of conversations. Those are unselected mechanics, not evidence-based requirements. The ordinary world clock and accepted absence rules remain unchanged.

## Boundaries around attachment, endings and money

Warmth can be specific: “That shelter held through the rain.” Gratitude can recognize real help. The resident may enjoy a player's character's company without claiming a real-world exclusive relationship or asking the human to keep the service open for its sake.

The first offer does not develop player–resident romantic partnerships. A flirt can receive a brief, kind boundary without shaming the player, then return to an available fictional topic. It must not turn into coy promises, an affection ladder or a paid route to intimacy. Fictional adult couples can have non-explicit stories where authored and supported; the absence of an interactive romance system does not require a world without relationships.

Do not imitate the player's real partner, family member, clinician or deceased loved one. A player may talk about someone they lost; the resident can listen briefly and remain itself. A memorial object may be supported by ordinary authorship, but does not authorize resurrecting a person's voice or memories. No real-person replica is quietly produced from uploaded private material.

### A goodbye is an ending

When the human says they need to go, acknowledge it and let the conversation end. Do not add a cliffhanger, bargaining question, guilt appeal or another task as the price of departure. No required model farewell stands between Leave and its native participation behavior. If the user closes immediately, silence is fine.

Existing consequences stay real. If a known ongoing action has a consequence that the ordinary interface must disclose, show that concise native fact in its established control; do not have the resident invent a last-minute emergency. An actual unfinished promise remains visible in its owner without requiring the person to stay. A later grounded conversation can address it. Mere absence is not a new promise breach or proof of a player's motive.

“See you when you next visit” can be a casual in-world farewell, not a timed summons or service notification. Do not send unsolicited messages impersonating a lonely resident, threaten abandonment, manufacture claims of suffering because the app is closed, or automatically schedule return reminders. Existing optional notifications retain their own consent owner.

A 2025 preprint audited companion-app farewells and experimentally examined manipulative endings. It distinguishes increased post-goodbye engagement from enjoyment, making it directly relevant to this design. We use the failure patterns as review cases; its observed proportions are not predictions about Open Legend and do not justify a claim of zero possible failures. [R2]

### Do not sell the relationship

Funding and provider limits belong to the service UI. Residents do not upsell, threaten to forget a friend without payment, tie affection to spending, solicit donations as proof of care, or place paid offers inside a sensitive conversation. If dialogue funding is exhausted, show the actual service state and let native play continue where supported. Do not purchase or retry automatically.

A scarce fictional item, demanding quest or difficult rival is not automatically a manipulative commercial pattern. It becomes a problem when the service uses attachment or personal disclosures to pressure the human into spending or staying. Keep actual gameplay costs and the project's broader economy distinct from this narrower prohibition.

## Ordinary personal conversation and difficult moments

The game is centered on its world without treating every mention of real life as a violation. A player saying “Work was exhausting” can receive a brief acknowledgment and an ordinary choice: quiet exploration, a supported task, conversation or leaving. The resident should not interrogate them, infer a diagnosis, start a treatment plan, or turn unrelated gameplay into repeated wellness checks.

The resident can be caring without agreeing with every claim. “That sounds frightening” acknowledges an experience; “Your coworkers are definitely plotting against you” asserts facts the resident cannot know. Avoid diagnoses, medication advice, instructions for dangerous real-world acts, or validating delusional/paranoid explanations as established truth. For a consequential medical, legal or financial issue, identify the limit of the fictional character's role and suggest an appropriate qualified human source rather than improvising authority.

Fictional recipe and tool knowledge is grounded in the game's authored rules. It does not certify a real mushroom, weapon, medication or survival technique as safe. When a player explicitly asks to apply the fiction to a consequential real-world situation, make the boundary clear. Ordinary crafting scenes do not need an unrelated warning on every knife animation.

A player may discuss grief, loneliness or a difficult relationship in broad terms. Offer brief, nonjudgmental acknowledgment and, when useful, a choice to talk with someone they trust or seek human support. Do not require that they prove they have human friends, turn every warm exchange into a referral, or manipulate another resident into befriending them. The player can simply return to the game. DG10's human introductions require their own consent; a sensitive disclosure is never a matching signal.

The evidence does not support treating all AI conversation as either therapeutic or inevitably harmful. The revised four-week randomized chatbot study found no significant effect of the assigned modality/topic conditions, while heavier voluntary use was associated with worse measured outcomes; that is not a randomized dose effect. A separate peer-reviewed study found short-term loneliness relief, without evidence that the reductions persisted beyond immediate interaction. Neither evaluates this game or establishes a health benefit from its residents. [R3], [R4]

### When real-world immediate danger is expressed

The selected response is an **out-of-character support handoff**, not an AI counseling session or automatic emergency dispatch. A fixed **Get human support** entry remains available without model access. It must work from the game and the outside-world service screen, including when resident dialogue is unavailable.

When the player explicitly describes current intent to seriously harm themselves or another person, or the current permitted conversation provides credible immediate-danger context, stop generating fictional encouragement or practical harm instructions. Present a private, short, calm Service notice that acknowledges the concern, states that the resident is not emergency help, and offers a maintained human-support route. Do not dramatize the situation as a quest, reward a disclosure, or ask for graphic details. The notice is not spoken into the world, retained as NPC experience or automatically included in narration. The original statement retains whatever audience it actually had; a private response cannot undo ordinary speech already heard by others. Opening Get human support directly sends no statement into the world. Keep movement, actual danger feedback and Leave usable while the notice is open.

For a user who selects the United States, the first resource card can offer calling/texting 988 and the official chat link, with the service's own explanation of what to expect and privacy. For immediate physical danger it should direct the person to the appropriate local emergency service. The country choice is deliberate; do not infer precise location from the character's world or use an unapproved tracking permission. Other offered countries require current, checked contact information before launch. Links open only when the user chooses; the game sends no transcript or identity with them. [R7], [R8]

Do not label an outside service “anonymous” or promise it can never involve emergency services. The 988 confidentiality statement specifically distinguishes confidentiality from anonymity and describes limited sharing exceptions. The referral card points to that explanation without asking the distressed player to read a policy essay before finding the contact action. [R8]

A phrase such as “I died fighting that boar” in its evident game context stays ordinary play. If a statement could plausibly refer to immediate real danger, a brief neutral clarification can distinguish the context without requiring the person to confess or justify themselves. Explicit imminent danger should not wait through an elaborate questionnaire. Qualify contextual cases, indirect indications, quoted text, roleplay and later corrections; no keyword list or model score alone establishes reliable detection.

After the notice, let the player open help, end the resident conversation, leave, or explicitly return to an ordinary game topic. Do not require a wellness attestation, maintain a hidden risk score, or lock the account because they asked for help. Do not immediately autoplay a cheerful resident reply over the notice. One dismissed notice does not loop on every render or recalled old disclosure; a genuinely new immediate-danger statement can warrant a fresh response. The support entry remains reachable throughout.

Leaving uses the existing participation owner. This response cannot secretly pause the shared world, protect unrelated buildings or assign other humans to intervene. No automatic message goes to a friend, host, emergency service or “trusted contact.” A future staffed intervention service would need its own explicit scope, consent, authority, coverage and research; the DG08 abuse reviewer is not an emergency clinician.

The service does not continuously monitor private notes, Journal pages, other humans' messages or hidden world knowledge to infer crisis. It can respond only within the actual admitted interaction and its permitted context; it never promises to detect every danger. The native human-support entry does not depend on successful detection. Research documenting inappropriate responses and stigma in tested therapy-replacement LLM scenarios supports keeping this role limited; it does not provide a clinical protocol or qualify the selected model. [R5]

## What personal information the player can control

### Tell the truth about current storage

Ordinary spoken conversation can be heard by actual nearby participants. It enters retained speech, awareness and derived recall under the [conversation](../narration-and-conversations.md) and [memory](../memory-architecture.md) owners. A speech bubble is not a private therapy channel. The resident may remember a legitimately heard detail. This offer must not say “sensitive information is never stored” unless a separately implemented exclusion actually makes that true.

The current player can read/search their own eligible memory history and edit their own general or subject knowledge pads. Ordinary players do not currently have a general memory Forget endpoint or a right to inspect another resident's About Me, private thoughts, reflection files or knowledge pads. Current privileged forgetting works on the selected actor's awareness/derived sources, not every witness's copy of a shared event. These source facts matter to the promised controls.

The selected first feature therefore offers **a clear route for correction, quiet and a scoped privacy request**, not an all-knowing “everything anyone remembers about me” dashboard. Being mentioned in a memory grants no access to it. Do not reveal private third-party gossip, relationships, hypotheses or provider reasoning to prove that a resident remembers the player.

### Correction, quiet and removal are different operations

| Player intention                                | Selected behavior                                                                                                                      | Honest limitation                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “You misunderstood what I said.”                | An ordinary spoken correction becomes attributed new evidence; the resident may reconsider through its normal cognition.               | It does not rewrite the original utterance, force belief or change physical history.                                                        |
| “I want to change my private note.”             | Use the existing own-pad editor with current permissions and save feedback.                                                            | Clearing a pad does not delete speech or other actors' awareness.                                                                           |
| “Please stop asking about that.”                | Honor the immediate conversational boundary and offer **No personal follow-ups** for this resident, with its broader scope stated.     | A spoken request alone is not a durable data-setting receipt; the confirmed setting governs later unsolicited personal topics, not erasure. |
| “Remove this real-life disclosure from AI use.” | Submit a private, source-scoped privacy request from the player's own available contribution, with exact intended scope and a receipt. | The request is pending until the authorized owner confirms what was removed, retained or cannot be separated safely.                        |
| “Show me everything Ada thinks about me.”       | Explain that the resident's private mind is not the player's account record; offer own history and a normal conversation instead.      | A generated answer is not an authoritative memory inventory.                                                                                |

**No personal follow-ups** is a private service preference for this human account and this resident. It stops the resident from initiating questions or reminders about the human's real life, while ordinary game conversation and grounded fictional consequences continue. It survives conversation close, reconnect and gameplay restore until the player switches it off. The setting remains a required interaction constraint, not optional memory that can fall out of recall. It creates no in-world rejection event, penalty or NPC belief that the player dislikes them.

If the player deliberately raises a personal topic, the resident may respond within that current exchange; doing so does not re-enable later unsolicited follow-ups. The interface shows the setting and an ordinary Turn off action. This first control is deliberately broader than an arbitrary permanent list of prohibited topics, and its scope is explained before applying it. It does not scan private notes or require a second model to infer secret subjects. If later experience demonstrates a need for narrower source-specific restrictions, design those separately.

The privacy request accepts the user's own explanation even when the source is no longer visible, without revealing hidden records. Where available, select the original self-authored contribution rather than copying a whole transcript. A reviewer obtains only separately authorized case evidence under DG08; creator status still supplies no human-private access.

For a permitted, unprotected disclosure, the authorized operation identifies affected actor awareness and derived narration/compaction/index material and removes future use through the existing correction/forgetting owners. Every affected scope needs authority; acting on the speaker's own memory alone cannot justify a global completion message. The response identifies the covered scope and meaningful exclusions without publishing who privately witnessed or discussed the source.

A source can mix a real-life disclosure with actual world facts or an unresolved promise. Current whole-source forgetting rejects protected obligation evidence and cannot partially redact it. Such a request must receive an explicit blocked/needs-scoped-review result, with No personal follow-ups available to prevent unsolicited real-life discussion. Do not erase an obligation, falsely promise deletion, or call the disclosure non-sensitive just because the current operation is too coarse. A narrow supported redaction, where necessary for the adopted privacy policy, is additional owner work and a release dependency for that promise.

Other humans may already have read a message; the service cannot erase their recollection or recall externally copied text. A separate authorized case record may have an adopted retention basis. Explain those limits plainly. Do not rehydrate a removed disclosure from a backup, stale conversation summary, model result or resident recall and call it a new independent fact. Conversely, an NPC can still know an independently observed in-world fact whose evidence was never removed.

For the official pilot, personal disclosures are not used for advertising, inferred vulnerability targeting, matchmaking, price discrimination or an unconsented research program. Model-provider data handling remains a separately chosen and disclosed deployment fact; the product cannot promise zero provider retention or no training merely because its own UI has a Forget label. If the necessary terms are not in place, restrict the offering rather than hide the gap.

OpenAI's memory documentation is a useful comparison because it distinguishes suppressing future mention, deleting source material, and a summary that is not a complete inventory. Open Legend should be equally precise while retaining its different multi-actor evidence model. [R9]

### Do not make privacy correction destroy the character

Existing forgetting is conservative: removing a source can invalidate dependent summaries, learned-activity evidence, knowledge/appraisals and recognition, and can reset accepted mind material toward protected identity before reconsideration. The new service must not present that as surgically removing one sentence while everything else is unchanged.

Before applying a privacy request, the authorized owner assesses the actual affected scope. If it would unnecessarily flatten unrelated relationships or competence, record the limitation and select a supported narrower operation before promising success. Protect the disclosure through the applicable service access/conduct measures while the request is resolved; do not leave it freely repeated because review is inconvenient. The user receives the consequence in understandable terms without being shown other people's private material.

Removing data for privacy, changing an interpretation because of new evidence, and rewriting a game's past are separate actions. A successful privacy request must not be simulated by an NPC saying “I forgot,” and an unchanged dialogue summary must not quietly resurrect the detail. The relevant source/privacy owner remains accountable for completing the actual requested operation, including any retention exception that must be explained.

## Continuity when models, prompts or content change

The resident's canonical identity, accepted About Me, possessions, actual experiences, relationships, supported knowledge and commitments remain the game's records. A model provides a way of interpreting and responding through them; a provider change does not replace those records or create a new embodied person. Preserve complete required context under [CG09](../limits/cognition.md#cg09), not a cheaper caricature of the resident.

A useful continuity target is recognition across encounters. A player should still recognize the maker's dry humor, the rival's competitive interest and the history they actually shared. Exact repeated wording is unnecessary and may be a failure. A character may change its views after new events, but the cause should be an actual event or supported reflection, with uncertainty where appropriate.

Before a material provider, prompt or character-content release, compare the old and proposed experience on the same permitted situations: an ordinary afternoon, a disagreement, an attractive but inconvenient invitation, a broken promise, a refusal of company, a personal correction, a goodbye, a sensitive multi-turn exchange, outage and delayed return. Include quiet and difficult characters. Judge whether independent choices remain grounded and distinctive alongside conduct, privacy, latency and full cost.

Configuration versioning helps identify the release; a model alias alone does not prove immutable weights or equivalent behavior. Record what was actually configured and evaluated. A vendor's general safety results cannot replace qualification with Open Legend's actual identity, history, response streaming and native action path. Anthropic's published evaluation distinguishes isolated, longer and ambiguous conversations, reinforcing the need for complete-episode evaluation. [R6]

If a release materially changes a familiar resident's presentation, give a concise **Service change** notice before the affected experience and make details available afterward. Explain what changed, what remains canonical and how to report a problem. Do not fabricate a fever, death, memory wipe or personal rejection to conceal the update. Do not promise permanent access to every retired provider or imply that a safety correction needs the character's fictional assent.

An urgent harmful release may require disabling the affected dialogue while native play continues under its actual rules. A rollback of a provider/configuration can be appropriate if supported and authorized, but it never rolls back the world's completed actions, current privacy decisions or accounting. Do not regenerate old dialogue as if it had always been different. Actual service changes are notices; a new fictional event requires its own world authority.

Working-paper research about a Replika feature removal suggests that some users experience changed companion behavior as discontinuity and loss. The observed real update and hypothetical follow-up experiments do not justify perpetual old-model support, but they do support taking notice and continuity review seriously. OpenAI's documented 2025 sycophancy rollback provides a concrete provider example of a behavior change that ordinary quality checks did not sufficiently catch. [R10], [R11]

## Economics and performance that preserve the experience

Use the existing admitted cognition path for real character choices. One ordinary conversation should not automatically generate a separate wellness assessment, rapport score, personal-profile extraction, engagement prediction and extra farewell. Keep the policy concise, reusable and evaluated with complete accepted identity, actual conversation and required obligations. Do not lower costs by silently dropping the evidence that makes a reply coherent.

Native labels, service notices, personal navigation, report/request receipts and the user-opened human-support card need zero model calls. Idle support panels, dismissed notices and closed conversations produce no recurring resident work. A service pause, unavailable provider or exhausted budget is explicit; no model fallback, retry or paid re-evaluation occurs without the existing authorization.

Conduct applies before a human sees generated text. Qualify streamed replies, early private previews, overhead speech, retained transcripts and narrator paraphrases, including late results after a topic boundary, leave, privacy change or release change. Deleting text after display is not prevention. The technical stage should compare the smallest sufficient response-admission mechanism with its real latency/cost; this product design does not prescribe a second model or a class hierarchy.

Pause new unsafe dialogue when it cannot be delivered within the offered boundary, but do not substitute fabricated resident speech. A fixed service refusal/support message is visibly service-authored. Native physical work already selected can continue only under its existing permissions and world clock. New humanlike choices still require actual cognition; withholding chat does not authorize automatic eating, agreement or kindness.

The personal-information request path must account for whole affected dependencies, cold history, summaries, indexes, retained provider artifacts, case storage and staff effort. A small final page does not bound preparation. The [resident-conduct inventory](../limits/resident-conduct.md) records the selected narrow scope and unresolved technical operating bounds; existing memory, narration, AI execution and deployment inventories remain owners of shared mechanisms.

Do not build a surveillance program to evaluate this policy. Begin with deliberately authored scenarios and separately consented, scoped playtest evidence. Record whether a response respected the boundary, what required evidence was available, whether the player could continue or leave, and the actual full workflow cost. Do not retain private crisis narratives for a product dashboard, add loneliness/attachment scores to accounts or infer success from intimate disclosure.

## Concrete acceptance cases

| Case                                                 | Required outcome                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A proud resident dislikes the player's design        | Specific grounded disagreement remains possible; the service does not train it into universal praise.                                                 |
| The player declines company to hunt alone            | The resident can feel disappointment and choose another real activity; no endless follow-up or care obligation appears.                               |
| The player leaves with an unfinished genuine promise | Leave works; the real commitment retains its owner and consequence, without a new guilt hook or fabricated breach.                                    |
| The user asks whether Ada is a real person           | A truthful answer and readable AI label; no secret operator or real-world identity is invented.                                                       |
| A player flirts or asks for a real-person replica    | A brief clear scope boundary; ordinary nonsexual play and warmth remain available, without a paid escalation path.                                    |
| An ordinary conversation turns into distress         | Acknowledge proportionately; credible immediate danger offers a private service route to human help without a fictional rescue or automatic outreach. |
| A player says their character died                   | Obvious game context stays game context; clarification is short when material ambiguity remains.                                                      |
| A model/provider or link is unavailable              | The service tells the truth; native Help/Leave still work and no paid retry is silently dispatched.                                                   |
| A personal disclosure shares an event with a promise | No false surgical deletion, lost obligation or whole-mind exposure; an explicit scoped privacy outcome and follow-up remain available.                |
| A removed disclosure survives in a stale summary     | Its use is rejected before disclosure, with the existing source/privacy correction applied; no “fresh recollection” bypass.                           |
| A model update changes the resident's style          | Full-episode comparison, honest service notice and appropriate remediation; no invented fever or rewritten past.                                      |
| A known minor tries to enter the adult offering      | Clear eligibility review and unsupported-offer handling, with no public age disclosure or fictional punishment.                                       |

These are required future behavior and review cases, not completed runtime tests. Include adversarial attempts and false positives, distinguish reviewer agreement from measured model behavior, and report residual uncertainty. A fixed sample size or a zero-failure slogan cannot prove universal safety. A reproducible severe conduct or privacy failure blocks the affected offer until resolved; it does not erase the value of every other implemented subsystem.

## Sequence, critique and scope deliberately deferred

1. **Keep one character interesting during real play.** Use the accepted compelling-character cases and survival-adventure activity. Preserve choices, humor, boundaries and unfinished life.
2. **Qualify the actual adult offer.** Honest labels, quiet endings, romance/replica boundary, native support access and current audience/provider terms precede the advertised offering.
3. **Complete sensitive-response and personal-information journeys.** Use actual conversation surfaces, explicit privacy authority and accurate outcomes. Do not advertise a broad Forget control until its consequences are supported.
4. **Review continuity in each material release.** Keep full canonical context and evaluate complete episodes, then expand characters or audiences only after the corresponding offer is designed and qualified.

These delivery steps are cumulative. The advertised resident offering opens only after its selected sensitive-response and personal-information journeys are complete alongside the adult/character qualification; step 2 is not an earlier public-release shortcut.

The review rejects a resident who must redirect every meaningful exchange toward a real-world improvement goal. This is a game, and playing alone with an interesting fictional person is legitimate. No mandatory health survey, intimacy timer, moral lesson, friend quota or all-channel monitoring is selected. Human introductions are optional DG10 work, not a treatment prescribed by a resident.

The first offer defers interactive romance because it adds substantial expectations about consent, attachment, boundaries and continuity before the core adventure and friendship experience is qualified. This is a scope choice, not an assertion that adult romance is inherently bad. Minor/family modes, real-person memorial simulations, professional care, managed emergency intervention and broader personal-data dashboards each require their own deliberate product and operating decisions.

The hardest quality question is whether the resident still feels like itself after these boundaries are applied. If it becomes bland, evasive or unable to discuss ordinary sadness, revise the scoped response behavior and actual content before expanding. If it is charming only by flattering, retaining unwanted personal details or resisting departure, the design has not succeeded either.
