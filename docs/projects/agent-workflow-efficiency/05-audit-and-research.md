## Overall diagnosis

The strongest avoidable cause is a mismatch between broad efficiency advice and specific expensive obligations, amplified by poor scope control and repeated reconstruction of progress. There were real engineering defects and environmental failures. The available record cannot assign percentages or hours among them.

The solution should remove conflicting obligations and make verification purposeful, not merely add more warnings about efficiency. Strong correctness and architecture guidance should remain available at the point of change. Comprehensive review found material bugs; eliminating it would trade delay for unreliable software.

The initial assignment covered seven connected phases, multiple subsystems, live character behavior, browser/database integration, performance, and human acceptance. The supplied review log reports 474 changed files. That is substantial work. It still does not justify indefinite optimization or repeated setup. There is no controlled counterfactual establishing how long this exact task should have taken.

## Accepted scale measurements

```text

```

At that revision, lines 253–255 specified ordinary, 100-animal, and 500-animal measurements, with 1,000 animals exploratory where admitted. They also permitted an affected population capability to remain unqualified after failed qualification, without dropping animals, threats, or physical correctness. The current [performance contract](../combat-and-threat-responses-tech-design.md#performance-contract) retains this distinction.

Therefore:

- Running the 500-animal case had a real task basis; it was not justified solely by the later word “glaring.”
- Required measurement does not itself require making that workload meet every small-world target indefinitely.
- The parent assignment also preserved existing speed requirements. A failed ordinary-world gate cannot be dismissed merely by calling other runs exploratory.
- The audit cannot declare all later performance fixes unnecessary. It can identify the missing scope decision: exactly which result had to pass for this task, versus which failed population measurement could remain openly unqualified.

Future instructions should force that distinction when a costly check is selected, without requiring the user to approve routine benchmark details. Existing hard gates cannot be silently downgraded.

Categories overlap; these are not counts of unique reads, mandatory instructions, or wasted operations. The supplied diagnosis reports a 22.6-minute broad verification run and a later 11.8-minute pass. The review log reports five deadline failures passing after an intrusive browser observer stopped. ## Primary research and applicability

Research retrieved October 21, 2026, America/New_York. Recommendations below are our application to OpenLegend; vendor examples are not universal mandates. The proposals deliberately avoid copying a complex multi-agent harness into this one-agent workflow.

### A. Codex instruction discovery

[Official AGENTS.md documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/agent-configuration/agents-md) describes root-to-working-directory discovery, closer instruction precedence, and a default combined project-document limit of 32 KiB. It also recommends checking actual loading and using concise review guidance.

Application: preserve root/local guarantees while shrinking the entry point and making topic reads explicit. Existing sessions may still have old instructions, so validate adoption in fresh sessions. The documented default is not proof of this desktop host's effective configuration; the local routing observations provide separate evidence of clipping.

### B. Repository knowledge as a map

[OpenAI, “Harness engineering”](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) describes replacing a large instruction manual with a short entry point and structured repository knowledge. It also emphasizes machine-visible application behavior and architectural boundaries.

Application: keep authoritative knowledge discoverable instead of duplicating it into every task. Do not import the article's agent-review loops or merge practices wholesale; their experiment and economics differ from OpenLegend's restrictions. Its reported productivity is not an estimate of our potential gain.

### C. Context engineering

[Anthropic, “Effective context engineering for AI agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) recommends high-signal context, just-in-time retrieval, compaction, and structured notes. It warns against both overly prescriptive instructions and vague prompts, and against accumulating exhaustive example lists.

Application: a small current-progress record plus selected contracts, not a diary or a full archive at every resumption. Keep decisions and unresolved risks available; reduce redundant tool output. This is engineering guidance, not a measured causal explanation of this branch's duration.

### D. Long-running agents: useful continuity, avoid cargo-cult ceremony

[Anthropic, “Effective harnesses for long-running agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents) identifies premature completion, poor handoff state, and insufficient end-to-end verification. Its experiments use feature lists, progress records, Git, and browser checks.

Application: preserve accepted scope and a resumable state, and verify connected behavior. Do not copy its every-session server startup or one-feature-per-session pattern into a continuously authorized implementation where valid environment/evidence already exists. The purpose of those steps is continuity, not repeated ceremony.

### E. Newer harness work directly supports adaptive verification

[Anthropic, “Harness design for long-running application development”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps) reports that a later model needed less scaffolding: the author removed fixed sprints and moved evaluation toward the end, retaining further evaluation where it added value. The article also documents quality gains and substantial costs from the earlier, more structured setup.

Application: choose verification timing according to the task's uncertainty and model reliability. Check high-risk assumptions early when that prevents serious harm or rework; do not enforce full review per small slice. This is a small set of reported experiments with different models/apps and changing scope, not a controlled promise that one final pass is always sufficient.

### F. Empirical evidence on repository instructions

[Gloaguen et al., “Evaluating AGENTS.md,” arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v1) studies SWE-bench Lite and 138 AGENTbench issues from 12 Python repositories. Generated context reduced average resolution in the reported settings and raised cost by about 20–23%; developer-written files generally helped success but also increased effort/cost. Trace analysis found more testing and exploration.

Application: audit obligations and redundant content rather than assuming more instructions improve results. This does **not** show that removing OpenLegend's authority or world contracts would help: the tasks, languages, models, and quality criteria differ. The paper's nuanced human-written result is more useful than the headline “AGENTS.md is harmful.”

### G. Environment is part of measurement

[Anthropic, “Quantifying infrastructure noise in agentic coding evals”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/infrastructure-noise) demonstrates that resource allocations and enforcement can alter benchmark reliability and results, and discusses other infrastructure confounders.

Application: keep resource/setup facts with measurements, avoid interfering workloads, and classify environment failures. It does not establish that Docker or memory pressure caused the OpenLegend delay. Repeating an unchanged failed environment is not a useful correctness test.

### H. Grade the intended behavior, not a brittle fixture

[Anthropic, “Demystifying evals for AI agents”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) discusses inspecting failed transcripts, validating graders, and combining different evidence types. It notes that valid solutions can be rejected by faulty grading and that automated evaluation does not replace human evidence.

Application: establish that the real consumer ran and that the fixture is judging the promised behavior. Repair shared test setup when necessary; do not declare product progress for that repair. Keep software correctness, independent character decisions, and human understanding/enjoyment as separate claims.

## Practices deliberately not proposed

- Mandatory multi-agent planning/review or another orchestrator. The current assignment explicitly uses one AI; future delegation should follow task authorization and expected benefit.
- A fixed number of steps, retries, review rounds, minutes, or experiments for every task. Such limits can either force churn or stop necessary work.
- More mandatory retrospective files, productivity logs, risk spreadsheets, or automated model judges.
- A ban on benchmarks or an assumption that all testing belongs at the end.
- A policy that small bugs do not matter. Rare authority, privacy, accounting, or data-loss defects can be release blockers.
- Automatic acceptance of stress failures, or silently weakening current first-playable gates.
- Rewriting all historical docs, all verification tools, or the game architecture to adopt the workflow.
- A promise that written routing guarantees agent behavior. Fresh-session observation and real delivered outcomes still matter.

## Confidence and remaining uncertainty

High confidence: the specific contradictory instructions, file sizes, accepted 500-animal measurement requirement, documented clipping observations, and concrete defects/fixture failures reported in the supplied logs.

Moderate confidence: those conflicts and context growth materially encouraged churn; narrowing mandatory reads, simplifying setup, and separating current work from bounded historical records should reduce it.

Not established: total wasted hours, the proportion caused by each factor, a complete idle/CPU/memory timeline, how often every document was reread, and the exact speed or defect rate of the proposed workflow. Evaluate those through ordinary subsequent tasks without turning adoption into another long research project.
