## Desired result

A reader can find current behavior, technical contracts, unfinished work and relevant evidence without loading historical collections. Each subject has one maintained owner. Documents serve the playable game and its extensible engine; they do not create another workflow framework or record every editing step.

## Operational design

The [documentation handbook](../../documentation-system.md) owns filing units, contract promotion, bounded records and navigation. [Documentation policy](../../../.agents/rules/documentation.md) owns updates, preservation and acceptance. The [front door](../../README.md) and topic indexes link real content; known owners can be opened directly. These are navigation choices, not mandatory reading lists.

Current feature and technical contracts retain useful established paths. New independent owners use `docs/features/` or `docs/technical/`; specialized world, limits, UI, asset and method collections retain their authority. Active project scope, focused tasks, research, dated evidence, completed work and decision history remain distinct. Related retries share a campaign; a file is not created for every command or check. A report spanning behavior and performance has one primary home and cross-links.

Completed projects promote useful current contracts before their remaining scope/history is filed. Missing required evidence stays open. No search service, generator, scheduled cleanup, redirect framework or new checking programme is needed. Scope and current progress live in the [adoption plan](03-instruction-edit-plan.md).

## Bounded adoption

Adoption has two ordered parts: operational instructions/navigation, then the named document migrations after coordinating with their writers. Product tasks can use the operational workflow while unrelated historical filing remains pending; report adoption progress honestly until both parts are complete. Do not run a fresh whole-repository content review or freeze product development until all historical files fit the convention.

Partition large reports by independently useful questions, grouping related retries and measurements. Do not mechanically create one file per existing heading/run or one giant file per year. Preserve accounting with explicit attribution to the original recorded scope; never sum overlapping cumulative figures into a fictional total.

The large performance tracker and old performance-project histories should adopt this layout when their owner next maintains them, rather than expanding this adoption into another broad cleanup. Existing research archives can be indexed in place until a concrete active-authority conflict needs repair. Repair moved links in a batch and check references; a few temporary forwarding headings are acceptable during migration, but not duplicate canonical bodies or endless redirect chains.

Adoption succeeds when a reader can find current contracts, unfinished scope, a relevant dated result, and completed history without loading the other categories; old required evidence and open gaps remain reachable; active instruction loading stays narrow; and the default against new maintained tests and the protected development-save policy remain unchanged. Validate representative navigation and affected links, not a new documentation benchmark or exhaustive content audit.
