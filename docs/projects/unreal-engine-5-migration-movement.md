# UE03 — Player movement over the Rust connection

Use the packaged player and the existing [UE02 session and stream](unreal-engine-5-migration-tech-design.md#ue02--entry-identity-and-the-first-frame). When the player presses forward on open ground, their character starts moving on screen immediately. Rust decides how far the body moved, what each witness saw and what survives a restart.

## How stock UE5 moves a character

Epic source tag `5.8.4-release` resolves to `f45729c0b6f097038e72b52d366a62baeca3275b`. The links below require Epic repository access. This traces ordinary grounded movement over UE’s packed network path. Use the licensed engine APIs inside the UE client. Implement Rust from Open Legend’s input, geometry and disclosure contracts. Keep Epic source, serialized move layouts and translated engine routines out of the Rust implementation and public client sources.

## Open Legend’s movement contract

**First frame.** For ordinary grounded steering the controlling player sees their body start moving across the ground on the first rendered frame that can use the keypress. The following camera and gait use that same displayed position. Another player sees the moving body at its latest observed position. Two sightings alone cannot reveal the path between them. For click-to-walk, show the destination marker immediately and follow the route Rust accepts. Only Rust can open a door, grant reach or sight, produce a witness account, or save a position. “Walk to and open” must still wait for Rust to establish reach and recheck the action. [Observer disclosure](../world-presentation.md#selected-per-fact-stealth-disclosure)

**Send intentions.** Reuse Enhanced Input, the local controller and camera, permitted scene queries and in-place animation. Use one local display pawn. If it is a Character, disable its movement tick and replicated movement. UE’s network roles, saved moves, Actor channels and proxy smoothing require its server. Convert held keys to a normalized world direction in double precision using horizontal camera yaw. Recompute on a movement-key change in every view. Only in behind-character follow view does deliberate mouse turning also recompute the direction while the keys stay held. Elevated orbit and shoulder aiming preserve the last requested world direction until the movement keys change. Pitch has no vertical travel component. While idle, orbit is purely visual. Switching angles mid-walk preserves the current world direction until the next deliberate steering input. Camera follow, correction, recentering and transitions between angles must never generate steering by themselves.

Post `kind: steer` to `/api/input` with the current cookie, Origin, `x-ol-client`, `x-ol-scope`, `inputEpoch`, `movementInputToken`, increasing `movementSequence` and `direction: {x,z}`. Release sends zero even if starting is now unavailable. While held, post `kind: renew` at the advertised interval with the epoch, increasing `renewalSequence` and that token/sequence in `movement`. Dispatch the start and stop promptly. During a follow-view turn, preview the latest mouse direction immediately. Keep one replaceable unsent direction. Start with one dispatch per advertised renewal interval during the turn. Send that direction if changed, otherwise renew. If switching views before dispatch, preserve the last deliberate world direction and send it once on schedule if still held and changed. Key-up or focus loss discards it and sends stop. Only dispatched directions enter replay history. A span ends at the next direction-change dispatch or key-up. Renewals preserve it. For the preview, bend only travel since the player began turning. Keep this unsent portion visual and replace it once when reconciling. Never rotate earlier travel or replay the same time twice. A superseded direction cannot be renewed or sent after release. Bind trigger, completion and cancellation. Focus loss or modal input releases the gesture. A locally uncertain route still sends the real intention. No client position, duration or per-frame message is needed. **Return one coherent answer.** The POST reports admission. The ordered view supplies the controlling body’s foot position, heading, support, dimensions, life and clock. The following fields let the client predict from rest and correct a partly processed hold. They use the controlling player’s initial view, authorized reset and changed fields on the existing stream.

| Field | Producer and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `steeringProfile` | Rust supplies the eligible grounded body’s **effective speed in metres per game-second** and maximum walkable slope (rise/run), with the simulation time when those terms took effect. Send current terms at entry and push changes with the confirmed pose and its simulation point. Reuse `bodySize.width` (diameter), height, support and the published clock. An explicit unavailable profile ends prediction for ineligible or unsupported locomotion without failing the rest of the view. Use the installed simulation’s speed and body owners. Omit private causes, other characters’ profiles and full world settings. |
| `steeringProgress` | In the existing control/epoch/token scope, return the highest retired sequence, the total settled game-seconds for that token and, while a direction is active, its sequence ID and cumulative settled game-seconds. The total includes accepted time from directions that retired between published poses and resets with the movement token. Initialize retired-through at zero for a new movement token. A newer applied direction retires superseded and skipped IDs below it. Retired means no sequence through that ID can contribute again. An admitted direction can have zero time so far. A blocked step can consume its entire interval while covering zero metres. Retire a superseded direction only once its last possible contribution is settled or excluded. A confirmed stop retires its own ID too, even with zero travel. An unsuccessful admission leaves the previous accepted state intact. |

Rust records progress where the elapsed turn is accepted, on both ordinary and protected commit paths. Intersect the input’s credited time window with the actual accepted simulation interval. Exclude outstanding clock debt, refused candidates and rolled-back work. Capture the pose, profile, progress and scope together at the same accepted point. The final permission check can reject the prepared publication. Attaching a later progress cursor to that earlier pose would lose movement on replay. Publish progress against a wall, terminal input and profile removal through existing revision and final permission checks. Coalesce input-only changes into the next ordinary view publication. Freeze the controller record for that publication, even if a newer input arrives during preparation. A stop at the same game time appears on the next ordered revision or an explicit fresh reset. Never change a patch at an already delivered revision. Key-up on screen is immediate. Keep one transient progress record per active controller. An unchanged renewal needs no new view. Update Rust full/patch production and all current decoders together. No new polling, SQL or save records. **Advance and correct the display.**

1. For each dispatched input span record its sequence, world direction and local monotonic time. On the first dispatched direction from rest, count the time from the actual input event to dispatch, bounded by the frame interval and remaining prediction allowance. Start the next sample at dispatch. Measure elapsed time once across frames. Accumulate game-seconds per direction using the published `clock.baseRatio × clock.speed`. Split at rate changes and freeze on pause. Packet arrival spacing is not a movement clock. Multiply by effective walking speed. Spend that distance along the supported **3D** path, including uphill and diagonals. Use the same public geometry release, foot anchor, width, height and walkable slope. A missing support or uncertain clearance stops the preview at the last safe point. Stock Character acceleration, sliding, step-up or animation root motion must not add travel. [Movement execution](../../native/simulation/src/movement/execution.rs) · [Support and slope](../../native/game-server/src/spatial/walking.rs) · [Body clearance](../../native/game-server/src/spatial/collision.rs)
2. Start each correction at Rust’s confirmed pose. Drop retired sequences. For the matching active direction keep the greater of zero and its accumulated game-seconds minus the reported game-seconds. Replay that remainder and later local input in order. Apply changed speed from its effective point. Coalesced updates may skip an intermediate profile because the confirmed pose already includes its effects. Compare the previous display with the replayed position at the same point in the gesture. For example, at a 1× clock, if W has been held for one second and Rust confirms the first 0.9 seconds, add only the last 0.1 seconds, within the lease below. Releasing W stops local advance immediately but keeps the outstanding interval for reconciliation. A late POST must never rewind the stream or restart a released gesture. [Prediction and reconciliation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.html)
3. Keep a bounded history of direction changes, not a record per rendered frame. Each new nonzero direction has a provisional lease from dispatch. A lost reply, HTTP 503 or stale reply for the same input token can follow acceptance. Keep the existing deadline and renew a still-held direction. Matching progress or a successful renewal confirms the direction. A renewal with a missing reply or a refusal under the same input token leaves its deadline unchanged. It could have arrived before its direction or repeated an already accepted renewal. Extend only on a successful renewal for the same input, measured from dispatch, before the current deadline. A combined renewal confirms movement and a pending release independently. A replaced token or elapsed deadline ends the affected lease. Never resend a nonzero direction to recover its reply. Across all directions, cap total unconfirmed time at one advertised lease beyond the last confirmed steering time. Return only the increase in the token’s settled game-seconds from Rust. An update that skips a retired direction still returns its accepted time. Renewals, new direction IDs and zero-time retirements cannot extend that total during a stall. On local expiry, drop the old preview and renewals and attempt a zero-direction stop. Wait on the existing stream while a walking key remains held. Opposing keys give zero direction and keep that wait intact. An ordered view must show an eligible pose and confirm idle movement by retiring that stop or publishing a replacement movement token within the same input epoch. Control, actor, life and timeline must still match. If the stop could not be sent, its reply is unavailable, or it returns HTTP 503, retry the same stop sequence once on the existing input cadence while the player is still waiting. Rust can then retire it even if the original start never arrived. A published confirmation cancels that retry. Neither reply authorizes movement or triggers a state read. Keep unconfirmed time charged until settled or the authority changes. Resume once with a fresh sequence for the held direction from the confirmed pose. The new movement starts at dispatch and earns no time from the gap. Releasing all walking keys, focus loss, input takeover by a menu or combat, an ineligible view, a changed epoch or lost stream or authority clears the held direction. Entering key-binding capture clears held keys immediately because the picker can consume their release events. A definite refusal or exhausted history also clears the gesture and attempts one stop when earlier motion may still be live. Release a live gesture even when starting a new one is unavailable. Ignore stale replies for older gestures. The session owns stream recovery and genuine departure.
4. Blend small residual errors on continuous clear support for at most one observed update interval. Correct immediately to the safe side of a barrier or across a discontinuity. If Rust consumed forward time but stopped short of the predicted step, suppress further lead along that blocked direction until an actual turn, confirmed forward progress or relevant newly permitted geometry change. Renewing the same direction cannot repeatedly push the body back through the obstruction. Replay moves only the display and follow camera. Picking uses what is visible, while target identity, contact effects and reach remain subject to current server permission.

**Doorway.** A player pressing forward sees motion immediately through an apparently open doorway. Use the same released opening dimensions, named floor and permitted leaf transform on both sides. Mesh bounds cannot determine passage width. If Rust accepted closure before the crossing, correct to its near-side pose. If the body crossed first, keep it on the far side. An undisclosed obstruction may also stop a body. Correct position without revealing the cause. **Code sharing.** Share the resolved inputs and a set of movement examples first. The current Rust step combines authoritative eligibility, occupied space, geometry work and ordered consequences. For the first client, write the small display calculation against permitted geometry inside the existing UE module. If both callers need the same substantial pure calculation or repeatedly disagree on it, isolate that calculation as an experiment. Use it in the actual Rust step and behind a narrow C interface in Unreal, with numbers and public geometry inputs only. Verify rights under UE01, package both targets and compare accuracy, frame time and build cost against the working adapter before retaining the dependency.

## Qualification under delay and loss

These timing and device checks qualify the release. The [existing-game migration scope](unreal-engine-5-migration-feature-spec.md#existing-game-migration-scope) defines the code batch and its focused checks.

**Gate B, both packaged targets.** Use Windows and native Apple Silicon with the same opening, input schedule, normal clock and one-second saves. Walk from rest beside a fixed landmark for one second. In follow view, hold W and sweep the mouse through a quarter turn. Both displayed and accepted paths must curve without stalling. On flat, clear ground compare the length of the accepted path with an equally long straight hold at the same speed. Measure along the curve and account for any missing travel time. Repeat with elevated orbit and shoulder aim. On clear ground both paths must stay straight, with only the ordinary renewal traffic until a movement key changes. Switch views between a mouse turn and its scheduled dispatch, then repeat with key-up before dispatch. Check that only the intended direction or stop survives. Switch during straight travel without changing the route, orbit while idle, turn diagonally, reverse, stop and give focus to a text field. Repeat on a ramp, beside fixed cover and on stacked floors.

Switch all three angles against the same permitted view with one visible character and another hidden behind cover. Across the switch, compare contact IDs, heard-only cues and available targets, allowing for framing and occlusion on screen. Change the character’s effective walking speed during a hold. Check clock conversion at two supported rates and pause/resume. If a rate transition replaces the control epoch, require a fresh gesture. Exercise a tap released before Rust processes any time, reordered POST/stream completions, a rejected elapsed turn and a stalled world that still admits both renewals and changed directions. Run clean, with 50 ms added each way, and with that delay plus seeded ±10 ms jitter and 1% loss per direction on the real HTTPS and event-stream connections. Keep issuing small acknowledgements while changing direction and confirm the client never accumulates more than a single lease of speculative movement. Drop steering packets while the stream remains connected. Check that a held key resumes from the confirmed pose, and that releasing it during the gap keeps the player stopped. Interrupt the connection beyond expiry, then reclaim control. Record actual RTT, loss and inputs. TCP retransmission can delay several updates together.

**Experiment — how often to send a turn.** A changed direction can require a fresh Rust actor/clock read and replace an unprocessed input window. A changed directional sight-guide heading can also select a full view reset. Compare one turn dispatch per renewal interval against two evenly spaced opportunities per interval, using the same recorded mouse sweep, lag, ordinary saves and valid held conversation. Keep the unchanged-direction renewal rate fixed. Count actual direction posts, fresh captures, displaced or refused elapsed work, full resets, bytes, corrections and latency alongside complete CPU and frame times at different render rates. Compare costs only for completed, equivalent routes and exactly one saved reply. Keep the renewal cadence unless the alternative gives a reproducible responsiveness or cost improvement while preserving the walking and device checks. If neither works, fix the failing owner and repeat. If guide changes drive material reset cost, compare a narrow guide patch separately against full resets with identical fields, authorization and withdrawals in every decoder. **Gate C, shared scene.** Race two real players against a permitted changing blocker. For an operable door, close it just before and just after crossing. If the selected Rust world cannot operate a door yet, keep that case open with its mechanic owner and exercise the supported structural edit. Fixed cover alone cannot establish the race. Have the other observer lose sight during the crossing. Two separated sightings must never draw an unseen route. Check ordinary saving and a cold return to the correct side. The current Rust projector refuses assembled contacts.

**Pass on both devices.** From rest on clear ground the character visibly moves relative to the landmark on the first eligible rendered frame, including before an acknowledgement on a delayed link. Repeated unobstructed walks within the lease must have no visible backward jumps at server updates. Record pauses caused by exceeding the lease separately. Speed changes converge, key-up stays stopped, and the camera follows each correction exactly once. A wrong-side pose after correction, invented sight or reach, duplicate action, movement predicted beyond expiry or lasting divergence fails. A late update can legitimately show earlier accepted movement.

Use the [paired timing rules](unreal-engine-5-migration-tech-design.md#experiments-and-stopping-rules). Mark key-down, first displayed displacement, first displayed confirmed progress, final rest, observer receipt/display and save acknowledgement separately. Measure prediction error against Rust at the **same input progress**, visible corrections in metres/pixels, correction frequency/duration, backward jumps, stalled frames, CPU/frame time and stream bytes. Report counts, median, mean, p95 and worst hitch with camera settings, zero-travel and missing outcomes. Compare against confirmation-only display on identical traces to quantify the gain and cost. A poor result keeps UE03 open for repair.
