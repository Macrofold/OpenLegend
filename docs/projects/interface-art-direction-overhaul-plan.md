## Purpose and authorization

Mike requested a complete overhaul of the running game's interface — research, art direction, artwork, design system, implementation, review and finishing — produced through the **direct AI method**: Claude Design (claude.ai) as the visual specialist for composition and critique, the OpenAI Images API (GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst) for artwork, and this agent for coordination and implementation. A second method (Higgsfield) produces an independent result on `codex/ui-overhaul-higgsfield-2026-10-18` for a later comparison. Neither method copies the other's designs, artwork or implementation.

The goal is an original identity that makes people, possessions, discoveries and consequential choices compelling while the world stays the visual focus. The work restyles and re-composes existing surfaces. It does not change actions, permissions, knowledge boundaries, recovery or world rules, and it adds no mechanics borrowed from reference games.

## Scope

Every current surface in the [coverage map](../ui-ux/interface-coverage.md): the play HUD and current work; Inventory with nested and paired containers; independent object inspectors; Character; Events and Character records; In view and the action picker/browser; Craft, Build, Create and Speak; Game, Settings & help and checkpoints; entry, interruption and recovery screens; and the authorized authoring, inspection, diagnostics and operations workspaces. Main's attack mode is also a current surface, though the coverage map does not list it yet: its on/off toggle, the attack panel (In view, Prepare, Attack or Attack normally, Release, Cancel) and the aiming reticle over the world. Out of scope: new gameplay, progression, slots or currencies; changes to camera meanings, speech/hearing privacy, inventory authority or save formats; paid generation during normal play. The gameplay the designs draw but the game lacks is specified separately in [Character and map additions](character-and-map-additions-feature-spec.md).

## Method and stages

2. **Three directions (done).** Claude Design developed three original directions — A Waxed Canvas, B Field Herbarium, C Ember Night — each with a direction sheet and four compositions over the real world capture (ordinary play, inventory with inspection, reading and identity, contextual action and creation) using real content, revised once after critique.
3. **Artwork for contenders (done).** One reference-guided item sheet per direction, then the finalists' full composition set (22 belongings, two portraits, two structures and the campfire per finalist), cleaned for interface use, placed into the editable compositions and judged at display size over the world.
4. **Owner choice (done).** One A/B board with the two finalists, B and C, was published. Mike chose to continue from C with changes (decision 6). The painted revision D (Hearthlight), with a generated painted kit in place, was rejected (decision 8). Of three modern versions, Mike chose E Lashing (decision 9); Claude Design then drew it as row H, "Lashing, cozy": ten screens over the real world capture and a component sheet, following the UX redesign specification. Each screen was rendered locally with the real pixel art and measured against the specification's spacing rules; one correction pass fixed the defects found (a submenu 8 px off its row, invented menu rows, an ambiguous task sentence, a missing new-events button, a duplicated placement message and an unreal pause reason). Mike kept it (decision 10) and asked for three variations to choose from. Claude Design drew Emberwood, Moonford and Dawnstitch on a new canvas, each as a component sheet and seven screens; after measurement one correction pass fixed the campfire-menu spacing in Moonford and Dawnstitch. Mike reviewed those (decision 11), and round 7 drew four new variations of the new layout, Wealdcrest, Mistfen, Inkfern and Lanternmoss, with a small generated art kit (brush strokes, organic edges, a woven texture, mist, ornaments, cord and ember progress bars, a minimap and a full-body figure of Mike); one correction pass followed measurement. Round 8 drew three variations of the converged layout (Brassbrook, Gildmoss, Bronzereach); Mike chose Gildmoss (decision 14), refined it over rounds 8b–8n (decisions 15–18) and made it the final design on 2026-10-26 (decision 19).
5. **Design system and specification (done for Gildmoss).** Tokens, type, materials, frames, emblems, item-art rules and every surface/state, validated with representative exemplars and specialist critique before the art family expands. The [production package](#production-package-2026-10-26) delivers them for Gildmoss; the [Gildmoss design system](../../apps/client/src/design-system/gildmoss/README.md) is the accepted specification.
6. **Production and implementation.** Complete asset family (the production package, done); implementation authorized October 27, 2026 and in progress, through the existing design-system, theme and component owners; live play and repair loop; viewport, scale, keyboard, reduced-motion and theme checks.
7. **Independent review, documentation and delivery.** Findings-only review by a different capable model with live access; fixes; canonical documentation; assets published; handoff with evidence and actual spend.

## Engineering approach

- Text, numbers, controls, focus and accessibility stay native DOM through the existing React Aria components. Artwork supplies illustration and material only; frames use scalable CSS/SVG or border-image slices, never a painted screenshot behind live controls.
- Item, resource, creature and action art binds to world-authored semantic symbol keys through the trusted catalogue, never by guessing from names. Content without authored art keeps an immediate, readable fallback. Saved worlds carry their own item definitions, so newly authored keys reach new worlds; any change for existing worlds needs an explicit owner decision.
- Item illustrations use the world's exact definition-to-art mapping and one shared renderer across admitted inventory, recipe, trade, equipment and quick-slot rows. The optional mapping is presentation data; it grants neither a capability nor permission to read an item. Existing semantic icons and readable sigils remain the fallback.
- Theme-specific materials and fonts live in the existing theme layer (`apps/client/src/design-system/tokens/`, `components.css`, `layout.css`); one component owner per pattern.
- Artwork files follow the repository's [external-storage contract](../asset-workflow.md#where-files-belong): payloads are published to the OpenLegend asset store and Git holds catalogues, exact locks, prompts and provenance (decided by Mike, 2026-10-19).

### Performance

- **What grows:** the number of art files requested per screen and their bytes; illustrated tiles in large inventories and long lists.
- **Shared preparation:** decoded images come from the browser cache and the existing selected-asset reader; one catalogue request for interface art, prepared before the interface mounts with the existing finite wait and fallback.
- **Invalidation:** an art release change; nothing per frame or per world update.
- **Measurement:** network request count and bytes on first load and warm reload, inventory open time with the populated baseline, and frame rate with panels open, compared with the original interface at the same viewport.

## Decisions

1. **Image budget (2026-10-19):** $80 hard cap for OpenAI image generation.
2. **Artwork storage (2026-10-19):** follow the repository's asset policy. The original request asked to commit binary artwork to Git, but the policy forbids any art payload in Git and `pnpm assets:check` enforces it, so artwork is published through the asset store with catalogues, locks and provenance in Git.
3. **Reference board (2026-10-19):** chosen by judgment with no per-game quota, preferring modern games; Baldur's Gate 3 is a prominent modern model.
4. **Claude Design inputs (2026-10-19):** Mike signed in to claude.ai in the desktop app's built-in browser. That browser cannot operate the page's native file picker, so the brief, captures and references were uploaded into Mike's own private Design canvas through the Artifact tool; Claude Design read them from there.
5. **Finalists (2026-10-19):** B Field Herbarium and C Ember Night. They are the two most different credible answers to the brief: reading-first ink-and-wash illustration on paper surfaces versus a dark, firelit HUD in the world's own pixel craft. A Waxed Canvas stayed closest to the current flat dark panels at display size; its single primary decision per screen and tabbed panel titles transfer to either finalist.
6. **Owner direction (2026-10-19):** continue from C. Keep its world-matched pixel art for items, portraits and structures and its dark, firelit feel. The interface itself becomes modern, functional and semi-stylized with painted effects — no pixel-styled menus, grid or dither textures, pixel buttons, icons or lettering — and takes nothing from B, whose panels read as a 2000s website and whose ruled meters Mike rejected. Modern premium RPGs such as Baldur's Gate 3 set the quality bar.
7. **Game speed (2026-10-19):** speed is a God mode debugging control, not a core interface element. The HUD keeps the clock and place; speed moves to a separate God mode Time panel. Today every player can change speed (time bar, Shift + ] and Shift + [) and the server accepts it from any player, so the change includes refusing speed changes outside God mode on the server, not only hiding the control.
8. **Modern, functional, subtly stylized (2026-10-19):** Mike rejected D's painted treatment. Textures (lacquer, brush strokes, painted gold) and orb-shaped launchers read as an old isometric game. The interface should be modern and very functional, with subtle style in the way Baldur's Gate 3 restrains its ornament, and that style should come from Open Legend's own world. Launchers become a modern dock of icon buttons; panels use flat or gently graded fills and hairlines, with no raster textures.
9. **Lashing, cozy, with a rethought layout (2026-10-20):** Mike chose E Lashing as the closest version and asked for it to feel cozy and game-like as well as modern, without its corner marks, with careful spacing, and with every surface's layout rethought from the player's needs rather than kept from the current interface (for example, no Compact workspace). A five-part code audit of the HUD, belongings, actions/making/speech, people/reading and system/creator surfaces informed a UX redesign specification: one home per task, a bottom-right dock instead of edge rails, owner and God mode tools separated from player surfaces, one composer for Say, Act and Try, a single Make panel, and a rebuilt right-click menu and task card. The specification and audit live with the method's design workspace; canonical UI documentation is updated when the chosen design is implemented.
10. **Flaxlight kept; three variations before the final version (2026-10-21):** Mike kept the cozy Lashing design, now named **Flaxlight**, and asked for three more variations in the same cozy, game-like theme that vary colour, stylization, layout, icons, effects and fonts, so that a final iteration can combine the best parts of each. At his request they live on a new Claude Design canvas holding only Flaxlight and the variations, started from Claude Design's own new-design page so it has its own chat; Flaxlight's boards, the pixel art and the inputs were copied into it. The variations are **Emberwood** (warm walnut and amber lit from below, with a hearth bar along the bottom), **Moonford** (moonlit river teal with the fire's warmth, edge sheets and a ring of quick choices) and **Dawnstitch** (a light linen look with berry stitching, cards and tiles). Character, Make and Build are drawn only for the final version.
11. **Round 6 review: a Baldur's Gate 3–style layout with tasteful stylization (2026-10-21):** Mike kept Flaxlight's dark green (to be darker), Newsreader-style type (with calmer italics and upright speech), its right-click list with submenus to the right, its slot style and soft panel-edge gradients, plus Emberwood's light glow, stronger highlights, item art in menu rows and the addressee's portrait when speaking; he wants a brighter gold, a more compact interface sized for larger screens, and liberal icons. Layout changes: portrait at the top left opening a large Character panel (character sheet, a figure with equipment slots, and a dense inventory) with Journal and Crafting tabs; bags as separate draggable windows, moving by dragging, with a quantity dialog for stacks; a minimap at the top right holding the camera controls, with the game menu behind an Open Legend logo button; a bottom hotbar of ten quick slots plus Speak and Act; no player work card or multi-step task flow; in-depth detail windows without actions; "Give" instead of "Offer to someone", and no "Talk about it"; a World Agent chat in God mode; stylized names above characters; speech captions with the speaker's portrait. He asked for borders, textures, transparency and organic variation placed tastefully. The full record lives in the method's owner-preferences file. Four new variations (Wealdcrest, Mistfen, Inkfern, Lanternmoss) explore those stylization approaches. The Baldur's Gate 3 screenshots Mike mentioned never arrived; "Try" and the pause rule are settled in decision 20.
12. **Round 7 review: the layout converges (2026-10-24):** Mike chose Wealdcrest's colour scheme, right-click menu, buttons and round slider; Mistfen's metallic-gold selected and silver unselected states; and Lanternmoss's lantern-lit panels, Gelasio and gold-rimmed minimap.     - **Character tab:**

      - Ten equipment slots.
    - **Map:** a drawn map with markers.
    - **Crafting:** simplified, with times in real seconds.
    - **Trade:** a simpler window with the conversation built in.

17. **Need-bar colours by tone (2026-10-25):** darkening the need bars did not fix them. 18. **Top right and the game menu (2026-10-26):** the minimap moves fully into the top-right corner, with closer zoom buttons. The place name sits below it. A small round day-and-night dial, deliberately not a clock face, sits in the gap at the minimap's upper left, with the day, year and time below it. Sunrise is on the left and sunset on the right: by day a sun travels clockwise in an arc across the top half, and by night a moon travels clockwise in an arc across the bottom half. The face is shaded by daylight with soft fades, so it shows roughly how bright it is outside, with small sunrise and sunset markers. The left and right points are the world's own dawn and dusk (6:00 and 18:00 in the bundled world); the top half spreads evenly over the day and the bottom half over the night, so the dial fits any world's hours. "Year" is an example until a world defines years. The Open Legend logo button that opens the game menu moves from the top-right corner to the end of the hotbar.
