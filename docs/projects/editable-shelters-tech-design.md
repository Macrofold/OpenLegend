## 1. Playable result and scope

A builder chooses a recognizable spot, installs four real posts and their cloak, brings a belonging beneath it, uses ordinary rest or conversation, and later changes or reclaims the shelter. An adjoining bay provides a separate supply or visiting space. The place remains physically present between visits without rent, arbitrary wear, a home-comfort score or a new wet-tinder prerequisite. Visitors can decline invitations and retain their own choices.

The first delivery is the DG13 **flat canopy**. The separately authorized **cloak lean-to** is now a qualified single-bay sloping arrangement of the same four-corner flexible-cover family, worked through in §7. It is not silently substituted for the existing first-release choice. The different two-bay layout uses the same support, attachment, coverage and conservation rules. One useful flat bay must work end to end before either extension.

The complete first player release includes coarse drying and actual chosen use. A wetting-only proof is an intermediate engineering stage. Light walls/doors, cooperative editing, remembered-home navigation, heavy collapse, general heat/ignition/spread and wet-tinder consequences are separate, gated extensions (§10). Construction is not a prerequisite for the accepted creative first playable.

The [base-world shelter owner](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md) owns materials, layouts, dimensions, work, harmless flexible-cover failure and the controlled rain/moisture profile. The engine supplies typed attachments, finite geometry calculations, exact object transitions, bounded work and truthful receipts. Another world can admit a different sheet, support stock and transmission rule without adding a branch for an item named cloak. No unused external-world loader or executable generated formula language is needed.

### 2.1 Canonical records and units

Add an `assembly` entity kind/component to the current model. Its root has one ordinary **world placement** at the site anchor, with the existing semantic ground support ID. It is not portable, a container, a walkable floor or an inventory item. Its bounded component contains:

- Exact installed family/layout pins, revision and active/retired lifecycle. The root's existing `EntitySpatial.heading` is the sole saved orientation, restricted to the family's admitted orthogonal choices; do not copy it into the assembly component. A retired root retains its identity/pins and retirement time/cause, with no live geometry or placement.
- Part records keyed by the actual item entity ID: admitted role, registered attachment reference and selected interface/shape pin. They reference material; they do not copy quantity, transform, ownership or moisture. The actual local transform belongs to the child's single placement descriptor.
- Joints keyed by stable local joint identity: cover ID/corner, support ID/socket and actual binding item ID. Ground contacts reference the existing support surface. No joint creates extra material.
- The current edit's root invocation ID, if any. The actor's existing action/plan owns duration, phase progress and completed receipts; the assembly does not run a second timer.

Extend `Placement.attached` through a closed, validated attachment descriptor. Preserve the existing tool-equipment attachment. Add registered assembly ports with a local transform and a separately registered garment port. An unknown port string cannot grant attachment behavior. For installed material, the physical parent is the assembly and absolute geometry is **derived** from its root placement, existing heading and that local transform. No independent world XYZ is saved on the child. Validate parent membership, slot uniqueness, cycles and exact item references together. The construction component indexes the same attachment; it is not a competing position writer. The logical root anchor contributes no extra collision body: only the actual parts supply physical shapes.

Each installed post, cover and cord is an individual one-unit item. A whole individual cloak keeps its ID. A selected homogeneous lot may supply individual units through `splitLot` at the committed installation boundary, with existing lineage and exact source-lot quantity receipts. Never create those units during preview. The kit can instantiate individual units directly. Neither the assembly root nor its display name contributes material.

The first committed post phase creates the root and its first actual attachment together; a draft with no installed material has no durable root. Partially installed posts are a valid incomplete assembly with no coverage. A root stays active while installed material or protected lowered salvage references it; it retires only after both are resolved. Store any admitted structural condition as one exact-pinned material attribute (`intact` or `unusable` in this profile), not a second assembly condition. Initial stock is explicitly intact; ordinary weather changes only moisture. Unusable stock cannot supply a support/binding/cover. New damage and repair producers remain outside this delivery.

Add a typed assembly-use claim on the actual item lot, naming the exact root/family, through the object owner. Installation creates it; deliberate reclaim clears it; involuntary lowering retains it. This is a physical use restriction, not a security grant. Installed-part membership must agree with actual attached placement and that claim. Lowered material uses an existing supported world-root item pile, with the actual lot contained there plus its claim; it is no longer in the active part/joint graph. Ground destinations do not add independent world XYZ to individual lots. Derive its recoverable list/reverse lookup from these item references rather than keeping a second writable salvage inventory. Remove failed joints from the active graph; their terminal outcome remains in the phase/event receipt.

Length is metres, time is game seconds, angles are radians and quantities use the existing whole-item unit pins. The two-dimensional plan uses local horizontal coordinates `(u,v)`; its world transform uses the existing Y-up frame. Post heights and cover corner heights are actual dimensions. A cover rectangle is measured **along its plane**, not in horizontal projection. Its plane is the upper material surface; collision thickness extends below it along the downward normal. Subtracting the whole thickness from the lowest upper corner gives a conservative global underside minimum for the worked slope. That shortcut is not a pointwise underside plane: at fixed horizontal coordinates, parallel-plane separation is `thickness / upwardUnitNormal.y`, within the finite extruded shape. Exact overlap/headroom queries use that shape. Geometry tolerances reuse the spatial owner; the authored usable-space margin is a separate product choice.

Moisture is one exact-pinned numeric attribute on each eligible material item, in `[0,1]`; it persists through carrying, wearing, installing and reclaim. Material definitions supply applicability and transmission, not current moisture. Initialize new kit material explicitly as dry. In an already running world, a newly enabled process may initialize only through an explicit reviewed current-state operation; absence of historical rain does not imply a reconstructed past.

For admitted homogeneous fiber, materialize elapsed moisture before split/merge. Splitting copies the same moisture amount and its law pin to both quantities, retaining provenance. Merge only equal current moisture, condition, exact law/definition/unit pins and compatible declaration/use provenance, with no active holds/references. Different wet/dry lots remain separate; no weighted averaging or pristine reset is selected. Individual cloth, posts and installed pieces never merge. Other state-bearing items retain the existing refusal. This is an explicit new PO consumer, not an exemption for arbitrary attributes.

### 2.2 Spatial representation without duplicate geometry

Keep the current authored static `map.spatial` as its existing authority. Add a **derived assembly overlay** to `spatialMap(world)`/the query input used by all physical consumers. Its source is the current root/part/joint records and pinned family shapes; it is discarded and rebuilt after load. Cache identity includes static geometry, assembly/placement revisions, exact shape pins and native backend configuration. A mutable candidate uses its candidate overlay, never the committed world's cache.

Posts contribute actual finite solid boxes. A supported sheet contributes a finite plane with thickness, masks and transmission; it has **no walkable top or support surface**. Extend the spatial shape/query contract for a non-walkable panel rather than representing a roof as a `WalkableSurface` or using its entire sloping bounding box as exact coverage. Planar clipping, ray intersections and overlap/sweep support are trusted computation in `packages/spatial`; timber/fabric eligibility and the supported failure response stay in the admitted world family.

Full bounds index every part, including overhangs, instead of only indexing the root's center. Installed children can be physically exposed/perceived without inventing independent world placements or treating all contained items as world roots. Add that explicit exposed-attachment read to spatial/entity/perception consumers. The current root-only participation filter is insufficient.

Do not append unseen assemblies to the client's currently public starter layout. Server physical queries can use the complete authoritative overlay; client projection and character route-planning inputs use only permitted observed geometry. Keep exact safety checks against physical geometry at execution. An unknown blocker can stop movement without revealing its private IDs or inventory. Learning a structure requires actual perception/inspection, not receiving a navmesh. Known routes retain observation revisions; a distant edit does not update an actor's remembered place.

## 3. Pure evaluation, admission and ordinary work

### 3.1 One route from intention to effect

The selected assembly host accepts a bounded typed plan: installed layout pin, actor-permitted site/surface, root/part selection, exact material lot IDs and quantities, operation and reclaim destination. A language request and direct controls produce the same plan. Authored vocabulary, examples, supported layout options and refusal wording live with the world family. Extend existing request descriptors for typed site/layout/material bindings rather than hiding a JSON graph inside the current scalar argument bag.

The domain assembly owner shares read-only phase evaluation (`evaluateAssemblyPhase`) between plan compilation, preview and each committing phase. It returns eligible/blocking/unsupported/incomplete/stale outcomes, required phase inputs, local geometry/use consequences and costs. It checks:

1. Current actor capability, permission snapshot and learned/inspected references; selected operation/family/pins.
2. Actual site support, whole footprint/overhang bounds, geometry, permitted work stances and usable exit.
3. Exact carried material, role certification, quantity, condition and competing holds.
4. Current assembly/joint/part revisions and whether another edit is active.
5. Occupants, possessions, cover-preservation constraints and all detach destinations.
6. Proposed part/joint/layer bounds and required root/phase/storage/query work admission.

Availability and preview use this evaluation without consuming stock, allocating durable IDs, drawing randomness, interrupting work or emitting events. A preview's digest binds scope/choices; it grants no later authority. Execution repeats the current checks. Do not implement menu availability by performing construction in a throwaway world.

If stopping current work releases held stock or allowance, evaluate that **explicitly selected interruption plus new action** using the existing action owner. The menu cannot promise feasibility from either the old allocation alone or an assumed free interruption. Unclear consequential input selection or occupied removal returns a scoped choice through the existing review path. Known exact plans can begin with one Build action; no per-post approval ceremony.

### 3.2 Phases, inputs and commit point

Compile Build/Extend/Dismantle into a bounded ordinary activity sequence. Each invocation identifies its assembly, phase, exact expected inputs and work stances. Approach is ordinary movement with real path/time and cancellation; fastening visits four reachable bindings. The body's current interaction anchor and handling reach must reach the binding's qualified work point on an exposed post face, below the cloth, rather than the nominal cloth corner inside the post. The authored heights in §7 deliberately fit the current person's anchor/reach; a larger body or higher roof is not automatically supported.

The current `canReachEntity` targets an entity's general interaction anchor, and `findApproachPath` searches integer-coordinate stances. Neither establishes reach to these elevated bindings. Extend the existing approach/reach owner with a typed local work point and the family's bounded face-aligned stance candidates, transformed through the actual part/root placement. Reuse interaction-anchor distance, exact line-of-effect, body clearance, support and route queries; do not create proxy entities, ignore the whole assembly in a ray, or require a finer universal navigation grid. Recheck the actual selected stance and binding at phase completion. The [world profile](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md#qualified-geometry-for-px05) owns their offsets.

Before the first post commits, the plan binds the chosen site and its construction request identity, not an allocated assembly entity. Its first phase receipt supplies the new root ID to the remaining typed phase bindings. A canceled partial build resumes through that actual root/receipt, rather than creating a second root from the same site proposal.

The authored work times are in [the world owner](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md#ordinary-work-and-rearrangement). A phase reserves only its own resources after its native action/work root exists:

| Phase                   | Held inputs                                                                  | Single committed material/geometry effect                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Place a post            | One exact post unit                                                          | Individualize if necessary, attach to the assembly at its validated grounded position; the post becomes a real obstacle.                            |
| Fasten a cover          | One exact cover and four cord units                                          | Attach those five actual units and four complete joints together. All required posts must still qualify. Overhead coverage starts at this boundary. |
| Untie/lower a cover     | No new material; bind the actual five installed units and their destinations | Remove all four joints and transfer the same cover/cords to valid destinations atomically. Coverage ends at this boundary.                          |
| Remove an unloaded post | No new material; bind its actual identity/destination                        | Detach that post after every dependent cover is handled; transfer the same unit.                                                                    |

Holds on installation inputs are **released in the same isolated transition that attaches the objects**. Do not call the craft-consumption operation to settle them: that would destroy stock which must remain installed. A failed final check publishes neither release nor movement. There is no externally visible unlocked interval. The ordinary claim receipt is terminal, and the phase receipt names the actual installed IDs/source quantities. Installed-part protection then replaces the work hold. Reusing the phase identity returns its actual receipt; it never splits or attaches twice.

Use one `draftWorld`/`finishWorld` transition for resources, objects, assembly, geometry invalidation, work and events; `WorldService` commits it through the existing writer. Reuse object/claim operations within that candidate. **Do not assume current `atomicObjects` publishes all those fields:** it copies a deliberate subset of object changes and cannot independently publish a compound assembly/hold/work transition. Extend or factor its owner boundary deliberately if shared reuse is needed; do not add parallel writers.

Completed post/cover phases remain real when later phases stop. The second bay's unused material is not held during the first bay. Cancel, capability loss, death/inactivity, explicit replacement, grant revocation, expired holds and unavailable material release unused holds and the active edit once. A blocked plan may retain its existing bounded intention/receipts, with no active reservation or permanent site lock. Resume recompiles only remaining work against current parts/resources; elapsed interrupted labor is recorded, not refunded or automatically credited to a newly selected phase.

During a compatible, uninterrupted same-version restore, the saved current phase retains its committed progress and holds. This differs from an explicitly canceled/interrupted phase. Never infer completion from elapsed wall time. Work allowance failure refuses new work; mandatory physical/state work uses the existing coherent required-work pause boundary, preserving the last complete world. No invisible second-edit queue or paid retry exists.

## 4. Support, coverage and usable space

### 4.1 Support calculation

For this family the support relation is finite and acyclic: qualified ground → post → four-corner cover, with one actual cord per corner. The family requires four distinct intact posts and four distinct intact bindings for each full cover. A shared post may supply the qualified two sockets, each occupied by a distinct binding. Ground contacts must remain on the same qualified flat support patch; posts do not dig, level, grow or move terrain.

Evaluate each corner's declared joint and local transform, supported post height/ground contact, socket occupancy and sheet dimensions. Cover corners must lie on one plane, with its four anchors forming the admitted rectangle. Calculate in-plane span from the anchors. On each plane axis, require the **actual sheet dimension to be at least the anchor span plus twice the authored minimum unused edge**; do not add overhang to the available material. Merely satisfying a horizontal distance is insufficient for a slope. Qualify minimum underside clearance over the entire advertised use envelope, including the lower overhang. Do not apply a universal timber-strength/stress formula.

Check each post's whole base against the qualified support patch, not only its center. The selected flexible family explicitly admits coincident cloth at its bounded overlap seam and replacement layer: its panels do not reject each other as two rigid solids penetrating. Every panel still counts toward layers, overhead clearance and other solid/body queries. Stable part order resolves equal-height transmission as a declared coarse approximation. This does not permit intersecting posts or give a new rigid material the same exemption.

The sheet's finite thickness also intersects each supporting post tip. The family permits contact among a declared joint's own cover, actual binding and post only where the intersection lies wholly inside the [qualified tip band](../worlds/base/editable-shelters.md#qualified-geometry-for-px05). Read-only evaluation may qualify the proposed joint; the complete material/joint transition must establish it before publication. Generic spatial queries still report the intersection, and the domain family decides whether it is a supported contact. Missing bindings, contact farther down a post, unrelated posts/belongings and body collisions still block. Never suppress an entire pair's collision checks or an assembly's obstacles to make its attachment pass. Charge the bounded joint/contact checks alongside other geometry work.

Missing certification or an unsupported span blocks a new phase. A real committed loss of one required support uses §5's flexible-cover failure once. Other coherent material rules can differ, but only behind a reviewed registered family. No graph of plausible names grants towers, bridges, heavy roofs or arbitrary new loads.

### 4.2 Rain coverage calculation

For a target point, intersect its upward vertical ray with supported cover planes. Keep intersections within the actual finite polygons, above that target; a roof beneath an elevated object does not cover it. Sort top to bottom, with stable part identity for equal-height ties. Reject a candidate that exceeds the admitted layer envelope; do not silently omit layers. Use spatial tolerances for contacts and boundaries, without inflating a dry footprint.

Let incident rain be `R`. Before layer `i`, `R_i = R × product(transmission of higher layers)`; below it, `R_(i+1) = R_i × transmission_i`. Its own outer material receives `R_i`, not the amount after its own protection. For a sheet target, compute incident rain before its own ordered layer, including earlier equal-height layers under the admitted stable tie rule; exclude only its own protection, rather than discarding every coplanar sheet. Gaps have no cover intersection. Multiplication conserves the remaining incident rain; it creates no collectible water. Two hypothetical transmitting-half sheets give one quarter below, not zero. These illustrative values are not the base cloth's tuning.

For a finite body/use/material footprint, clip it against the projected cover/region polygons and integrate the piecewise constant transmission by area. Include height ordering and the target's authored exposed surface. Return covered area, remaining exposure and validity/coverage status. An incomplete query returns unknown/unavailable, never dry. The bounded rectangles/planes make this an analytic calculation, without a droplet simulation or a point-sampling grid that misses narrow seams.

The initial material profile admits only intact weather cloth; ordinary weather supplies no damage. A missing panel is a real gap. A later patch/hole or damaged transmission needs a separately admitted bounded region/state rule; an unspecified tear does not receive invented leakage arithmetic. The cover-preservation check compares exposure over the named occupant/use envelope, not the assembly's name.

### 4.3 Openings, rest and navigation

Derive usable ground space from the cover projection minus post solids, actual obstacles, applicable possession extents and body clearance. It is not a saved `indoors` flag or a guaranteed person count. For movement, inflate obstacles by the current body radius/skin, check headroom at the body's whole footprint and use existing exact support/overlap/sweep plus route providers. A central dry point is insufficient. Ground remains the existing terrain/support; construction creates no floor or elevated connector.

For the first human rest preview, the current physical check remains the person's upright `radius 0.28 m / height 1.75 m` profile. Additionally check a conservative displayed reclining envelope, `1.75 × 0.56 m`, with the authored `0.10 m` margin on each horizontal side: `1.95 × 0.76 m`. This **new shelter-use query** prevents advertising rest where the displayed body clips a post or supply pile. It does not claim horizontal collision is already implemented, change Sleep elsewhere, reserve a bed, add a rest bonus or force sleep at full energy. A place-directed Rest choice moves to a valid checked anchor, rechecks fit and invokes the existing eligible effect. If energy is full, offer ordinary standing/waiting/conversation instead of fictional recovery.

Preserve a reclining user's displayed envelope when evaluating a new installation or reclaimed-material destination, alongside the actual physical shape. Do not rotate all existing actor physics as an incidental construction change. Mechanical posture-aware doors/walls remain a later SW stage blocker. Nearby moving bodies and exposed possessions require a bounded full-extent query for editing; the current point/root index alone cannot prove an empty footprint. New posts cannot be installed through those occupants or belongings. Ordinary users are not locked to a home or granted exclusive floor occupancy.

Both open edges and the two-bay connection must admit a clear approach/exit for the actual body. Installed posts affect movement/sight and use; cords use only their authored overhead binding geometry. Cover planes are non-walkable obstacles for applicable queries and support their admitted sensory transmission. An open canopy has no enclosed-room, lock, heat or acoustic-privacy promise. Existing speech/hearing remains controlling.

Record old/new affected bounds and source IDs at a geometry edit; invalidate only intersecting geometry, exposure, route and perception dependencies where the provider supports it. Recheck the next movement segment against current physical geometry immediately. The current Recast provider may rebuild the whole bounded map/profile in its existing worker: **tile-local rebuilding is not delivered and is not a first-shelter prerequisite**. Retain that fallback's measured qualification gate, coalesce redundant build inputs and distinguish technical preparation from game labor. Camera-only, moisture-only and name-only changes do not rebake navigation.

The server navigation coordinator currently keys preparation/publication on `world.map` identity and timeline. A part edit changes the assembly overlay without replacing that static map, so introduce an explicit derived-geometry revision through coordinator task/message, worker key and `WorldService.preparedNavigation` acceptance. Include the permitted knowledge-geometry revision for an actor's route. Recheck both at publication; an old result cannot pass merely because the original map object remains. Keep the current one-worker/queue admission and unavailability outcome. Export exact panels to the backend with non-walkable area semantics: Recast's slope classifier must not turn a horizontal fabric panel into a walkable roof. The deterministic support query must also reject it as a floor.

## 5. Alteration, failure, reclaim and permission

### 5.1 Exact edit behavior

| Request                                      | Required sequence and consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Add the second bay                           | Reuse the two declared edge posts; install two new posts, then a new cover/four cords. The completed first bay remains effective.                                                                                                                                            |
| Replace a cover                              | Name incoming/outgoing objects and all destinations. Install-before-remove is allowed only if extra real cover/cords, sockets and layer limits permit it. Otherwise offer remove-before-install with actual temporary exposure. A keep-covered constraint may refuse (§7.3). |
| Remove one cover                             | Untie its four joints and reclaim the same five materials. Other independent covers remain. No material condition is reset.                                                                                                                                                  |
| Remove a shared post                         | Show both cover dependencies; prepare the cover-first sequence. Direct ordinary removal of a loaded post refuses with that remedy.                                                                                                                                           |
| Change installed orientation/height/location | Detach affected covers and unloaded supports, carry those exact materials, then rebuild a valid arrangement. Changing a preview is free; an installed transform is not a teleporting edit.                                                                                   |
| Dismantle                                    | Lower covers first, then unloaded posts; the exact destination plan is visible once. Do not ask for unchanged per-part confirmations. Preserve completed phases if a later destination fails.                                                                                |

The assembly retains its root identity through extensions/replacements while it remains the same physical place. When no installed parts or protected salvage claims remain, the same assembly owner removes root placement/geometry and writes its retained retirement tombstone, using `rootMembershipChanged` and the existing record/history boundaries. Current `retireItem` consumes item quantity and is not an assembly-root retirement API; extend validators and retained-entity residency for this new non-material root explicitly. It deletes no occupants, containers, materials or memories. A completely lowered shelter with unreclaimed material keeps its root/claim reference, without overhead coverage. Building at another site creates a new physical root. Old observations keep their old subject/location; a future personal place association may explicitly relate the sites, but cannot rewrite history. Declared title does not mutate during any transfer.

### 5.2 Supported outside failure

An admitted creator edit or future qualified outside event that removes a required support invokes the same compound owner. Finish material progression to the event time, update the actual support once, find every dependent cover, and lower each affected cover **once**. End overhead coverage at that boundary. Deactivate its joints and put its actual surviving cover/cords in a recoverable site placement with the existing assembly reclaim restriction. Surviving independent posts/covers remain. Destroyed material yields only the outside event's explicit actual outcome; it does not produce an intact replacement post.

The light-cover family supplies a non-solid draped fallback, so the same material can remain at the site without injury, blocking a reclining user or covering an exit. Use a dedicated supported pile for that actual lowered cloth/cord bundle, with the registered fallback's nonblocking physical shape; do not merge unrelated solid belongings into it or make every item pile non-solid. Its bounds still participate in exposure, discovery and reclaim. It is not automatically picked up or worn. This is an explicit world approximation, not a general falling-object model. No ordinary rain damage, structure-combat family or hostile demolition is added here. Unsupported ground-loss/heavy-load consequences block those new configurations before admission.

A support edit may affect multiple neighboring assemblies. Discover all reverse dependencies and charge the complete candidate before publishing; insufficient mandatory work stops the transition coherently, not after lowering only some covers. Creator tools cannot delete an attached item or definition through a raw cascade. If a changed ground support cannot provide the admitted fallback, refuse that creator operation and expose the dependency; do not guess occupant relocation.

### 5.3 Reclaim destinations and protections

Reclaim preselects actual supported carrying/container or reachable ground destinations and checks capacity, access, posture, exposure applicability and support. Under the first active moisture profile, cloth goes to direct carrying or exposed ground; other parts may use an admitted container. If a destination changes before effect, the phase stops with the cover still installed. If a previous phase/outside event already lowered it, it remains at its actual site and pickup may fail separately. Report both facts; neither return the roof to the air nor manufacture an inventory copy.

Installed and involuntarily lowered parts have an assembly claim reference enforced by the **ordinary object-access/mutation owner**, including pickup/drop/move/equip, splitting, retirement and creator edit routes. Only an authorized assembly detach/reclaim transition may clear it. After deliberate reclaim to an ordinary shared ground pile, ordinary pile access still applies; do not imply a new anti-theft law. Subsequent construction still needs input-use permission. A cloak and spare tarp remain mechanically different after release.

### 5.4 Current permission, not custody or title

Add scoped construction grants beneath the existing **operational authority repository**, with world, actor, qualified site bounds/surface or exact assembly ID, operation set, eligible material-use references and revision/revocation. The prepared scene has one builder; its finite kit is explicitly authorized for these uses. Qualifying builder-produced stock must retain certified eligible-input provenance. Another person's stock needs an explicit supported construction-use grant/transfer; ordinary handover supplies custody alone and remains insufficient in this first profile.

The server validates the current account/control scope, then supplies a plain immutable construction-permission input to the domain command/preview/native advancement. Add that parameter through `nativeOperationAvailable`, `executeCommand`, activity compilation/completion and `advanceWorldSlices`; do not put writable security authority in the rewindable assembly component. ## 6. Rain, moisture, actor knowledge and save lifecycle

### 6.1 Two registered processes, not general climate

It is a finite process/event schedule, not a seasonal-weather engine. Equivalent new-material producers/consumers can reuse it without a model query per part or time step.

At every interval, calculate each eligible material's effective incident rain `r` from §4.2. For the base approximation:

- While `r > 0`, `m_next = min(1, m + r × elapsedGameSeconds / 1800)`.
- When `r = 0` and the separate drying consumer is installed, `m_next = max(0, m - elapsedGameSeconds / 5400)`.
- Wetting-only intermediate builds have no negative contribution. There is no simultaneous evaporation, heating, fuel, water inventory, body effect or craft penalty.
