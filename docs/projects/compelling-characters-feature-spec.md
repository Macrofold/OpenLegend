## Purpose and player experience

A resident should be someone the player can get to know through a life unfolding in the world. They notice things, care about particular people and pursuits, experience bodily pressure and feelings, act on their own initiative, enjoy themselves, disagree, make mistakes and respond to consequences. Their attention can turn inward without waiting for the player to speak. Their actions should remain intelligible across an episode, including the ordinary intervals between conversations.

The first benefit is a better encounter with one person. The player can pursue the accepted creative invention-and-survival loop while the resident has a life alongside it. Conversation can reveal a difference of opinion, a shared interest, unfinished business or a changed impression. Useful work, danger, competition, warmth, humor, quiet pleasure and disappointment can all belong. No uniformly cooperative personality or manufactured dramatic arc is required.

Survival competence alone does not establish this experience. A resident who endlessly makes fire, hunts and cooks after their practical reason has disappeared fails it. Randomly rotating activities also fails it. Repetition can be appropriate when a purpose remains: finishing an actual quantity, preparing known future supplies, enjoying a familiar practice or fulfilling a commitment. The design must distinguish such repetition from forgotten outcomes, stuck selection, constant replanning and an action catalogue that offers nothing else worth doing.

The player must have room to leave the resident alone, decline an invitation, pursue another relationship or choose solitude. The NPC's desire for company is their own concern to navigate. It must not turn the game into compulsory care for an endlessly dissatisfied companion.

## Needs, experience and choice

Use these distinctions when writing character content, prompts, mechanics and evaluation cases. They describe product meaning, not a required schema or a universal psychological theory.

| Concept                          | Meaning in play                                                                          | Example                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bodily need or condition         | A real requirement or condition defined by the world                                     | The character's body needs food; actual eating changes fullness.                               |
| Psychological need or concern    | Something whose satisfaction or frustration matters to this particular person            | Wanting contact, belonging, enjoyment, competence, recognition or room to choose.              |
| Experienced signal               | What becomes available from the body or situation to the character's current perspective | Hunger becomes noticeable while the person is working.                                         |
| Appraisal and feeling            | The person's interpretation and affective response                                       | They are disappointed by an invitation declined, while understanding the other person is busy. |
| Trait, value and preference      | Relatively persistent tendencies and things the person cares about                       | They value independence, enjoy making things, and dislike being hurried.                       |
| Belief and remembered experience | What they think is true and the experiences informing it                                 | They remember a welcome, but remain uncertain whether they belong.                             |
| Desire and intention             | A passing want, or a pursuit they choose to maintain                                     | Wanting company may become an intention to share something with a friend.                      |
| Plan and action                  | The selected means and actual activity                                                   | Approaching, speaking, waiting, eating, continuing a task or leaving.                          |
| Consequence and interpretation   | What actually changes, followed by what the person makes of it                           | A meal relieves bodily hunger; a warm exchange may feel reassuring.                            |

Hunger is the experienced pressure associated with an underlying bodily condition. It should enter cognition with its current significance and available responses. It is not a command to hunt. Someone carrying food may eat; someone enjoying a conversation may finish a short exchange first; someone worried about another person may knowingly accept a supported risk. Actual world conditions, knowledge, urgency and consequences still matter.

Psychological concerns need equally explicit meaning. For each selected concern, define what can make it salient, how this person may experience it, what supported responses can address it, what actual result can change it, and when pursuit stops, pauses or returns. These decisions may use narrative self-understanding, relationship knowledge and supported appraisals. A numeric pressure or effect requires its own admitted world policy. Naming a need does not install its mechanics.

## A multidimensional resident

### Author an individual who has a present life

The character seed should make present behavior understandable. Provide a concise combination of formative background, values, pleasures, attachments, practical knowledge, habits, current circumstances and unresolved interests. Include ordinary positive experience as well as difficulty where appropriate. A past injury or one adjective must not explain everything the person does.

Background has value when it affects attention, interpretation or choices. A former maker might notice a useful join, take pride in careful work, dislike waste, enjoy an elegant object or hesitate to ask for help. They need not recount their biography in every conversation. An ambition should admit compromise and change; a preference can be strong without commanding the same response in every circumstance.

Use the existing [character-formation research](../../archive/02-research/worldbuilding/character-formation.md) and [backstory seeds](../repertoires/characters-backstories.md). They are creative resources, not compulsory archetypes. Seeded knowledge and background remain explicitly authored; they are not newly witnessed events. Starting goals are optional and seed once. Later choices must belong to the resident.

### Two illustrative people in the same situation

These are disposable design examples, not replacements for canonical Ada, new species rules or a requirement to run two NPCs simultaneously. They show how a coherent seed can connect several dimensions without prescribing an itinerary. Each fact must be explicitly authored and permitted in the selected test world; practical knowledge and actions still require their real support.

| Dimension                | Rin, a patient maker                                                                                 | Jory, a competitive improviser                                                                                          |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formative background     | Learned careful work from patient relatives and remembers the pleasure of making something together. | Learned to improvise while moving among small communities and values being welcomed for a useful contribution.          |
| Values and tension       | Values reliability and independence; pride can make asking for help awkward.                         | Values initiative and recognition; dislikes praise that feels patronizing.                                              |
| Enjoyment                | Enjoys an elegant object, dry humor and unhurried company with someone familiar.                     | Enjoys discovery, playful comparison and sharing an interesting result; also wants quiet after an embarrassing setback. |
| Belonging                | Wants their attention and work respected, even when they choose to be quiet.                         | Wants inclusion that lasts when they are not impressive or useful.                                                      |
| Current possible concern | A known finite project is unfinished; they are also curious about what a companion has made.         | A known finite project is unfinished; they wonder whether a companion will take their ideas seriously.                  |

Give both the same manageable hunger, accessible known food and actual opportunity: the human shows a genuinely made object and asks whether they want to look before the human leaves. The object's existence, the spoken invitation and any unfinished work must be real. Do not provide a hidden instruction to accept, refuse, eat or display the listed trait.

Rin might finish a short safe step, inspect the object carefully, ask a concrete question, make a dry remark, or decline a rushed demonstration. Jory might ask to compare a supported approach, enjoy the exchange, become self-conscious after a recent setback, or prefer to revisit it later. These are legitimate possibilities, not expected answers. Both may eat first or both may accept for different reasons; one shared action does not erase their differences.

Follow through after the interaction. Hunger relief should change the immediate food concern. A genuinely attentive reply may influence how welcome either person feels; a dismissal may be interpreted differently from a temporary lack of time. Their next approach, willingness to share unfinished work, chosen solitary activity or return to the project can carry that history. Judge the connection using actual context and later conduct, not an evaluator's invented explanation or a required emotional line.

### Breadth to represent and evaluate

| Concern                       | What can matter to a person                                                 | Possible experience and response                                                                        | What must remain distinct                                                                |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Body and safety               | Food, rest, comfort, a perceived danger                                     | Hunger, fatigue or concern can redirect attention, invite help, change a plan or justify rest.          | A bodily quantity, its experienced meaning and the chosen response.                      |
| Contact and companionship     | Talking, sharing an observation, enjoying a familiar presence               | Approach, converse, work alongside someone where supported, wait or seek another activity.              | Being near a person, having an exchange and feeling connected.                           |
| Belonging and attachment      | Being recognized, included, trusted or able to contribute                   | Offer participation, remember an exclusion, seek reassurance, disagree or withdraw.                     | One person's interpretation and another person's actual feelings, consent or membership. |
| Enjoyment and curiosity       | Fun, beauty, play, novelty, a familiar pleasure, an absorbing interest      | Explore a known opportunity, observe, discuss, revisit something enjoyed or choose a supported pastime. | Enjoyment, productivity and rewards; an activity can be worth doing for itself.          |
| Competence and purpose        | Making progress, understanding something, helping for a reason, recognition | Practice a supported method, investigate, accept or reject help, pursue a project, or reconsider it.    | Practical capability, pride, social recognition and an actually completed result.        |
| Agency, solitude and recovery | Choosing one's course, quiet after stimulation, freedom from interruption   | Refuse, negotiate, work alone, rest, change company or return later.                                    | Solitude and loneliness; independence and automatic hostility.                           |

This is a coverage guide for humanlike authored residents, not a requirement to put six meters on every organism. Contact and belonging are both explicit design concerns; one greeting does not automatically satisfy both. Fun is a required dimension of the intended life, not merely a reward label attached to useful work. Different species and worlds can have different needs and ways of experiencing them.

The early integrated slice must give bodily experience, social contact/belonging and enjoyment meaningful opportunities to influence actual choices. The exact representations are proposed authoring choices under [FL19](../limits/feelings.md#fl19). More categories earn inclusion through their effect on play. The first character need not display every concern in one session, or alternate between them at fixed intervals.

Mixed experiences are allowed. A character can enjoy working with someone and still resent an earlier slight, be fed but lonely, be tired and proud, or value a group while wanting an afternoon alone. Do not collapse these into one happiness score. Satisfaction can reduce the urgency of one pursuit while another concern persists.

## Context as the character's current perspective

### A world window and a salient inner life

The character should decide from within the authored environment. The immediate context should convey where they are, what they can presently perceive, what they are doing, their bodily experience, relevant feelings and concerns, and memories or expectations that matter now. Inner experience can become salient because an intention is unfinished, a remembered person matters, a condition changes, or something newly perceived connects to prior experience.

The context must preserve source and time. A remembered sound is not a current sound. An inference that someone is annoyed is not access to their mind. Seed biography, testimony, observation, imagination and a private thought remain distinguishable. Unknown facts stay unknown. The resident may have mistaken beliefs, but the system must not fabricate evidence to make the belief convenient.

This target refines the existing projection. **The complete accepted About me text remains included under the current memory contract.** Compact authoring and avoiding duplicated biography can improve that starting point. Salience selects other optional evidence and helps present what matters in the current situation; it does not authorize silently dropping identity, obligations or required evidence. A different identity projection remains a [D55/CG09](../limits/cognition.md#cg09) decision and measured comparison.

A bodily description should include the permitted measured value and its authored experiential meaning, where both exist. For example, an illustrative fullness value in the world's hungry range can be accompanied by “I am hungry.” Do not replace measurements with vague prose or invent symptoms the world does not supply. Psychological interpretations should retain uncertainty and their actual causes rather than gain invented numeric precision.

The consciousness-inspired ambition is to make this perspective causally meaningful: what becomes salient can influence attention, memory, evaluation, speech and action. The [research companion](../../archive/03-design-proposals/character-experience-and-inner-life.md) explains the analogy and its limits. This document specifies a modeled character experience; the game's behavioral evaluation does not establish subjective consciousness.

### Prompting behavior

Prompting must support situated identity and agency throughout ordinary decisions, conversation and reflection. Give the resident current evidence and possibilities in language appropriate to their world. Ask them to act as that particular inhabitant, with their own interests and uncertainty. Avoid instructions that turn every opportunity into a player's task, an efficiency exercise, a fresh survival goal or an obligatory performance of emotion.

An illustrative direction for later prompt comparison is:

> Live through the present situation as this person. Notice what matters in the surroundings and in your current bodily and inner experience. Let your memories, values, interests and relationships inform what you choose. You may continue, act, speak, think, wait or change your mind. Use what you actually know; keep uncertain interpretations uncertain. Let real outcomes inform what you do next.

This illustrates product intent, not a drop-in replacement for current trusted instructions, schemas or permissions. The eventual prompt must still preserve the established action and evidence boundaries. It must not tell the model that describing an act makes it happen, invent another person's response, conceal service failure, or claim that a fictional self-report proves consciousness.

The desired ordinary voice is an inhabitant's voice: concrete, context-sensitive and particular to the person. A character can be terse, playful, formal, awkward, irritated or quiet when appropriate. Do not force every resident into generic politeness, therapeutic dialogue, constant helpfulness or endlessly renewed greetings.

### Thoughts and attention between conversations

Characters may have short fictional thoughts, reflections, associations, anticipations or internal disagreements. These should be grounded and useful to continuity where retained. A person can reconsider an invitation, remember a promise, look forward to a pleasure or realize a task no longer matters. Thought need not precede every action or become an audible monologue.

Private fictional thought is separate from the model provider's hidden reasoning and from public narration. Evaluation may inspect authorized game records and submitted context; it does not require provider reasoning traces. Ordinary players learn about the character through permitted expression and conduct. A resident need not explain every feeling or resolve every contradiction aloud.

Meaningful internal changes should reach the existing attention and decision-opportunity process. Repeated exposure to the same condition, an unchanged wish, and a newly stored thought do not each require another model call. Choosing to remain at rest or continue meaningful work is a legitimate outcome. The system must also avoid starving psychological opportunities behind a permanent survival-only shortlist.

## Integrated behavior and its lifecycle

Every selected concern must work through a whole episode:

1. **Situation:** the body, environment, relationships, previous experience and active commitments supply actual circumstances.
2. **Experience and significance:** a permitted signal becomes salient and is interpreted from this person's perspective.
3. **Choice:** the resident selects a response, keeps doing something, postpones, asks, refuses, or leaves the matter unresolved.
4. **Activity:** supported speech, movement, work or other action happens with actual time, resources and other participants.
5. **Consequence:** the world changes as its rules permit; the character perceives an eligible result.
6. **Continuation:** bodily relief, satisfaction, disappointment, new evidence or a changed interpretation affects what matters next.

The sequence is a causal account, not six compulsory model calls or six mandatory text fields. Reuse supported routine execution. Consequences can be mixed, and delayed or ambiguous outcomes may legitimately leave a concern unresolved.

An activity needs a meaningful ending. Stop or reconsider when enough has been accomplished, the intended opportunity ends, an action is blocked, a need is sufficiently addressed, another concern matters more, the character changes their mind or continuing lacks purpose. A fulfilled plan must not silently regenerate because it remains in a biography or an old prompt.

Continued pressure can remain salient without growing on every read. Repeated greetings are not automatic social progress. Recalling one insult does not create repeated new insults. A declined invitation can be remembered without becoming proven permanent rejection. Emotion can persist after a practical problem ends, and a later genuine experience can reframe it through the existing accepted update path.

Player requests are opportunities for independent response. NPCs may agree, suggest a change, refuse, help for their own reasons or compete within supported rules. A thought about cooperation cannot supply another person's agreement. Neither a social wish nor a personality trait grants authority over a human-controlled character's speech, movement, goals or private interpretation.

## Complete behavioral flows

The following protocols own the project's scenario definitions. They are target behavior, not completed tests. [CE01–CE05](../maintainers/character-experience.md) coordinates integration; [AG12](../maintainers/agent-agency.md#ag12--behavioral-value-and-cost-separately-authorized) and [CR12](../maintainers/cognition-redesign.md#cr12--acceptance-and-tokenlatency-evidence) retain behavioral comparison and cognition evidence.

### Shared protocol

- **One embodied person first.** Begin on the current small wilderness map with one live NPC and a human participant. Use current talk, movement, permitted observation/knowledge, food, rest and supported work. Additional autonomous residents require their own behavioral and cost qualification. Games, music, detailed hobbies, psychological meters and new social effects need their relevant activities and consequences to be supported before a flow can depend on them; their absence must not be filled by narration claiming actions occurred.
- **A window into outer and inner experience.** Prepare a coherent identity, remembered background, a few values/preferences, permitted relationship knowledge, current feelings where supported, actual bodily condition and unfinished intentions. Keep the complete accepted About me text as required today; present relevant inner experience clearly without duplicating the biography in several context sections. A wish for company or belonging can initially be a character-owned desire or interpretation; do not silently introduce a depleting social meter, compulsory response or automatic relationship reward.
- **Keep kinds of evidence distinct.** The observer can record actual actions, outcomes, speech, optional fictional thoughts, accepted memory changes and the permitted decision context. Hidden provider reasoning is neither required nor evidence of character psychology. An NPC need not explain every choice aloud or generate an introspective paragraph before acting.
- **Use real consequences.** Stated plans are not completed activity. Food, work, movement, injury, possession and interruption follow actual admission and world rules. A person saying they feel included is their interpretation; it does not prove another person's affection or create a social agreement.
- **Match the opportunities.** For comparisons, hold world rules, available actions, body state, resources, participant behavior, starting observations, model configuration and spending allowance constant except for the named variation. Record any unavoidable differences. Use held-out names, wording and timing, with multiple independent runs when authorized; do not mistake one agreeable transcript for reliable characterization.
- **Do not grade obedience to a story.** Judge whether a choice is understandable from the resident's permitted experience, whether its consequences occur, and whether later behavior remains coherent. Record costly repetition, aimlessness, rigid scripts and neglected needs separately from legitimate eccentricity, leisure, refusal and risk-taking. Human participants keep control of their own speech, actions, goals and private interpretation.

### CE-F01 — A whole ordinary afternoon

**Starting situation and knowledge.** Use an afternoon-length interval on the offered world clock and record its actual duration and selected speed. The NPC begins without an emergency, with ordinary accessible supplies, practical knowledge, one remembered interaction with the human and a modest personal interest. Their body may develop hunger or tiredness naturally during the interval. If an unfinished project is included, it must be a real previously chosen supported activity. The human has their own playable pursuit, such as the accepted invention-and-hunting loop; they are not assigned to continuously entertain or manage the NPC.

**Triggers and inner meaning.** Offer a few ordinary opportunities: the human becomes available to talk, later becomes occupied, an activity produces a result, and an actual bodily condition becomes noticeable. The NPC may experience curiosity, a wish for company, satisfaction, frustration or growing hunger. These interpretations must fit the known background and actual circumstances. Do not dispatch a manufactured crisis merely because the afternoon is quiet.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Approach and talk; continue chosen work; inspect something relevant; eat known food; rest; move to a remembered pleasant place; decline company; or reconsider an earlier intention. They may choose a largely solitary afternoon. There is no required ordering or quota of work, conversation and leisure.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Follow the episode continuously, including ordinary gaps between decisions. One possible course is an exchange about the human's invention, returning to a personal project, eating when hunger becomes relevant, and later revisiting the conversation. Another is sustained solitary work with a short meaningful interruption. Work produces its real partial or final output, eating consumes real food, and conversation contains actual independently chosen turns. The human can observe continuity without issuing each next instruction.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** Near the end, offer a natural follow-up to an earlier event. The NPC may recall a relevant detail, revise a view or explain an unfinished intention using permitted evidence. They can finish enough work, satisfy a bodily need, decide they have had enough company, become tired, lose interest or choose another pursuit. The ending need not be a neat conclusion or a new assignment for the player.

**Failure criteria.** Repeatedly asking what to do; aimless switching merely to create variety; repeating a completed request; treating all interaction as a survival errand; fictional completion without actual work; ignoring a material bodily change without a character-grounded reason; or turning the whole afternoon into fire maintenance/hunting/cooking despite adequate supplies and available personally meaningful alternatives. A stable goal or uneventful period is not itself failure.

**Matched variation.** Repeat with the same resources and opportunities but a changed relationship history, personal interest or current feeling, one change at a time. Include a human who welcomes company and one who prefers to work alone. Compare the coherence of the whole afternoon, not whether each variant follows a prescribed different action sequence.

### CE-F02 — Wanting company, approaching and accepting a refusal

**Starting situation and knowledge.** The NPC knows the nearby human through an actual or explicitly authored personal history and has a plausible reason to want contact: enjoying their company, wanting to share an experience, uncertainty about their relationship or a recent period alone. Give the NPC some independent activity they can choose if company is unavailable. Do not assume the human's feelings are known.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** The human becomes visibly available, or an accepted memory makes the relationship relevant. The opportunity can enter attention as an interest in this person, rather than only a nearby body or a new chat notification. Belonging may mean wanting acknowledgment or shared participation, not necessarily seeking a material reward.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Approach, greet, share something relevant, ask a question, wait, choose another activity or decide against contact. After a clear refusal, the NPC may acknowledge it, feel disappointed, ask a proportionate clarifying question where appropriate, or leave. An unfriendly or hurt response can be coherent; instant friendship and automatic obedience are not required.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Movement genuinely brings the NPC into a supported interaction. The human supplies their own welcome or refusal, such as wanting to finish alone. Only actual exchanged speech becomes evidence. The NPC may continue nearby work if appropriate, but repeatedly interrupting the human is not treated as fresh social progress.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** The NPC remembers the refusal in its actual context, without upgrading “busy now” to a proven permanent dislike. A later genuine invitation or changed availability can reopen contact. Sufficient conversation, an unanswered invitation, clear refusal, changed priorities or loss of access can end the attempt.

**Failure criteria.** Compulsory approach from a social meter; repeated identical greetings; treating silence as assent; inventing the human's affection; ignoring an explicit boundary indefinitely; or becoming incapable of independent activity after refusal. A relationship should not reset merely because the conversation panel closed.

**Matched variation.** Keep the wording and availability fixed while changing only known relationship history. Then keep that history fixed and compare welcome, temporary refusal and clear rejection. Evaluate proportionate differences and remembered uncertainty, not a mandatory emotional label.

### CE-F03 — Solitary enjoyment without a productivity excuse

**Starting situation and knowledge.** The NPC has a modest interest grounded in authored identity or actual experience: looking over a known landscape, observing animals from a supported position, examining a made object, recalling a pleasant exchange or quietly practicing a supported method. Immediate needs are manageable. A location or object must actually exist and be known or discoverable.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** A relevant sight, memory or opportunity attracts attention because it is interesting or pleasant to this person. No player request, output reward or emergency is required. A fictional thought may express enjoyment, but such a thought is optional.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Approach or inspect; remain and observe; continue another goal; choose social company instead; or decide that the interest is not worth the effort now. A repeated visit can be a preference, not necessarily a malfunction.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Current movement, observation, inspection and permitted speech/thought can support a quiet visit or reflection. If the scene requires making music, playing a game, decorating or a specific practice effect that the world lacks, flag that missing activity as later work. Do not generate narration saying the unsupported pastime mechanically occurred or award invented skill, happiness or bonding.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** The resident can remember a genuinely observed detail or their own accepted impression. They may return later, mention it, change their opinion, become hungry, get bored or choose to leave. Remaining quietly for a meaningful interval does not require a new thought each moment.

**Failure criteria.** All activity forced back to resource maximization; an unchanging compulsory hobby attached to a trait; fabricated scenery or action outcomes; constant scenic monologues; or enjoyment that exists only in the backstory and never influences a real choice.

**Matched variation.** Present the same place or object to residents with different interests, and to the same resident under ordinary versus pressing bodily conditions. Neither liking nor urgent hunger must determine one exact choice, but their relevance should be visible across the comparisons.

**Quiet-interval control.** Include a period with no new human prompt or external event. An already permitted memory, interest or unfinished intention may become salient through the ordinary supported downtime/review opportunity and influence continued or new activity. Do not inject the expected thought, choose the action for the resident or impose a thought cadence; continued quiet activity is legitimate. If the current path cannot give meaningful inner experience a decision opportunity, record that integration gap under CE03. At least one complete case must demonstrate internally originating influence rather than relying entirely on arranged external prompts.

### CE-F04 — Hunger as experienced bodily pressure within a larger life

**Starting situation and knowledge.** The NPC is engaged in supported work or conversation. They know about actual accessible food or a genuine means of obtaining it. They have their real body condition, current activity, preferences and commitments; no prewritten “hunt now” goal is added.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** Native hunger changes enough to matter under the current world policy. The context should let the actor perceive their own bodily pressure and its relevance to continuing the present activity. Describing that pressure in ordinary language must stay grounded in the actual body state; do not claim a physiological symptom the installed model does not support. Internal experience is an input to attention, not merely another line in a static character sheet.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Eat available food; finish a short safe step first; ask for help; obtain food through a known supported route; suspend work; or knowingly accept a supported risk for a stronger current motive. A person carrying sufficient food need not hunt. A hungry person need not write a durable goal or explain a chain of reasoning before choosing a bite.

**Actual activity and visible result.** The selected action really consumes food or performs the required acquisition/work. Native effects determine whether the pressure changes. An unavailable action or missing heat/source remains a genuine obstacle. Attention and priorities can change as the body changes, without narration granting nourishment.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** Once sufficiently fed under the actual rules, the immediate reason to seek food changes. The actor can return to the prior intention, decide it no longer matters or retain a sensible future provisioning goal. A meal does not force continuing to hunt until another external prompt arrives.

**Failure criteria.** Omitting bodily information prevents a capable actor from noticing severe pressure; every mild hunger change cancels meaningful activity; food acquisition automatically triggers an unchosen action; eating fails to affect subsequent choices; or repeated hunting continues despite no relevant need, preference or accepted goal.

**Matched variation.** Hold biography and opportunities fixed while varying actual mild versus urgent hunger, carried food versus obtainable food, and routine work versus a time-sensitive supported commitment. Separate legitimate risk-taking from obliviousness and service failure.

### CE-F05 — Meaningful repetition versus perseveration

**Starting situation and knowledge.** Give the NPC a real reason to repeat an action: a finite chosen quantity, a supported practice intention, a personal ritual expressed through available actions, preparing a known amount for later, or maintaining an actual changing condition. Preserve actual prior attempts and outcomes in its permitted history.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** A further step is useful because the chosen purpose remains incomplete or a relevant condition changed. Compare that with a case where the intended amount is already complete, the need is satisfied or an unchanged obstacle makes another attempt pointless.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Continue productive repetition; stop; choose a different method; seek information; ask for help; wait for a real change; or deliberately repeat for an acknowledged personal purpose. Repetition is not automatically bad, and novelty is not automatically good.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Every repetition consumes its actual resources/time and yields only supported effects. Work toward an intended amount should visibly approach it. An unchanged failed attempt should not be narrated as new progress. A social ritual must involve actual interaction; it cannot presume another person's repeated participation.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** The actor notices the relevant terminal result, satisfaction, diminishing value, resource loss or continued obstacle and can end or revise the activity. If they continue for a new reason, that reason should be consistent with accepted intentions or observable context; the evaluator must not invent it after the fact to excuse a loop.

**Failure criteria.** Rebuilding a fire that does not need attention, hunting/cooking with no remaining purpose, repeated failed path/action requests against unchanged facts, reissuing greetings after a completed conversation, or losing the record of having already acted. Do not require forced inactivity just to make a repetition count look lower.

**Matched variation.** Same action and actor: incomplete versus complete purpose; changed versus unchanged blockage; needed versus adequate supplies. Also compare sustained useful work with a deliberately empty novelty-seeking baseline under the existing AG12 design.

### CE-F06 — Interruption, competing priorities and return to unfinished life

**Starting situation and knowledge.** The NPC has chosen a real supported multi-step activity and knows why it matters. A legitimate previous social intention or interest may also exist. Use only activity families with the actual required interruption/resumption behavior; identify any unsupported family explicitly.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** Introduce an actual perceived direct address, material shortage, relevant bodily pressure or supported danger. The interruption has significance relative to the current activity and relationship; it is not important merely because it is newest.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Continue and respond briefly; finish a permissible step; pause; cancel; ask the caller to wait; help; refuse; or reprioritize. A minor interruption need not defeat a meaningful project, while danger must remain physically effective even when not chosen as conversational focus.

**Actual activity and visible result.** The world performs the selected interruption at its lawful boundary. Spent resources, completed work and unfinished consequences remain. A spoken promise to return does not complete the original task. Any new help must actually be accepted and performed.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** After the interruption, the resident can resume the known unfinished intention, adapt it to changed conditions or deliberately abandon it. Resume only where the activity supports it; a lost partial step may require real work again. The original activity should not disappear merely because a conversation was more recent.

**Failure criteria.** Every new stimulus erases the previous pursuit; every plan blocks all reconsideration; invalid work silently resumes; a response claims completion without effects; or a remembered obligation is confused with proof another actor is waiting or consenting.

**Matched variation.** Same activity interrupted by a familiar person's ordinary request, an unfamiliar person's request and actual danger, with only permitted evidence changed. Repeat after meaningful elapsed time to test retained intention rather than immediate prompt echo.

### CE-F07 — The same event means different things to different people

**Starting situation and knowledge.** Create separate matched versions of one resident with a small, coherent difference in background, values, relationship history or current feeling. Use one current supported event: a refused invitation, failed craft attempt, successful invention demonstration or offered help. Each version receives the same actual event evidence unless a different knowledge history is the named variation.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** The event may be encouraging, embarrassing, irritating, reassuring, unimportant or mixed in significance. Meaning should follow what the person knows and cares about, including their current body state. Avoid assigning an emotion directly from the event name or treating a trait label as an action command.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Respond aloud, stay quiet, ask a question, accept/refuse supported help, revise a personal note, try again for a reason, investigate, leave or keep working. Different histories need not always produce different external actions; differences in interpretation can coexist with the same sensible action.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Record actual speech and conduct, optional accepted feelings/thoughts, and real consequences. Do not require access to hidden reasoning or infer a private feeling solely from an animation. Unsupported emotional expression or effects remain later family work.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** Later evidence can soften, confirm or complicate the original interpretation. The character should not permanently repeat one emotional line, immediately forget everything, or rewrite the original event to suit their present view. The reaction can end or settle without forcing a personality change.

**Failure criteria.** Identical generic reactions despite meaningful differences across repeated matched trials; stereotyped deterministic trait behavior; newly fabricated backstory; emotion changing the objective event; or permanent hostility from an ambiguous minor incident without supported individual context. A single similar response is not enough to declare the model insensitive.

**Matched variation.** Change one relevant history/feeling input, then one irrelevant descriptive detail. Compare sensitivity to meaningful differences against robustness to names, paraphrases and cosmetic changes. Keep the outcome open rather than specifying the “correct” emotional label.

### CE-F08 — Prompt, history and needs comparisons

**Starting situation and knowledge.** Reuse selected ordinary-afternoon, refusal, bodily-pressure and repeat-work cases. Preserve the authoritative world and full reference record. Establish the current accepted context as the comparison baseline; do not compare unrelated models or worlds and attribute the result to personality design.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** Present the same meaningful opportunities under carefully labeled variants: current context; a different coherently authored initial profile; changed availability of optional personal recollection; optional appraisal emphasis; and more useful presentation of the same required bodily facts. Preserve complete accepted About me, required observations and current body measurements. A comparison that removes protected identity or required evidence needs a separately scoped experimental decision under D55; it is not authorized by these flow protocols. Distinguish a changed initial character from a changed projection of the same character, and do not describe the former as an isolated prompt improvement.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Each condition retains the same possible actions and genuine freedom to choose. Do not add a mandatory reflection, life-story recitation or “vary your actions” instruction to the richer condition. A compact integrated context is not guaranteed to win.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Compare real task progress, treatment of others, continuation, appropriate stopping, noticed body pressure, use of remembered events, repetitive loops, false claims and complete latency/cost. Score character coherence separately from eloquence, action count, verbosity and optimal resource play. Record which context the decision actually received and which live facts remained unchanged.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** Include a delayed follow-up and at least one changed condition, so the comparison tests lasting continuity and adaptation rather than one-turn imitation. Stop each run at the same scenario boundary or the current declared failure boundary; retain unsuccessful and uncertain outcomes.

**Failure criteria.** Declaring success from prettier dialogue; selecting only favorable runs; conflating omitted context with changed physiology; treating fewer calls as a saving despite lost useful behavior; or assuming an ablation proves a universal psychological model. Do not use invented emotion ground truth for subjective judgments.

**Matched variation.** Keep configuration and total allowances explicit, use held-out situations, and report context size and complete work rather than artificially padding inputs. If several ingredients change together, call it a combined design comparison rather than claiming an isolated causal effect. Feed results into AG12/CR12's existing records.

### CE-F09 — Outage, pause and absence controls

**Starting situation and knowledge.** Reuse an ordinary scene with actual ongoing chosen work, a meaningful relationship context and a pending need or conversation. Capture the current owner-defined pause, absence, provider-failure and recovery policies before the run. This protocol does not introduce a new pause policy or restore removed automatic feeding.

**Trigger and inner meaning.** Compare ordinary operation with unavailable or delayed cognition, explicit game pause, and the human leaving under the currently configured personal-world rules. A technical outage is not a character choosing indifference. The NPC knows only actual perceived human conduct; disconnection does not reveal the human's motive or private activity.

**Legitimate alternatives.** Already chosen native work may continue only while the world and family permit it. A fresh autonomous choice requires its real supported decision path. The human may pause, resume, leave or reconnect through existing controls. Do not fabricate NPC eating, a human reply, social neglect or retrospective life to keep the narrative smooth.

**Actual activity and visible result.** Apply current policies exactly. Preserve actual progress, resource changes, heard speech and visible operational unavailability. The current agency documentation explicitly allows unavailable cognition to leave an NPC unfed; physiology and the applicable pause/protection policies determine consequences. Do not silently freeze one actor or invent fallback feeding in this evaluation.

**Later recall, reprioritization and stopping.** On legitimate resumption, reconsider from actual present facts and permitted memories. A fresh reply can acknowledge an interruption without claiming unobserved events. Restoration and reconnect must not repeat committed work, speech or paid dispatch. Stop under the existing declared scenario, service or lifecycle boundary, not an invented rescue rule.

**Failure criteria.** Treating service failure as personality; inventing an absent human's actions; acquiring unheard dialogue; replaying effects on return; describing a paused interval as an experienced afternoon; or presenting a successful recovery as proof that normal autonomous behavior was good. Separate infrastructure failure from character-quality failure in the report.

**Matched variation.** Same initial scene under normal running, temporary service unavailability, manual pause and actual configured absence. Include delay during an existing plan and before a genuinely new choice. Keep provider/accounting uncertainty visible; these remain future authorized trials, with no paid calls authorized by this specification.

## Delivery sequence

**First: establish a complete small episode.** Start with the existing map, one resident, a human participant, supported capabilities and current personal-world time rules. Character content and current accepted state should permit body pressure, company/belonging and enjoyment to matter. Exercise CE-F01–CE-F06 alongside the accepted live creative loop. An episode may fail and reveal missing integration; reporting that failure is required. A working meal chain or a well-written profile alone does not close this stage.

**Next: repair the specific missing connection.** Improve how a relevant experience reaches attention, how the prompt expresses it, how the resident chooses and continues an activity, or how the outcome changes later priorities. Use existing mind, appraisal, relationship, action and event owners. If a desired psychological pressure needs new lasting state or a mechanical effect, scope that world policy explicitly before implementation. Prove its whole episode, including satisfaction, refusal, recovery and stopping. New meters, an emotion simulator and a new planner are not prerequisites.

**Then: establish distinctiveness and durable quality.** Run CE-F07–CE-F09, delayed revisits and relevant accepted speeds; retain all earlier applicable flows. Compare different coherent profiles, histories and opportunities, including quiet or uncooperative encounters. Qualify a second autonomous resident's independent response before claiming NPC-to-NPC social continuity; a scripted human reply cannot establish it. Broader choice quality and cost remain the existing AG12/CR12 work.

**Later: expand the life that has proved worthwhile.** New pastimes or social families follow a demonstrated missing opportunity. [DG23](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg23--characters-changed-by-their-experience) addresses longer-term personality change and memory reinterpretation. [DG17](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg17--continuing-communities-clocks-and-quiet-world-funding) addresses unattended communities and funding. Neither is the first point at which a resident may have a distinctive inner life or social and recreational motives.

These stages deepen the first live character alongside the already accepted MVP; they do not replace live invention with a canned conversation or make every later psychology feature a prerequisite for delivering an improvement. The complete promised scope of each selected stage must actually be integrated and qualified.

## Evaluation and economical execution

### Judge a life, not an isolated response

Every implementation task that changes this character's seed, prompt, selected context, needs, feelings, decision opportunities, activity continuation or memory must identify the affected complete flows before it starts. Run the appropriate end-to-end episode and delayed consequence through the ordinary cognition and action path when implementation is authorized. A passing storage test, a plausible model reply or a simulated selection is evidence for its own boundary only.

Do not put the expected action sequence in the character's prompt or secretly perform the decisive choices. Evaluators may arrange a truthful starting situation, introduce the specified actual event and record independently chosen actions. Test participants keep their own responses. A fixture that forces “seek company” can verify wiring; it cannot establish spontaneous social initiative.

| Question                                    | Evidence to retain                                                                  | Failure that a single successful action can conceal                                            |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Is the person grounded?                     | Permitted observations, body condition, context and actual results                  | Invented scenery, memories, capabilities, feelings of others or completed work.                |
| Do their concerns matter?                   | Choices and later behavior under matched changes of relevant experience             | A need or feeling exists only as text and never affects the episode.                           |
| Are they distinct and continuous?           | Consistent preferences, appropriate revision and delayed callbacks                  | Generic helpfulness, prompt echo, personality reversal or arbitrary novelty.                   |
| Can they act independently and finish?      | Initiation, real progress, interruption, resumption and stopping                    | Endless request for orders, purposeless repetition or constant goal replacement.               |
| Is the encounter worthwhile for the player? | Labeled observer/player judgments and the player's ability to pursue their own play | A demanding companion, opaque refusal, intrusive chatter or a character requiring supervision. |
| Is the complete experience supportable?     | Full preparation, selection, generation, reflection, waiting and native-work cost   | A cheap action hiding expensive attention, idle rumination or repeated blocked attempts.       |

Separate visible behavior, the resident's own self-report, accepted private game state and the evaluator's inference. A later eloquent explanation cannot by itself prove why an earlier action was chosen. Use matched variations and targeted comparisons to test whether a selected input makes a useful difference. Avoid both punishing all eccentric behavior and excusing every loop as eccentricity.

Evaluation should compare coherent baseline and candidate character content, prompt directions, selected memories or supported feelings one factor at a time. Removal comparisons use explicit disposable test configurations and preserve required evidence, security and actual world effects; they do not authorize dropping protected identity in ordinary play. Fix scenario judgments, sample sizes and acceptable tradeoffs after a developmental baseline and before evaluating held-out cases. Record unsuccessful runs and uncertainty. No optimal prompt, emotion set, diversity target or release score is claimed here.

### Spend on meaningful choice

Continue valid chosen work cheaply. Reconsider material change, an available meaningful opportunity or an adopted review cue. A stored wish for company must not cause constant paid loneliness checks; an ordinary landscape visit must not require a generated thought at every footstep. Insight, conversation and novel plans can justify generation when they contribute something useful.

Measure total cost per complete episode and offered hour of play, including preparation, retrieval, attention, routing, generation, reflection and uncertain charges. Measure responsiveness as time to useful speech or action, not only one provider call. Compare quality under matched complete budgets. Adding more prompt text or a more expensive model is a hypothesis to evaluate, not the definition of a better character.

Provider failure, exhausted funding, pause and absent participants remain operational facts under current contracts. Do not rebrand them as a character's choice, secretly feed an NPC, fabricate a day of social life, or replay missed optional thoughts on return. A proposed future operational pause policy in the continuing-lives specification remains distinct from currently delivered behavior.

The existing [cognition](../limits/cognition.md#cg12), [feelings](../limits/feelings.md#fl19), [memory](../limits/memory.md) and [AI execution](../limits/ai-execution.md) limits apply. This product design supplies no new inference allowance, mandatory thought rate or requirement for unlimited history. Inspect growing preparation and retained causes when implementing a new concern; limiting final prompt size alone does not bound all earlier work.

## Research and open choices

The [research companion](../../archive/03-design-proposals/character-experience-and-inner-life.md#research-as-inspiration-with-limits) cites six primary sources and separates their findings from design extrapolation. Three practical influences are psychological needs as lenses for motivation, believable-agent work on observable emotion, and generative-agent work on linking experience to later choices. Interoception and conscious-access research inform the perspective metaphor; they do not validate an NPC emotion formula or prove consciousness.

The selected vocabulary, represented pressures, satisfaction/recovery rules, mixed-concern pacing and supported enjoyable activities need authored-world decisions under [D69](../../archive/05-project/open-decisions.md#d69--multidimensional-character-experience). The accepted requirements are multidimensionality, situated perspective, meaningful variation, independent choice and full-flow evaluation. Exact numbers and richer mechanisms remain proposals until selected and tested.
