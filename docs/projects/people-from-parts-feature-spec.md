# People built from parts — feature specification

| In progress | Creator and reusable tooling paths are implemented; finished art and the complete playable pilot remain to qualify. | 2026-11-06   |

## What Mike can make

Start with a description, choose a concept and get back **one recognizable person plus the parts needed to dress them**. That package includes the person's editable body and face settings, hairstyle, the selected world’s fitted coverage undergarments, the separate garments and accessories in their default outfit, the assembled preview and its animations. The next person can wear the first person's coat at a different height or build. A preset gives the author a starting silhouette. Each slider can take values between the presets.

This design covers [DG37, ND39–ND42](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg37--character-appearance-a-creator-and-varied-populations). The art study, player creator and ordinary NPCs share one appearance recipe. The authorized representative delivery includes the reproducible offline kit, in-game editing and removable equipment. These paths are implemented; finished asset acceptance and the connected game journey remain required before routine production.

The player benefit is a recognizable population whose people retain their identity while changing clothes, moving and sharing actual possessions. A short muscular traveller and an older slender craftsperson can wear the same coat without becoming the same silhouette. This supports the project's ordinary encounters and persistent people; it introduces no attractiveness score, automatic social reaction or strength inferred from appearance. [Embodied actions and possession](../repertoires/actions.md#bod-posture-and-embodied-expression) retain their own mechanics. A completed walking animation does not grant a new action or prove that a resident recognizes its wearer.

## Independent appearance controls

Height, frame, skeletal breadth, muscle, fullness and local proportions are independently editable within the family's qualified envelope. Muscle controls visible anatomical development; fullness controls soft tissue. An older person can be muscular, a broad person can have little muscle, and a lean person need not be tall. Frame selection does not select fitness, personality, pronouns or coverage. The [bundled control table](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#control-definitions) owns exact targets and defaults. Its eight build buttons are resolved recipes, not eight available bodies.

Percentages describe travel through an authored range, not kilograms, body-fat percentage, health or skill. A maker can specify height 75%, muscle 70%, fullness 25%, shoulders 65% and neck length 45%, then choose a face and hairstyle and adjust their supported features. Height also accepts metres; adult age appearance also accepts years. Both routes produce the same saved value. The [recipe contract](people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md#recipe-and-input-resolution) defines exact precedence, precision and errors. Hair styles and face presets are discrete choices; local face features and supported hair lengths remain continuous. Fullness never becomes a forced consequence of a feminine frame, age or profession.

The initial age-appearance range is adult only. It changes face/neck/hand form and complexion while preserving selected build, height, identity, hair pigment and posture. It does not change the person's birth information, memories, physical health or gait. There is no automatic ageing process in this delivery. Children require age-appropriate anatomy and a separately designed family, not a scaled adult.

Plain clay is useful for checking anatomy, but is not finished skin. Before expanding the kit, one complete covered person must pass both the untextured shape check and a textured comparison with the selected concept and existing quality references. A technically connected mesh, shared vertex numbering or a functioning slider cannot substitute for that acceptance. The [authoring contract](people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md) specifies the missing sculpt, surface and reproducibility work.

## Creating a named character

1. **Describe the person or supply art.** Establish height when known, build, age appearance, identifying features, work, garment construction and which pieces come off independently. Use existing character descriptions for identity. The [authorship contract](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#character-authorship-and-changing-personality) keeps current possessions, facts known by others and future behavior with their own owners.
2. **Choose a concept.** On the full path the artist or agent shows three meaningfully different directions with the signature features at the game's camera size. Mike chooses the identity and outfit. A supplied, already-selected concept enters at the construction-sheet step. Never mix an unselected face into the approved character or pay for every final part before this selection.
3. **Fit the person first.** Show the complete shaped body in the [world’s baseline undergarments](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#baseline-coverage), unobstructed face views, the hairstyle separately and a measured front/profile lineup. Fit the closest recipe, preserving the stated height and identity landmarks. The actual body and face must match the selected construction views before the wardrobe is built. A face preset is an editable starting shape. Changing the eyes' shape moves the lids and nearby face. Changing their colour edits the iris. Both show in the close view.
4. **Make what the sheet requires.** Reuse a matching library part, adapt a compatible pattern or make an isolated piece from construction views. Include inner and outer surfaces visible at openings. Each garment and ornament can be inspected alone, moved to a second compatible person and removed. Deliver an explicit default outfit list with exact part revisions. A drawing that hides the back of a coat requires a recorded construction choice.
5. **Judge the result in motion.** Compare the covered person, the dressed person and a different build wearing the same outfit. Rotate, walk, run, reach, turn the head and remove an outer layer. Check height and silhouette at the game camera, then eyes, seams, clearance and foot contact close up. Preserve the chosen concept, each requested candidate, editable parts, checks, failures and approved assembly through the existing art workflow.

**When the kit misses.** Show the closest attempt beside the selected sheet, identify what it misses and distinguish missing evidence from unsupported shape. Another front view can resolve an unclear shoulder. A broader waist within the supported family can use a slider. A different joint structure calls for a proposed family extension, a new family or a different concept for Mike to choose. A fused portrait model, forced nearest preset or changed body to fit a coat cannot pass this step.

## What dragging does

At authorized character creation, the creator opens with a covered full figure beside the available frames and build presets. Dragging a labelled height slider moves continuously and shows both percentage and metres. Selecting the shoulder handle in a front view and dragging sideways changes shoulder breadth. A profile view exposes nose projection and the vertical neck handle. The same controls have keyboard and numeric alternatives. Select a feature explicitly before dragging, rotate from the background, and offer front, profile, close and dressed views. One gesture is one undo step. Cancel restores the previous shape. Saving applies the reviewed recipe once. Choosing an existing resident to play preserves their authored look. A creator needs the appropriate edit grant to change that resident.

For example, a maker can start from a broad frame, set height to 75%, reduce muscle, leave overall stature fixed while lengthening the neck and choose a hood with a short braid. In the [bundled profile](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md) the result stands 1.95 m. The collar opening, head pivot, braid and earrings follow the changed neck and head. The actor's skill at lifting, speed, biography and identity are authored separately.

Show the supported travel of each control. A locked dimension stays fixed. At a combination boundary, stop at the last supported value and identify the constraint, or let the maker undo the other change. A wardrobe-specific defect is reported against the part. An author can inspect the supported body in its underlayer even if their work-in-progress jacket fails. Do not shorten the person without telling the maker. Share the draft between inputs, show the rotating figure without capturing world controls, and keep an existing preview during loading only while the same person and outfit still have permission to be shown. A committed removal or lost permission clears the obsolete appearance immediately. The creator requires labelled, focusable controls, visible focus, numeric entry, keyboard undo and a reduced-motion still pose.

Start with build, height, adult age appearance, face and hair; put the local anatomical controls in an expandable detail area. A preset change replaces only the group it names: a build preset keeps height, face, age, hair and outfit; a face choice keeps local control values and revalidates their fit. Reset names its group and remains undoable. Keep the figure visible beside the controls. An unsupported input stays visible with its reason and cannot be saved as a successful fit. Previewing a new age or build does not author biographical facts. The first delivery supports exact recipes offline; the later creator must reproduce those same recipes rather than implement another interpretation.

## Presets, populations and equipment

The bundled world starts with two adult frames on one topology, eight editable build recipes, and separately chosen faces, eyes and hair. Its [profile](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md) defines sliders, intended bounds, kit contents and clothing placements. A named character stores exact choices. A crowd author can draw from world-owned ranges in a controlled seed and resolve values and part revisions once. Keep authors' ability to explicitly reproduce or intentionally repeat a look. The canonical set consists of the mesh, fitting rules, control shapes and recipes. Its size does not determine how many people can exist.

In play, removing a coat reveals the shirt, removing the shirt reveals the fitted body and selected baseline undergarments, and the coat can go to another person through ordinary inventory actions. The second wearer keeps the same coat's identity and condition. A player's right to inspect or remove it comes from the game. The visual ordering follows the actual committed equipment. A wardrobe preview on a mannequin only changes the preview. A failed or missing visual fit offers a truthful fallback or marks the study incomplete. It cannot duplicate a valuable item, undo a valid equipment action or disclose the contents of a hidden layer.

The proof excludes automatic support for arbitrary human scans, children, other species, freely simulated cloth on every item and live AI generation during dragging. An additional authored family can offer materially different anatomy without adopting the bundled human rules. No new runtime, model acquisition, generated art or asset publication is part of this design delivery.

The broader control set is a staged goal, not permission to hold the first playable accepted character until every face control and wardrobe slot exists. Deliver the approved covered/dressed trio and compatible motion first, then qualify the family and repeatable batch tool; mark the smaller result honestly. Each later executable or asset-production change is an experiment until its isolated correctness and cost checks pass. Human art acceptance remains separate from measurements. [Experiment decisions and stopping rules](people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md#isolated-experiments).
