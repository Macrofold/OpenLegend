This project redesigns how OpenLegend makes art: one procedure that turns a request such as "turn this character sheet into a rigged model for the game" into finished, reviewed, preserved files, with explicit tool defaults, explicit artistic latitude and no built-in look. Mike asks; an agent, or a junior artist following the pages, does the work. The normal full path has three review points; unresolved consequential choices are resolved before dependent work, and the prototype path needs no candidate-selection round.

The procedure itself lives in the [art workflow pages](../production-workflows/art-flows/README.md). This document owns the purpose, the decisions and their reasons, the scenarios, acceptance and what remains; the [technical design](art-workflow-tech-design.md) owns the page structure, the helper tools, source/export handoffs and remaining delivery tasks.

## 1. Purpose

OpenLegend's art problem is not making one beautiful image. It is producing, at a steady pace, the people, creatures, tools, structures and states that the first playable game needs, in whatever look Mike has currently chosen, without Mike having to manage tools, prompts or files.

The workflow is:

- **Concrete:** every stage names its default tool, command, output, check and stop, with costs.
- **Adjustable:** a simple or full path, per-step options, tools, fidelity, candidate counts and the stopping point are request settings with defaults, and supplied material skips the steps it already answers.
- **Look-neutral:** the look comes from the art direction through one stable entry point.
- **Play-first:** acceptance happens in the game's own view, against checks that matter to play.

### 3.1 The outdated "artgen" skills

Mike asked what the claude.ai-synced skill `/anthropic-skills:openlegend-art-pipeline` is and how it differs from the repository's `openlegend-art` skill.

- **What it is.** The synced skill (and its companion `openlegend-art-cleanup`) is a command-by-command runbook for a tool called `artgen` (`art-direction/pipeline/artgen.mjs`). It ran a brief, generated eight concepts through fal.ai (four Nano Banana 2, four FLUX.2 pro, about $0.56), let Mike pick in a browser page, made A-pose reference views ($0.15 each) and generated eight 3D candidates (Hunyuan3D v3, Tripo H3.1, TRELLIS.2 and Rodin Gen-2.5, about $3). It then pixel-cleaned them in Blender and finalized a GLB into `art-direction/assets/generated/`, with a $10 job cap.

The only trace is a note in the [Midjourney plans page](../../art-direction/midjourney/08-plans-costs-rights-privacy.md) that artgen's executable implementation was never supplied. The skill would fail at its first command.
- **Difference.** `openlegend-art` was the real, maintained workflow: a router into the Standard and Higgsfield procedures covering every kind of art, with no named defaults or commands.

### 3.2 Artistic freedom

Mike asked whether turning a character sheet into a model leaves artistic freedom or is purely mechanical. **It is not mechanical.** Judgment enters when:

- resolving views that disagree;
- inventing what the sheet does not show (backs, undersides, under a cape);
- choosing a body and rig family;
- interpreting materials for the current look;
- deciding topology;
- repairing generated defects;
- acting in animation.

Image-to-3D services invent hidden geometry on their own. The workflow therefore makes the latitude explicit: a **fidelity** setting (`exact`, `faithful` by default, `interpretive`) with named **locks** and **free** areas. Every consequential interpretation is recorded in the study record, so Mike can see what was decided rather than copied. The vocabulary matches the runtime presentation contract's pinned features and cosmetic freedom. Under `exact`, the Gildmoss lesson applies: "exactly as shown" means the source's own texture and colour, verified on a matched-scale sheet.

### 3.3 Platforms and models are request settings with defaults

Mike asked whether the platforms and models in artgen (fal.ai, Tripo, Nano Banana and others) should become options. **Decision:** every stage has a default from the platforms the project already uses. A request can name a platform or model for any stage, and that overrides the default for that stage only. "Standard", "Higgsfield" and "both" remain as presets because Mike uses those words. Volatile facts (model IDs, prices, commands) live in one page with check dates, [tools and defaults](../production-workflows/art-flows/tools.md). One meaning changed: "standard" used to mean direct tools outside Higgsfield. It now means the default roster: image and geometry generation remain on Higgsfield ([OD1](#od1--default-generation-platform)); [§3.9](#39-nvidia-motion-authoring) adds a separately qualified motion route. A `both` comparison keeps its documented direct side. A named tool that needs a new account or key is never set up silently: the agent says what is needed and asks.

### 3.4 The workflow carries no look

Mike's direction: "We shouldn't hardcode any specific artistic interpretation into the workflow itself." **Decision:**

- **One stable entry point.** The workflow reads the look from [current direction at a glance](../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-direction-at-a-glance), which names the current world-art, interface and concept owners.
- **World-owned production choices.** Surface treatment, anatomical proportions, facial accents, modeling scale, animation timing and review conditions come from the selected world's art direction. The [bundled-world production notes](../../art-direction/decisions.md#how-the-current-look-changes-production) govern its present plain-3D look.
- **Shared procedure.** Select tools, produce the requested assets and inspect them against that direction. A world can replace its look without changing the shared procedure. [Engine and world separation](../engine-and-world-boundaries.md).

### 3.5 Structure and conventions

- **One procedure.** The separate Standard and Higgsfield pages are gone: their procedure folded into the shared README, and their tool facts into [tools](../production-workflows/art-flows/tools.md).
- **A dedicated 3D route.** [3D models](../production-workflows/art-flows/3d-models.md) is the step-by-step route for props, characters and creatures; the production page keeps the package rules and the other recipes.
- **One skill body.** `/ol-design` is an alias of `openlegend-art`.
- **Three decision points on the full path.** Mike picks a direction, picks a candidate and accepts; the simple path makes one result for him to review. Everything else is the agent's job, and technical checks never stand in for acceptance.
- **Naming and working folders.**
  - Study IDs: `YYYY-MM-DD-<subject>[-<purpose>]`.
  - Candidate IDs: `c`, `v`, `m`, `k`, `f` plus two digits.
  - Working folder: `~/Documents/ChatGPT/OpenLegend-art/<study-id>/study/` with stage folders.
  - Default storage: study material to `art-studies` / `curated-private`, editable sources and packages to `art-library` / `curated-private`, the domains every archived study already uses. Every meaningful output is uploaded as soon as its batch finishes, and only the exact references are kept locally; only very likely temporary scratch stays local (Mike, October 31).

### 3.6 Simple and full paths (November 1, 2026)

Two requests met on November 1. The prototype work merged into `main` made model and animation generation default to one usable prototype export, with automatic rig, clip, material and format settings, and added a path to finalize a selected model. Mike then asked for a path for simple art generation and a path for full art generation, with options for each variation (a polished animation or not, a cleaned-up model or not, ideation and concept steps or not, steps skipped because of what is supplied), and for prototype or full art to be easy to ask for. **Decision:**

- **Two paths, one setting.** `quality=prototype` is the simple path and `quality=finished` the full path, chosen in ordinary words ("quick", "rough", "prototype"; "finished", "polished", "game-ready"). A quality word decides; a destination such as "for the game" does not, and asking to pick gives that step the full path's candidates. Model and animation generation default to the simple path; finished or game-ready assets and every other request default to the full path; "polish/finalize" moves a prototype to the full path at Finish.
- **Step options.** Concepts, views, candidates, cleanup, rig, clips, motion polish, materials, format and critique each have a default per path, and naming one changes that step only, as in "a prototype, but with a polished walk".
- **Supplied material skips what it answers.** A sheet or concept skips concepts, clean sheet views are cropped, a saved model starts at Finish, and a compatible rig and adequate materials are kept.
- **Default clips.** An animated walking character for OpenLegend gets `Idle`, `Walk` and `Run`: the prototype work's idle and walk plus the `Run` the game's loader requires, with named motions added. Mike confirmed on November 3 that a walk is expected by default.
- **What no option removes.** Both paths keep the [selected consumer's required checks](../production-workflows/art-flows/README.md#what-no-option-removes) and preserve every meaningful output. A standalone motion-source or library clip may contain only its named action; integration assembles the consumer's complete set.

Most requests want either a quick usable look or finished art, and neither Mike nor a junior should decide every step. The simple path reduces optional work; dated provider estimates belong in [tools](../production-workflows/art-flows/tools.md), and actual cost includes repair and inspection, not just generation. The full path keeps the review points that caught rejected art. A prototype skips the fidelity sheet and critique by default; `fidelity=exact` still requires its matched-scale comparison, and explicit step options can request critique. These checks do not by themselves establish artistic acceptance or family qualification. The step options are request words read by the agent, not provider settings or a service.

### 3.7 Repair only the failed dependency

The [character-production research](../../art-direction/research/3d-pixel-worlds/character-production-2026-10-31.md) supports selective part generation, directly useful meshes, rough motion before polish and checking the exported result. These refine the existing procedure; they add no required provider comparison, retopology, high-poly sculpt, cloth simulation or inverse-kinematics pass.

**Experiment status:** selective repair and reuse already existed in the handbook. The additional handoff/recheck detail and any newly selected production technique are unqualified experiments where their workload, correctness or runtime effects are uncertain. The [isolated comparison](art-workflow-tech-design.md#isolated-workflow-experiments) must run before adopting extra routine process or claiming an efficiency improvement; documentation review is not that evidence.

- **Part control has two meanings.** A separate generation job can repair a head or accessory without remaking successful work. Separately, the selected world can require a part to remain removable or adjustable. Future people have that requirement under [PPL](../maintainers/art/people.md); it cannot be waived by choosing prototype quality. A rigid prop without exchangeable parts does not acquire it merely because it was generated in pieces.
- **Use what already works.** Refine a direct mesh if its form, requested deformation, material mapping and export meet the brief. Rebuild topology or bake detailed geometry only to solve a demonstrated problem. Simple utility-map baking for a chosen material method is a separate decision.
- **Polish resumes the selected asset.** Preserve its exact source, rig, requested clips and adequate materials. The [repair table](../production-workflows/assets-and-animation.md#repair-map-and-downstream-effects) determines which downstream checks become stale; no wholesale regeneration or repeat candidate contest is implied.
- **The short path has a real exit.** A prototype must load and perform its stated use with the required identity and proportions. Record cosmetic limitations, but do not call a broken requested motion usable merely because it is labelled a prototype. A checked export, a shared-family qualification and Mike's artistic acceptance are separate outcomes.

### 3.8 Prompt direction serves the requested experience

[Prompt direction](../production-workflows/art-flows/prompting.md) translates the existing brief into an operation the selected tool can perform. It is part of Brief, Prepare and the relevant repair step, not an eighth stage. The existing Purpose field connects visible decisions to what someone should notice, understand or enjoy: a familiar person remains recognizable while acting independently, a tool's construction suggests its actual use, and a place invites curiosity or quiet appreciation. The [repertoire priorities](../repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md#make-execution-and-presentation-earn-their-attention) already own those goals; this project adds no gameplay system or ranking.

The purchased-library review supports concrete instructions about form, references, contacts, materials and motion, but not adoption of its engines, art styles or body families. Its character recipes rely on supplied assets and rig/clip data as well as prose. Use the smallest relevant lesson and diagnose the responsible layer before adding detail. Prefer retaining a suitable source or making one focused correction over regenerating accepted work. New infrastructure, automatic provider fan-out, numerical self-scoring and mandatory comparison campaigns would increase cost without an established player benefit.

A prompt-only request ends with a usable draft and material assumptions. An execution request follows the existing quality path and output checks. Static authored surface character may express construction, age and taste without a simulation for each mark; an appearance used to report a changing game condition must match that state. State coverage comes from the requested consumer, not from a blanket requirement that every prop needs variants. Beauty and expression need no extra mechanic to justify them.

This is a completed guidance design, not evidence of improved generated-art quality or enjoyment. Existing production proof and player-evidence owners retain those questions. The [source record](../../art-direction/research/ai-asset-prompting/README.md) keeps the evidence and exclusions optional for ordinary art requests.

### 3.9 NVIDIA motion authoring

**Decision, November 5, 2026:** Kimodo is the core offline human-animation authoring source within the existing workflow. Mike accepted the [first hosted source preview](../../art-direction/studies/2026-11-05-kimodo-api-test/README.md) and selected this direction. Source generation and Blender import are demonstrated; end-to-end character qualification remains open. The [research record](../../art-direction/research/3d-pixel-worlds/nvidia-animation-2026-11-04.md) distinguishes **MotionBricks** pose-to-motion from **Kimodo** text-to-motion.

#### Activation and defaults

1. **Resolve whether motion is needed before choosing a provider.** Activate the animation procedure for an animation-only request, an explicitly animated character, or an accepted deliverable whose consumer requires clips. Concepts, UI, static models, rigid props, an unanimated rig and “for the game” by themselves do not trigger generation. `animations=none` remains an explicit override; if it conflicts with the named runtime consumer, report the unmet contract rather than silently generating clips.
2. **Keep useful work.** Retain supplied meshes, skeletons, skin weights, materials, attachments and adequate clips. Share accepted motion, generated or licensed, across compatible characters; a new body or outfit does not need a new generation. An animation-only request never reruns image generation, modeling or auto-rigging merely to obtain movement. An explicit source comparison may intentionally request fresh generation.
3. **Default new and replacement human motion to Kimodo.** Start here when authoring the shared library, not only when existing libraries lack a clip. Retain accepted actions the brief does not replace. The hosted `sprited/kimodo` SOMA-RP route avoids a local GPU and gated text-encoder setup; source generation works, while each target-rig route still needs qualification before final export. Its tested model is v1.1, not official v1.2. Pin its deployment; no moving `latest` or undocumented model upgrade. Official SOMA-RP v1.2 is an optional local/self-hosted route, not a setup prerequisite for ordinary art work.
4. **MotionBricks is optional.** Use it only when authored start/history and target poses make a transition worth generating. It does not take a text prompt or solve the surrounding scene. Supply production-safe poses, bake the result, then use the same retarget/finish/check path. Ordinary keying remains appropriate for a short transition. No need for a transition means no MotionBricks work.
5. **Keep unsupported anatomy on its existing route.** Neither model becomes a default for wolves, bears, wings, cloth, facial animation, vehicles or arbitrary creatures. SOMA compatibility is a body/rig property, not a character's name or a world-specific species label. Specialist/library/Blender routes remain available.

“Default” selects the first suitable new-motion source; it is not evidence of working credentials, a completed adapter, accepted art or general rig-family qualification. Before qualification, reuse existing clips where they meet the request and identify the missing generation capability. If a requested new clip cannot be made on the selected route, offer the concrete catalog/manual alternative and its cost/quality consequence. Do not silently call another provider, substitute v1.2 for v1.1, claim a preview video is a skeletal clip, or reroll after a paid failure. Ordinary offline correction and already-authorized work continue.

#### The experience Mike and an artist should get

Mike can say “give this person a hesitant wave,” “a rough carrying walk,” or “polish the existing run.” The agent identifies the target asset, reuses its body and existing work, states the motion, source, quality, expected cost and stopping point in the usual plan line, and performs the work. Technical model names and tokens are setup details, not questions Mike must answer for every clip. `tools.clips` can override the source; `motion=rough|polished` still controls finish quality and never doubles as a provider field.

The simple path generates **one candidate for the requested new or replacement action**, does the necessary import/retarget/contact check, previews it and preserves it with honest limitations. The full path adds deliberate acting, contact/transition cleanup and the existing critique/review. It does not automatically buy three motion generations: use one first draft unless alternatives are explicitly requested or a documented comparison needs them. A rough clip may have cosmetic limits; broken anatomy, wrong timing/scale, a missing required action or an unusable export is a failed result, not a prototype exemption.

For a walking animated character, `Idle`, `Walk` and `Run` remain the default set; retain already adequate versions. The current runtime loader requires `Idle` and `Run`, while a selected catalog entry may additionally require its declared strides and strikes. New names such as `Offer` or `Work` are valid study/library assets but become runtime behavior only through an existing or separately designed action consumer. Adding a clip never creates an action, a new state, a hit or an item transfer.

#### What to make first, and why

The objective is a small repertoire of readable action meanings that supports this game's independent people, practical invention and persistent consequences. These are selection priorities for a requested study, not a mandate to produce a large clip pack or implement every repertoire proposal.

| Priority         | Useful motion                                                                                   | What makes it valuable                                                                                                      | Scope boundary                                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First            | The missing preparation, strike and recovery for an already playable dangerous action           | Players can react, understand a miss and learn timing; responsiveness survives artistic polish                              | Existing action windows and outcomes control the presentation                                     |
| First            | One real work action, such as attending the hearth or using an admitted tool, with interruption | Work and invention visibly use the actual tool, contact and material rather than an interchangeable emote                   | Choose the real action and its owner first; clip generation does not add crafting mechanics       |
| Next             | Carrying, lowering or offering a real held object                                               | Practical movement and social purpose become legible; one motion can support several authored objects with compatible grips | Native possession/transfer controls the object; paired actors and contact need review             |
| Next             | A distinctive voluntary greeting, hesitation or small response                                  | People have character without requiring unique movement sets for every NPC                                                  | Express authored/selected visible conduct; do not expose hidden beliefs or undisclosed identities |
| Later, as needed | Sit/stand, reach or recovery transitions                                                        | Connect useful actions without abrupt posing                                                                                | MotionBricks or hand-keying uses safe input poses and authored geometry; no new traversal system  |

Source/reference selection still follows the current world art direction. Neither a particular human style nor Threewater March's work vocabulary belongs inside the importer or a model-selection heuristic. The [repertoire review in the research record](../../art-direction/research/3d-pixel-worlds/nvidia-animation-2026-11-04.md#7-repertoire-audit-and-bounded-motion-coverage) and production handbook retain the broader coverage and meaning.

#### Controls and acceptance

- **No runtime inference:** generated output is an ordinary baked asset. Players need no NVIDIA GPU, model weights, account or network call for animation playback. The game uses its existing clip consumer; this design makes no runtime speedup claim.
- **One position and outcome authority:** root policies, support, locomotion pacing, action time, cancellation and effect timing follow current game owners. A pleasing model trajectory cannot move the authoritative body, decide a contact or reveal unobserved action/state. A supplied object target is a constraint to check, not an inferred world fact.
- **Rights are inputs:** production uses the SOMA route and appropriately licensed inputs. MotionBricks' bundled BONES samples and their derivatives are evaluation-only; SMPL-X Kimodo is excluded from production. Do not confuse permissive model terms with rights to every demo asset.
- **Cheap recovery:** use the existing study record and provider job ID; recover a known job rather than resubmitting. Keep credentials out of artifacts. A timeout with unknown submission status is unresolved spend, not a free retry. Preserve the original output and editable correction so a revision does not require regeneration.
- **Qualification is narrow:** a specific host/deployment, source format/rest convention and target rig/map pass a small real study before use becomes operational. Qualification of the mercenary does not qualify MPFB, another anatomy, every garment, all model versions or crowds. Existing V3D family and owner art acceptance stay separate.

## 4. Scenarios

Each scenario names its trigger, what Mike sees, the decision points and a meaningful failure. New-person scenarios use the accepted [PPL body/parts design](../maintainers/art/people.md), with routine production gated by family qualification; retained study exceptions are not a general production method.

1. **Concept study.** _"Concept art for Tavin Vale, three directions."_ The agent reads the look and Tavin's facts, states the plan line (about $0.35), generates three distinct directions, fixes anatomy and placeholder defects with targeted edits, and shows one labelled sheet. Mike picks c02 or asks for another round. The study ends archived. _Failure:_ the default model keeps producing the same face; the agent audits a second model on the same brief and says so.
2. **Prop sheet to a finished model.** _"Turn this spear sheet into a finished model; keep the blade and bindings exactly."_
   - **Plan.** Preserve those locks, resolve actual dimensions and the held/ground consumer, then reuse usable views. Use the full path's default candidates only if a suitable accepted source is absent; estimate that selected route before spending.
   - **Pick.** Compare the candidates at matched scale and in the game's renderer. Mike selects one; the report distinguishes raw-source gaps from final export requirements.
   - **Finish.** Keep working geometry and UVs, repair only failed shape/material/attachment details, and check the delivered grip and orientation against the selected target. A rigid spear needs no humanoid rig.
   - **Accept.** Mike reviews the checked package; it is preserved. Integration remains a separate requested step.
   - _Failure:_ a generator fuses the binding into an incorrect grip shape. Diagnose the reference or source-method failure, repair or generate only the affected piece, and recheck fit. Generating that piece separately does not require another runtime draw object.
3. **State sheet.** _"Production models of the wattle panels, intact and damaged."_ The agent reads the shelter specification, finds intact, damaged, nearly broken and salvage, and makes one source with four states built from real parts. The state check confirms the four read apart at play distance. _Failure:_ the game has no "burnt" state; the agent does not make one.
4. **Tool override.** _"Concepts with Nano Banana Pro, 3D with Rodin."_ Nano Banana Pro runs through Higgsfield, already connected. Rodin is offered through fal.ai, which needs an account and key that are not configured, so the agent reports the requirement and asks, while preparing everything else. (Tripo, by contrast, is already reachable through Higgsfield.)
5. **Resume.** A new thread receives `2026-11-02-mara-reed-3d/m02`. It reads the record's Resume section, recovers any outstanding job, fetches the archived files and continues at Stage 5 without regenerating.
6. **Comparison.** _"Use both flows for a character appearance study."_ Shared inputs are frozen once; Standard and Higgsfield run independently with distinct IDs; one index compares coverage, quality, cost and Mike's involvement.
7. **Creature.** _"A wolf for the game."_ A library or specialist source fitted to a nonhuman rig, with stalking, bite wind-up and recovery clips authored before idles; the threat check reads the bite from three sides.

   **Focused creature-motion revision.** _"Finish the bite motion on this approved wolf so its preparation is readable."_ Keep the selected body and compatible rig, read the action's native timing and adapt a suitable clip before authoring missing motion. Show preparation, contact and recovery coherently; inspect the actual clip with the existing threat check. _Failure:_ a still looks threatening but motion hides the preparation or slides the support feet. Repair the motion or contact, not the creature's accepted anatomy. A clip review does not prove the encounter is fun or integrated correctly; integration stays a separate requested scope.

8. **Quick prototype.** _"A quick static model of this stone tool from the sheet."_ Reuse an adequate source or make one candidate from the usable supplied view, keep simple materials, normalize only as required, reopen the export and inspect its form/scale in the intended view. Preserve it and stop; no rig, animation, candidate sheet or polish pass is added. _Failure:_ the grip or silhouette violates a named lock; repair that defect or report an incomplete result. A minor surface blemish can remain for later polish.
9. **Animation only.** _"Give this selected rig a reaching motion; keep the model and materials."_ Reuse the exact target, block the reach and its return, check the hand against the actual target position, export and reopen. Preserve other clips. No new body, garment set, concepts or unrelated motion library is required. _Failure:_ a same-named source skeleton has a different rest pose; correct the mapping or pose and recheck contact rather than declaring compatibility from names.
10. **Finish a retained prototype.** _"Polish this face and coat, keeping the walk."_ Inspect the selected revision, repair the failed face/material or weights, then repeat its affected export and motion checks. A texture-only correction keeps geometry and the rig. A topology change rechecks affected weights and textures. _Failure:_ the available service can rig only its raw output, so rerigging there would discard local repair; use a supported local route or report that dependency before spending.
11. **Prompt-only repair.** _"Rewrite my material prompt for this accepted chest; keep its shape and do not generate."_ Inspect the source and lighting context, distinguish muddy texture from multiplied colour or illumination, and return a supported material-edit prompt with reference roles and protected regions. _Failure:_ the chosen route cannot retain the mesh or accept the supplied input. State that limitation instead of promising a repair through unsupported words. Stop at the text; no job, asset or study folder is required.
12. **Character sheet to game model.** _"Turn this sheet into a finished, rigged model; keep the face and coat exactly."_ The [people design](people-from-parts-feature-spec.md) is accepted and its pilot authorized. Routine production still needs a qualified family and an actual character request; the [parts procedure](../production-workflows/art-flows/people.md) supplies the covered body, adjustable face, hair and removable garments. Report a missing qualified part with the closest result and specific mismatch; do not ask again for the settled design decision or use a whole-person generation shortcut.

- **Plan.** `faithful`, with locks on face and coat; use the accepted parts route to select the body and compatible parts, identify permitted missing parts and estimate their actual tool work. Do not invent a universal whole-person candidate count or price for a route that lacks measured evidence.
- **Pick.** Show the requested alternatives and relevant assembled views beside the reference and cast, using the existing full-path decision points. Mike selects the candidate.
- **Finish.** Complete only the accepted route's missing construction, fitting, rig and required clips; check part separation, budgets and play checks, obtain the existing critique, and show the matched-scale result.
- **Accept.** Mike accepts and the package is archived. Integration is a separate request.
- _Failure:_ a candidate fuses the satchel to the hip. Keep the accepted body, rig and other usable parts; repair the affected permitted part or its source views, then regenerate only that part if needed within the existing spending authority. Recheck its attachment and required part separation.

13. **Quick person prototype.** _"A quick animated walking model of Tavin from this sheet."_ After the people kit is qualified, choose the closest covered build, adjust it to the sheet, select the face, hair and separate clothing, then preview the dressed character with existing gait clips. Record measured height and visible departures. When the kit lacks Tavin’s body or a required part, identify the gap before offering a different source. Quick requests keep the same people gate and modular construction.
14. **Missing human action.** _"Give this existing rig a rough, hesitant offer of the basket."_ The agent checks whether a compatible clip exists, identifies the grip and target, and generates one missing clip through the qualified Kimodo route. It preserves the target body, retargets and corrects the grip, and shows the actual character at game scale. The clip is a study until an action consumer supports it. _Failure:_ the model gives a wave or the basket passes through the hand; correct a useful source or stop with the cause, not a paid retry loop or a successful-transfer claim.
15. **Pose transition.** _"Smooth the rise from this supported resting pose."_ The target action and safe source poses are supplied. The artist tries ordinary keying or explicitly selects MotionBricks, bakes the transition and checks contacts and interruption. _Failure:_ only restricted demo poses are available; retain the setup and author safe poses, never ship a derivative of the demo.
16. **No access or uncertain paid job.** The selected Kimodo host lacks authorized credentials, or a submitted job's response is lost. The agent completes the brief, maps and existing-clip reuse it can do, records the exact access or unknown-job state, and recovers through the provider. It does not create an account, invent a price or send a duplicate prediction.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

1. A junior following the pages can take a supplied character sheet through the accepted parts route to an archived, checked, owner-accepted rigged GLB, using the documented commands, defaults and decision points after applicable design decisions are settled. The [first proof run](art-workflow-tech-design.md#8-delivery-stages-and-tasks) supplies partial tool evidence; it does not establish owner acceptance, modular people or independent junior execution.
2. No workflow or handbook page states a specific look; changing the look requires editing only the art-direction entry point and the sections it names.
3. Every stage has a named default tool, a cost estimate method and a recovery method; overrides work per stage.
4. Every study record shows fidelity, locks, interpretations, decisions and costs.
5. Candidates are reviewed in their named consumer before acceptance. OpenLegend output uses the game's own look at the default camera, with the profile's applicable play checks recorded.
6. The guidance checker passes and no link to the removed pages remains.
7. A request chooses the simple or the full path in ordinary words and changes any single step (concepts, views, candidates, cleanup, rig, clips, motion polish, materials, format, critique) without restating the rest; supplied material skips the steps it already answers.
8. A selected prototype can be finalized without replacing valid geometry, rig, clips or materials. The AF07 proof record identifies what changed, which evidence was reused and which affected checks were repeated; unsupported source editing or consumer features remain explicit gaps.
9. A prompt-only request produces actionable text without executing it; a focused asset correction retains its source and scope. The relevant guide is discoverable without loading the source library, and neither draft wording nor passing static/routing checks is reported as proof of art quality or player enjoyment.
10. Static requests make no motion-provider calls; animation-only requests preserve the supplied character and adequate clips; a missing compatible human motion selects Kimodo only through a qualified route. Named overrides and actual fallback choices appear in the record.
11. Generated motion becomes editable target-rig clips with correct units, timing and root policy. Review covers contact, loop/one-shot meaning, ordinary game-scale readability and relevant interruption, without changing gameplay semantics.
12. Qualification records actual source/deployment/rig versions, correction effort, total spend including failures, exact retained artifacts and remaining limits. No source code, documentation or vendor claim alone marks a route working; MotionBricks remains optional.

### OD8 — Training on uploaded art

**Findings (checked 2026-10-31):**

- **Meshy:** its [terms](https://www.meshy.ai/terms-of-use) (dated 2026-09-19) let it train on non-Enterprise customers' inputs and outputs unless the customer opts out by emailing support@meshy.ai. The terms state no drawback or feature loss from opting out.
- **The opt-out covers only a direct Meshy account,** which this workflow does not use. Today's Meshy and Tripo jobs go through Higgsfield, so Meshy's customer for them is Higgsfield, not Mike. This is inferred from how the route works, not stated by either party.
- **Higgsfield:** its [terms](https://higgsfield.ai/terms-of-use-agreement) (updated 2026-10-23) let it train on uploads and outputs. The only way to stop future use is to delete the content or the account. Enterprise agreements are excluded, and deletion does not undo training already done. The terms do not say whether providers behind Higgsfield may train on what it passes them.
- **OpenAI API:** it does not train on API data by default and keeps it up to 30 days for abuse monitoring ([business data](https://openai.com/business-data/)).
- **Tripo:** it states it does not train on paid users' content.

**Options:**

1. Accept training for ordinary art.
2. After each study is archived, delete its Higgsfield-side content. This loses Higgsfield's job history and Canvas for that study, and stops only future use.
3. Route designs Mike wants kept private through the OpenAI API for 2D, and through direct Tripo, or direct Meshy with the opt-out, for 3D. That needs those accounts (OD1, OD2).
4. Ask Higgsfield whether its providers train on routed content, or about Enterprise terms.

**Mike's decision (October 31):** opt out only where it is really easy and costs nothing. Meshy's email opt-out qualifies, but only once a direct Meshy account exists, so it is sent if and when one is opened. Higgsfield has no easy opt-out (deleting content loses its history and stops only future use), so nothing changes there. Uploads still respect each reference's rights.

## 11. People families, a character creator and NPC variety

The [people feature specification](people-from-parts-feature-spec.md) owns the accepted creator and wardrobe design. Its [technical design](people-from-parts-tech-design.md) owns fitting, rig adjustments, export and qualification. The [operator procedure](../production-workflows/art-flows/people.md) takes a description or selected concept through the covered body, face, hair, individual garments and assembled default outfit. PPL01–PPL02 are designed; PPL03 activates the routine procedure only after the family qualification. The [authoring contract](people-from-parts-authoring-contract.md) specifies independent body/face/age controls, material finishing, exact recipes and the reusable baker.

**How the family varies.** The [bundled profile](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md) supplies the starting frames, editable build presets and height range. They share one arrangement and numbering of body vertices. A slider moves between authored surface shapes, adjusts joint positions for measures such as limb length, or changes a material value such as iris colour. Vertex correspondence makes blending possible. The family qualifies the combinations it presents, including mixed values, neck and eyelid seams, joints, skinning and pose contacts. Build, hair and clothing carry identity at play distance; the sculpted face must also hold up in a portrait or close study.

**How clothes follow.** A shape change moves the body’s surface in its rest pose. An artist builds a corresponding garment fit using the part’s template and fit data. Bone weights then tell the fitted mesh how to bend as its wearer moves. Rigid plates and earrings use separate attachment rules. Fit the dressed body across the promised ranges and poses to catch collisions, exposed skin and foot or grip errors. A matching bone name or an automatic weight transfer cannot establish fit. The October 31 study already found long garments splitting during a stride. [Motion evidence](#od7--motion-source-for-shared-clips)

**Where generation helps.** Use concept art for faces, clothing construction, cultural details and the dressed silhouette. Select existing parts first. For a distinctive face, generate or sculpt that part, then an artist may wrap the shared head layout onto it and bake detail. Recheck landmarks, eyelids, neck, controls and moving expressions. Generate a missing garment, pack or prop separately. Every new named character, including a signature character, retains the same reversible body and clothing construction. The established one-piece studies supply historical comparison only. Meshy’s automatic rigger can animate an individual mesh (34 joints on the mercenary); the five sheet models demonstrated clip playback, not a family of sliders.

**Production order.** Once authorized, audition a complete concept-authored body on the candidate technical layout in the [world-selected fitted coverage](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#baseline-coverage), then fit a challenging outfit and exercise representative motions on different proportions. The selected SAM/MHR pilot and source restrictions in [OD3](#od3--the-humanoid-body-family-and-rig-for-the-cast) govern this work; do not reacquire a donor or reintroduce rejected stock anatomy. AF07 can measure the work of wrapping a selected part. After qualification, prepare the first-playable people, then grow the kit where their sheets show actual gaps (AF10). Compare matched-scale turntables and preserve recipes alongside the source parts and exact releases.

**One recipe for a named person or a population.** Save the selected family revision, continuous measurements, face and parts and colours; use the separate default-outfit recipe only to create actual equipped items; appearance sampling uses its own visual seed, and authored names keep their authored choices. A world can select ranges for a population. AF11 proposes baking batches from qualified parts without new provider calls and checking repeated combinations. The older example of ten builds, twelve heads, ten hairstyles and fifteen outfits yields 18,000 possible selections before colour; it is arithmetic, not evidence of distinct, compatible or attractive people. NPC recognition follows the [perception and knowledge contract](../worlds/base/perception-recognition.md).

**Delivering variations.** Bake the requested assembled GLBs offline first, preserving the separate source parts. A later interactive creator and live equipment changes require safe morph/skin instance ownership, in-game switching and a measured comparison of memory, draw calls, loading and update cost. Sharing parts may save duplicated geometry while extra skinned meshes can add rendering work. No crowd advantage has been measured. [Renderer qualification](../maintainers/3d-pixel-art.md#v3d05--rigged-character-and-equipment-family) · [Design tracker](../maintainers/art/people.md)

The selected output keeps its own scope: an animation-only request preserves its target, a static model need not acquire a rig, and another anatomy is not forced onto the human skeleton ([V3D06](../maintainers/3d-pixel-art.md#v3d06--nonhuman-and-state-composition-proof)). Fixed-outfit studies retain their explicit scope; their exports do not satisfy removable in-play clothing. Runtime assembly needs actual item state, hidden-skin rules and per-wearer renderer ownership. More skinned parts can add draw, material, skinning and bounds work; qualify the actual exported scene rather than introducing a general mesh-merging service.

The world owns appearance ranges and visual sampling separately from simulation randomness. Art does not grant possessions, injuries, age, rank or other facts. Recognition uses acquired evidence, never mesh IDs or private generator metadata; deliberate concealment retains its information rules. A pose can express an admitted action or selected performance, without revealing private motives or causing that action.
