# Continuing NPC lives — product and behavior specification

| Status      | Current progress                                                                                  | Last updated |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Not started | Product and behavior proposals are documented; technical design and runtime delivery remain open. | 2026-10-03   |

**Status: proposed detailed behavior, October 3, 2026.** This specification expands the accepted continuing-world direction into a reviewable product proposal. It does not claim the behavior is implemented, approve paid execution, change the bundled world's human protection, or settle commercial prices. Technical design is deferred at Mike's request.

## 1. The experience and the recommendation

Open Legend should feel like a place where particular people have lives. A player should be able to leave someone pursuing a recognizable intention, pursue their own adventure, and return to a person worth talking to again. That might mean useful help, a surprising choice, a disagreement, unfinished work or simply the pleasure of familiar company. If Ada chooses to make cord, the result and her explanation should agree with the supplies, elapsed work and what she actually knew. Correct progress is the foundation; the payoff is a relationship and a world that develop without constant supervision.

The recommended product rule is **continue commitments and supported activities; spend new reasoning on meaningful changes; deliberately reduce optional new activity in quiet places**. The world need not simulate every footstep or compose every unobserved conversation. It must preserve the people, resources, choices and consequences that a player can later encounter.

This is both a creative and an economic choice. A community benefits from routines, unfinished intentions and occasional developments. It does not become more believable merely because every resident generates text at a fixed interval. Routine competence should be cheap; difficult choices should receive attention; unusual new behavior must explain its ongoing cost before it is admitted.

The first playable contribution belongs in the accepted [one-map, one-live-NPC creative loop](../../archive/05-project/first-playable-mvp.md): gather, invent and craft an actual new sling, hunt, harvest and eat, with another supported invention also exercised. The same resident should retain their own goal, continue supported work while the player explores and remember a relevant interaction. A cord timer, carrying bundle or shelter demonstration alone does not satisfy that loop. Preserve existing delivered work and qualify the remaining experience through its existing owners.

Begin with continuation during the personal world's permitted running time and its existing pause/absence policy. A small community that continues without connected players is a later service milestone, with its own supported work, self-care and funding evidence. The illustrative roof-repair story remains a later consumer of the shelter feature. The [package delivery sequence](five-product-feature-specs.md#game-first-delivery-sequence) connects these contributions; completing every stage of this specification is not a prerequisite for useful invention, construction or social play.

### 1.1 What this adds to existing work

The [background contract](../product-scalability/background-progression.md) already accepts legal coarse outcomes, independent mechanical/cognitive/social/presentation detail, preserved activity ownership and truthful unsupported behavior. The [PS tracker](../maintainers/product-scalability.md) identifies PS02 and PS03 as proposed runtime work. Current elapsed-time integration, ordinary NPC agency, saved methods and human departure handling are foundations, not completed unattended-community delivery.

This proposal selects the first product scope, describes everyday and adverse behavior, recommends how initiative and funding should work, and defines the evidence required before expanding. It does not replace the existing agency, action, perception, time or spending owners. The first scope uses the current mechanical clock. Separate calendar pacing, broad dangerous-logout rules, federation and cross-world economic compatibility remain their existing PS05–PS07 work.

### 1.2 Intended audiences

The visitor wants to encounter a coherent place without managing all its inhabitants. The returning resident wants investments and relationships to remain intelligible. The creator wants to choose a kind of continuing world and understand its demands. NPCs need useful autonomy under their actual knowledge, capabilities and permissions. The operator needs a service promise whose mandatory work can be afforded even when nobody logs in.

### 1.3 Why a player would care what happens next

Continuing life should create room for player choice. A resident may need the same material the player wants, offer useful knowledge, decline a request, change a plan after a discovery or pursue a personal interest. The player can cooperate, negotiate, compete through supported actions, investigate or leave them to it. Ordinary conversation, curiosity, leisure and aesthetic interests are valid motives alongside survival and production. No one particular personality or peaceful village story is mandatory.

For example, a resident preparing fibers for their own project may be interested in the player's newly invented sling but unwilling to give up all their supplies. The player can find another source, discuss a smaller contribution or proceed alone. On returning from a hunt, they encounter the resident's actual progress and can show what their invention did. This is an illustrative combination of existing capabilities, not a scripted quest or a requirement that an NPC unlock the player's invention route.

An autonomous world can also produce disappointment, rivalry, deception, injury or death where supported. Those possibilities should arise from motives, evidence and world rules, not forced drama scheduled to prove that life continued. Quiet satisfaction is also a successful outcome. The [playability gates](five-product-feature-specs.md#playability-gates) require evidence that continuity enriches the player's own pursuits and reduces supervision before population or unattended activity expands.

## 2. Product commitments

1. **The same people continue.** Detail changes do not copy, replace or reroll an individual. A particular companion cannot be in two places because two players independently want company.
2. **Chosen work has consequences.** Progress, consumed materials, injury, fatigue, custody, shared resources and accepted promises survive leaving and returning.
3. **A prediction is conditional.** Expected completion is an estimate. Intervening shortages, a heard alarm or a removed bridge may change the outcome before it occurs.
4. **Knowledge has a route.** A danger somewhere in town is not knowledge for every resident. Seeing, hearing, being told and an installed supported sense remain different sources.
5. **Absence does not manufacture a life for a human.** An NPC's autonomy does not authorize speech, romance, purchases or new goals for an absent human-controlled character.
6. **Quiet life is an explicit policy.** A quiet world starts fewer optional encounters and ambitions. It continues supported work and handles actual obligations; it does not owe a later performance of every omitted thought.
7. **Failure remains meaningful.** Resource scarcity, bad choices and supported world dangers can cause failure. Missing compute is reported as service unavailability, rather than rewritten as an NPC's deliberate irresponsibility.
8. **More expensive is not more important by definition.** A public official may need complex reasoning; an ordinary friend can matter just as much to a player. Neither model choice nor subscription grants hidden survival advantages.

These commitments extend the [accepted scalability principles](../product-scalability/README.md), the [participation contract](../product-scalability/participation-and-protection.md) and the [engine/world boundary](../engine-and-world-boundaries.md). Existing privacy, spending and human-protection obligations remain controlling.

## 3. What continues, at what level

### 3.1 Independent kinds of detail

An activity should receive the detail its actual interaction needs. A visible worker may need a credible pose and progress without another decision. A distant leader may need an important private decision while their walking remains inexpensive. A crowd may need physical presence and aggregate sound without individually generated chatter.

Creators should see ordinary terms such as **active**, **quiet** and **paused**, with an explanation of what continues. These are service/activity descriptions, not different copies of the world. Players should ordinarily see people doing things rather than a technical detail-level indicator. A visible operational interruption belongs in service feedback, not in fictional narration.

| Situation                                            | Recommended behavior                                                                    | What a later player can rely on                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A supported task proceeds under unchanged conditions | Continue the chosen method and calculate lawful progress to relevant changes            | Work and resource use agree; no decision per small step       |
| A traveler follows a known viable route              | Advance through supported route stages; retain bodily needs and encounter opportunities | Arrival, current location and possessions are coherent        |
| A resident has an idle opportunity                   | Consider a bounded new initiative under the world's activity policy                     | A new choice is possible, not guaranteed every fictional hour |
| A resident has a real obligation becoming due        | Handle its evidence and supported continuation; obtain a required choice when needed    | The obligation is not silently dropped as optional chatter    |
| A meaningful stimulus is actually perceived          | Continue a previously chosen contingency or make a fresh scoped decision                | The reaction uses what this resident learned                  |
| An unfamiliar mechanic lacks background support      | Use its declared funded detail, approved approximation, restriction or refusal          | The creator knew the limit before relying on it               |
| The host is paused or unavailable                    | Follow the existing pause/recovery contract and disclose stopped progression            | No fabricated catch-up or hidden completed future             |

### 3.2 Activity continuation is conditional, not a scripted biography

A chosen activity has an understandable objective, method, current step, relevant location, intended inputs and conditions for stopping. For example: gather available reeds at the river, stop when the bag is full or fatigue makes the trip inappropriate, return to the camp and prepare cord if the known materials remain available. This is a bounded chosen course of action, not permission to invent a month of the character's life.

The activity may continue through routine steps already justified by that choice. It cannot reinterpret "make cord" as "steal a neighbor's rope," decide to sell the result, accept a dangerous escort job or abandon an old friend merely because those outcomes would be convenient. Those are new choices with new consequences.

A resident can have known habits and standing plans, but their origin matters. Creator-authored starting routines, the resident's later choices, learned methods and a player's request are distinguishable. A request is not obedience unless the resident actually accepts or the world explicitly supplies that kind of control.

### 3.3 Initial activity coverage

The initial behavior family should be deliberately small but complete enough to sustain a recognizable day. The proposal is supported gathering, finite crafting/preparation, ordinary travel on known paths, eating from accessible known supplies, and resting. This selects candidate consumers; each still needs confirmation against the current implementations before technical design.

For gathering, the same finite source is shared with other actors. A long unattended interval cannot award a full yield to every collector. A completed unit can be retained when later work stops. For crafting, required tools, materials, location and declared work stages remain real. For eating, the character must know about accessible food and use the same consumption rules. For resting, interruption and changed bodily conditions follow the chosen world's rules.

If one family cannot yet support an interruption safely, the first release narrows its supported continuation and tells the creator. A prototype may prove one complete task before admitting the full set. It must not advertise a self-sustaining community while excluding the activity its residents need to stay alive.

## 4. Needs, competence and believable autonomy

### 4.1 Reduce new decisions without making residents helpless

Lowering optional thought frequency can accidentally make hunger urgent before a resident next decides to eat. The product therefore requires a viable routine: enough chosen continuation and useful reactions to handle known needs at the offered activity rate. A creator should be able to understand a failure as lack of food, access, knowledge, time or an actual choice—not a hidden chance that the thinker never ran.

This does not install an omniscient survival controller when an AI service fails. A resident can continue an already chosen meal plan or a supported authored immediate behavior. Unknown food, unexplored routes and unaccepted assistance remain unknown. A character who cannot solve a genuinely new problem may need a new decision, assistance or suffer consequences allowed by the world's rules.

Distinguish inability within the fiction from inability of a required service. If a required supported decision is unavailable and allowing time to continue would impose an otherwise avoidable imminent need or danger consequence solely because that service failed, apply the coherent operational-failure boundary in section 9.3. Do not freeze only that actor while competitors continue, or hide the outage as a deliberate refusal. Optional unanswered opportunities may simply lapse under the disclosed policy. This requirement applies to provider outage and unresolved execution as well as exhausted funding.

Before unattended operation is offered, demonstrate ordinary survival with the selected initial knowledge, supplies and habits. Test shortages separately. Abundant test supplies are not evidence that residents cope with an actual supply interruption.

### 4.2 What deserves a new decision

New reasoning is justified when the current method becomes invalid, an important goal or obligation changes, a consequential unfamiliar opportunity appears, or the resident acquires evidence requiring a choice their existing plan does not cover. Routine progress, repeated equivalent stimuli and unchanged scenery do not automatically justify another paid decision.

Examples include finding the promised tools missing, learning that a friend needs help, discovering a route blocked, or deciding what to do after completing the current task. Repeated footsteps by the same harmless neighbor should normally be one continuing circumstance. Being struck remains a physical event even when the attacker is outside attention.

A completed job may naturally lead to rest, another already planned step or an idle opportunity. It need not force an immediate new ambition. The product should permit satisfaction, ordinary leisure and uneventful time; nonstop crises and productivity are not the only evidence of life.

### 4.3 Preserve personality without prescribing all choices

Routines should express useful differences: one person prepares food early, another seeks company after work, another persists with a project despite inconvenience. These differences come from authored identity and actual chosen behavior. They are not a universal fixed schedule derived from a trait label.

Independence must be visible before the community grows. The first live resident already needs meaningful choices under the accepted agency contract; do not postpone that requirement until a later autonomous-community stage. They can explain a preference, refuse or amend a request, continue a personal goal and reconsider when circumstances change. A perfectly reliable worker who only acts after player instruction does not demonstrate this experience. Nor does arbitrary refusal: a player should be able to discover enough permitted context to decide how to respond, without receiving the resident's private reasoning as a diagnostic readout.

The system should remember successful methods and relevant disappointments through the existing memory owners. A single failed attempt need not permanently change a personality. Skill progression and personality evolution belong to ND04; this feature consumes current knowledge and choices without silently adding numerical growth.

## 5. Interruptions and competing activity

### 5.1 Interrupt at the event that matters

When an alarm reaches Ada halfway through work, resolve her legitimate progress up to that moment. Retain completed work and resource use. Then apply an existing chosen contingency or request a new decision based on the heard evidence. Do not finish the entire task first and retroactively describe it as interrupted.

An unseen enemy arriving is not itself an alarm. The world needs a supported route by which someone detects or encounters the arrival and communicates it. If no one notices, people may continue their work. This allows surprise and imperfect information without requiring universal surveillance.

### 5.2 Interruption outcomes must be understandable

| Change                                    | Product outcome                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Resource depleted by another actor        | Preserve completed work; stop or use an already authorized alternative; explain the actual shortage |
| Tool removed or becomes unusable          | Stop at a valid stage; do not substitute another person's tool silently                             |
| Route or work site changes                | Recheck the next relevant step; wait, reroute through known supported options, or reconsider        |
| Relevant permission revoked               | Stop future prohibited use; retain lawful completed consequences and explain the access change      |
| Injury or fatigue changes capability      | Apply actual bodily consequences and the current plan's stopping conditions                         |
| Alarm, request or important conversation  | Record permitted evidence; prioritize according to urgency, obligation and current choice           |
| Player cancels an authorized task         | End remaining work; preserve completed effects and the declared disposition of unused resources     |
| Service cannot obtain a required decision | Preserve the unresolved choice and expose unavailability; do not fabricate the answer               |

Not every interruption cancels a whole project. A worker may pause a current step and resume it later if its conditions remain valid. Some activities lose partial progress or material by their actual rules; that must be visible before starting when reasonably knowable.

### 5.3 Several people can affect the same task

Shared supplies, a narrow route or joint work makes individual progress interdependent. Two residents cannot each consume the last item, and a helper's arrival does not automatically mean they consented to work. A multi-worker result must reflect actual accepted contributions. Departure preserves completed contributions and changes the remaining estimate.

A resident cannot reserve the whole community's supplies indefinitely through a vague long-term intention. Only the actual supported commitment reserves resources under its existing rules. Broader storage ownership and barter remain their own features, but unattended work must use them consistently.

## 6. Entering, leaving and returning

### 6.1 Arrive in the present

When a player approaches, the game presents the resident at a valid point in the current activity. It does not replay hours of walking or choose a convenient location disconnected from the route. A person cooking cannot appear across town merely because that makes their introduction attractive.

Only interaction-relevant detail needs to become ready. Seeing a distant building does not require every inhabitant to start thinking. Sound, ranged effects, a fast approach and unusual senses may require attention before visual proximity. The product promise is readiness for supported interaction, not a universal activation distance.

Any preparation delay is technical waiting. Before destination admission, retain a valid source-side situation and do not invent destination hunger, attack exposure or participation to explain the delay. An already-present actor still experiences the actual continuing world's consequences while an interaction is prepared; technical waiting is not temporary immunity. Do not add fictitious travel duration or charge a failed arrival as successful travel. A second player joins the same actual situation. One player leaving does not simplify an encounter that another is still actively affecting.

### 6.2 Leave without changing the odds by watching

Walking away does not complete unfinished work, cancel danger, replenish resources or reroll a random result. Background outcomes may be coarser than a hypothetical detailed trace, but repeatedly crossing the detail boundary must not be a reliable way to get better yields, safer fights or different checks.

If an outcome cannot be represented fairly at reduced detail, keep the relevant interaction detailed within its funded envelope or decline the unsupported transition. A conveniently described result is not sufficient. The [participation owner](../product-scalability/participation-and-protection.md) retains the separate dangerous-logout and protected-outcome requirements.

### 6.3 A useful return account

On return, foreground the player's current location and condition, what happened to activities they had a right to know about, and what now requires a decision. Let them investigate more detail. The return experience should answer "Where are my things? Who is available? What changed? What is still unfinished?" without presenting a compulsory essay.

Distinguish personal experience, a named resident's report, public news, observed present conditions and estimates. An absent human does not remember an NPC conversation they never heard. If a coarse event retained only its meaning, later narration cannot produce an exact historical transcript.

Changes should be discoverable through the world as well as a summary: supplies have moved, a task is incomplete, a resident mentions an interruption, or an accessible record explains a promise. An unavailable narrator should not hide the actual gameplay facts.

### 6.4 Time away should create context, not a compulsory chore bill

The same principle applies while playing. After a resident chooses a viable routine, the player should not need to repeatedly tell them to eat, resume the same work or take each ordinary step. Material shortages and changed goals can invite intervention; routine supervision should not consume the session. While a task continues, the player can explore, pursue an invention, hunt, talk or inspect another supported opportunity. Waiting can express scarce time and a deliberate tradeoff, but an unexplained countdown with nothing useful to choose is not the desired activity.

Tune the initial world's supplies, work durations and ordinary needs around completing the creative loop and having room for a voluntary next goal. If playtests mostly become repetitive provisioning or waiting, revise those authored conditions and the unnecessary interactions before adding more residents. Preserve meaningful survival choices and harder authored worlds; this is a recommendation for the initial experience, not a universal removal of need or labor.

The product should avoid treating every real-world hour away as social neglect or enqueuing every missed optional encounter on return. Relationships can change through actual events and choices, but the service should not invent accusations that a protected departed character knowingly ignored a request.

Real obligations still matter. If the character actually accepted a delivery before departure and the world's disclosed clock continues, its handling follows that contract. The authoring and acceptance flow must make this clear. An obligation may be deferred, delegated, fulfilled or broken only through its supported rules and actual decisions.

## 7. Quiet-community initiative

### 7.1 Quiet does not mean empty

A quiet community should retain visible routines and allow a smaller stream of meaningful new developments: visiting someone known, changing a project, helping with a real need, discussing a recent event, or pursuing a supported opportunity. A week need not contain a forced crisis. When the world is active, its existing commitments and participants can naturally create more opportunities.

The recommended default is lower optional initiative while no human is actively participating in the relevant community, with a clear creator-facing description. This affects new starts; it must not erase an already accepted promise or secretly lower an engaged opponent's competence. Model quality, initiative frequency and mechanical fidelity are independently chosen.

### 7.2 Allocate opportunity across people and purposes

New initiative should not all go to the loudest actor, the highest-status role or the person with the longest private history. Consider outstanding needs, real obligations, neglected opportunities, existing commitments and relationship relevance within permitted scope. Reserve room for ordinary residents and new arrivals to act; otherwise a few politically central agents can consume the community's entire narrative life.

This is not a fairness quota requiring every resident to generate a thought each day. Unused optional opportunities disappear. A quiet artisan can have a satisfying routine without an expensive monologue. The exact allocation policy should be judged against observed diversity, coherence and cost, and should be understandable to creators at the level of activity styles.

### 7.3 Avoid cascades of manufactured significance

A new conversation may prompt a real reply or later reflection, but it must not automatically require every witness to deliberate and then generate reactions to all those reactions. Group repeated background circumstances before expensive interpretation. Limit new accepted activity to what the world can sustain; do not let an NPC's suggestion automatically authorize unlimited downstream spending.

The scene specification owns shared social resolution. Until a social family is supported there, this feature should use existing permitted interactions or continue quietly. It cannot fill a missing social mechanic with a whole-town story that creates goods, promises or consent.

## 8. Economic integrity inside the world

### 8.1 Production is actual production

Unattended output uses actual inputs, tools, work, knowledge, capacity and supported world laws. A shop does not fill itself with plausible inventory because someone later visits. Travel takes a supported route and time. Trade preserves actual custody and agreement. Magical creation may be a legitimate authored source, but remains an explicit rule with its costs and permissions.

Long periods can be resolved efficiently where the activity family supports it. That efficiency is not a license to award independent full output to competing workers or ignore the point when an input ran out. A rate estimate must stop or change at the relevant depletion, demand or intervention.

### 8.2 Scarcity creates play without constant economic collapse

The first community should begin with enough supported knowledge and accessible supplies to demonstrate ordinary life, while including a shortage the player can understand and affect. This is initial world authoring, not a hidden perpetual subsidy. If the world needs scheduled replenishment, trade arrivals or fantastical production, those are visible authored mechanisms.

Avoid requiring a realistic market, full ecology or currency before proving this feature. Shared finite food, a limited tool, a promised item and interrupted work already expose the important causal relationships. Later barter and ecology can add pressure without replacing the original resource history.

### 8.3 Time and exports

The first scope retains current clock meanings and does not accrue production during an actual host pause or downtime unless an explicitly supported later policy says otherwise. Human inactivity while a funded host continues is different from a crashed or paused host. The return account must distinguish them.

The world cannot multiply exportable output by changing calendar labels or stepping across detail boundaries. Cross-world trade remains gated on clock and economy compatibility. Service spending and real-money obligations never rewind with fictional time.

## 9. Service economics and behavior at the budget boundary

### 9.1 Sell a supportable continuing world

The service should describe three distinct promises: preserving stored history, continuing supported mechanical life, and funding optional new intelligence and richer expression. Buying storage does not promise perpetual reasoning. Paying for optional activity must not buy preferential adjudication, hidden direct bonuses, immunity or an opponent deprived of its promised service. More legitimate preparation or production can affect later outcomes; disclose that effect rather than promising that all funding differences are competitively neutral. The recommended shared-world model funds a common activity profile, with incompatible privately subsidized production subject to the existing import rules.

The recommended creator view is a plain-language activity profile, a current funding allowance, observed usage, a forecast range and an explanation of the dominant cost sources. Before enabling unattended operation, show whether the selected community can sustain its ordinary needs and commitments within the qualified profile. A creator can lower optional initiative, choose a smaller supported starting population, remove an expensive optional mechanic or fund more activity.

Do not present a precise forecast before sufficient observations exist. A new world should say that its estimate is provisional and show which assumptions matter. A small quiet sample does not cover festivals, mass return, invention cascades or emergencies.

### 9.2 Cost model in product terms

For a selected period, total cost is the sum of supported mechanical progression, model decisions, conversation and expression, relevant memory work, persistence and delivery, plus operational headroom. The bill depends on event density, history size, new activity, interacting participants and required outcomes as well as population.

An intentionally illustrative sensitivity calculation shows why the decision policy matters. Assume 100 residents and a hypothetical **$0.001 per complete discretionary decision**. This is an arithmetic input, not a current provider price, offer or forecast. It excludes all other costs.

| New decisions per resident | Decisions for the community per real day | Illustrative decision-only cost |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------: | ------------------------------: |
| One per day                |                                      100 |                           $0.10 |
| One per hour               |                                    2,400 |                           $2.40 |
| One per minute             |                                  144,000 |                         $144.00 |

Changing that assumed cost to $0.01 multiplies every result by ten. A cheaper model does not remove the difference between meaningful reconsideration and indiscriminate polling. Conversely, reducing decisions so far that people fail to eat or interact is not a viable saving. Measure the complete workflow: choosing an action may cause tools, validation, memory and later reactions, not one isolated text completion.

Use the remaining funded envelope to admit new optional activity after accounting for mandatory supported work, existing commitments, uncertain charges and a measured burst reserve. Exact prices and reserve sizes belong to later measured operational and commercial work. A fixed dollar promise for arbitrary invented mechanics would be dishonest.

A fictional promise is not a perpetual compute reservation. A resident may make an unrealistic promise and later fail; retain that social fact without guaranteeing funded fulfillment. Automatically continuing work must declare a bounded funded operating window or renewable service envelope and what happens if renewal fails. A standing obligation may remain meaningful for years, but cannot force unlimited future inference or authorize new spending by itself. At the boundary, use the already disclosed continuation, notification, deferral or coherent suspension rule; never erase the obligation to make the budget appear balanced.

### 9.3 Proposed degradation order

1. Stop redundant interpretation of unchanged circumstances and avoid generating ambient detail nobody needs.
2. Reduce new optional initiatives, rich retellings and speculative investigation under the disclosed quiet policy.
3. Stop admitting new optional expensive commitments when their continuation cannot be supported.
4. Preserve already admitted mechanical consequences, necessary evidence and supported urgent responses within the funded operating envelope.
5. If even that envelope cannot be maintained, or a required unavailable service would otherwise impose an outcome solely through its failure, use an explicit operational pause at a coherent boundary under existing pause/participation authority; announce what stopped and require an authorized resumption.

The fifth step is a service failure or exhausted service allowance, not an ordinary optimization. It cannot selectively freeze a losing participant while opponents continue, erase an already admitted attack or suspend a producer while silently honoring its dependent exports. Coupled activity needs a coherent common boundary. A creator cannot obtain a competitive escape by deliberately withholding funding. Public/shared deployment needs the corresponding PS05/PS06 admission and operational policy before offering this mode.

A world resuming after suspension continues from its actual preserved point. It does not buy or invent a backlog of missed optional life. A charge with uncertain completion stays uncertain; a provider failure does not trigger an automatic paid retry.

### 9.4 Growth that must be controlled

| Growing source                              | Product control and boundary behavior                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| More residents                              | Admit a measured activity profile; explain unsupported population/activity combinations before promising continuity                        |
| More new events from one person or object   | Treat repeated equivalent disturbance as a continuing circumstance; restrict additional optional reactions while preserving actual effects |
| More interacting work and shared resources  | Admit only supported coupling; preserve real contention rather than assuming independent progress                                          |
| More long-lived history                     | Keep permitted current understanding useful and retrieve relevant evidence; never require formatting the whole past for every choice       |
| More unfinished investigations and requests | Show pending work and its reason; reject or defer new optional work before an unbounded waiting backlog develops                           |
| More supported mechanics                    | Explain which require funded detailed execution and which inherit economical continuation                                                  |
| More observers arriving together            | Reuse the same present and preparation; offer an honest supported arrival wait instead of running all missed history                       |

No universal population, thought count or model tier is selected here. These are not unlimited dimensions: their actual admissible values require qualification against the selected product profile. [PS-L03, PS-L04, PS-L10, PS-L13 and PS-L17](../limits/product-scalability.md) own the shared restrictions.

## 10. Player, creator and operator journeys

### CL-J01 — Leave a useful task and return halfway

Ada accepts a request for cord and chooses an available method. The player leaves to pursue their own supported goal while work is underway. On returning, they can recognize progress, understand a relevant interruption and decide whether to help, change their request or continue independently. They did not need to supervise each work step or repeat the original request. Ada may also have an unfinished interest of her own; the encounter need not become another compulsory chore.

In the later shared-world case, another player visits before the task finishes. Both encounter the same unfinished work and supplies. The first player's later return does not restart it or take priority over the second visitor. The visible progress and Ada's account explain what happened at appropriate detail.

### CL-J02 — A real alarm interrupts

A guard detects a threat and shouts. Ada hears a permitted fragment and stops work if her chosen contingency or fresh decision calls for it. She may know that there is danger without knowing who caused it. Her cord is partly complete and the used fibers remain used. If the alarm was out of range, her lack of reaction is consistent with her knowledge. The player can discover the difference through actual evidence.

### CL-J03 — Two people need the last supplies

Two gatherers choose the same finite source. One arrives earlier; another has a larger bag. Their outcome follows actual access, progress and the world's contention rules, not which is currently visible. The unsuccessful person can wait, seek a known alternative, ask for help or reconsider. They do not repeatedly restart the identical failed approach forever.

### CL-J04 — The promised helper does not arrive

Two residents agree to work together. One is delayed by an actual blocked route. The project receives only the labor actually performed. The worker may hear about the delay or interpret it incorrectly; the agreement and its fulfillment remain separate from that interpretation. A summary never places the helper at the work site while their body is still elsewhere.

### CL-J05 — A competent routine meets genuine scarcity

A resident with a chosen meal routine eats when appropriate from known accessible supplies even when optional initiative is low. Remove the supplies, then repeat. The routine fails for a specific reason and creates a meaningful choice or consequence. The test must not confuse unlimited pantry access with adequate autonomy.

### CL-J06 — A player changes the environment midtask

The player removes a relevant tool or blocks a route under valid authority. Future work changes at the relevant moment; completed work remains. A different authorized person supplies a replacement and the resident resumes only if it fits the current method or they choose to adapt. No lost tool reappears because the task was originally planned with it.

### CL-J07 — Return after a long absence

The player finds a recognizable community with current work, relationships and consequences. Optional events are fewer under the quiet policy. The summary provides important permitted changes and unresolved commitments without inventing a transcript or months of missed personal memories. Continuing hosted time and actual host suspension are clearly distinguished.

### CL-J08 — Funding becomes insufficient

The creator receives an understandable warning and can inspect the major causes. New optional activity is reduced or declined. Already admitted work follows its funded contract. If the supported baseline cannot continue, operational suspension is explicit and coherent. Restoring funding does not silently execute delayed paid calls or backdate new decisions.

### CL-J09 — A player tries to farm detail transitions

The player repeatedly leaves and returns during a yield, journey or hazardous activity. The attempts do not regenerate resources, duplicate output, reroll an already established occurrence or remove an ongoing conflict. Compare many equivalent cases for a systematic advantage, not only one identical trace.

### CL-J10 — A quiet place receives a crowd

Several players approach from different directions. Interaction-relevant work becomes ready once for the actual shared present. Residents do not all deliver accumulated greetings and reflections simultaneously. Necessary encounters remain responsive; additional optional starts obey capacity admission and the attention rules.

### CL-J11 — A background account contains a secret

One resident steals from another unseen. Only permitted evidence enters each person's memory. A town-level operational view may know the actual custody change, but it does not teach every resident the thief's identity. A victim can notice a shortage and investigate. An explanation is allowed to remain incomplete.

### CL-J12 — A novel authored mechanic arrives

A creator proposes a self-replicating magical workshop. Its description does not guarantee cheap unattended execution. The creator sees the actual supported behavior, growth implications and any restriction before enabling it. Existing supported crafts remain usable if the proposal is declined. New powers do not silently acquire unlimited resources, agents or paid decisions.

Three design conclusions deserve emphasis. First, selective reconsideration has direct small-study support [CL-R02], while large population demos leave substantial reliability and operating questions [CL-R04–05]. Second, ordinary simulation developers repeatedly encounter hidden coupling and emergent loops [CL-R08–10]; a language model cannot remove those constraints. Third, persistence, current recollection and ongoing activity are separate product promises [CL-R06, CL-R12]. The detailed behavior above is intended to make those promises independently testable.

## 12. Alternatives and tradeoffs

**Pause every empty world.** This is simple and economical, and can remain an explicitly chosen session-based product. It does not deliver continuing communities. The recommended primary mode continues funded supported life and discloses its reduced optional activity.

**Run every resident at foreground intensity.** This maximizes opportunities for autonomous novelty but creates open-ended costs and more opportunities for inconsistency. Use increased evaluated attention for situations that need it, not an unconditional permanent promise.

**Generate a plausible history when a player returns.** This can be cheap for an isolated story, but it conflicts with shared resources, other visitors, actual commitments and intervention. The recommended world records real supported outcomes as they occur; a later account describes them.

**Use one universal needs-driven routine.** This may be inexpensive but can flatten individuality and violate current actor-choice requirements. Continue chosen plans and supported world-authored routines; do not replace the resident's intent whenever paid reasoning becomes inconvenient.

**Guarantee identical microscopic outcomes at every detail.** Exact equality is useful for particular supported mechanics, but a universal guarantee would obstruct the economical approximation accepted by the parent design. Require legal history, preserved commitments and no systematic exploitable advantage; declare the remaining approximation.

## 13. Proposed stages and acceptance

### Stage 1 — One continuing activity

Deliver one current supported task for the existing live resident, with real supplies, progress and interruptions. During the accepted invention-and-hunting loop, the player can leave the resident to it, pursue their own goal and return to intelligible progress and a useful conversation. Existing autonomous choice and recall remain required; this stage does not turn the resident into a passive work queue. Qualify CL-J01–03, CL-J06 and CL-J09, including ordinary completion, deprivation, cancellation, a heard and unheard alarm and save/reopen. The second-observer case qualifies later shared operation; it does not require another resident or player for the initial personal playtest. The first stage is incomplete if it only shows a timer filling.

### Stage 2 — A viable quiet day

Combine the selected work, travel and self-care families with existing chosen routines. Demonstrate sustained provisioning where resources permit it, understandable failure where they do not, and no repeated failed travel or work loops. The player can pursue a creative or exploratory goal without becoming the resident's constant caretaker, and can identify a choice that expressed the resident's own priorities. Lower optional cognition without making either competence or that independence disappear. This is the initial PS03 consumer. Expand to a small group when relationships and competing intentions add interesting choices, rather than using population as the success measure.

### Stage 3 — Meaningful new developments

Add a bounded policy for new unobserved developments and only the supported social families from the scene feature. This expands existing agency; it is not the first point at which an NPC may make a meaningful choice. Demonstrate real participation, preserved promises, imperfect knowledge and return explanations through an encounter that gives the player a reason to respond: an opportunity, disagreement, discovery, changed relationship or welcome ordinary company. A month of test time should not require a month of invented dialogue on entry; historical length and present interaction cost must be measured separately.

### Stage 4 — Funded shared operation

Qualify long absence, burst arrivals, actual growth in memories and objects, recurring hazards, quiet and busy communities, funding warnings and coherent operational suspension. Integrate applicable PS05/PS06 policies before relying on cross-boundary pauses or contested absentee consequences. Offer unattended continuation when it produces a worthwhile return experience at an affordable complete cost; a successful personal world need not wait for this service. Expand published capacity only to workloads actually qualified.

### Behavioral acceptance matrix

| Acceptance question                                | Required evidence                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Does the task continue for the same person?        | Identity, location, intention and completed effects survive leaving, returning and current-format restoration                                                       |
| Are interrupted outcomes causal?                   | Changes before completion affect remaining work at their occurrence; no work is credited beyond a stopping event                                                    |
| Can people live at the offered activity rate?      | Supported self-care works with known accessible resources; shortages remain real and diagnosable                                                                    |
| Does shared activity actually happen?              | Labor, consent and participation agree with bodies, time and resource records                                                                                       |
| Does detail cycling change incentives?             | Repeated paired scenarios reveal no reliable yield, risk, resource or randomness advantage from entering/leaving                                                    |
| Are residents informed only through valid sources? | Hidden thefts, unheard alarms and private conversations do not leak through summaries                                                                               |
| Does the whole community function?                 | Long runs identify stuck loops, permanent shortages, runaway output, missed commitments and weak return experience                                                  |
| Can the player pursue their own goal?              | During the actual creative loop, routine NPC work/self-care proceeds without repeated instructions; waiting and maintenance leave room for meaningful choices       |
| Does the resident seem independently motivated?    | Players can identify a preference or decision from observed conduct, including a credible refusal or changed plan, without reading private diagnostics              |
| Is returning worth doing?                          | Players encounter discoverable continuity and voluntarily choose what to do next; company, curiosity, help, conflict and deliberate solitude are all valid outcomes |
| Is quiet operation actually cheaper?               | Complete costs for the same supported scenario are measured across quiet/active conditions, including required work and memory                                      |
| Does history growth remain usable?                 | Long-return cases recover relevant changes, correct outdated beliefs and abstain from unknown detail without whole-history reconstruction                           |
| Is overload honest and fair?                       | Noise, mass return and funding exhaustion cause the declared admission/degradation behavior without selective fictional immunity                                    |

Also test a provider outage just before necessary self-care, a preparation delay for an already-engaged actor, and a standing obligation outliving its funded operating window. These must respectively preserve the published failure contract, avoid immunity and retain the obligation without unlimited implied spending.

Quality comparisons must include real human play and review of actual causal records. Scripted fixtures establish specific behavior, not sustained enjoyment or model quality. Proposed study workloads may include a small camp, a larger town and multiple quiet communities; they are experiment conditions, not promised population limits. Success thresholds for cost and latency must be selected from the offered service and measured route before release, rather than copied from another game's benchmark.

Observe where a player spends effort: choosing and trying ideas, enjoying interaction, repeating instructions, servicing needs, waiting or decoding what happened. Ask what they remember about the resident and what they would choose to do next; a correct activity log does not answer those questions. These are proposed playtests, not measured enjoyment claims. If repeated supervision dominates, simplify routines and interaction. If returns produce little interest, improve the initial motives, opportunities and discoverable consequences before increasing optional thought frequency. If unattended life adds cost without a worthwhile experience, defer that service while retaining useful continuation during active play. Do not repair a weak result by fabricating surprise, guaranteed cooperation or an obligatory crisis.

## 14. Remaining adoption decisions and later technical questions

The recommendation is to first prove an independently motivated, continuing resident inside the accepted personal creative loop, using current time and absence rules. The later unattended service uses a quiet-by-default profile, supported work/self-care, bounded optional initiative and explicit funding, and is earned by the return-experience and cost evidence above. Mike's acceptance of those product choices remains separate from commissioning this proposal. The existing central decision register retains clock, background-harm, dangerous-logout and commercial-funding decisions; this document does not silently close them.

Before enabling the full service, choose the initial community and activity profile, its publicly described optional-initiative behavior, the measured funding envelope, and any new protected harm categories through their existing owners. This is a bounded adoption list, not a reason to postpone the complete activity design above. Technical design will map these behaviors onto current owners and establish qualified implementation limits; no class or storage design is selected here.
