# Shared equipment and scoped institutions

**Status:** In progress — comprehensive product and behavior proposal for DG32; adoption, technical design and runtime delivery remain open. Written 2026-10-09 UTC against the inspected `fffdafcf3f7b92d63e1a43e551627cde67708dea` baseline. This document proposes an independently useful first service and separately gated extensions; it does not establish a government, launch an economy or report implemented lending.

## 1. The shared undertaking: an outing becomes possible

The Clearing Tool Library has one useful bow. Neri owns it, but does not want to accompany every hunt or give it away permanently. Sol has accepted responsibility for lending it under Neri's stated terms. A player would like to try an actual ranged hunt before acquiring a bow of their own. They can see what the bow does, arrange compatible ammunition separately, borrow it, leave for the encounter and bring that same bow back. A later borrower can then use it. The pleasure is the wanted outing and the different tool, with a recognizable relationship of trust around them.

This is an optional authored service in the [shared-equipment profile](../worlds/base/shared-equipment-and-institutions.md), not a prebuilt village added to every new wilderness. Neri and Sol are role names for the selected authored episode, not instructions to rewrite existing characters or create a new human-control route. The first qualification involves one owner, one willing keeper and one human borrower, one exact individual bow and one actual depot. Neri has also accepted the narrowly described fallback keeper role; this demonstrates continuity without requiring a third continuously simulated employee. Other worlds can supply different people and equipment through the same explicit contracts.

The borrower does not become an employee, receive free hunting proficiency or acquire guaranteed meat. Current hunting binds actual tools and ammunition and performs one finite attack; a shot can miss, an animal can escape, and harvesting/cooking remain separate supported work. The service promises access to the particular usable bow, not success at the activity. Nobody must hunt: inspecting the tool, declining, returning early or choosing another activity are valid outcomes. A resident keeper may refuse a new request or choose another goal. Being appointed supplies permission, not a compulsory work schedule.

The first loan is deliberately unsecured and has no automatic deadline. Neri chooses to risk temporary unavailability or loss of this one bow because enabling the encounter is worthwhile. There is no universal appraisal, forced full-value deposit, accumulating interest or penalty for missing a login. This is real trust with a finite exposed asset. Calling it a library does not make the risk disappear. [IC-R02](#ic-r02--what-does-a-loan-actually-enable) informs that choice: fully secured lending can withhold access from the person who needed it in the first place.

Ordinary gifts, immediate barter, jointly going out with the owner, or making/acquiring a personal tool remain alternatives. A small woven pouch takes five normal real seconds of native work, and actors have no total carrying-load limit. A bag library cannot be sold as a carrying-strength upgrade or justified by invented scarcity. The bow episode must show a real benefit over those simpler alternatives. If the owner simply giving this bow is the better experience, keep the gift and do not build an institution merely to give the interaction a grander name.

The institution earns its place when cooperation repeats: the owner need not be present at every checkout, borrowers can return without waiting for an employee, and successive people can rely on understandable terms while preserving each person's agency. Those are concrete new behaviors beyond DG22's named association. They justify a small service; they do not justify a parliament, corporation hierarchy or generalized financial system.

## 2. Inherited contracts and the selected boundary

[DG22 durable agreements](durable-agreements-feature-spec.md) already proposes commissions, voluntary exact settlement, narrowly recorded associations and an optional unsecured advance. Its first commission explicitly excludes same-instance loans. The advance is spendable consideration with a possible repayment claim; it is not held collateral. This specification selects the missing same-object lending and scoped service authority under [INV-20.5g–l](../maintainers/inventions-and-world-evolution.md#dg32--shared-equipment-and-scoped-institutions). It reuses existing identity, commitments, item custody, perception and persistence rather than introducing another inventory, reputation or financial account.

At the inspected baseline, [persistent objects](persistent-objects-feature-spec.md) retain exact physical identity and distinguish custody from a declared individual owner. An object can have one declared holder or none. That metadata is not a complete ownership law, and an ordinary declared owner cannot currently edit container access. Creator controls can set a broad access list; an absent list is public and an empty one denies access. Current work reservations do not establish indefinite lending custody, and a handover requires the recipient's assent. The selected library therefore needs genuine new ordinary permission and return consumers. A menu, steward label or attractive chest cannot substitute for them.

Five relationships must remain separate in every explanation:

| Relationship | What it means here | What it does not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membership | A person has joined the stated association and may request its advertised service | Withdrawal, signing, promotion, title or control of another actor |
| Title | A particular individual remains the selected tool's declared principal under the new lending rule | Account authorship, source licensing, physical possession or world administration |
| Custody | The actual item is at a place, in a container or held/equipped by a person | Ownership transfer or freedom to consume it through an unrelated action |
| Mandate | An owner has authorized a named keeper to perform exact lending acts with selected assets | Power to appoint themselves, change the terms, sell the asset or grant wider authority |
| Claim | A known accepted obligation remains, such as returning this bow | Automatic access to bags, remote seizure, guilt, mind control or a platform debt |

The first service uses individually titled assets. It does not pretend that an association record can already own property, and it does not need collective title to work. Real-world account ownership of a definition remains governed by invention and publication rules. Lending a bow neither licenses its manufacturing method nor changes historical creators. A fictional official cannot alter invention locks, creator permissions, real billing, membership allocations or moderation powers.

D11's wider monetary economy remains open. D18's wilderness direction does not authorize a compulsory starting government. The selected institution requires neither answer: it is voluntary, local and funded by a real object its owner can afford to lend. The institution/economy repertoire supplies possibilities, including ECON-017 and ECON-123; priority labels do not establish implemented behavior or require all neighboring political mechanics.

The first scope is one same-world service, one depot and the exact bow, with one outstanding loan for a borrower at this service. It consumes the existing unresolved-commitment envelope; it does not add sixteen more promises beside DG22's sixteen. This is a qualification profile, not a universal one-bow world limit. Numbers and discretionary choices are recorded in [INST-L01–INST-L08](../limits/shared-equipment-and-institutions.md). Broader membership, several assets and several simultaneous services need their actual complete workloads qualified before being offered.

## 3. Establishing the library without inventing a legal person

### 3.1 A real owner offers a specific asset

Neri selects the actual individual bow, identifies the intended lending depot and reviews its current condition, installed capability and authority. A name such as “library bow” cannot transform a stack, repair damage or make an unsupported launcher useful. If an asset is not individual, has ambiguous title, is pledged elsewhere, is held by someone who has not agreed to release it, or is needed by active work, it cannot become available merely because Neri signs a record.

The mandate states: this exact item may be lent to an eligible member after that person accepts return of the same item; no purchase price, consumable supply, fee, interest, substitution or sublending is included. Sol may present and accept those terms, complete the approved checkout, inspect the returned object and take the service out of new-lending availability. Sol cannot sell it, lend it to themselves through their own acceptance, add compensation, erase a claim, give another officer power or take arbitrary goods out of the depot. Neri can separately approve Sol borrowing on the same visible terms. That approval is an owner decision, not an exploit of the keeper role.

Existing sufficient grants are reused. A person should not repeatedly approve the same unchanged mandate for every ordinary transaction. A material widening names the change and needs the owner's fresh assent before it affects future lending. The borrower sees their own terms, not the entire institution's private correspondence. The appointment itself does not expose every member's possessions or past disputes.

Neri retains title and may stop future lending. They cannot retrospectively change an accepted loan or seize the borrower's unrelated property. If they want to donate the bow to another individual, gift or title-transfer behavior must perform that distinct authorized act; a contribution to a container is not silently a donation. Transfer of an already-loaned tool's principal is excluded from the first service until the new principal accepts the outstanding terms and the borrower can learn the change through legitimate evidence. The object does not disappear while that administrative wish is unresolved.

### 3.2 Appointing someone willing to keep the service

Sol explicitly accepts a limited job. Its meaning is permission to serve requests when willing and available, not continuous attendance or obedience. A player can be keeper using the same scope; a resident can consider lending within normal decision context. No hidden model loop polls the chest, and no script makes an appointed NPC prioritize the institution above food, sleep, safety or other concerns.

Neri separately accepts fallback service. If Sol resigns or Neri revokes Sol's mandate, the named fallback can take future keeper duties under its accepted scope. Being offline is not resignation, and an empty scene does not elect a successor. A later observed death or supported permanent retirement can make Sol unavailable; the recorded fallback authority can remain usable without pretending that an unaware character has learned the death. No actor's beliefs are updated merely because a permission is now exercisable. Neri's own unavailability leaves new checkout unavailable, with existing return still possible.

The association steward controls only DG22's record functions unless they also hold this explicit mandate. The tool principal controls this asset's future lending; the keeper controls only admitted lending acts. A founder cannot take everybody's tools by changing their own title. A newcomer can request the offered service and make an ordinary contribution without receiving withdrawal or promotion powers. This deliberately small distinction responds to the real permission failures described in [IC-R09](#ic-r09--newcomer-powers-can-be-dangerous).

### 3.3 Preparing a depot that can actually accept the return

The depot is an ordinary real container at a supported reachable place, with additional narrowly authored library permissions. At setup it must fit the actual bow, expose permitted information and permit the selected current owner/keeper operations through every relevant action route. Borrowers do not receive general access to its contents. It is not a magic remote locker or a theft-proof territorial domain inferred from its name.

Checkout leaves real compatible room reserved for the return of that exact bow. The reservation follows the outstanding loan, not Sol's active work, and is unavailable for new unrelated deposits. It accounts for actual packing and any changed supported item state; it cannot treat unknown size as zero or promise acceptance of arbitrary modifications. This is an explicit new PO consumer. If retaining usable return room is not supported, the service must refuse checkout rather than advertise an offline return that may later overflow.

The return grant is correspondingly narrow: the borrower can place the qualifying item into its reserved location and end the return obligation. They cannot inspect unrelated hidden stock, take another tool, deposit arbitrary cargo or use the depot as unlimited personal storage. The grant survives membership exit, keeper replacement and service wind-down for the accepted loan. The keeper need not be online or manually accept the same already-authorized return. Physical reach, the correct object, current execution safety and real destination still matter.

Ordinary protection is no more than the actual selected rules. A detached depot is not protected merely because a human owner logs out. The first profile must select a supported stable depot and qualify its actual access routes before making the return promise. Later destructive mechanics must explicitly say how they interact with the reserved room and claims before admission. The failure behavior in §7 is necessary even in a peaceful episode; it is not a reason to implement a general war or insurance system.

## 4. Borrow, enjoy the use and return

### 4.1 A short review with consequences the player can understand

The player encounters Sol or the legitimately known service. An authorized view shows this bow's actual identity and known supported use, ammunition requirement, available or last-known status, who may authorize it, and the named return location. Hidden inventory and another borrower's unseen position are not a stock feed. If the bow is away, “last recorded lent” is honest; “available in three minutes” is not unless a supported reservation and arrival actually justify it.

The first service has no automated waiting list, priority subscription or queue-polling notifications. Sol can hear a request, explain the known situation and consider an alternative. A promise to wait is not a second loan of the same object. For a larger service, a transparent ordinary request order can be useful, but active reservations, unattended queues and fairness qualification must be selected together before such a promise is offered. A named rank never silently overrides an accepted borrower.

The checkout review is compact: **Borrow this bow**, **return this same bow here**, **ammunition is separate**, **no fee or deposit**, and **you may return without the keeper present**. It shows an unsupported or unsafe return route before assent. Optional explanations give the individual principal, permitted use, known condition and outstanding obligation. It does not make the user read an institutional constitution for a single outing.

A guest can take this first trial under the same explicitly permitted mandate without joining an association. Membership remains a separate voluntary choice for repeated cooperation. Accepting a loan does not enroll someone, promise attendance, disclose private biography or consent to future changed terms. The owner may choose a genuinely restricted lending circle in another profile, but that restriction and its benefit must be clear; the first profile avoids an unnecessary membership ceremony.

The borrower and authorized keeper assent to the exact current revision. A changed bow, destination, amount, condition, principal or security term invalidates the old acceptance and returns to review. Existing invitation/commitment limits apply; no hidden counteroffer creates parallel claims. Loss of reach, a consumed tool reference, active work or changed authority can prevent execution even after a friendly conversation. Nothing transfers until the actual current transfer can complete.

Successful checkout moves the real bow once, records the accepted return obligation and retains its actual return room together. An uncertain response says the result is unresolved and offers recovery of that operation; retry cannot issue a second bow or create a second claim. A successful sentence from an NPC is not a receipt. A failed checkout leaves the item and title where they actually are and does not charge a fee, reserve imaginary stock or create debt for an object the player never received.

### 4.2 Permission to use is not ownership of every consequence

The borrower can carry, equip and use this compatible bow through existing supported actions. They need their own authorized ammunition and actual bodily capability; the library does not grant a manufacturing technique or force an NPC companion to help. One finite hunt outcome supplies ordinary evidence. No automatic repeat, kill guarantee, meat yield bonus, relationship reward or generated quest completion is added.

The loan's scope forbids deliberately selling, gifting, sublending, consuming as material or materially modifying the entrusted bow without a new agreement. That restriction must be enforced wherever those acts can occur, including native commands, natural-language execution, nested-container movement and newly admitted mechanics. An unqualified action family cannot silently route around it. This is an explicitly selected entrusted-item rule, not a universal theft law inferred from every declared owner in the world.

Ordinary custody changes that do not dispose of the bow remain possible where supported: unequipping, putting it in an authorized carried container and retrieving it. Dropping it is a deliberate loss-of-custody risk, with a truthful consequence before the action when the borrower can know it. The feature does not glue the bow permanently to their body. Later recovery must involve the actual object and legitimate access. A finder does not inherit the borrower's debt, and seeing a bow does not expose the original private loan; permitted distinguishing information can support returning a found object without revealing unrelated records.

Current bow use does not establish a general wear, maintenance or repair system. Do not invent a durability meter to make a library interesting. If the qualified bow has no ordinary degradation, return has no fictional cleaning charge. If a later admitted mechanic can damage it, physical return still relinquishes custody and satisfies the duty to bring back what remains; a separately accepted, evidence-backed damage claim may remain. “Pristine” is not a way to reject all returns or demand impossible reversal of normal use.

The player can abandon the outing, keep using the bow within the agreed scope, ask to buy it through a new authorized agreement, or bring it back immediately. The first loan has no countdown and no accruing debt. The principal can ask for it back through ordinary communication, and can decline further lending, but cannot convert a later preference into a retroactive due date. If repeated indefinite loans make a shared stock useless, test an explicitly negotiated short outing arrangement or a gift before adding penalties.

### 4.3 An ordinary return requires no ceremonial approval

At the named accessible depot, **Return borrowed bow** identifies the exact held item and destination. The action can approach through ordinary movement; it finishes only on actual reach and a successful single transfer. The return-only grant does not depend on current membership, Sol's presence or a new model response. The reserved space cannot be consumed by another deposit while the loan is active. A second return attempt recognizes the completed result rather than awarding another benefit.

Successful return ends the physical-return claim, releases the associated return reservation for normal service use, and leaves the real object in depot custody under its unchanged title. The keeper may later inspect and choose to lend again. Return does not automatically relend it to a waiting actor, demand conversation or establish that the keeper witnessed the event. The authorized service record can be learned on a later permitted inspection; personal memories remain observer-relative.

The borrower receives a receipt for their action and can see their own loan no longer requires return. Sol does not get telepathic knowledge, and Neri does not learn the player's route or hunting result. A revealed public service board may show stock within its authored audience, but a private loan record is not broadcast as gossip. Reputation, gratitude and disappointment arise only from actual character evidence and decisions.

## 5. Authority changes, exit and the institution's own promises

### 5.1 Revocation stops new use of authority, not accepted obligations

Neri can revoke Sol's future checkout mandate immediately through the supported owner action. A still-pending checkout rechecks authority and fails without moving goods. A checkout already completed remains an accepted loan; revocation cannot retrospectively make the borrower a thief or cancel their return route. New authority does not backdate old decisions. A successor receives only the specific current service records and asset powers needed for the mandate, not unrestricted private correspondence.

Sol may resign without waiting for every borrower. Their future authority ends, the fallback takes only its previously accepted scope, and unresolved physical custody remains real. Resignation cannot delete a borrowed bow, move personal stock into a treasury or change who owns a loss. If no willing qualified keeper remains, pause new checkout. The return-only service continues for outstanding loans while its physical destination remains available. A shutdown is better than secretly coercing the last person to staff a desk forever.

Members and guests may leave the association or stop dealing with it. Their existing loan and ability to return survive. Removing a person can stop new loans after the removal is authoritative; it cannot confiscate their carried belongings or erase a deposit. An unaware person learns removal only through permitted evidence. The underlying authorization may reject a new request generically without revealing the remover's private reasons or other members' information.

The owner can withdraw an unloaned asset once all applicable work, holds and current custody allow actual retrieval. With a loan outstanding, withdrawal stops future lending but preserves its accepted terms and reserved return route. The owner is not trapped in association membership: they can leave and retain a winding-down claim and narrowly necessary depot retrieval permission for their own item. Those residual rights confer no office over other assets. They can waive or settle their claim explicitly; ending an active commitment view does not secretly waive somebody else's claim.

### 5.2 Move a service by keeping its commitments, not its signboard

A planned depot move must provide an actually reachable compatible return place with equivalent narrow access and room before withdrawing the old promise. The first profile does not support unilateral rerouting of a loan. The borrower accepts a material destination change after learning its real consequence; until then the original return remains usable, or the service records its own inability to perform rather than blaming the borrower. Moving only the displayed location while leaving the actual container elsewhere is not migration.

If the library simply needs its packing room for another purpose, it waits for return or negotiates an alternative. It cannot fill a reserved place and then report that the borrower missed a deadline. New unrelated storage requests fail against the actual reservation, explaining the occupied obligation to authorized users. The borrower does not need to see all the goods causing a conflict. This cost of reliable offline return is visible to the operator and included in the economics below.

If a physical change unexpectedly makes the old destination unreachable, the loan becomes **return blocked** when that fact is legitimately established for the viewer. The borrower retains custody; there is no phantom deposit or failed-return penalty. They can seek an actual alternative from the principal/fallback, directly return by consensual handover to the entitled principal, or settle under new terms. A keeper cannot demand extra goods for repairing the institution's own broken return route. If nobody can currently receive the item, it stays real and the obligation stays unresolved; the UI must not claim it was returned.

### 5.3 Dissolution is an orderly end to service, not a destructive action

Closing the association ends new invitations and coordination under DG22. Closing the library first stops new checkout, cancels unexecuted proposals, exposes permitted winding-down information and retains the exact open asset/return relationships. Goods already at the depot remain individually titled and actually placed. Owners retrieve them through their scoped rights; borrowers retain return rights; held collateral, if enabled later, remains protected for its lawful disposition. Clearing an access list would make the container public and is expressly not a closure operation.

The record may be closed while physical wind-down continues. It cannot declare everything settled merely because the last officer left. The depot cannot be retired nonempty or while its outstanding return-room promises lack accepted disposition. Someone may elect to retain the site, settle and release claims, or use a supported actual relocation. None of these moves people or objects remotely. An abandoned institution can leave real stranded property and unresolved disagreements, but it cannot require perpetual paid NPC administration to preserve their truth.

The first service has no collective treasury, inheritable office, dues or profit shares. Larger pooled ownership would require a separate explicit principal/beneficiary rule, joining and exiting stakes, votes, contributor assent and dissolution distribution. Inventing those mechanisms for one bow would add more work than play. [IC-R05](#ic-r05--simpler-sharing-can-be-the-better-system) provides a useful precedent for deliberately reducing institutional machinery.

## 6. Knowledge, interface and independent characters

Use existing person, item, agreement and inventory surfaces. The object detail can identify a permitted entrusted-item state and the actor's available return action. The agreement view explains known terms and actual next actions. The service view shows only authorized stock and roles; it is not a live omniscient membership or borrower tracker. A short visible status distinguishes proposed, checked out, return blocked, physically returned and separately disputed loss. Do not compress those into a red “bad member” label.

Term changes remain readable on narrow screens and with long names. Exact identities and amounts appear beside plain consequences, with keyboard-reachable controls and an explicit final action. A stale form retains the user's intent while showing what must be reviewed. No background refresh scrolls away a selected item or silently accepts different terms. There is no need for a new full-screen guild dashboard in the first episode.

Public rules can be posted or explained where actors can legitimately encounter them. Private lending terms and individual disputes remain scoped to their participants and any expressly appointed reviewer. A new keeper learns only current necessary records through granted access. Former keepers retain legitimately learned memories but lose future privileged inspection. Losing office does not erase memory, and keeping a memory does not preserve a live access grant.

NPCs can propose, refuse, negotiate, use and return through the same native actions and current evidence as humans. They can value access, dislike a term, trust someone, want their property back or decide lending was a mistake. Their acceptance must not be generated just to keep the player's task moving. A loan does not override food, sleep, danger, absence or the character's independent goals. A service failure is not narrated as the keeper maliciously refusing; pending cognition keeps the existing dots-only treatment rather than exposing provider mechanics in character speech.

Native return, permission rechecks and committed receipts must work with no paid inference. The world need not stop while a keeper considers a new request. Normal human inactivity and protected-body behavior continue; the library does not create logout repossession or invisible work. Detached stock follows actual world rules. Reminders, if the player explicitly wants one through an existing communication route, are messages with their real audience and cost, not a polling loop or endless pressure to return to the game.

## 7. Failure, death, disappearance and recovery

| Event | Honest first-service outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Bow is taken or committed elsewhere before checkout | Current admission fails; no duplicate loan, transfer or debt. Keep an editable request and explain the known unavailable item. |
| Transfer succeeds but the response is lost | Recover the same operation's result before trying a new loan. The borrower can possess the one bow while a receipt is uncertain; do not issue a replacement. |
| Keeper disconnects, declines work or resigns | New lending waits for a willing authorized keeper. The already-granted physical return route remains usable. |
| Owner or keeper dies | Actual supported mortality controls the person and possessions. Named scoped fallback authority can continue where valid; no relative automatically inherits title or claims. If no entitled actor remains, stop new lending and retain actual custody and unresolved title pending a supported disposition. |
| Borrower becomes inactive | Current human absence protection governs the same body and carried bow. No automatic return, forced login, interest or seizure. The exposed asset remains unavailable for other lending. |
| Borrower dies or the bow is dropped | Follow actual corpse, ghost, revival and item-custody rules. Death is not a returned-object event. A recoverable actual bow can be returned through qualified access; nobody may search private possessions merely because they hold a claim. |
| Bow is damaged or destroyed by a supported mechanic | Preserve actual remains/outcome evidence. Physical recovery does not invent a pristine replacement. First unsecured lending has no automatic damages charge; voluntary replacement, title transfer, loss acknowledgment or waiver need their own honest agreement. |
| Library removes the borrower | Stop future eligibility as authorized; preserve their current return route, own evidence and any collateral disposition. Do not relabel a valid checkout as theft. |
| Depot is full of unrelated goods | Refuse the competing deposit against its real reserved return room. If corruption or an unsupported change already broke that promise, show return blocked and recover explicitly; never overflow or delete goods. |
| Depot is moved, enclosed, destroyed or made inaccessible | No automatic failed borrower. Keep the bow in its actual custody and establish a reachable alternative or direct consensual return. The institution cannot forfeit security because it removed the return path. |
| Principal wants a different bow as replacement | A similar-looking object is not this loan's return. Review a new exact settlement, including title, custody and the old claim's release. |
| No one wants to operate the service | Close new lending, preserve return/custody and expose only authorized wind-down actions. Do not appoint an unwilling keeper or create a new NPC. |

“Death” above consumes the actually adopted mortality law. The inspected branch's present collapse/ghost behavior and DG21's separately identified newer-main death/Continue work must not be silently conflated. Revival or Continue retains the proper identity and actual current holdings; it does not reset a loan, regenerate a destroyed bow or bind the debt to a newly created relative. There is no inherited player obligation from an NPC ancestry label.

Restoring a supported current-format save restores a coherent world state with the same accepted terms, actual item placement, scopes, return room and once-only outcomes. A pre-checkout local snapshot cannot be combined with the later bow as though both copies were legitimate. A returned object cannot remain simultaneously equipped by the borrower. Incompatible development saves are rejected under AGENTS rather than repaired by guessing missing ownership or obligations.

[DG25 recovery](recoverable-worlds-feature-spec.md) and [DG29 travel](participants-and-world-travel-feature-spec.md) control escaped consequences. This first same-world service grants no cross-world movement of entrusted items. A later compatible route needs the current principal's actual permission, complete object/loan/return consequences and reconciled restore policy; account pack rights alone do not suffice. External human records or observed events cannot be erased from people's experience by rolling back the fictional dispute. Real charges, grants and creator allocations remain under their real accounting owners and are never repaid through a restored library chest.
