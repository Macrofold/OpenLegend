# Renderer comparison: repertoire selection and player value

This review connects [shared presentation and renderer comparison](renderer-comparison-feature-spec.md) to the experiences OpenLegend exists to support. Source baseline: `Macrofold/OpenLegend` at `7e95b415f18cc58828eacbddc88da8a86693c3d0`, reviewed on October 31, 2026. The design adds no gameplay rules, accepted world lore or engine-performance findings.

## Scope and evidence

The repertoire directory contains **42 Markdown files: 28 canonical catalogues, ten shared guidance/navigation/reading records and four proposed worlds**. The catalogues retain 7,872 non-action entries and 385 action examples. These are catalogue counts, not implemented capabilities or an implementation queue.

All 42 files received a selection review. Across the 28 catalogues, this covered every category and entry ID/title/priority listing, the introductory patterns and selection guidance, and the full behavior/build-gap text of selected presentation consumers and contrasting examples. The 25 combination scenes and four world proposals were read as connected experiences. Supporting records were read for catalogue ownership, priorities, coverage, selection method and evidence limits; the atlas's 148 game and 29 world takeaways/cautions and the three historical progress ledgers were included.

This is not a fresh mechanical verification of all 8,257 entries, a rereading of every row's source/build text or external research dossier, a recertification of historical reading checkboxes, or a measurement of enjoyment. The file inventory below records what each source contributes to this design and its disposition. Catalogue counts are retained from the source inventory; no row was added, deleted or reranked.

## The experience this design should make better

The strongest connected comparison is **a familiar camp, an outing, and a return to the same changed place**. The player recognizes people, understands a route and danger, does something useful or creative, and sees a result worth keeping. Quiet appreciation of a beautiful place is also a valid payoff. These experiences expose rendering costs while explaining why the work matters.

OpenLegend's particular opportunity is to connect practical invention, independent people and persistent consequences. Good presentation can make those connections apparent: a newly admitted tool has an understandable form, a person remains recognizable while choosing their own activity, and a changed entrance or moved possession is visible on return. More anonymous actors, more particles or a more expensive lighting effect does not establish those qualities by itself.

The selected dimensional pixel-art direction remains in [current visual requirements](../../art-direction/decisions.md#current-visual-requirements--october-16-2026). Natural proportions, expressive recognizable people, purposeful light and coherent material/color groups should work at ordinary play scale and during movement. This review selects no photorealistic replacement, new UI theme or asset-production campaign.

The same event can serve several purposes. A changed entrance can improve defense, make a home distinctive and show another person's contribution. These are reasons to preserve a coherent presentation, not requirements for a new subsystem for each interpretation. Playback supplies repeatable graphics work; live interactions establish native effects, and player evidence establishes whether the experience is understandable and worth repeating.

## Selection decisions

No new catalogue rows or priority changes are justified by this renderer project. Existing options already supply mastery, expressive creation, genuine opposition, companionship, useful invention, beauty and lasting consequences. The selected addition is a concrete consumer: renderer acceptance and workload choice should protect those experiences.

The [existing presentation policy](../repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md#make-execution-and-presentation-earn-their-attention) remains the selection owner. The [feature specification](renderer-comparison-feature-spec.md) owns the developer journey and supported comparison. The [technical design](renderer-comparison-tech-design.md) owns shared code, adapters, lifecycle and measurement. This review introduces neither a parallel gameplay backlog nor a second implementation priority system.

Three existing proposals sharpen the comparison without expanding the required feature set. [ACR-247](../repertoires/automation-creators.md#case-direction-and-continuing-cast-handoffs) asks whether an inviting opening is usable by the actual body and tool; use that question when reviewing picking and route cues. [ACR-271](../repertoires/automation-creators.md#readiness-evidence-and-personal-continuity) preserves timing, precision, uncertainty and age when an alternate presentation is chosen; a substitute cannot grant new senses. [ACR-207](../repertoires/automation-creators.md#playful-authoring-with-observable-results) permits baking settled background spectacle while leaving meaningful interactions live; a repeatable graphics fixture must label that boundary.

### Simplicity and extension boundaries

- A common admitted appearance/shape description should support current invented objects without duplicate recipe-specific graphics code. It does not permit generated executable shaders or a universal model-generation framework.
- Stable identity and readable conduct let people and creations become familiar. Automatic emotion, allegiance or threat-colored effects cannot expose private state or replace the selected evidence contract.
- Use the same current evaluated pose for relevant color, shadow, outline, equipment and cloth work. Held-pose art direction cannot silently erase a required action cue or change collision meaning.
- Distinguish material appearance from physical properties and world-owned light from graphics illumination. An ice-looking floor, shadow, glowing ink or magical heat effect must not acquire rules from a backend convenience.
- Retain independent backend optimization. Shared meaning does not require identical draw calls, batching or resource layout, and no shared-code percentage is a product goal.
- Defer unused special worlds and elaborate effects. A second real renderer justifies narrow adaptation; it does not justify a universal graphics engine, new ECS or separate game.

## Evidence and decisions after implementation

There is no measured renderer winner, new art acceptance, live invention result or player-enjoyment finding in this review. Existing catalogue selection judgments remain distinct from current code and evidence. The comparison must report actual effective settings, equivalent work and missing coverage; human readability/art review and live gameplay qualification remain separate.

[SIM-237/239/240](../repertoires/simulation-experiments.md#creators-visitors-and-the-life-of-a-published-world) make the distinction concrete: faster construction or a prettier screenshot does not establish attachment; working subsystems do not establish a worthwhile evening; maker pleasure does not establish visitor enjoyment. [SIM-269](../repertoires/simulation-experiments.md#continuing-places-pleasures-and-return-contracts) similarly requires affected participants before an accessibility claim. These are standards for interpreting later evidence, not new participant studies required to implement the initial adapter.

The later choice of default renderer and continued dual support uses those results and actual maintenance cost. This does not block the specified initial implementation. No unanswered gameplay choice is introduced here: none of the optional catalogue examples becomes a required new mechanic by appearing in the matrix.
