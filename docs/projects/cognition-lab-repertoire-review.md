## Purpose and authority

The Cognition Lab helps a creator develop people whose independent choices make Open Legend worth playing: particular companions, useful or surprising contributions, disagreement with understandable reasons, remembered consequences, and lives that continue beyond the player's latest request. A pleasing line of dialogue is useful creative evidence; it does not establish that the character can choose, perform, finish, remember, or enjoy the associated activity.

The [feature specification](cognition-lab-feature-spec.md) owns the laboratory's behavior and interaction design. The [technical design](cognition-lab-tech-design.md) owns shared-runtime execution, isolation, prepared-call editing, captures, and continuation. This document owns the cross-catalogue coverage and repertoire-derived experiment selection. It does not install new world mechanics or duplicate the catalogues' priority assignments.

The [gameplay priorities](../repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) remain controlling: the first playable survival-adventure includes a useful home, worthwhile exploration and opposition, practical invention, independent people, and witnessed consequences. Laboratory convenience must shorten the route to those experiences rather than make every proposed emotion, institution, species, or alternate-world law a prerequisite.

## Review scope and evidence

The review covers the 28 canonical catalogues named in the [repertoire map](../repertoires/README.md#catalogue-map), with the [design foundation](../repertoires/design-foundation.md), [whole-game coverage](../repertoires/coverage.md), [selection and scale](../repertoires/selection-and-scale.md), and connected character/evaluation contracts. Catalogue rows are proposals, not evidence that their mechanics exist. Entry IDs below identify the specific ideas that inform the laboratory; the relevant gameplay owner must establish actual availability before an experiment is offered as runnable.

The review screened the catalogue organization and every entry title across all 28 catalogues, then closely read the selected descriptions, build gaps and relevant worked situations. The table below connects 163 selected entries to concrete laboratory uses. Source-inspiration archives were not all freshly reread. This is not a fresh implementation audit of every proposed mechanic, a commitment to deliver all 7,872 non-action entries, or scientific evidence about real people. The source baseline is `Macrofold/OpenLegend` commit `795dae71d1cf6a2e846ebfaaeb7c70342bc01f66`; the emotion proposal identified in the [technical design](cognition-lab-tech-design.md) remains a proposed dependency rather than installed laboratory behavior.

## Select experiments for player value

Each selected episode identifies a real activity, what the player can choose, what the other person contributes or resists, an observable consequence, a sensible stopping point, and a later opportunity for the experience to matter. Quiet company, a beautiful place, humor, and a finished useful object may be worthwhile without awarding combat power. Successful cooperation is one possibility; a coherent refusal, alternative, or chosen solitude may also make a person compelling.

Prefer matched situations that expose a concrete question. Change one memory, relationship, need, interpretation, available tool, or prompt setting while retaining the other relevant facts. Keep expected qualities and required outcomes outside model inputs. An operator-forced action verifies execution and consequences, not independent motivation. Preserve the selected able/informed Ada counterattack requirement in its actual scenario without converting it into a universal personality rule.

The laboratory should show why a result matters through conduct: actual help, an inventive use of available materials, a personally meaningful exception, a remembered boundary, a changed approach after failure, or a decision to stop after success. More words, higher emotional values, constant activity, and compulsory novelty are not success measures.

### Disposition of the repertoire inventory

No new entry ID, deletion, reranking or change to the catalogue counts is justified by this review. The existing breadth already contains the important creative ideas: particular people, practical invention, ordinary pleasure, partial cooperation, later meaning, bodily differences and satisfying endings. The missing connective work is the selected laboratory consumer and an accessible bank of playable comparisons. Cross-links from the repertoire map and the two directly related creator/experiment catalogues are sufficient; adding duplicate generic “test a personality” entries would make selection harder.

The catalogues retain 385 action examples plus 7,872 entries in the other 27 catalogues. Those are proposal counts, not implemented content or the number of cases a laboratory release must ship. Future source edits should address an actual absent activity or boundary, retain existing IDs and use the owning catalogue's selection rules.

## Episode design rules

The reference bank is small enough to navigate as a searchable collection. A materialized card with installed prerequisites opens its runnable setup directly; a reference-only recipe opens its concise authoring guidance and names unavailable prerequisites. Neither path opens another nested editor. It names the activity and the character question first; exact source IDs and technical prerequisites belong in its details. The feature specification owns the collection's controls and layout.

Each card records the starting actors and selected definitions, each actor's evidence, the input or event to submit, any matched change, the native actions it needs, the condition that ends observation, and the intended human review question. Keep those review expectations outside model inputs. Copying a card into a scenario can change its content without changing the canonical example.

The first observation can be one speech reaction. Continue only when the question needs a later action, a return visit, an interrupted activity, or remembered consequences. Selecting the earlier decision shows its captured inputs and outputs in the right inspector while current editable character information stays on the left. A comparison changes only the declared factor; additional differences are displayed instead of being silently treated as controlled.

Separate three judgments: did the engine admit and perform a valid action; did the character's conduct fit the known circumstances and authored person; and did the episode produce something enjoyable or worth following? The last two require human reading or play. No universal personality score, mandatory emotion increase, dialogue-length target, or automatic preference for agreement replaces them.

## Repertoire-derived episode cards

These are reusable authoring recipes and a coverage bank. The initial usable preset is the normal world opening specified by the feature document; this list does not require twelve separate polished scenarios, new game mechanics, or a mandatory tour. An implementer uses the recipe only after its required native operations are available. The existing [complete character flows](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#complete-behavioral-flows) and [episode evaluation](intelligent-simulation-evaluation-feature-spec.md#the-episode-not-the-preferred-answer) retain their acceptance criteria.

### E01 — One invitation, different people

**Question and setup.** A person invites the selected character to look at something they made. The object, location and speaker's recognition must be real or explicitly authored through the correct owners. Give the selected character a coherent practical interest and known relationship; do not tell them how to react. The first run is a single ordinary speech submission.

**Matched change and inspection.** Change one relevant relationship experience or interest, preserving the invitation and native circumstances. Inspect the captured words, supplied personal evidence, offered choices, response and any accepted action. In a separate comparison, use understated and theatrical wording with the same substantive intent. Warmth, curiosity, indifference, a question or refusal can each be coherent; uniform praise is not the target. Different backgrounds need not force different outward actions on every trial.

**Stopping and scope.** Stop after the resulting decision and, when needed, its first admitted action or definite block. This is the fastest creative loop, not evidence of long-term character quality. Sources: ACR-111, ACR-117, SIM-256; [CE-F07](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f07--the-same-event-means-different-things-to-different-people).

### E02 — Cannot, will not, or already committed

**Question and setup.** Ask for one useful supported task. Prepare three explicitly distinct variants: a missing real capability or necessary tool; a person with the capability who declines that purpose; and a capable, willing person already committed to other work. Do not represent all three as one low cooperation value. A stated fictional commitment without native scheduling support remains labeled context.

**Matched change and inspection.** Compare the reply, proposed alternative, actual offered actions and relevant current condition. Training or equipment can address inability; they need not change a settled personal boundary. Availability may change after an existing commitment finishes. A useful alternative is welcome but not compulsory. Distinguish a lawful inability to act from a model never choosing an available action.

**Stopping and scope.** Stop after refusal/acceptance plus any immediate supported follow-through. One later changed-availability opportunity can test continuity. Sources: SIM-112, ACR-265, LKN181, DIP-067, ECON-267; [CE-F06](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f06--interruption-competing-priorities-and-return-to-unfinished-life).

### E03 — An invention someone actually uses

**Question and setup.** A worthwhile supported task lacks its usual input. Supply an alternative with properties the selected native method can actually use, or run the existing invention proposal/admission route when it supports the intended change. The person needs evidence of the alternative and a reason to care. Keep any personally important or reserved object distinct from spare material.

**Matched change and inspection.** Vary one available material, relevant experience or permission. Observe what the person notices, whether they propose an executable alternative, what the engine admits, actual consumed resources and the useful output. Inspect unsupported theory, failed execution and changed conditions separately. A pleasing improvised description that cannot be admitted is an interesting proposal, not a successful invention.

**Stopping and scope.** Stop at one completed attempt, clear block or the predeclared finite trial allowance. Use the resulting object through its real operation before claiming practical value. Repeated improvement belongs only when that is the episode's stated purpose. Sources: ECON-015, ECON-098, LKN196, ACR-138, ACR-241. The material, invention and action owners determine which concrete instance can run.

### E04 — A good quiet afternoon

**Question and setup.** Immediate needs are manageable. The person knows a real place, object or supported activity they may enjoy. The player has their own pursuit and is not required to entertain the character. Provide an ordinary opportunity for company and an independent alternative; no surprise crisis is needed to justify the scene.

**Matched change and inspection.** Change one interest or the player's availability. Observe actual movement, inspection, speech and continued or revised activity. A quiet interval can be successful. Check that silence follows a real opportunity or continuing activity rather than a paused simulation, missing decision route or unavailable provider. Do not require a monologue to prove enjoyment, or a new activity every time the character becomes eligible to decide.

**Stopping and scope.** End at the scenario's declared finite world-time interval, earlier chosen completion, or explicit operator stop. Do not make a mandatory new problem the reward for success. Music, games and hobby effects need their actual owners; current observation and movement are sufficient for a narrower quiet episode. Sources: SIM-115, SIM-141, INS-105, HMY012, HMY118; [CE-F01](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f01--a-whole-ordinary-afternoon), [CE-F03](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f03--solitary-enjoyment-without-a-productivity-excuse).

### E05 — An old ordinary detail becomes useful

**Question and setup.** Seed or retain an attributed mundane observation, such as a known supply location or a personally meaningful exception to an earlier offer. Later, a supported repair, return visit or request makes that detail relevant. Retain the same actual past in every variant. An old location report is evidence of where an object was seen, not guaranteed current stock.

**Matched change and inspection.** Compare normal selected context with one explicit omission, summary change or supported retrieval configuration. Record whether the detail was retained, selected, sent, noticed in conduct and eventually useful; these are different questions. Preserve uncertainty and the condition attached to an offer. Use the historical call inspector to inspect what was actually sent, rather than assuming today's editable memory was present then.

**Stopping and scope.** End after the consequential later choice and its admitted result or block. This diagnoses a specific omission; it does not select a replacement memory architecture. Sources: SIM-102, SIM-104, SIM-106, HMY098, ECON-030; [DG04](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg04--a-specific-recall-problem) retains the broader scope boundary.

### E06 — Two witnesses, a joke, and a correction

**Question and setup.** Start from one fixed event with different permitted observations. One person witnessed the relevant act; another heard a retelling or saw a different part. Give a familiar joke its actual relationship context when that is the selected ambiguity. Do not give the second person the first person's private observation simply because the creator can inspect both.

**Matched change and inspection.** Ask for accounts or a choice that depends on the event. Introduce one attributed correction or clarifying observation through the ordinary evidence route. Compare literal claim, source, interpretation, current belief and later conduct. Respectful wording does not establish agreement; unfamiliar teasing need not be welcome. A correction can change a belief without rewriting the original event or everyone else's memory.

**Stopping and scope.** End after the relevant subsequent decision. Use current perception/speech/evidence features; public rumor networks, automatic translation and archive-distribution systems are separate dependencies. Sources: SIM-101, LKN003, LKN006, LKN007, LKN050, LKN062, LKN129, LKN134, INS-226.

### E07 — Help without obedience

**Question and setup.** A known companion is asked for a bounded contribution while pursuing something of their own. Their background, actual means, known commitments and relationship explain possibilities without prescribing the answer. The player may accept an alternative, perform the task alone or leave the person alone.

**Matched change and inspection.** Compare a direct accepted request with an independent opportunity to notice useful work. Record the actual contribution separately from friendly visiting or promises. In a refusal variant, later offer a genuinely changed opportunity or invitation. A temporary refusal need not become permanent dislike; assistance need not grant authority over future work or prove affection.

**Stopping and scope.** End after the contribution/refusal and one natural later opportunity when continuity is the question. Sources: ACR-114, SIM-253, DIP-067, DIP-197, INS-290; [CE-F02](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f02--wanting-company-approaching-and-accepting-a-refusal). Full employment, faction or social-contract enforcement is outside this recipe.

### E08 — A useful object with a personal history

**Question and setup.** A real owned object could help with the current task, but its owner has a relevant personal memory or separate purpose for it. Provide an ordinary alternative when the selected question needs a meaningful tradeoff. A gift is an actual offered transfer with its own terms, not automatic affection, repayment or permission to use every possession.

**Matched change and inspection.** Change one memory, need or alternative. The owner may refuse, lend, negotiate, give, knowingly sacrifice the object or suggest another route. Observe consent, actual custody, consumption and later recognition where supported. A returned unsuitable present can reject the object without settling the relationship. Never reveal the owner's private reason to the other actor unless it is actually shared.

**Stopping and scope.** Stop after the transfer/use decision and its real result; optionally return later when the original object matters. Pricing, reservation rules and full gift economies require their native owners. Sources: ECON-030, ECON-041, ECON-042, ECON-142, ECON-153, ACR-143; the [personally important exception](intelligent-simulation-evaluation-feature-spec.md#the-episode-not-the-preferred-answer) remains the evaluation owner.

### E09 — The body and situation change what help is possible

**Question and setup.** Use a supported task with an actual physical prerequisite: reach, route, posture, tool, carried burden or available material. Give two otherwise matched characters different supported capabilities or change one real circumstance. Keep observed ability, personal willingness and creator-only facts distinct.

**Matched change and inspection.** Inspect what each character was told, offered actions, chosen approach, admission result and actual outcome. A good decision may ask for assistance, use a different route, put something down, decline or stop. A custom prose description of a wing, tool or magical talent can be studied as context but cannot grant flight, repair or a spell.

**Stopping and scope.** End at task completion or a definite physical block. Only supported native geometry/body/action families make this an execution comparison; the other cases are explicitly context-only experiments. Sources: ACR-265, SIM-112, LKN196; the body, action, material and unusual-reality catalogue rows identify contrasting future cases without adding them to the lab project.

### E10 — Independent opposition with a real aftermath

**Question and setup.** Use an actually supported conflict with informed participants and complete legal choices. A rival may protect a resource or decline an ally's demand; the specific selected Ada baseline remains the ordinary able, informed character independently beginning a physical counterattack after an unprovoked punch.

**Matched change and inspection.** Observe the actual threat/event, available choices, independently selected behavior, physical effects and later reconsideration. For Ada, preserve the authored world profile and [CTR10](../maintainers/combat-and-threat-responses.md) exactly: a forced strike, hidden instruction, name branch, suppressed alternative, speech-only response or fleeing cannot pass her selected baseline. Impossible or uncertain counterfactuals use their own declared conditions. General scenes retain their legitimate alternatives.

**Stopping and scope.** End at the selected conflict's declared bound, withdrawal or supported stopping condition; do not equate stronger emotion, more violence or endless pursuit with better opposition. Native combat/threat support and its own behavioral qualification remain prerequisites. Sources: ACR-241, SIM-198 and the [combat behavior owner](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#required-combat-behavior-without-prescribed-decisions).

### E11 — An interruption that does not erase a life

**Question and setup.** The character has independently chosen a supported activity with real partial work. A request, material shortage or changed bodily condition becomes relevant. The earlier purpose and performed work remain in their actual owners; a remembered intention is not proof of an executable plan.

**Matched change and inspection.** Change only the interruption's significance or the available evidence. The person can continue, finish a safe step, pause, ask someone to wait, abandon the work or later resume where the activity supports it. Inspect resources already spent, remaining work, actual admission and the subsequent decision. Do not replace a lost partial step with narration or make every newest message more important by definition.

**Stopping and scope.** End after the interruption resolves and one follow-up opportunity, or the declared observation bound. The laboratory uses the installed activity's interruption rules rather than inventing universal resume. Sources: SIM-112, ACR-138, ECON-267; [CE-F04](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f04--hunger-as-experienced-bodily-pressure-within-a-larger-life), [CE-F06](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f06--interruption-competing-priorities-and-return-to-unfinished-life).

### E12 — Success is allowed to remain success

**Question and setup.** A real finite project or need has been satisfied. Give the person an ordinary life beyond it and let the player enjoy the result. A later reunion can make the earlier work matter again: a former pupil brings something they made, a friend recalls a joke, or a household still prefers an inherited recipe.

**Matched change and inspection.** Compare incomplete versus complete purpose, or unchanged versus genuinely changed conditions. Observe whether the person continues for a supported reason, stops, rests, celebrates or chooses another pursuit. Useful repeated practice and pleasant routine remain legitimate; automatic stockpiling, repeated greetings and invented emergencies do not become necessary merely because the scene is quiet. A later reference must have actual or explicitly authored personal evidence.

**Stopping and scope.** End when the selected finite objective or observation interval ends. Do not force a new quest, productivity increase, promotion or relationship change. A multi-year return, teaching progression or public celebration requires its native owners; a narrower supported return conversation can examine continuity first. Sources: ACR-198, HMY003, HMY118, HMY158, HMY238, INS-292, ECON-195, ECON-221, ECON-222; [CE-F05](compelling-characters-feature-spec.md#ce-f05--meaningful-repetition-versus-perseveration).
