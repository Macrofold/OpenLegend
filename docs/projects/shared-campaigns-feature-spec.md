## 1. The Ash Road: something worth doing before the battle

Nessa wants to carry her supplies between Reedbank and Stonebend without taking the long path around an abandoned workshop. Orrin likes that longer path and its lookout, but wants his neighbours to have a practical choice. Neither needs a council, a campaign rank or a promise to attend tomorrow. The old road passes through two real gates, initially closed but operable. A dormant machine, the Cinder Warden, can later close them through the authority and power supplied by two relay braziers. It is an adversary with one body and a finite purpose: keep this particular road closed.

A player visiting Reedbank can follow the visible connection from its gate to the western relay. The short approach reaches the warded relay front; the longer walk around the ruined wall reveals an accessible rear disconnect. Opening that connection prevents the Warden from closing this gate later. It does not dissolve or open the gate. The player can open the actual gate through its supported interaction, either before or after disconnecting the relay. They can then carry their own supplies through it, tell Nessa what happened, or leave. The changed route is the reward that matters first.

That evening another player visits Stonebend. The eastern relay is different: its disconnect is behind a shutter operated by a winding socket. There is a compatible removable crank in the nearby store, reached by an ordinary clear path; a legitimately acquired compatible rigid tool can also work. The player can retrieve the actual crank, use a tool they already know, ask a willing person for help, or try an admitted invention. Nothing requires the western player to return or surrender a unique quest key. Opening the shutter and disconnecting the eastern relay can prevent the Warden's operation altogether.

The two undertakings are genuinely linked. With both relays connected, the Warden's shutdown access is protected by their shared ward. Severing either removes that protection from the one actual body, making a direct shutdown possible at the workshop. It also removes the Warden's authority and power to close that relay's gate. A player at Stonebend therefore has a new option because of work done at Reedbank, even if the two never meet. Severing both leaves no powered route and prevents activation. There is no replacement boss conjured because the planned confrontation became unnecessary.

If a relay remains connected during an announced operating opportunity, the Warden can make one physical attempt to close the corresponding road gate. People can intervene at a relay, approach its exposed shutdown control, or let the attempt finish and reopen the route afterward. The first profile threatens access through the two campaign fixtures, not people's lives or homes. Its conclusion can be prevention, shutdown during movement, a successful obstruction followed by recovery, or an honestly unresolved dormant machine. There is one history, not a separate victory for every party.

This is a proposed authored campaign, not a claim that the current game already supports magical relay networks, winding fixtures or a campaign controller. The first complete delivery is a useful outing whose result helps another place. The forecast and shared history should make that outing more meaningful. If they mainly add menus, waiting or obligations, the smaller local story is the better product.

## 2. Scope, source authority and what this design changes

The accepted [shared-campaign direction](../product-scalability/shared-campaigns.md) calls for finite distributed sources, local causal participation, approximate forecasts, one canonical outcome and meaningful activity before and after a peak. [PS07 and PS08](../maintainers/product-scalability.md#ps07--federation-and-forecast-canonical-campaigns) retain implementation and qualification ownership. PS-D05 leaves the practical notice, revision, participation and recognition choices for this design to propose. Nothing in the inspected sources requires a new user decision before this voluntary nonlethal proposal can be written.

The inspected task baseline is `fffdafcf3f7b92d63e1a43e551627cde67708dea`. Current movement, object custody, perception, native work, individual agency, time, multiplayer control and current-format persistence remain their actual contracts. The campaign-specific fixtures, construct behaviour, published forecast and contribution record are proposed consumers, not delivered capabilities inferred from a fantasy description. The separately authored [Ash Road profile](../worlds/base/shared-campaigns.md) owns this world's content and selected values; the engine must not make relay braziers or the Warden universal laws.

The first scope contains two small existing communities in one world, two exact relay sites, two exact road gates and one exact construct. Community means particular people and a place they use, not a minimum population, a municipal simulation or membership authority. Nessa and Orrin are adult residents with distinct motives; they may decline participation, prefer another project, misunderstand a report or enjoy the reopened road without joining an association. Their existence is not a standing paid obligation to monitor the event.

The first Warden is explicitly a nonliving machine. While dormant it has no physiology, personal thoughts, unattended projects or paid cognition. This is a deliberately authored family, not permission to freeze an ordinary NPC while a real-world notice period passes. It has no telepathy, copied minds, possession or requirement on DG33. Its active behaviour follows a small published physical purpose. A later intelligent villain would need actual senses, reports, agency, resources and qualified decision costs rather than inheriting this inexpensive machine model.

Campaign participation grants no new PvP, human injury, absent-body attacks, death, logout retention or property-damage authority. The first machine neither attacks people nor damages unrelated possessions. It can operate only its two declared gates under the selected fixture rules. People remain physical obstacles; it cannot push through a body, injure a blocker or teleport past one. Ordinary danger already present in a world retains its own rules. A later lethal campaign depends on the relevant harm and recovery owners and cannot be smuggled into this profile as a difficulty adjustment.

The campaign does not require unattended communities, shared scenes, a marketplace, governments, generations, a special-minds framework or travel between worlds. A small local success can be delivered before the moving Warden is qualified. Cross-world campaigns remain a complete later expansion in [§12](#12-expansion-that-preserves-the-campaigns-meaning), consuming DG29 and DG25 rather than replacing them.

## 3. Two local undertakings with physical results

### 3.1 Reedbank: discover a better approach

The western site teaches the connection through observable consequences. The gate, its winding point and the relay are visible from useful ordinary positions. The relay front is not a false interactable that simply demands a quest flag: inspection identifies its warded housing and, from the appropriate side, its actual connection around the ruined wall. Walking around is an ordinary supported route. No climbing, jumping, crouching, sound-sensitive enemy or hidden pixel target is assumed.

At the rear, a person in reach can deliberately disconnect the relay with a compatible carried rigid tool. The tool is retained. The proposed first work takes 120 game seconds, two normal real seconds at the current 60:1 rate; it is a short committed action, not the content of the outing. It can be cancelled before completion with no disconnection or salvage. Current reach, tool custody and permission still have to hold at completion. Two people starting against the same connection cannot each extract its parts or earn two source removals.

Successful disconnection removes that relay's powered connection once. The exact coupling is left as a real accessible object at the fixture, not automatically deposited in the actor's inventory. Its ownership and access are authored openly before the work. The first profile allows the completing actor to take the released coupling, while leaving it in place is also valid. Other people's property is not confiscated to create this reward. No metal, food or money is generated by an accomplishment counter.

The physical gate remains in its actual position until someone operates it. Its ordinary winding point works whether or not the relay is connected: a player can open the route immediately, accepting that the still-powered Warden may close it later. The selected gate interaction opens the actual movable gate into its authored clearance, taking 120 game seconds under unchanged conditions. It refuses a blocked sweep and gives an actionable obstruction explanation based on permitted evidence. It cannot clip through a person or silently move their bag. The new fixture family must qualify collision and custody before being sold as a usable road. Where no valid opening can be performed, the relay is still disconnected, but the road is truthfully still obstructed.

The distinction matters to play. A player can stop after severing the power because that already helps the other front; another can later move an abandoned object out of the gate's path, open it and make the first delivery. Both have specific contributions. Repeatedly opening and closing the same gate does not create additional liberation credit or manufacture salvage.

Nessa's desire to use the road is a reason to care, not an automatic task assignment. She can describe what she actually knows, offer her own lawful supplies if she chooses, or travel later for her own reason. Her reaction must follow acquired evidence. A campaign service recording the disconnect does not insert a personal memory into her mind. A provider outage is not Nessa becoming ungrateful.

### 3.2 Stonebend: choose a tool and keep a useful object

The eastern relay presents a small practical problem rather than repeating the western route. Its access shutter has a visible winding socket. The store's crank is an actual finite tool, with its location discoverable from inspection or a legitimate resident report. The route to it is unobstructed ordinary walking. Retrieving it is a useful option for someone arriving without tools; a player already carrying a compatible tool can avoid that detour.

The crank fits by supported capability and physical compatibility, not a character-name or quest-name check. An invention may propose another compatible tool through normal native admission, with its actual materials, rights and cost. A clever description alone cannot declare the shutter open. The first campaign must remain completable with the authored crank if generation is unavailable, and using that crank must not force an unnecessary invention purchase.

Winding the shutter uses the exact tool without consuming it, takes the same proposed 120 game seconds, and exposes the actual disconnect. Disconnecting then takes 120 game seconds. The brief work times allow interruptions and cooperation to mean something without turning repeated holding into a skill test. The shutter is a physical fixture with qualified clearance, not a disappearing wall. Its raised state persists under the declared mechanism; it does not require a second player to stand indefinitely on a pressure plate.

Orrin would like the crank returned to the store because he uses it for the gate. A loan or gift is an ordinary actual agreement where that optional system is available; the campaign does not silently enroll a borrower into DG32 or create a debt from proximity. The authored free-use crank can instead be used at the fixture and left at its agreed store. If someone takes it elsewhere, a compatible alternative remains possible. The campaign does not manufacture another crank or reveal its current private possessor.

This is not permission for unrestricted destruction as an alternative to understanding the mechanism. Breaking the shutter, burning the relay or shooting a pin is available only if the relevant physical/material/action family actually supports the proposed result and the actor has permission. An unsupported approach remains an honest invention or capability question. Do not put a decorative alternate action on the menu and have every method secretly call the same success script.

The east gate can likewise be opened physically before or after disconnecting its relay; disconnection prevents the Warden from closing it again. The intact shutter, gate and removed parts remain in the world. Later use, damage, loss and ownership changes follow their actual families; a campaign archive is not a perpetual protection spell. The first short road is useful without requiring a hunger buff, travel speed reward or new carrying-capacity law.

### 3.3 What each source actually changes

| Actual condition | Shared effect | Local physical result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both relays connected | The dormant Warden's shutdown access is warded; it has two possible powered routes, not two bodies. | Either gate can be opened by actual work, but remains eligible for a later Warden closure. |
| Exactly one connected | Shutdown access becomes operable; only an actually open still-connected gate is a valid destination for the one proposed operation. | The severed gate can be opened by actual work and cannot be reclosed by this Warden; its route is not opened by narration. |
| Neither connected before launch | Activation is prevented; the unique Warden remains inert and can be permanently disabled through ordinary shutdown work. | Both routes are secured against this machine; actual gate opening and carrying can still be worthwhile. |
| Last connection removed during the operation | The Warden loses powered work and stops at its actual supported position. | Completed movement and gate changes remain; unfinished closing does not complete itself. |
| Warden permanently shut down | It cannot launch or operate either gate again. | Remaining fixtures do not vanish; disconnected parts are not replenished. |

A disconnected relay cannot be rearmed in the first profile. This is a finite one-way dismantling of an obsolete mechanism, openly stated before commitment. It is not a universal rule that players may never repair machines. A future repair or adversarial restoration would need real components, permission, timing, outcome revision and a useful reason; it is not introduced merely to preserve repeated battles.

The profile proposes one coupling from each relay and one crank total. These are exact objects, not a promised number of generic units of valuable metal. Their actual definitions and uses must be installed and qualified. If a recovered coupling has no useful supported purpose, describe it honestly as a keepsake or replace it with a demonstrated useful authored part before advertising salvage value. The road and changed choices already provide the primary benefit.

## 4. One operation, genuine prevention and an ending

### 4.1 What the Warden can actually do

The first Warden has one stored operation charge. It does not replenish from time, donations, player attention or a reset. The two relays are necessary connections, not inexhaustible material sources. Launch commits that charge to one attempt. Cancellation after actual launch does not refund it; refusal before launch spends nothing. A crash with an uncertain launch must recover the actual result before admitting another request.

The operation's purpose is to close one currently open, connected campaign gate. The Warden receives only the declared fixture signals: whether its two relays remain connected and their associated gates' operational positions. This narrowly authored link is not a general sense of people, supplies, private plans or hidden activity. It follows an actual supported route from its workshop, carrying no conjured army. If both eligible gates are open, the first profile uses the shorter currently supported route; an exact tie uses the fixed authored western preference. The published conditional forecast explains this rule once that information is legitimately available.

The initial qualification proposes a movement speed of 0.011 world units per game second, one tenth of the ordinary 0.11 reference, and an operation budget of 3,600 game seconds including movement and fixture work. These are provisional content choices: at normal speed the machine has at most one real minute of active work. The actual route must fit that budget with useful visible approach, and the numbers must change if the result is a tedious procession or an unreadable rush. A faster world clock shortens the real interval; qualification at normal speed does not establish a fair 8× encounter.

The machine cannot cross unsupported terrain, pass through bodies, shove people, open private containers or reroute through a protected home. If a route is blocked it uses the already supported bounded navigation behaviour for this family. It does not acquire a general infinite path search. The active budget continues only with actual world progression. Exhausting that budget, losing all relevant power or failing its finite operation ends the attempt at the actual supported position. A service stall is not in-fiction time spent walking.

At the destination, closing the gate takes the proposed 120 game seconds. The sweep must be clear at completion. A person or object blocking it prevents closure; no crushing damage is invented. People can deliberately obstruct a harmless machine, and this counts as a legitimate intervention if it actually prevents the attempt. That is a consequence of the selected nonlethal world law, not an exploit to repair secretly with damage. If this makes the outing uninteresting, improve the local work or omit the procession before considering a separately approved dangerous profile.

The operation ends after one successful closure or the active budget expires. The Warden becomes permanently spent. It does not inspect the other gate and begin an unadvertised second attempt. Its body remains as a physical object under supported obstruction/removal rules. It cannot freeze in a doorway that the declared recovery route can never clear: the authored layout must give a walkable bypass and an actual supported way to shut down and move or dismantle the spent fixture. If those conditions are absent, the profile is not ready to launch.

A gate closed by the Warden can subsequently be reopened through the same real winding interaction. There is no invented seven-day repair tax. The setback is a temporarily blocked useful route and the story of how people responded. It is not described as a massacre, a destroyed town or lives saved. Bigger stakes require bigger actual laws and appropriate consent.

### 4.2 Shutdown and simultaneous intervention

Once at least one relay is disconnected, a person who can actually reach the Warden's exposed control can attempt permanent shutdown. The proposed work takes 120 game seconds, with ordinary cancellation and current reach checks. It need not require combat or a perfect-timing minigame. Starting work does not freeze the moving machine; if it leaves reach, that attempt stops. A person can act at the dormant workshop, intercept a reachable portion of the route, block a safe passage, or finish the shutdown after the operation ends.

If both relays remain connected, the control is visibly warded to someone who can inspect it. No universal secret code, repeated attack counter or paid model guess bypasses that condition. A new supported invention can alter the situation only through its admitted actual effects; it cannot declare the campaign complete in prose. Conversely, the campaign must accept a lawful effective invention rather than require a special verb solely to preserve the intended sequence.

All work resolves against the actual current world. If a relay disconnects just before gate closure, the work cannot complete with power it no longer has. If the gate closed first, disconnecting later does not retroactively open it. If two actors complete shutdown and disconnection together, there is one machine outcome and each actual distinct action can be recognized. The campaign record never pays twice for the same physical success or treats a cancelled attempt as a successful contributor.

The first permanent shutdown is the canonical ending of that adversary. Its dormant, spent or broken object can remain in the world; later removal is not another defeat. A ceremonial retelling may be proposed later as explicitly noncanonical content. New players do not get a restored Cinder Warden merely because they did not see the original operation.

### 4.3 Meaningful outcomes without mandatory victory

| Outcome | What remains true | What someone can usefully do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both sources removed before an operation | Preparation prevented the attempted closure; no operation charge was spent and no battle happened. | Open remaining gates, disable the inert core, carry supplies, discuss the actual prevention. |
| One source removed and core disabled | One community created a direct solution for the other; there was one shutdown. | Finish the second local mechanism if its parts or accessible road are wanted. |
| Operation stopped in transit | Its actual movement and spent charge remain; the target gate has its real position. | Clear a supported obstruction, finish shutdown and use the route. |
| Warden closes a gate | Its limited operation succeeded; that local setback is real. | Reopen the gate, remove any remaining source and improve the route's ordinary use. |
| No valid destination or no ready service | No fictitious attack occurred and no victory is fabricated. | Continue immediate local work; read the changed service offer if interested. |
| Campaign service ends unresolved | The operator's future event promise has ended; actual fixtures, goods and accepted history remain. | Pursue ordinary supported local shutdown/recovery while the world remains funded and accessible. |

None of these outcomes creates an unlimited supplier, a quest wage or a permanent obligation on Nessa or Orrin. Successful local work stays successful even if the overall campaign service later fails. The shared layer must make that relation easier to understand, not require the player to wait for a global verdict before their opened gate becomes real.

## 5. Forecasts that can change without lying

### 5.1 Two kinds of time, displayed separately

The current world advances 60 game seconds per normal real second. Twenty-four real hours would be sixty game days if continuously running; a two-real-hour service window would be five game days. Neither interval can be imposed as cost-free waiting on ordinary living residents. The machine is dormant by its explicit law, while residents eat, sleep, act or remain absent under the actual world policy. Empty or manually paused worlds do not produce offline preparation, hunger, marches or an imagined invasion.

An announced service opportunity is a promise about when an operator is prepared to admit the optional campaign operation. A physical forecast is a conditional estimate of what an actual body can do after that operation starts. Keep them visibly different:

> **Operation may begin:** Saturday, 18:00–20:00 in your displayed time zone. It cannot start earlier. **Physical arrival:** no march has started; after launch, the current route is estimated at about forty seconds at normal world speed, if the route and power remain unchanged.

The exact arrival estimate must come from the actual qualified route and current speed. The example is illustrative, not a guaranteed tuning result. A world date may appear alongside it, clearly labeled as conditional on continued progression. Do not convert an operator's wall-clock appointment into an exact fictional arrival that must be forced through a paused world.

The operator proposes at least 24 real hours between publishing the first advertised activation offer and its not-before time. Preparation, gate opening, disconnection, shutdown and complete early prevention are available immediately; no countdown locks them. This notice is for people who want an optional shared moment. A private local playtest can omit the advertised event service entirely and still exercise the complete local story.

The first offered window lasts two real hours. It is an interval in which one operation may begin, not a two-hour boss battle or guaranteed final-minute opportunity. The public card says this plainly. Launch requires a fresh authorized operator action, current world/service readiness, a valid powered open gate, and at least one consenting present participant at the disclosed staging opportunity. No person is teleported, enrolled into speech, marched or charged merely because the window opens.

Once the operation begins, it follows actual game time. Leaving the service window does not delete a body or undo movement. Manual pause, loss of all controlling presence under current policy or a technical stop pauses supported mechanics; the public forecast says the operation is interrupted and its arrival is unknown. Resume reconciles the current attempt, current authority and remaining work rather than launching another. Ordinary local play remains available whenever its normal world service is available, including outside campaign windows.

### 5.2 Publication, confidence and revision

The first forecast has four readable conditions, without an invented numerical confidence score:

- **Proposed:** an operator is considering an opportunity; it is not yet a reserved service promise.
- **Announced:** the not-before time and service window are committed, with the actual conditions and known uncertainty shown.
- **In progress:** an actual operation has launched; permitted route and arrival information reflect current evidence.
- **Changed or ended:** delayed, prevented, interrupted, completed, cancelled or unresolved, with the last truthful reason the viewer may know.

The first operator bulletin deliberately publishes the two campaign fixtures' connection and gate states, the Warden's broad operational state, service dates and publicly authorized outcomes. The authored profile makes these campaign facts public; it does not make private inventories, identities, scouting routes or conversations public. A future hidden campaign must choose a narrower publication policy rather than inherit this openness accidentally.

One authorized world operator owns the service offer and its revisions through current administration. Native physical outcomes remain authoritative for what actually happened. A narrative director or generated summary cannot override a disconnected source, restore the charge or change the chosen target. An ordinary resident's rumour is testimony, not the operator's canonical schedule. The interface preserves this distinction even when the rumour sounds more confident.

Any material change updates the canonical permitted bulletin as part of resolving the actual change, before another campaign request relies on superseded terms. Optional generated prose may follow; a model is not required to change “two connections” to “one.” Revisions retain their earlier public statement and explain the change: a source was severed, a route became blocked, service is unavailable, the operation was prevented, or the current estimate is no longer supported. Sensitive causes use an appropriately general explanation.

Prevention immediately withdraws the advertised threat. Do not continue advertising a battle to protect attendance metrics. A player who arranged to come can see that earlier work succeeded, learn what remains possible and decide whether to visit. The operator may host an optional gathering if it is actually supported and wanted, but it is a separate offer, not a manufactured replacement emergency.

A change must never move the operation earlier than an already announced not-before commitment. An operator-caused new risk, destination or material participation burden requires a new review and at least 24 real hours' notice before the changed optional operation. A genuine physical event after launch can make an arrival earlier or later than its estimate; describe that as changed physical prediction, not a new permission to attack before the operation's original admission.

### 5.3 Missed windows, revision limits and notifications

If the announced window closes without launch, the offer expires. The Warden stays in its actual dormant state. No late automatic attack fires when somebody next logs in. The operator may publish at most one replacement activation window, with at least 24 real hours' notice from that revision. Where the communities want broader time-zone coverage, the proposed replacement is offset by twelve hours in local time, such as thirty-six hours after the original start. This is a service candidate to test, not a claim that two windows cover everyone.

There is still only one physical operation charge and one operation. If the first starts, the replacement cannot create another. If both offered windows pass without a launch, end the advertised activation service as unresolved. The actual campaign mechanisms remain available under ordinary funded world access. A new public event programme needs a new explicit scope and funding; it cannot quietly turn this finite offer into recurring weekly administration.

Interested users can choose in-game notifications for material changes. Default participation does not subscribe them to repeated alarms, email or external messages. The same result is not announced as a modal, chat line and toast. A quiet notice links to the current permitted bulletin and its retained history. Missing a transient notice does not lose the information. No reminder is itself an NPC thought or a recurring paid generation call.

Local UI expiry and real service commitments remain real across world pause, save/load and rewind. Restoring an earlier gate configuration cannot resurrect last Saturday's attendance promise. The [restoration rules in §10](#10-interruption-restoration-and-ending-the-service) explain how actual world history and present service commitments are reconciled.
