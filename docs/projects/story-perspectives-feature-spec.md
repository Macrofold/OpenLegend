# Optional story perspectives: after you left

## 1. The experience worth adding

A player watches a familiar resident make something at camp, leaves to continue an adventure, and later reads a brief account of that resident actually continuing the familiar activity. The first profile does not promise the conclusion of an unfinished joint project or assume that the player helped with the work. The pleasure is seeing a small part of the world carry on. The resident does not perform for the reader, wait indefinitely for them, or acquire a scripted attachment because a story would be more touching that way.

The selected first mode is **After you left**: an optional, privately saved, historical vignette about one familiar resident's permitted external activity at a familiar camp. It reveals a small additional fact to the human. It does not make the controlled character a witness. A player who prefers to discover everything by returning and talking can leave it off and play the complete game.

This is a product choice made for [DG15/ND36](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg15--an-optional-story-perspective), within the requested design assignment. It selects the new external-source and recipient policy described below. It does not activate that policy in existing worlds. [D57](../../archive/05-project/open-decisions.md#d57--narration-composed-responses-and-durable-conversations)'s separate question of narrating private internal stimuli remains unselected. Private thoughts, secret motives and other humans' experiences are outside this proposal.

The [gameplay priorities](../repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) remain controlling. Readable opposition, worthwhile rewards, useful improvement and another interesting challenge must work through ordinary play. This vignette is neither a way to explain a broken adventure nor a prerequisite for encounters, invention, shelter, competence, human cooperation or a journal. Contextual lore is useful when it answers a particular need; its existence is not evidence that more prose improves a game. [SP-R01](#sp-r01)

## 2. Existing foundations and the new permission

[Narration and conversations](../narration-and-conversations.md) owns source truth, permitted context, generation, publication, history and failures. [NC07–NC12](../maintainers/narration-and-conversations.md) retain their unfinished delivery and acceptance work. This specification owns only the selected product consumer; [NC20](../maintainers/narration-and-conversations.md#nc20--optional-after-you-left-perspective) coordinates its additional work through those owners. The [base-world profile](../worlds/base/story-perspectives.md) owns the particular camp, activity and scene boundaries. The [narration limits inventory](../limits/narration.md#sp01--selected-external-perspective) owns its proposed non-correctness bounds.

“Public camp” describes authored fiction, not access authority. Being a creator, knowing the resident, standing nearby earlier or discovering an event in a retention index does not independently grant the new view. The world must expressly permit this limited scene projection, the reader must choose it, and each source must qualify. A narrative importance score, a camera angle and a time delay supply none of those permissions. Historical spectator systems illustrate why timing and information scope must be considered separately. [SP-R10](#sp-r10) [SP-R12](#sp-r12)

### The first audience

The first profile is a private world whose participant history contains only the requesting human account, with one controlled character and the authored familiar resident. It is not a shared world that happens to have only one person connected. A world that has admitted another human is ineligible for this profile. Copying a shared world or its private history does not reset that fact.

Before another ordinary human participant or spectator receives world access, new scene capture and generation close and pending publication is cancelled. Admission means account enrollment or invite redemption granting access, even before body control, physical presence or a first connection; characterless spectators count. Separately authorized operational inspection is not ordinary participation and grants no story-recipient permission. Previously published eligible single-player entries can remain readable by their original owner while their original source permission and current world access remain valid. They are not copied into the newcomer's Journal. Re-enabling the first profile in that now-shared world is unsupported. The invitation/participation owner performs this transition before admission; a failed story cancellation must not create a moment in which another human's activity is captured.

The choice is deliberately narrow. It avoids making competitive intelligence and other-human permission a prerequisite for one optional narrative experiment. It does not establish a future multiplayer policy, a universal ban on shared stories or permission to disclose NPC internals. Cooperative games distinguish participation from optional scene sharing, and historical defects show that alternate viewing routes also need review. [SP-R03](#sp-r03) [SP-R04](#sp-r04)

## 3. The smallest complete scene

The first [base-world profile](../worlds/base/story-perspectives.md) follows ordinary cord-making at the already familiar starting camp. The human's character must have actually encountered the resident, legitimately recognized their displayed identity, and witnessed a completed instance of that same native activity at that camp. A generic authored acquaintance alone cannot prove that the player has seen the activity. The camp is anchored to a real previously perceived campfire; this does not require a general place-bookmark system or introduce a map discovery.

After the character physically leaves the authored camp area, one subsequent real completion by that resident, at that same camp, can supply the vignette. It must occur within one game hour of that departure and be outside the character's actual awareness. The resident may have chosen the work before or after departure. No new work, goal, expression, conversation or cognition turn is scheduled to obtain it. The game does not extend the action's duration so that the player has time to leave.

The anchor is a real previously witnessed activity occurrence, not a boundary crossing alone. Walking in and out without a new qualifying shared activity does not rearm it. One anchor can support at most one captured scene, including across reconnect, save/restore, preference changes and story-policy revisions. If the player remains to witness the completion, ordinary awareness applies and there is no distant view of that same occurrence for that reader. If the resident chooses something else, stops work or leaves the camp, the feature has no obligation to produce a story.

One simple eligible outcome could support:

> Mira finished another length of cord at the camp.

This is illustrative copy, not an authored NPC deed or a promised model result. The name, earlier example implied by “another,” completed action and familiar camp all require permitted evidence. If only completion is supported, one grounded sentence is enough. “Mira set it down and rested” requires those separate actual occurrences; the first profile does not wait for or manufacture them. A more elaborate scene is not inherently better.

This selected disclosure permits the narrow historical fact that an already familiar material activity produced its normal kind of result. It excludes quantities beyond that occurrence, current ownership or location of its output, inventory totals, recipe details, new resources, secret capabilities and plans. A prohibition on every material consequence would remove most of the point; unrestricted resource reporting would turn it into scouting. The selected compromise gives closure without advertising a new opportunity or authoritative current stock.

The remaining risk is product value: a cord completion may be too slight to deserve its own mode. That is a reason to stop after the small comparison in section 11, not to fabricate feelings, add weather, force a resident to work, or build a general cinematic system. A campfire going out is a smaller source-backed external event, but a maintenance reminder is a poor first payoff; it remains outside this selected scene.

## 4. A complete player journey

### Choose the perspective

In the existing story preferences, the available qualified world offers **After you left**, off by default. Its explanation is short: “Optional glimpses of familiar activities after you leave. These show you a little more than your character witnessed.” A second sentence explains that opening an available new entry uses the existing narration allowance; the world continues under its ordinary time rules. Detailed source and privacy explanations are available without becoming an onboarding lesson.

Turning it on enables future eligible capture. It does not scan all earlier history, generate a backlog or reinterpret existing private narration. The feature is absent or clearly unavailable in unsupported worlds. A disabled choice never advertises an interesting hidden event as the reason it cannot be enabled.

### Share an ordinary activity and leave

The player experiences normal camp play, including a genuinely chosen resident action. There is no special “make a story” task, actor marker, reward or friendship meter. Normal event-time awareness supplies the anchor. Leaving is physical departure from the profile's camp boundary, not closing Talk, hiding the Journal, switching tabs or disconnecting.

The scene window measures game time. A paused world does not complete work or advance that window; an active world follows its existing simulation and absence policies. Closing the browser does not create an offline episode. If the character did not actually leave, reconnect alone does not qualify. The mode never changes the clock or wakes the world.

### Open at a convenient moment

The Journal has an **After you left** view with a quiet **Read a glimpse** action. Opening the view or checking availability has no model cost. There are no unsolicited banners, unread counters for ungenerated scenes, push notifications, expiry countdowns or emotionally framed reminders. Availability wording stays neutral; it does not reveal the resident, event title or outcome before the reader requests the view.

If a permitted scene exists, the player can explicitly request its generation. The request uses one reserved bounded Narrator attempt. Repeated clicks, another tab and reopening the same pending entry refer to that same attempt. The player can continue playing while it is pending. If the current narration allowance or shared execution slot is unavailable, the request is declined before generation; it does not wait in a hidden paid queue.

If there is no permitted retained scene, show **No glimpse is available.** Explain on demand that only a few kinds of past activity qualify. Do not say “nothing happened,” “Mira is still there,” or “your friend is waiting.” The absence of an entry is not a report about the world. Repeated availability checks cannot disclose excluded event counts, names or diagnostic reasons.

### Read and continue

The entry has the title **After you left**, its occurrence time in the world's calendar, and the persistent label **Your character did not witness this.** Generation time is separate in entry details. The prose is historical even if requested soon after the event. There is no “now” heading, live position, map pin, route, teleport, remote interaction or automatic conversation draft.

It opens as a readable Journal entry, preserving the current world view, activity and input draft. Reading does not pause time, reserve a safe location or stop hunger, danger or another person's speech. The player can close it immediately, with focus returned to the opening control or a sensible existing world control. Current danger and direct conversation remain discoverable through their ordinary interfaces. The design adds no modal gate before taking an action.

Published permitted text stays in the same private Journal entry for later reading. Changing voice affects future requests only. Reopening does not generate, repeat world effects, count as a witnessed occurrence or notify the resident. Text has no automatic dismissal deadline. Scaling, contrast, keyboard reading and dismissal, screen-reader order and reduced-motion behavior follow the existing interface rules. The first profile adds no voiced cutscene or automatic audio. ## 5. Exactly what may be disclosed

Every supplied detail, including title, description, source label, optional context, search result and later history preview, uses the same narrow permission. Source inclusion happens before generation. The Narrator is not asked to read an omniscient packet and decide which secrets to omit.

| Information                                                                                        | First-profile treatment and reason                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Familiar resident's already recognized display identity                                            | Allowed through the anchored identity binding; no new name, disguise resolution or hidden relationship.                            |
| Previously perceived camp identity                                                                 | Allowed as that familiar place; no coordinates, map update or newly discovered route.                                              |
| Same eligible external craft's completed historical outcome                                        | Allowed as a coarse completion of the already familiar activity; actual action receipts govern success.                            |
| Source occurrence time                                                                             | Allowed as historical time, with no claim that it remains current.                                                                 |
| The player character's earlier witnessed activity                                                  | Used only for the minimum permitted anchor and eligibility; no ordinary personal-memory background is passed through.              |
| New recipe, ingredients, item specification, stock totals, output destination or current inventory | Excluded. Reading must not teach an actionable unknown capability or remotely inspect possessions.                                 |
| Private thoughts, goals, intentions, memories, commitments or emotional interpretation             | Excluded even if they would explain the action. Private-state inspection authority is not this mode's grant.                       |
| Speech or conversation transcripts, including private words repeated by an NPC                     | Excluded from this first source family; no paraphrased conversation or attributed motive.                                          |
| Another human's identity, presence, action, speech, effects or private context                     | Excluded by profile eligibility and projection, including derivative information about another human.                              |
| Threats, combat, injuries, death, hidden affiliation, resources elsewhere or future plans          | Excluded. Important retained events are not automatically eligible.                                                                |
| Who helped, caused, requested or emotionally inspired the later action                             | Excluded unless a future independently selected policy permits that exact external causal claim; chronology alone is insufficient. |
| A supported external gesture or rest after the completion                                          | Outside the first craft-only profile. The narrator cannot append one as color.                                                     |

Appearance and voice can express style only within admitted facts. “After you left” is chronology, not “because you left.” “She carried on because she missed you,” “he proudly fulfilled his promise,” and “the lonely camp waited for your return” are not permitted embellishments. Do not create a parasocial obligation or pretend that a quiet resident has a human relationship with the reader.

A human can choose actions after reading the additional information. The product promises no automatic character knowledge, not an ability to make the human forget. For example, the player may decide to return to camp; the actual journey and new perception must still establish current facts. The vignette is not accepted as in-character testimony that the resident has an item now. If a future multiplayer design requires preventing useful foreknowledge, it must select a different information policy before rollout; a delay and disclaimer are insufficient. ## 6. Source capture, waiting and generation

Only one departure anchor and one latest unrequested scene are retained for this reader/world. A source window closes after its first eligible completion, after one game hour, when the character physically reenters the camp, on a new qualifying anchor, or when permission closes. A captured historical scene can remain available after the reader returns; only an unused departure window closes. The completed scene is a compact permitted projection of one primary occurrence plus no more than two necessary supporting references. If mandatory evidence does not fit, that occurrence is unavailable; the system does not drop the inconvenient cause and narrate the rest.

An unrequested scene is kept for at most seven real days from capture and can be replaced by a later qualifying scene. This is a new, explicit bounded buffer for voluntary reading. It is not today's automatic narration queue, a promise of complete history, or an archive of every resident action. Replacing an unopened candidate buys no generation and removes no published Journal entry. There is no loss of game reward when it expires. Once a reader has requested a particular scene, a new occurrence does not silently replace that request's source.

The current ordinary 300-game-second admission interval and 600-game-second waiting age continue to govern ordinary automatic narration. Applying the ten-game-minute waiting policy unchanged to voluntary later reading would defeat this use case. The new buffer therefore has its separate inventoried lifetime. For this mode only, seven real days from capture replaces the ordinary original-occurrence freshness limit; the true historical occurrence time is never relabeled as a recent event. Requests dispatch only when the shared slot is available, with no post-request waiting queue. A preparation that cannot proceed under current execution admission is declined before dispatch. The new age rule does not exempt a generation from current source authorization, spending, cancellation or the single queued/running narration slot. [SP02–SP04](../limits/narration.md#sp02--capture-and-unrequested-scene)

The first profile permits at most four dispatched glimpse attempts per human account across worlds in a rolling twenty-four real hours. This is a qualification ceiling, not a daily assignment or a reason to return. A pre-dispatch refusal consumes none; a dispatched failed or cancelled attempt still counts because it may have incurred cost. There is no paid bypass, streak, replenishment reward or countdown. Existing funding and provider limits can stop requests earlier. World restore, new worlds and preference changes do not reset the real-account attempt record. Saved entries remain readable at no new generation cost.

An ordinary narration request has priority over an optional glimpse. Before dispatch, a conflicting glimpse is cancelled and its unused scene can remain available. During dispatch, new ordinary work requests cancellation of the optional publication through NC; spent or uncertain provider work keeps its receipt and cannot be resent. Ordinary work still follows its actual capacity and spending admission, rather than receiving an invented critical-event bypass. The optional mode must not consume ordinary introduction milestones or its cooldown. This priority handling is new required behavior; measure the resulting ordinary latency and drops before enabling the mode. If it cannot preserve the ordinary experience at acceptable cost, leave the optional feature off.

One request makes at most one Narrator call. It has one to three grounded sentences, at most 1,200 characters in total, within the existing per-sentence and complete-input limits. There is no minimum word quota. A request with one useful fact can yield one sentence; the style must not pad it with weather, sound, a gesture or an inferred feeling. A model cannot choose the audience, expand the source family, execute actions, invent a missing episode or request more evidence autonomously.

## 7. Stop, failure and delayed completion

Turning the preference off stops future capture, clears the active departure anchor and unrequested candidate, and cancels pending publication. It leaves already published, still-permitted private entries readable. Deletion and erasure remain with their existing authority/privacy owners; this does not advertise a current reader self-service Journal deletion action. An Off switch should not unexpectedly destroy the reader's saved prose. Turning it back on requires a fresh qualifying witnessed activity after enabling; previously legitimate identity recognition can remain. It neither restores discarded anchors/candidates nor dispatches older paid work.

Closing the entry is different from turning the feature off. Closing preserves the request; the authorized entry may finish privately in the Journal without a banner or stolen focus. A disconnect also does not buy a new attempt. A request already explicitly dispatched can complete to retained private history only while all current grant and source checks still pass. Turning off, losing world access, changing the source grant or sharing the world cancels publication even if the caller's browser is absent. This is stronger than assuming that current source revision checks alone cover the preference and participation changes.

| Situation                                                                                      | Visible result and consequence                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| No source, permission unavailable, capacity occupied or allowance unavailable before admission | A plain availability or refusal message; no generation, empty story or charge for narration.                                                                                   |
| Source becomes invalid before dispatch                                                         | No story is generated; the pending request becomes unavailable without revealing the revoked detail.                                                                           |
| An admitted generation fails, is rejected or is interrupted                                    | Exactly **Narration failed.** for an otherwise readable failed entry, following NR01; no substitute event prose or player Regenerate button.                                   |
| Permission is revoked while work is in flight                                                  | Publication and source access close; show a neutral unavailable state rather than a failure entry that reveals the source. Actual or uncertain spending remains accounted for. |
| Another tab submits the same request                                                           | Read the same pending/completed/failure entry; no second dispatch.                                                                                                             |
| Reader closes the Journal                                                                      | Play continues; the same authorized request may finish privately, without an attention-grabbing arrival.                                                                       |
| Reader changes narrator voice                                                                  | The existing request keeps its bound voice; only later independent requests use the new setting.                                                                               |
| A restore returns to before the occurrence                                                     | The discarded future cannot remain as an active scene in that restored world; reconcile history under the save/privacy owners, never replay the craft or regenerate it.        |

The existing [NR01 failure policy](../limits/narration.md#nr01--narration-failure-and-regeneration) controls. A later click cannot quietly disguise a retry of the same failed scene as a new glimpse. A genuinely new eligible occurrence may support a later independent request. Provider failure never changes whether the original craft succeeded or whether the resident remembers doing it.

## 8. History, correction and character knowledge

The Journal stores a distinct private narration entry with its historical scope and permitted source links. It is not placed into Talk as if anyone spoke it. It does not enter the character's awareness, memory search, conversation compaction, commitments, knowledge, goals, action experience, relationship interpretation or NPC context. Clicking the resident's name cannot bypass that boundary. Existing character inspection continues to show what its own authority allows.

The first profile supplies no public share URL, export, group broadcast or image/video generation. Any later export or shared edition belongs to the separately selected journal/privacy/rights scope in DG16. Access to the private entry remains scoped in all current reads, search results, caches, streams, source inspection and restore/export mechanisms already owned by NC and the privacy system. Camera-style presentation is not evidence that the character possessed the same information. [SP-R11](#sp-r11)

Retain the minimal authorized scene evidence needed to explain the saved prose when raw events compact or expire under their existing owners. Do not retain a hidden full transcript, NPC biography, inventory snapshot or otherwise forbidden original packet. Compaction is not revocation; a still-valid compact source can continue to support the historical sentence. A genuine evidence gap remains a gap and must not be filled from the resident's current location or another actor's memory.

A corrected, forgotten or disclosure-revoked source makes affected derived prose unavailable under NC's correction rules. The entry can explain that its source is no longer available without repeating the withdrawn facts. No paid regeneration runs automatically and no fictional event is reversed merely to repair text. If a word choice invented a motive, withdraw the affected prose and preserve the diagnostic distinction between a true underlying event and an unsupported narration. Source handles are useful evidence, but their presence does not prove every sentence's meaning; qualification must review entailment and implied causation.

Published history uses the existing NC retention policy; this proposal adds no automatic historical expiry or promise of unlimited free lifetime storage. Its bounded capture and generation constrain new growth but do not qualify every lifetime-history query. Separate the one seven-day unrequested candidate from a reader's already published Journal. Erasure, current access, backup reconciliation and current-format restoration remain governed by their established owners. Deleting a copy cannot make its human reader unlearn it.

## 9. Economics and performance in product terms

The first offering buys prose only when a human chooses a permitted scene. Opening settings, checking availability, admitting a native source, replacing an unrequested candidate, reopening history and dismissing an entry require zero new model calls. Genuine resident decisions continue through their normal funding and agency rules; the feature neither purchases extra resident turns nor changes a resident's action to make its own paid output more interesting.

The complete generation input remains within the existing 24,000-byte limit and 1,800-token output allowance. The smaller product text limit is not a claim that the provider bills only visible prose. Reserve and account for the configured complete request, rejected output, cancellation and uncertain charges through the existing spending owner. Optional narration cannot borrow another person's allowance, use unapproved study funding or trigger a silent top-up. Four daily dispatched attempts put a finite ceiling on this mode's request count; they do not establish a dollar price or a complete hosting-cost guarantee.

At most one live anchor and one unrequested scene per reader/world bound prospective preparation. A scene's retained permitted projection is at most 8,000 UTF-8 bytes, including its required source labels and minimal supporting context. The generation prompt's voice and instruction overhead must still fit the complete request bound. Only the selected current source family is considered; there is no periodic search over everyone's minds or entire world history, no per-frame model judging, and no recency feed ranked by engagement. If actual source shape cannot be projected completely within this envelope, decline that scene and record the qualification failure privately.

Ordinary NPC replies, actions and mandatory context retain their priorities. Do not make a resident's About me, required conversation, commitments or correction evidence smaller to fund this optional narrator. There is no per-reader simulation clone, remote camera rendering or second world running for the scene. A completed historical action is described once; replaying text does not replay its computation.

The main recurring burden may be disclosure and prose quality rather than raw token cost. Each added source family, language, identity state or viewing route needs a reviewable account of what has been revealed and what can still be corrected. FFXVI's production account reports substantial manual work and shipped spoiler mistakes around much more tightly authored lore. That is evidence for conservative scope, not proof that a particular automation can solve Open Legend's problem. [SP-R02](#sp-r02)

Qualification must record actual cost per requested and successfully read passage, rejection and cancellation cost, source retention, ordinary narration interference and useful-scene availability during natural play. A cheap passage that nobody values is still avoidable work. A safe mode that produces almost no eligible moments is not a successful delivery merely because every permission check passed. Do not add a general activity-planning system or new storage history to repair that result.

## 11. Delivery sequence and the decision to continue

**First, ordinary play and ordinary narration must already be worthwhile.** If the camp interaction, resident agency, current action feedback or adventure is weak, improve that through its existing owner. DG15 does not need DG11's study, DG12's library, DG13's canopy, DG14's practice, a six-resident simulation, weather, public-world discovery or custom voices.

**Then qualify the selected disclosure projection without generated prose.** Use reviewed examples of allowed completion, witnessed completion, unknown identity, another human, missing source, private context, duplication and revocation. Select the exact authored camp boundary and known activity under the base profile. Review every visible label and history route. This phase establishes whether the information policy can be expressed honestly; it does not authorize a deployed mode or replace the subsequent technical design.

**Next, deliver one complete voluntary request and return journey.** A genuinely eligible retained completion supports one bounded call, one readable entry, ordinary failure, stop, cancellation, saved rereading and source correction. Preserve the existing NC operational spine. Delivering an isolated prose demo without cost, privacy, absence and history behavior does not complete this step.

**Finally, compare its value with the same ordinary game without the mode.** The product review should observe naturally chosen camp activities and subsequent adventure, without scripting the resident to supply the desired scene. Ask whether a reader can distinguish the historical fact from current character knowledge, whether the entry adds an enjoyable sense of continuation, whether it changes behavior mainly into checking on the resident, and whether declining it leaves the adventure intelligible. Record sparse availability honestly. No participant recruitment or paid run is authorized by this document; the existing verification and spending policies govern any later evaluation.

Continuation requires a recognizable player benefit at a proportionate cost and no reliance on forbidden context to make the prose worthwhile. Two independent reasons to stop are enough: readers find the entry trivial, or keeping it interesting requires broader surveillance or invented emotional significance. Simplifying to ordinary return-and-conversation is a successful product decision if that provides the better experience. Do not award items, competence, friendship points or study benefits to make viewing popular.

Expansion is per source family. A second familiar craft, a permitted completed public project or a genuinely public natural event each needs its own useful payoff, source/audience policy and cost review. Private internal perspectives, distant threats, multi-human worlds, cinematic camera control, voiced playback and shared exports are separate decisions. They are not unfinished components of this first mode. A generic omniscient or private-thought framework is not a dependency of one known-craft vignette.

## 13. Critique and the revisions retained

The tempting design was a dramatic narrator that notices interesting things anywhere. It would require broad retention, disclosure judgments, an interruption policy, many new scene families and repeated paid selection. It also invites the narrator to manufacture connective emotions. The selected design instead asks one narrow question: does a real continuation of a familiar activity add enough pleasure to justify a voluntary paragraph?

The critique removed a promised rest/gesture after the craft because those would be invented unless separately committed. It also removed a campfire-out first scene: that would be easier to source, but risks turning optional narrative into a reminder to maintain a camp. No weather or shelter dependency was added merely to obtain better scenery. One sentence remains valid when there is only one useful fact.

The design explicitly admits a small amount of additional historical resource information—the familiar completion itself—because banning all material consequence would leave little worth reading. It does not claim perfect resistance to human metagaming. The first audience and source restrictions, rather than an impossible promise that the human will not use information, keep that tradeoff reviewable.

The new seven-day candidate buffer is recorded as a new consumer requirement, not hidden inside current automatic narration timing. It allows optional later reading without creating a generated backlog. The lower-priority cancellation and Off behavior are also explicit new work. If those additions cost more than the vignette is worth, the right outcome is to defer the mode, not weaken ordinary narration or widen access.

The final acceptance question is enjoyment during an actual adventure. If players mainly see a trivial completion, feel obliged to monitor the resident, or need this entry to understand a required action, the design has missed its purpose. Returning to camp and discovering the resident's life through ordinary interaction remains a complete and often better experience. This specification therefore preserves a small complete trial and a clear stopping decision, without a roadmap that makes more machinery inevitable.
