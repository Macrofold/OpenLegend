# People built from parts — family authoring and build contract

**Designed target; production experiments remain open.** This is the implementer/artist contract for [ND39–ND42](../maintainers/needs-design.md#dg37--character-appearance-a-creator-and-varied-populations), subordinate to the [feature specification](people-from-parts-feature-spec.md) and [technical design](people-from-parts-tech-design.md). The [bundled profile](../worlds/base/people-appearance.md#control-definitions) owns human control names, age samples, ranges and presets. No command or file format below is claimed to exist until its implementation task passes.

## Source choice and quality boundary

The [Wayfarer source experiment](../../art-direction/studies/2026-11-05-adjustable-people-redo/README.md) establishes different things about three routes:

| Route                                     | Actual result and permissible use                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Textured image-to-3D bodies               | Meshy reconstructed clothed outer shells and shallow faces. Those particular candidates cannot supply complete interchangeable anatomy. This is not evidence that all image-to-3D tools or the normal art workflow fail                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Anatomy constructed from scripted volumes | Pinched shoulders, poor limb/extremity joins and synthetic forms failed visual review despite correspondence checks. This is the failed no-template experiment, not the normal native concept-to-model workflow; do not make it the canonical body generator                                                                                                                               |
| SAM/MHR plus generated heads              | Complete corresponding body surfaces are useful as a construction foundation. The delivered files omitted UVs, textures, usable skinning and MHR parameters. Plain body materials and authored fullness contributed to the soft/rubber appearance; textured heads were at a different finishing stage. Current sculpt/fit approval is owned by the study, not implied by structural checks |

Use the selected SAM-derived corresponding body surface for this pilot, refining it against the owner-approved fit references. Mike approved the inspected CC0 Blender Studio `Head (Animation) – Realistic` topology on November 7, 2026, fitted to H01/H02 likeness; its stock likeness and shoulder bust are not game content. Build the common head, neck join, face controls and expressions from that source while retaining the SAM/MHR bodies. This scoped head choice does not authorize other template donors, rejected stock anatomy or an assumption that the full MHR parameter/pose model is present in the provider GLB. The scoped MHR source exception and actual publication permissions retain their existing owners. A different body source needs the normal source decision, not a hidden fallback.

The concept remains the art target. Covered front/profile/back construction views resolve pose, proportions and hidden sides; they are not a substitute for it. Loose garments conceal anatomical information. Record uncertain measurements instead of treating the clothing envelope as skin. Fuller coverage accepted for a provider input does not replace the final fitted underwear review. Muscle and fullness must be actual geometry where they affect silhouette, rather than painted shading. A skin finish cannot repair a bad shoulder, ankle, hand or head/body proportion.

Rigidly register the source's anatomical facing before fitting; an imported identity matrix does not establish a neutral head pose. Geometry guidance, removable source hair and colour projection use the same recorded calibration. Inspect the source's intended evaluated surface, including subdivision or sculpt modifiers, before transferring its topology. A bare control cage can lose the nasal and eyelid forms present in the source; qualify any budgeted refinement on the actual exported surface. For the selected head, distinguish the visible eyelid margin from the hidden inner return: forcing that return outside the globe inflates the lid.

When small material detail disappears, inspect useful UV coverage and gutters before increasing image resolution. Projected colour has no anatomical ownership: register brows and lips on the final fitted surface, excluding source eye whites, temple hair and nostril shadows from skin. Inspect clay and colour separately so pigment cannot excuse a wrong eye opening or nasal profile.

**Required first art boundary:** one complete covered body and head must look convincing in clay, textured close views, and the ordinary game camera beside the mercenary/approved head references. Inspect scapulae, clavicles, shoulder sockets, elbows, pelvis, knees, ankles, fingers and toes from front/profile/back. Show the same person before/after each sculpt or surface experiment. Mike's approval of the physique and finish is separate from topology, rig, animation and budget acceptance. A junior tool developer consumes these approved assets; code and this document cannot substitute for an anatomist/artist's approved endpoint sculpts.

## External family package

Keep source payloads and generated exports outside Git through the existing asset workflow. The private production package contains `family.blend`, its authoring descriptor, required images/maps, part sources/bindings and qualification receipts. Asset references use the existing exact release/file reference structure, not another version system or a new download service. Working sources may be edited locally; publishing uses the existing authority. No backward readers or old-recipe migrations are needed during development.

`family.blend` is the editable geometry/UV/weight source; the authoring descriptor maps controls and bindings to those sources. The compiler derives one numerical buffer set for both the offline evaluator and browser; nobody separately hand-maintains their coordinates. The local `family.json` package index supplies the exact family reference and verified file records (`path`, `bytes`, `sha256`) for `master`, `profile` and `compiled`, plus texture and qualification records. This local index is not the storage manifest. Use these stable object roles, with the descriptor mapping roles to actual object names:

| Role                           | Required data                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `body`, `head`                 | Ordered neutral positions, ordered faces, UVs, named relative shape keys, material regions, weights; neck boundary ordered consistently across both meshes      |
| `eyes`, `mouth`                | Separate eye globes with iris region, lids on the head, teeth and inner mouth sufficient for closure/jaw inspection; no painted eyes over a featureless surface |
| `scalp`, hair/brow/beard parts | Real scalp under removable hair, root bindings, length targets when supported, hat variant and any secondary bones                                              |
| `rig`                          | One named joint hierarchy, rest transforms, anatomical landmark-to-joint bindings and stable joint IDs; no marker-sphere substitution for a rig                 |
| `fitSurfaces`                  | Region-specific torso/limb surfaces, skirt/hem bridge, and supported layer envelopes; each has its own ordered topology and shape bindings                      |
| `coverage` / wardrobe parts    | Separate mesh sources, UV/material regions, fit bindings, weights or rigid anchors, layer compatibility, hidden-face masks, motion examples                     |

The frame key supplies its complete endpoint relative to Basis. Each sculpt, face or age target supplies the difference from its Blender `relative_key`, after the same object/unit conversion; subtracting Basis from a frame-relative sculpt would count the frame twice. Reuse each transformed neutral anchor once per mesh during compilation.

Authoring data includes `units` (`metres`), `referenceStatureMetres`, `objects` (roles above), `topology` (vertex counts plus face/UV hashes per canonical mesh), `controls`, `facePresets`, `ageSamples`, `materials`, `landmarks`, `rig`, `fitSurfaces`, `layerOrder`, `parts`, `presets`, `envelope`, and `qualification` (exact checked recipe/part/pose references and failures). Source rights and provider provenance stay in the existing asset receipt rather than new inline account records. The compiled types in [person-family.ts](../../apps/client/src/characters/person-family.ts) own the numerical layout; the world profile owns names, choices and ranges.

Publish an acyclic graph through the existing asset authority: first an exact **public-safe family descriptor** containing control/selection/envelope semantics, then the compiled runtime artifact and runtime maps that depend on it, then the selected presentation release. Explicitly approved private runtime releases retain current account admission and authenticated delivery; the [private Main pilot](metahuman-foundation-poc-tech-design.md#11-stop-conditions-adoption-and-extension) does not change ordinary workflow adoption. The descriptor omits its own reference and the compiled artifact digest; otherwise their hashes would depend on themselves. Compilation receives the exact descriptor reference explicitly. The existing `assets prepare` can calculate it from the frozen manifest bytes for an offline build; that output remains unpublished until normal publication and current-permission checks pass. Both recipes and compiled data name that reference; `compiled`/`compiledPath` select the separate exact runtime file. Publish the descriptor before its dependent runtime artifacts, and verify their metadata agrees before installing the family. Private masters, concepts, prompts and source lineage remain in separately permissioned source-bundle records, outside runtime dependencies and ordinary recipe projection.

The descriptor's existing asset revision identity identifies one **frozen family build**, not only its visible labels/ranges. Allocate a new descriptor revision whenever geometry, targets, maps, bindings, motion or build-tool inputs change, even if the public profile is identical; publish one compiled artifact for that revision. Record the frozen input digests and tool versions in the ordinary private build receipt. Rebuilding an existing recipe uses that exact descriptor and its pinned compiled artifact, rather than assigning revised geometry to the same family identity.

The descriptor manifest stores its public definition in `metadata.appearanceFamily`, without `reference`, `compiled` or `compiledPath`. A selected release advertises `metadata.appearanceFamilies: [{descriptor, compiledPath}]`; `descriptor` is the exact published reference, and the selected file map supplies the compiled artifact's checksum/bytes. Both belong to the selected, currently permitted release closure. A trusted selected-world initializer chooses applicable families and installs its own wardrobe/stature rules. An ordinary save reopen never silently adopts a new family; custom openings retain their own opt-in.

Each `controls[id]` defines `kind` (`bipolar`, `frame`, `stature`, `age` or `styleLength`), numeric bounds/default, the fixed unit/display conversion below, target names, affected object/region IDs, protected landmarks, and any continuous corrective drivers. `bipolar` targets identify negative/positive deltas at each structural frame; a shared delta can reference the same target twice. `frame` owns the two body anchors. `age` names ordered sample years and geometry/map references. A style publishes its own length targets or declares no length control. UI labels and group ordering are selected-world content. A bipolar control’s geometric neutral is always `.5`; its default may select another value. The interpolation equations below are exhaustive, with no separately authored curve semantics. The evaluator accepts only these fixed arithmetic kinds; no expression language, Python driver string or generated executable code is accepted from metadata.

Each `facePresets[id]` names its neutral head target and structural crown/eye/jaw landmark data in the family’s canonical axes, origin and metres. Qualify its neutral neck join and anatomical crown at reference stature on both frame anchors. Frame-dependent data has corresponding endpoints; shared data may reference the same target twice. Face selection chooses one identity’s targets and landmarks, never a blend of unrelated generated meshes. Head-length changes carry authored trunk compensation just like other proportion changes.

Landmarks have two fixed kinds. A `surface` landmark addresses either a mesh triangle with barycentric coordinates and optional local offset, or a named shared-seam ring with an ordered index. A `structural` landmark stores a neutral internal point and frame/proportion-control deltas on a small authored landmark cage. Joint centres use structural landmarks: following the fattening skin would incorrectly move the skeleton. Muscle, fullness, belly, chest tissue and age have zero deltas on these joint points; frame, breadth and segment-length controls move the relevant points. Eye/jaw supports follow the selected head's structural controls. Joint bindings identify one head landmark, one tail landmark and an authored roll axis. Eye centres, crown, soles, navel, sternum, jaw, hand grips, ear lobes and garment supports must be explicit. Do not rediscover them from raw index guesses or nearest-point searches on every build. Family qualification checks their anatomical placement.

A part record contains `source`, `family`, `fitClass` (`surface`, `bridge`, `rigid`), `region`, `layerClass`, `compatibleInnerLayers`, `binding`, `materialSet`, `hiddenRegions`, and any `variants`, explicit default `variantOrder`, `correctives`, `secondaryBones`, `motionRecipe`. Bindings use canonical source topology, never exported GLB indices. The full outfit has one hierarchy/pose per wearer; parts can reference a subset of its joints. World equipment ports and actual item identities remain outside these geometric records.

The compiled part groups `pieces: [{meshId, fit}]` so one actual item can contain rigid plates and soft straps. Every drawable body/part mesh needs `jointIndices`/`jointWeights`: rigid pieces use one joint, soft pieces use at most four influences. Helper-only fit surfaces may omit weights. A rigid rest-fit anchor alone does not attach a mesh during playback. Render triangles must map through any UV-split vertices to the same canonical face order, because masks address those face ordinals. Do not bake a per-person simplification after authoring bindings; qualify the shared canonical reduction first.

Each `objects[meshId].metadata.materialId` assigns one material to the entire compiled mesh; Blender polygon material slots are not translated. Bake colour regions such as mouth cavity/teeth into its atlas with appropriate tint exclusions, or author separate corresponding canonical meshes when distinct material properties are needed, before freezing the family.

An optional piece `groundSupport: {foot, vertices}` identifies its anatomical foot-joint index and nonempty canonical sole-vertex indices. The points must be rendered and skinned without cosmetic-joint influences; a motion-equipped family names one of its planted feet. Both evaluators derive a nonnegative constant support offset from the lowest selected fitted sole point and include those points with the existing anatomical sole guides. The actor and offline rig apply that offset once to the complete visual assembly in Rest and playback; fitted rest/bind data, bare stature and world body anchors remain unchanged. Preview framing and handles use the same offset. Removing/replacing footwear updates both support motion and depiction together. Each distinct rendered skin-weight tuple at a UV-split probe owns contact; identical tuples may be shared. The first kit qualifies paired boots with matching support depth, not asymmetric or single-shoe stance.

Optional compiled `secondaryMotion` rows are `{part, joint, driver, lagSeconds, maxAngleRadians}`. Joint/driver values are stable indices into the parent-first rig; the driver precedes the nonroot target. The target carries the matching `rig.joints[].secondaryPart` tag; its descendants cannot cross into core or another part, and anatomical skin cannot weight these cosmetic joints. Lag seconds must be positive and angular limits lie in `[0, π]`. The native motion compiler accepts only explicitly named extra attachment joints with `{parent, localMatrix}` declarations (sixteen column-major rest values); it refuses replacing a real source node or repairing an undeclared missing bone. The [E7 bake](people-from-parts-tech-design.md#assembly-storage-and-the-renderer) consumes active-part rules after body/contact fitting and writes ordinary playback keys.

Compiled `motion.legs[].soleProbes` is a nonempty list of `{meshId, vertex}` references into anatomical body meshes. Use actual canonical heel/ball/toe skin points and their existing render-split weights; a toe-joint height is not the sole surface. The thigh/shin/foot links are direct parents for the two-segment solve. Each clip retains original `times`/local rotations/root positions and supplies sorted `supportTimes`, including every original time and the same end. Family qualification derives that fixed extra-key schedule from measured between-key dips across its envelope; fitting consumes it without adaptive search. After original-grid XZ planting, sample the immutable fitted curve and original source bend plane at those times, raise only the necessary ankle goals using the admitted probes, then bake secondary response. Root motion and foot/toe world orientation remain intact. The bounded correction refuses unresolved probes; full skinned geometry and interpolated playback still require separate qualification. Motion reuse keys include actual probe coordinates, rest identity and active secondary parts, so unrelated pigment/protected-foot edits do not rebuild clips.

For a qualified motion family, a bounded sole correction may also run after the actual blended pose and before skinning, reusing the same admitted probes and source-plane solver. Its source guide must mirror actual clip instances, times, weights and evaluation order, including interrupted transitions with simultaneous instances of one clip. Preserve root/local translations and foot/toe orientation. This visual correction adds no world movement, terrain/support authority or physiological rule. The [renderer motion experiment](people-from-parts-tech-design.md#assembly-storage-and-the-renderer) owns setup/playback, allocation and lifecycle qualification; a remaining contact failure cannot be accepted merely because the runtime keeps its best bounded pose.

Source channels hold their nearest endpoint outside their own time range, including when another channel starts earlier, as required by the [glTF animation contract](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html#animations). Compiled playback is linear: changing STEP transforms on selected joints or their ancestors require a prior linear bake; constant STEP transforms remain usable. Stride fitting remeasures speed only on a uniform original source grid. For nonuniform source keys, both evaluators retain the authored speed scaled by the target/source hip-height ratio.

Each binding names its source canonical mesh or `fitSurface` ID and exact topology pin; triangle indices are local to that source. `layerOrder` is the family’s ordered list of fit stages. Resolve a part’s declared body/envelope support before fitting it; a part-dependent envelope names the supporting part and resolved variant from an earlier stage. Reject missing support or same/later-stage dependencies at admission. This is a bounded inner-to-outer pass, not a general dependency solver. Fit stages do not define world equipment ports or draw order.

Each variant declares `source`, `binding`, `envelope`, `compatibleInnerLayers` and `hiddenRegions` for that cut, reusing exact source references where data is shared. An explicitly requested variant must satisfy its declared body envelope and inner-layer configuration or fail with `part-fit`. When no variant was requested, choose the first eligible entry in the authored `variantOrder`, never incidental map/file order; a single-cut part needs no list. Validate the list once for unique known variants. No eligible cut is a `part-fit` failure. Qualified overlap between cuts permits an atomic switch when the private draft edit completes; their topology is never interpolated. Preserve the item’s identity and material choices. Preview the valid covered body while an outfit fit is unavailable; do not change body controls to make a cut fit.

Reject an unknown cut, an unknown part, or a cut request targeting no selected/automatic part. A typo never falls back to the default cut or silently adds a garment. Coverage is an authored selection role, required and nonempty for the human baker's covered output; frame and personal gender do not choose it implicitly.

For a surface binding, store per garment vertex a region-qualified triangle `(A,B,C)`, barycentric weights and offset `(x,y,z)` in metres. The deterministic frame is `e1=normalize(B-A)`, `n=normalize(cross(B-A,C-A))`, `e2=cross(n,e1)`. At the shaped triangle evaluate `wA*A+wB*B+wC*C+x*e1+y*e2+z*n`. This retains authored local ease; it does not claim stretch simulation. Region/side assignment and nearest-face search happen at authoring, not per fit. Reject a collapsed/flipped binding triangle. Bridge surfaces use the same rule on the fitted hem cage, not the nearest thigh. After final reference fitting and evaluated lining, transfer base cloth weights from the supporting source triangle once, retaining deliberate sleeve/hem/rigid overrides and the four-influence cap. If a cut or projection correction changes that support, re-author the canonical binding; do not retain its earlier triangle weights. A per-body nearest-surface trial can diagnose the fault, but batch/live qualification requires corrected canonical bindings or an explicit qualified corrective weight set. It does not authorize nearest-face searches on every recipe evaluation. Layer envelopes follow their declared inner garment and are evaluated before the outer layer; an unsupported inner layer fails rather than receiving an arbitrary inflation offset.

`people.bind_surface(sourceMesh, supportId, positions)` implements that authoring step with one BVH over the caller’s eligible support triangles; all positions use the same canonical axes/metres and the support retains its full topology pin. `people.surface_skin_weights(binding, sourceVertexWeights)` interpolates named weights from those same canonical vertices. Preserve their raw influences until the existing compiler selects four and normalizes once. Store the resulting binding/groups in the editable part; runtime fitting consumes the recorded data without a nearest-surface search.

`hiddenRegions` entries name the target canonical body/part mesh, its topology pin and authored face-region IDs. Union masks separately per matching target from the currently present compatible garments; an unmatched mask leaves faces intact. Derive from the intact sources each time rather than destructively accumulating removals. Install masks with the same completed part set, and restore exposed regions immediately when an outer part disappears or loses permission. A failed coat load cannot leave hidden shirt/body faces. Export mappings retain this exact target identity through triangulation and vertex splitting.

`envelope` contains the qualified per-control bounds and only necessary coupled linear inequalities: `terms` is a list of `{control, coefficient}`, with `maximum` and a human-readable constraint ID. Evaluate `sum(coefficient * normalizedValue) <= maximum`. Stature/age use their declared normalized conversions for this calculation. An artist may add such a conservative boundary after demonstrating a failure, then requalify its legal side; do not automatically infer an allowed volume from sparse passing points. A released family supports its declared envelope, not only a whitelist of presets. A drag intersects the segment from the last valid draft to the requested draft with each interval/half-space and stops at the earliest boundary; numeric/offline input reports rejection. This needs no search, arbitrary expressions or collision solve. If useful shapes need a nonconvex envelope, first try an authored corrective or a separate qualified cut/family through the normal decision gate rather than adding a constraint language.

## Recipe and input resolution

The body/face/hair/age field layout below belongs to this human authoring profile. Generic engine storage and wire validation use installed family-declared controls and roles; other worlds need not provide these human fields.

The resolved recipe is `{family, controls, selections}`, shared by offline creation, persistence and preview. The installed human profile maps its named controls to body proportions, adult age, local facial features, supported hair length and greying, and its selections to face identity, hair/brow/beard styles, skin/eye palettes, marks and coverage. Only authored, qualified options are exposed. Stature is metres, adult age is years, and frame/sculpt/length controls are normalized floats. Age describes appearance only. Concept provenance belongs in the build receipt; the separate outfit request names exact parts/variants and never becomes a second authority for actually equipped items. No arbitrary shader or remote URL is part of a recipe.

1. Select one exact family and its approved preset. Resolve defaults from that release once. A request without a build preset uses the manifest neutral; changing a build preset affects its build fields only.
2. Apply explicit values over preset values, including explicit zero. Numeric percent input `p` maps to `p/100`; do not guess whether an unlabelled `75` means `.75`. Height percentage maps linearly to metres over the selected family interval. Age percentage maps linearly to apparent years over its interval; geometry between age samples has its separate interpolation below. For simultaneous unit and percent entries compare their normalized values with absolute tolerance `1e-6` (floating-point conversion noise only); a larger discrepancy is a conflict, not a reason to choose silently. This tolerance never rounds the stored value.
3. Resolve discrete parts and all defaults to explicit values. Keep the requested values at full input precision; UI rounding never changes the saved recipe. Do not infer fitness from gender, age, height, hairstyle or profession. A natural-language brief can suggest a recipe for review, but the baker performs no inference or paid calls.
4. At recipe admission check finite numeric values, known controls/parts, declared bounds, exact family compatibility and required fields. Reject unknown controls and unsupported combinations with their names and values. Reuse the resolved result downstream; do not repeat input validation per vertex. Do not clamp offline input or silently substitute the nearest preset.
5. Save the fully resolved recipe with measured dimensions and exact source references. A preset's later edit cannot change this saved person. Height 75% and 1.95 m must resolve identically under the initial bundled range. A second build from that resolved recipe must agree in geometry, choices and measurements within floating-point tolerances, not necessarily produce byte-identical GLB packaging.

An illustrative authored request is height 75%, breadth 55%, muscle 70%, fullness 25%, shoulders 65%, leg proportion 60%, neck length 45%, apparent age 55 years, the second face with eye spacing 48% and nose width 55%, and the tied braid at length 70%. It must use these controls, not turn the sentence into another unconstrained whole-person generation. User-facing keys may be friendly labels; the saved IDs are those in the selected family.

## Geometry evaluation

Author source geometry once at the reference stature with an upright neutral A-pose and separated limbs. Freeze topology/UVs before authoring shape keys. High-detail source sculpts may differ in topology; wrap the canonical surface onto them and bake detail instead of replacing canonical vertex order. Retopology/LOD is a separate offline experiment and cannot be repeated independently for each slider endpoint.

For each selected record with corresponding frame endpoints, evaluate `D(f)=(1-f)*D_F+f*D_M` once. This applies to neutral head/structural-cage data, body/face control deltas, age samples and frame-dependent correctives. Shared data can alias both endpoints. Face selection first chooses that identity’s records; frame interpolation never mixes unrelated identities.

Evaluate every recipe in this order:

1. **Frame and face.** For frame value `f`, interpolate the two body anchors `B=(1-f)F+fM`. Select that face preset’s calibrated neutral head and structural landmarks on the common head topology independently. Hair, pigment and age are not properties of the frame. Both anchors share the same neutral values for the other controls.
2. **Local shape.** For bipolar value `s`, define `n=max(1-2s,0)` and `p=max(2s-1,0)`. Add `n*negativeDelta+p*positiveDelta`, interpolating each delta between its frame anchors using `f`. Deltas are measured from each frame's neutral, never from an already modified mesh; otherwise composing controls double-counts deformation. Face deltas follow the selected head preset, with shared targets where they remain valid. Apply each surface and structural-landmark delta once here, including body/face length targets and their authored trunk compensation. Hair/style length is evaluated only in step 7. Add all independent deltas before correctives.
3. **Coupled shape.** Store only demonstrated interaction corrections. Each correction names its positive/negative excursions from step 2 and corresponding frame endpoint deltas `C_F,C_M`. With excursion product `w`, add `w*((1-f)*C_F+f*C_M)`; a shared correction references the same delta at both endpoints. Head corrections belong to the selected face preset. This yields zero when any required excursion is neutral and a continuous increase toward the tested corner. Examples are broad shoulders + chest volume, and fullness + muscle. No generic collision solver runs inside shape evaluation. A missing correction leaves the affected combination unqualified.
4. **Age.** Between adjacent age knots `a,b`, use `t=(age-a)/(b-a)` and add `(1-t)*ageDelta[a]+t*ageDelta[b]`; each delta is relative to the neutral-age head/body surface. Sample exactly the endpoint at the bounds. Preserve skull identity, eyeball dimensions, joint centres, chosen stature/build and hair pigment. Sculpt large folds/volume changes; surface maps supply fine detail. Shared age deltas are allowed only after checking both face presets, frame anchors and muscle/fullness cases. For a demonstrated age/feature interaction, author correction deltas at the same age knots for the selected face. Multiply their adjacent-knot interpolation by the named feature excursion product: `w*((1-t)*C[a]+t*C[b])`; frame-dependent samples first use the same frame interpolation as step 3. Neutral-age and unneeded samples are explicit zero. Apply these corrections here, not again in step 3. This repairs a collapsing lid or mouth without a second unrelated old-person mesh.
5. **Landmarks and seams.** Read the already evaluated structural landmarks to build rest joints; surface landmarks are evaluated on the final unposed meshes. Length targets and their trunk compensation were applied once in step 2 against neutral measurements; do not apply them again here. Do not apply a second bone-length scale to geometry that already contains that length change. The head's neck boundary follows the body's ordered ring, and adjoining positions, weights and normals agree. For normal sharing, connected declared seam pairs form one group: average each unique canonical vertex’s original normal once, then assign that normal to every member. Repeated pair IDs must not cause sequential overwrites. Runtime grouping belongs to the immutable family preparation; incremental evaluation recomputes affected seam-connected meshes without mutating reused earlier buffers. Refine an unsound neck contour jointly on both source meshes before freezing the family, copy the shared result back to both rings and regenerate affected canonical data; preserving correspondence does not require preserving a malformed contour. Head length protects total stature through its authored compensation. Verify the crown before uniform stature scaling; invalid compensation is a failed fit, not a reason to rescale away the requested proportions.
6. **Final stature.** Scale the entire fitted anatomical assembly and its rig once around the sole plane by requested/reference stature. Eye/mouth geometry placed in step 7 uses those scaled landmarks and the same factor on its reference dimensions, exactly once; face edits preserve globe size at reference stature. Hair height and boot sole thickness never enter that denominator. Fit footwear to the foot; preserve its supported ground-contact offset separately.
7. **Eyes, hair, coverage and equipment.** Place eye centres from the scaled structural landmarks and fit lids around those globes; transfer brows and ear accessories by their bindings. For normalized style length `s`, use `n=max(1-2s,0)`, `p=max(2s-1,0)` and add `n*shortDelta+p*longDelta` to that style’s neutral mesh, with zero displacement at fixed roots. Its reference length is `L0+n*(Lshort-L0)+p*(Llong-L0)` in declared metres; a style without targets rejects a supplied length. Apply length once before fitting the roots/selected hat variant to the shaped scalp. Fit coverage and wardrobe from inner to outer using the technical design's surface/bridge/rigid rules. Apply only qualified hair/part-fit corrections here; body, face and age corrections are already complete. Shape first, bind second, pose last.
8. **Motion and export.** Fit one rest hierarchy, compute matching inverse binds and use the shared weights (or an authored corrective weight set for a demonstrated failure). Transfer the selected compatible clips through the maintained motion owner. Compilation makes each joint’s adjacent quaternion keys sign-continuous (`q` and `−q` are the same rotation), so component interpolation in source guides cannot take the opposite path between keys; authored timestamps and physical key rotations stay unchanged. Bake required secondary responses from the actual final clip. First fixed exports use skinning and permitted secondary bones; animated pose-corrective morphs require the separate renderer morph-lifetime qualification, not an assumed Blender-driver export. Apply hidden-face masks only on an export copy; preserve the intact master. Export, reimport and check the actual posed output and measured anatomy.

Preserve distinct canonical vertices at UV cuts. When retained source data proves a smooth cut with exact corresponding positions, target/frame deltas and skin rows, declare same-mesh aliases through `seams`. This shares normals without welding geometry, UVs or skin; coincident positions alone do not establish smoothing intent.

Canonical topology, neck correspondence and UV identity are required for every body/face target. Canonical meshes must pass Blender geometry validation without repair before compilation; repair and requalify a refused master before freezing new family data. The compiler never saves its in-memory repair. Recomputed normals/tangents must match the exported triangulation and normal-map bake. The master has complete torso/pelvis/limbs; skin under clothing is not an empty shell. Separate head/eye/mouth surfaces may have deliberate anatomical openings; a blanket watertightness count is not a substitute for inspecting them.

## Skin, age and material production

Use one coherent skin UV layout across head/body and all targets; reserve sufficient face area for close review. Generated head paint is projected/baked to that layout, colour-matched across the neck and inspected in neutral light. Remove directional highlights/shadows from source colour where they would be lit twice. Preserve nose, eyelids, lips, ears, hands and joint forms in geometry. Paint broad believable colour variation; fine pores are optional and cannot justify noise or shimmer.

Start with a base-colour atlas, one tangent-space normal map and scalar roughness; add a roughness map only when its isolated comparison improves the required look. Use ordinary opaque skin/material rendering already supported by the game, with no new subsurface shader or per-character light. Build skin palette variants from authored masks and colours; do not multiply the entire textured head by a tint that also darkens eyes, lips and hair. Eyes, hair, fabric, leather and metal retain their own regions/materials.

Age samples share UVs. Interpolate age colour in linear colour space; interpolate decoded tangent normals and renormalize before encoding. Bake the requested age/skin/marks once per offline character; do not ship all age maps in each fixed character GLB. Keep the neutral face identity under the age detail. Default hair greying is explicitly zero unless the recipe selects it; no age-to-grey or age-to-stoop rule is implied.

Compiled UVs retain Blender's bottom-origin image convention; browser bitmap decoding flips image rows once. Textured meshes require UVs. `tintMask` is a linear red-channel weight between authored `baseColor` and the selected tint, preserving lips/other untinted regions. Generic `colorBlend` interpolates that tint toward its authored colour over a named control's declared interval; this implements independent hair greying without a human rule in the renderer. Age maps cover the declared age interval. Each endpoint normal uses its own map or the base `normalTexture`; effective normal-map presence must be consistent across samples in both consumers.

**Experimental cutout materials.** Optional `alphaCutoff` in `[0, 1]` selects standard glTF `MASK`: retain fragments where `baseColor[3] × colorTexture.alpha >= alphaCutoff`, using alpha one when no base colour texture exists. Age paint, palettes, tint masks and `colorBlend` affect RGB, never this coverage. The native baker packs RGB and coverage at their maximum dimensions, resampling temporary RGB in linear colour and alpha linearly before material blending; source images remain unchanged. This preserves thin coverage instead of reducing it to a smaller age atlas. The live wearer shares the colour/opacity lease when they name the same image; distinct age paint retains an additional base-image lease for stable coverage. No separate mask image or recurring bake is introduced. Optional `doubleSided: true` disables back-face culling and enables back-face lighting; absent/false is single-sided in both consumers. Without `alphaCutoff`, materials retain opaque rendering. The native baker uses the Blender 5.2 Alpha-node mask pattern rather than the removed `blend_method` property. Native/browser family parity and the isolated [MHP performance comparison](metahuman-foundation-poc-tech-design.md#controlled-performance-experiments), including any additional coverage-map residency, remain required; this seam does not adopt a source or change the normal art workflow.

The initial texture packing experiment uses: skin colour 2048², skin normal 1024², hair colour 1024², eyes 256², wardrobe/coverage colour atlas 1024², optional wardrobe normal 512² and optional packed material map 512². At maximum this is **7,929,856 base-level pixels**, below the existing 8,388,608-pixel bundle limit; mips and decoded formats still count toward residency. These are starting allocations, not a guarantee that the face/cloth will pass at those resolutions. Reallocate within the existing total after a matched close/game/moving-camera comparison. Keep editable high-detail sources outside the runtime bundle. Use scalar colours for simple bronze/linen regions when they preserve the target look; never remove geometry to conceal a bad fit.

Final source-to-runtime qualification uses the current `art-tool.py report` character budget, including its 40,000-triangle assembled export check, joint/influence/material/texture/byte counts. Do not treat the larger generic render-root allowance as permission to bypass this helper. Protect neck, fingers, eyelids, cloth openings and cloth-bone transition seams during reduction; a static silhouette pass does not qualify a moving seam. Inspect textured and clay exports, plus daylight/firelight and camera motion, before accepting a smaller map or mesh.

## Reusable baker and failures

The existing `scripts/art/art-tool.py` CLI owns `assemble-person --family /external/family.json --recipe FILE --out /external/new-directory`, dispatching to `scripts/art/people.py`. Repeating `--recipe` requests a bounded sequential batch; `--part ID` and `--variant PART=CUT` select its separate outfit. Keep import/export, reports, source-motion preparation and external-output checks with their existing owners. The module exposes `resolve_recipe`, `evaluate_body`, `fit_parts`, `bake_materials`, and `assemble_person`; all single and batch entry points call these same functions. Split further only for a demonstrated second consumer or an unwieldy responsibility, not one module per slider. This is offline Blender work, not a new service/package/plugin framework.

Already-baked wearer scenes use explicit linear action curves and the existing exporter with `force_sampling=False` (`ACTIONS` mode), preserving the fitted support times. Blender's NLA mode forces frame sampling and can discard fractional keys. Remove only zero-user prior actions between covered/dressed scenes so names and per-companion clips remain exact; retain their shared baked materials until both exports finish. Other source-authoring scenes retain the normal NLA bake. Reimport must compare actual times and poses, including between-key sole checks, rather than just clip names/counts.

Repeating `--recipe FILE` makes a batch through that same command. One input writes its package directly to `DIR`; multiple inputs write `DIR/0001`, `DIR/0002`, and so on in request order, plus `batch-report.json` with each input, output, status and failure reason. Repeated recipes are allowed. Continue independent entries after a failed person, retain successful entries, and return nonzero if any entry failed. Require a fresh output directory; never overwrite an input, accepted release or earlier result. No second batch recipe schema is needed.

Before opening a master, resolve the exact sources through the existing asset workspace. Run Blender with automatic script execution disabled. Check topology/UV/required-role hashes once per admitted source package. Use the maintained scripts to apply the fixed data operations; imported/generated data cannot execute a driver expression. A batch retains the immutable family across consecutive uses, copies only evaluated geometry/material work for the current person, and releases that person's temporary objects/images before the next. Release the current master before loading a different family. Keep one active bake initially; no speculative workers or persistent cache of every float combination.

Each successful output directory contains `resolved-recipe.json`, `covered.glb`, `dressed.glb`, `measurements.json`, `report.json`, and a receipt naming source references, exporter/tool revision, clips, corrections, stage timings, counts and qualification scope. Editable part sources remain referenced rather than copied into every character. `covered.glb` always retains required coverage; `dressed.glb` reflects the exact requested outfit. No implicit publication, equipment grant or game installation occurs. Batch publishing uses the existing asset owner only after the ordinary review boundary.

Write a build into a task-owned staging directory, then expose the successful directory when both exports and required checks finish. On failure retain an inspection candidate/receipt externally, but do not emit a successful package or replace an accepted release. Failure kinds are `invalid-recipe`, `missing-source`, `unsupported-shape`, `part-fit`, `motion-fit`, and `export-check`, carrying the affected control/part/pose and measured deviation where available. Return a nonzero exit code. A numeric check passing cannot override required visual rejection. An unqualified shape can be exported explicitly as an inspection candidate, never silently as a supported family member.

## Qualification sequence

1. **Source and baseline:** preserve the rejected results separately; use the currently selected physique/head references. Finish one covered neutral person through geometry and material review. Record source acquisition cost, sculpt/wrap time, UV/material time and repair time separately.
2. **Three-body proof:** create the two contrasting adults and exact intermediate recipe from corresponding surfaces; verify each head/neck and coverage. Show the same Wayfarer source outfit on all three, front/profile/back and Idle/Walk/Run. This proves those recipes, not every control.
3. **Build controls:** at 1.75 m and age 35 compare the eight build recipes, then isolate muscle and fullness at `.1/.5/.9` on each frame. Require lean-muscular, full-muscular, lean-low-muscle and full-low-muscle cases. Change one local control through `0/.25/.5/.75/1`, with all others neutral, on each frame and the intermediate. Use a continuous sweep to identify between-sample artifacts, not only screenshots of endpoints.
4. **Faces, age and hair:** qualify both faces with every local axis, then the age knots and each adjacent midpoint. Compare ages while locking body/face identity/pigment; compare muscles while locking age. Check eye closure, jaw opening, lips/teeth, head turns and the neck. Remove/swap hair, check the scalp, short/braid length endpoints, hat-on/off and earrings. Expression probes are rig/art checks, not delivery of dialogue lip-sync.
5. **Interactions and parts:** add only the combined cases that exercise shared regions: breadth + muscle + fullness; chest + short neck + shoulders; leg + neck + head length at fixed stature; aged eyes + eye aperture; jaw + lips; fullness + padding + plate; boots + trousers; hat + braid. For each include midpoint and both sides of an observed failure. Test ordinary cuffs/necklines and remove/reapply in another order. Measure skinned sole contact through the actual Walk/Run blend and start/stop transitions, including a speed change from a pure stride; synchronized playback and floor-safe clips need their own blend check. Equip/remove footwear during an existing cosmetic stride or crossfade must preserve its phase and update contact together. Do not enumerate the Cartesian product of all controls and garments. Every common wardrobe item must cover the released envelope and declared layer combinations; otherwise repair it or keep it out of that release.
6. **Motion and export:** use accepted source clips through the maintained retargeter; new/replacement human motion follows the selected Kimodo route and its separate qualification. Check upright idle, one complete Walk/Run cycle, reach, turn, crouch/seat and step poses on the actual final export. Retain corrected source knee-plane behavior; do not hide bad retargeting by inflating cloth. Preserve cloth seam transition geometry or reject its reduced output. Bake cloth response against the clip actually exported, not an earlier motion file.
7. **Repeat and integrate:** rebuild the same resolved recipes and compare results. Qualify repeatable batches against their declared subset; an accepted baked person can enter play while the rest of the family is produced. The live creator/modular experiment follows separately. Changing the renderer, physical stature, projection or cognition boundary requires its own checks below; asset checks alone do not qualify them.

Structural passes require finite coordinates/weights, exact canonical correspondence, valid references, correct rest/bind transforms and no unintended disconnected limbs, inverted garment openings or missing anatomy. Height matching uses the existing ±1 cm threshold. The ±3% stature landmark threshold is only a coarse sheet-matching filter; facial likeness, shoulder/ankle shape and visible clipping require visual acceptance even inside it. Proposed reproduction tolerances are 0.1 mm maximum position/landmark difference at the same recipe, exact part choices/topology and identical clip timing; compare separately from image-compression/GLB byte identity. A full frame-by-frame cloth clearance diagnostic may locate suspect regions, but buried inside-surface contact alone is not visible clipping. Inspect the rendered exterior and coverage in the failing pose before choosing a repair.

## Decisions, dependencies and maintained records

The product decisions in this contract are settled for implementation planning: separate muscle/fullness, adult-only age appearance, concept-led anatomy, SAM as a scoped foundation, common topology, independent heads/hair, ordinary baked materials, offline-first assembly and no implied gameplay/cognition effects. Remaining human decisions are acceptance of actual sculpts, identity, finish and measured quality/cost tradeoffs. They cannot be closed by documentation. Actual endpoint coordinates, map pixels and passed envelope samples are asset deliverables, not unspecified product behavior. Unsupported children/species and automatic physiological ageing remain outside this family.
