## Approach, risk and decisions

Initial estimate was 500–800 changed logic lines excluding tests; the first delivery grew to about 1,000 source/tooling lines including necessary nearest-surface protection. The source-guided revision adds about 550 lines of offline authoring and actual boot-bound preparation. The durable scope remains one renderer/art revision. Moderate to high art/fit risk; no new authorization, storage or provider boundary. The first figure's fixed 0.025 m cells are preserved; the closer sculpted replacement uses 0.0125 m cells. Actual browser comparison showed that upright geometry still looked smaller than camera-facing sprites at normal pitch; a fixed uniform 1.4 display scale supersedes equal source-height matching. Lower pitch can make him look larger. Native body dimensions remain unchanged. Preserve adult proportions and the original bearded face, dark hair, mail, quilted/leather layers, wraps and rust-red/gold cape. Eyes, brows, nose, mouth/beard and embroidery are explicit colored blocks rather than detail inferred by lowering image resolution. [A3D16/A3D17](../limits/3d-pixel-art.md#a3d17--sculpted-source-guided-mercenary) own exact asset envelopes.

Only exposed cell faces are exported, grouped by rigid moving part with one opaque vertex-color material. No per-cell scene objects, camera-dependent reconstruction, texture sampling, screen-image pixelation or geometry level changes on zoom. Garments occupy solid cells; covered skin is absent. Source-guided segmentation and designed motion limits require actual walking fit checks; subtracting cape overlap in the standing pose alone proved insufficient. The cape has real thickness and authored clearance; bounded rigid limb movement and a rigid cape replace thin simulated fabric for this iteration. Tradeoff: folds do not behave as freely flowing cloth. The earlier simulation is preserved as the comparison asset.

## Performance and completion

Cost grows with rendered characters × exposed faces/rigid parts × lights/shadow/reveal passes. Authoring removes hidden cell faces once; GLB decoding and immutable material/geometry preparation are shared per scene. Actor changes update only a fixed set of part transforms; camera motion never invalidates or rebuilds the model. Entity replacement/disposal invalidates only that actor; scene destruction releases shared resources. Measure asset vertices/triangles/parts, load time and matched browser frame/draw/resource counters through the actual renderer; distinguish software-backend evidence from hardware performance claims.

Completion requires the new visible game model, preserved prior model, readable authored face, invariant blocks under zoom/orbit, representative garment coverage, verified lifecycle/fallback, accurate documentation, full affected-diff review and local commits. ## Revised art direction after explicit rejection

Mike rejected the first authored block figure as ugly and asked for a result much closer to the actual original 3D model. Equal-sized cuboid body sections, square shoulders/jaw, a horizontal red scarf, large eyes and chunky buckles lost the original fitted adult anatomy and draped outfit. Smaller cells alone are insufficient. Preserve this rejected prototype and the original study as distinct comparisons; do not overwrite either asset/source.

The next iteration uses the actual imported mercenary geometry as an offline anatomical/outfit scaffold. Normalize its evaluated standing shape once to about 2.1 m and sculpt fixed 0.0125 m cells around its tapered limbs, sloped shoulders, shaped ribcage/waist, cheeks/jaw, relaxed hands, boots and diagonal cape drape. Source rig weights/landmarks guide the same rigid-part hierarchy. Deliberately repaint coherent skin, hair/beard, mail, quilt, wraps, leather and cape/gold regions, including restrained permanent facial features on the shaped head. The original texture atlas is not a noisy color source or a runtime effect. Garment outer surfaces gain thickness and clearance around the bounded moving clothed body, without a separate bare-skin layer under those clothes. New geometry/source provenance must acknowledge the imported source lineage rather than claiming wholly first-party anatomy.

1. Inspect the original standing pose and body/cape landmarks; record pivots and a bounded, reproducible conversion/retouch method in `scripts/sculpt-voxel-mercenary.py`.
2. Export new `mercenary-sculpted` Blender/GLB/provenance files; preserve both earlier model sets. Inspect actual front/back/profile/face renders against the original before integrating, and correct silhouette/material/face defects rather than accepting a merely smaller-celled box figure.
3. Point the existing fixed-block renderer at the new asset; adapt actual pivot dimensions, source cell size, garment-safe gait and fixed normal-play scale. No camera-dependent rebuilding, detail level, character-image pixelation or gameplay collision change.
4. Recheck fixed geometry/color through orbit/zoom/projection, original-model likeness and adult proportions, visible face, daylight/firelight, actual walking/coverage/support, selection/read-through/fade, fallback, delayed replacement and teardown. Reuse unchanged interaction evidence; rerun visual/fit cases against the actual new source. Update tracker/current contracts/evidence and commit all completed task work locally.

This revised source/tooling work is moderate to high art/fit risk because holes, standing-pose evaluation and rigid segmentation can distort the original shape. Cost still grows with characters × fixed part faces × light/effect passes; geometry preparation, surface merging and palette painting happen offline, shared GLB/material preparation happens once per scene, and only finite part transforms/depth effects recur. No extra service, model provider or paid retry is needed. Existing imported-asset rights and final creator style acceptance retain their separate owners.
