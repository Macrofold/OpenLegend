# Capacity, activity budgets, and sustainable worlds

**Status: product requirements and measurement plan, not a capacity benchmark, price quotation, or infrastructure selection.** [Performance](../performance.md), [encounter scaling](../encounter-scaling.md), [delivery and scale](../../archive/07-technical-architecture/data-delivery-and-scale.md), and [save/load](../save-and-load.md) retain technical ownership.

## 1. What must scale

Distinguish total accounts, community members, simultaneous connected players, spatially active actors, current interactions, active minds, and retained history. One million stored characters is not one million simultaneous believable decisions. A platform may serve many communities while supporting a much smaller unrestricted local encounter.

Infrastructure utilization, social occupancy, and simulation continuity are different problems. Packing quiet worlds onto shared infrastructure can help utilization without making them sociable. Connecting communities can help social activity without eliminating cognition costs. Retaining history need not require continuously running every resident.

Independent worlds serving proportionally more active players are not inherently uneconomical. The dangerous commitments are unbounded fixed background work, unlimited autonomous populations, and permanent expensive activity disconnected from usage or funding.

## 2. Capacity is an interaction envelope

Do not adopt an unexplained universal "1,000 players per region" rule. Use supported workload envelopes incorporating bodies, density, effects, observations, communication, ongoing decisions, persistence, and presentation. The actual thresholds remain unqualified.

A mostly stationary concert, a crowded market, and unrestricted mass combat are distinct workloads. Smaller spatial cells do not reduce work when everyone can affect people across the borders. A district-wide cap also fails to protect a doorway where every admitted person gathers.

Capacity accounting must consider neighboring interaction areas and groups converging into a dense cluster. Existing players can increase load after entry through summoning, machinery, speech, combat, or invention. Therefore user admission and action/capability admission are complementary rather than interchangeable.

## 3. Designed high-capacity activities

A concert can have one canonical stage performance, many listeners, nearby social interaction, and a limited set of foreground speakers. Additional viewing locations can receive an actual broadcast or supported fictional equivalent. These are real different places viewing one event, not copied stages with contradictory outcomes.

Establish venue rules before participation: permitted combat, construction, autonomous population, amplified speech, and other costly effects. Do not suddenly outlaw an already-admitted action or erase its consequences because the audience became larger than expected.

Crowd attention can naturally become more aggregate under the [declared sensory policy](attention-and-scenes.md). This reduces unnecessary linguistic and cognitive work without making people nonexistent. Background coordinated behavior retains individual exceptions and physical effects. It is not a guarantee that unrestricted all-to-all interaction becomes cheap.

A gathering on an open landscape may not have the convenient restrictions of a venue. Be honest about the supported envelope. Prefer a visible congestion boundary or queue to letting entrants reach an unresponsive, inconsistent simulation.

## 4. Admission and player experience

Show known congestion before travel when possible. Reserve capacity for an accepted arrival so teleport preparation does not finish at a destination that has already lost its slot. Shared preparation should serve multiple arrivals without duplicating history or characters.

Capacity policies need fair treatment of residents, groups, reconnecting players, current encounters, and ordinary visitors. Owning a home preserves the home; it is not an unlimited guarantee of immediate entry to any crowded location. A coherent safe arrival or queue option is preferable to spawning inside an overload or an unrelated danger.

Open-field admission boundaries may be visually jarring but remain an accepted last-resort possibility. Explain the capacity restriction rather than inventing a false physical impossibility. Place waits where feasible outside active hazards and preserve routes out. Do not imprison a player merely to maintain a population count.

Slot occupation and attention spam can become denial-of-service gameplay. Define inactive occupancy, queue fairness, return reservations, rate limits, and protections against a group deliberately excluding another. Exact values are tuning decisions. Hidden priority tied to spending or importance can damage trust and needs independent product approval.

## 5. Mechanics require computational admission

An invention must declare bounded affected entities, interaction reach, timing, propagation, spawned agents, and ongoing work through supported capability families. A physically plausible proposal can still be computationally inadmissible.

Bound aggregate world and host work as well as each operation. A thousand individually valid small effects can collectively overload a location. Recursive conversations, summons that summon, constantly changing global senses, and distributed mechanisms with tight feedback require explicit handling.

Do not admit a thousand active creatures and then silently stop simulating half. Reject, defer, reduce an explicitly negotiable proposed scope, or offer a compatible alternative before the unsupported work takes effect. Existing obligations and costly accepted effects still need a defined lifecycle.

World authoring should explain why a proposal is unavailable and what kind of narrower operation is supported. Natural-language creativity remains useful without granting unbounded computation, generated executable code, privacy bypasses, or a larger spending allowance.

## 6. Graceful degradation order

The preferred policy preserves already-promised effects and information:

1. Eliminate redundant computation and reuse valid shared work without changing behavior.
2. Defer optional reflection, narration, analytics, and maintenance that can safely lag.
3. Reduce optional background initiative and use already-declared coarse or crowd policies where the world permits them.
4. Batch compatible work and coalesce replaceable presentation while preserving distinct accepted events and evidence.
5. Stop admitting new participants or effects before the supported envelope is exceeded.
6. Use explicit world-supported slow progression or service-failure behavior when necessary; expose it honestly rather than silently dropping time or outcomes.

These are priorities, not a claim that every stage is currently implemented. An important in-progress fight cannot secretly receive a less capable opponent or ignored damage because the host is overloaded. Technical delays should not become unearned hunger, retrospective action time, or new knowledge.

Time dilation is a secondary policy, not unlimited capacity. Different local progression rates complicate travel, external attacks, and shared commitments. It requires a coherent coupling contract before use, not an ad hoc timer change in a hot process.

## 7. Cost model and funding

Evaluate cost using retained state, active native simulation, network/presentation delivery, required unattended activity, and complete AI workflows. A useful symbolic decomposition is:

`service cost = retained-state cost + active-world cost + background-obligation cost + inference/workspace cost + operational overhead`.

For cognition, record opportunity rate, selected route, context size, generated output, reflection/tool overhead, retries or uncertain charges, and actual provider/accounting evidence. Selection-only execution is not necessarily free. A permanent process or workspace per fictional person is not a semantic necessity, but shared compute does not remove variable inference costs.

Compare costs to consistent usage and revenue denominators: cost per active player-hour, per quiet community-day, per meaningful conversation/scene, and per peak supported encounter. Average concurrency, peak concurrency, paying users, and monthly active users are different populations. Avoid favorable economics produced by comparing one to another.

Use actual quality-qualified model routes and observed activity rates. Do not embed a remembered vendor price or rely on a future price decline. More capable baseline models for important characters can amortize over a community, but an NPC count such as fifty officials per five thousand players is not sufficient evidence: frequency and context still matter.

## 8. Bound the quiet-world liability

The [DG17 continuing-lives proposal](../projects/continuing-lives-feature-spec.md) makes a first isolated operating contract concrete: a finite supported interval, actual resources, authorized funding, required-service readiness, coherent whole-world pause and explicit resumption. The [prepared base-world profile](../worlds/base/continuing-communities.md) supplies qualification values, not a recurring subscription or perpetual-life guarantee. [PS03/PS06](../maintainers/product-scalability.md#ps03--a-bounded-unattended-community) retain delivery; PS-D06 still owns adoption and any broader customer promise.

Persistence should not imply free unlimited autonomous civilization forever. Worlds need a sustainable, explicit allowance for unattended activity. Retained state, ongoing essential commitments, optional new drama, and premium detailed simulation have different cost profiles.

Supported policies may include inexpensive default background continuation, community-funded additional activity, opt-in higher fidelity, or independent/self-hosted worlds under separate operational terms. These are funding possibilities, not a selected commercial price plan. Paying for more detailed simulation must not quietly purchase invulnerability, guaranteed victories, or stronger authority in a shared campaign.

If funds or capacity are unavailable, preserve accepted history and obligations and expose the applicable activity restriction. Do not erase history, assume characters intentionally refuse to speak, or create a burst of deferred optional minds when funding returns. Archival or deletion requires separately approved retention policies and notice; this suite grants no destructive authority.

## 9. Durable consequences versus checkpoints

Checkpoint frequency is not the durability of acknowledged changes. Less frequent full capture, compact records, shared commit work, and delayed derived summaries are distinct from leaving minutes of accepted actions unsaved.

Reducing write frequency only helps if that work is a measured bottleneck. Larger buffers can increase memory use, recovery cost, and loss exposure. Never claim that allowing more rollback necessarily makes the game more stable.

An explicitly isolated casual world might eventually permit a defined rollback window. That is a separate product policy, not the default overload valve for a federated economy. Purchases, trades, exports, access changes, paid-inference accounting, and global campaign contributions cannot escape into durable external state while their source is allowed to forget they happened.

For example, rolling back World A after an export while World B keeps the received object duplicates value. The correct durability/reconciliation contract belongs to the current data owners. This suite requires that player-facing rollback promises name their scope and exclusions; it does not select a new database mode or an arbitrary five-minute window.

## 10. Qualification workloads

| Workload                                                                                   | What it reveals                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One community with many active players versus many communities with one active player each | Fragmentation, fixed background overhead, and social versus infrastructure economics     |
| Clustered versus dispersed players at equal total count                                    | Density, interaction amplification, cold preparation, and cross-area work                |
| Concert versus market versus unrestricted combat                                           | Different capacity envelopes; no universal local player limit                            |
| Quiet unattended community followed by mass return                                         | Accumulated required work, cold history, preparation burst, and no optional-thought debt |
| Repeated promotion/demotion and teleportation                                              | Thrashing, replay exploits, allocation churn, and duplicate history                      |
| Dense crowds with limited focus and real attacks                                           | Cost reduction without dropped physical effects or illicit knowledge                     |
| Budget-limited long relationships                                                          | Whether affordable NPCs remain coherent, responsive, and distinct                        |
| Popular official and coordinated worldwide event                                           | Queue fairness, important-character cost, and broad access to meaningful participation   |

Numbers used to size experiments are workload inputs, not advertised capacities. Measure tails, cost, backlog, correctness, and player-visible quality together. A million synthetic idle entities is not acceptance for a million concurrent players. Native-only performance does not establish full network, model, storage, or client capacity.

## 11. Go/no-go criteria

Do not expand a configuration whose sustainable budget makes NPCs incoherent, whose quiet-world liability grows without bounds, whose capacity policy rewards griefing, or whose transitions change decisive outcomes opportunistically. Narrow the supported world/activity envelope before presenting an unqualified scale promise.

A successful early result is a compelling small community with bounded background cost, truthful transitions, fair admission, and memorable relationships. Broader concurrency then becomes an incremental measured program rather than a product promise funded by hypothetical efficiency.
