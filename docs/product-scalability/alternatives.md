# Alternatives, rejected shortcuts, and reconsideration triggers

**Status: secondary design record. These possibilities are not equal-weight alternatives to the accepted federation, protected-domain, and adaptive-progression direction.** The [suite index](README.md) states the primary principles.

## 1. Why keep this record

The discussion explored several models before converging. Preserve useful options and reasons without making future contributors rediscover rejected assumptions or treating every brainstorm as an accepted requirement. A revisit needs a specific product or measurement trigger, not merely that another model sounds interesting.

## 2. Secondary models

| Model | Potential value | Why it is not the primary answer | Revisit when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Independent named home worlds | Simple initial continuity and strong creator autonomy | Social fragmentation and unattended cost remain; naming timelines is not an economic solution | Early community tests favor self-contained groups, or compatible federation is not yet needed |
| One seamless canonical geography | Coherent travel, economy, and public history | Tight cross-area coupling and concentrated demand are harder; seamlessness is not required for meaningful belonging | Measured gameplay demand justifies seamless travel beyond explicit connections |
| Personal/guild domains with public hubs | Strong ownership and intimate relationships; already part of the preferred hybrid | Hubs alone do not produce a shared evolving civilization, and private autonomy still needs funding | Relationship-centered play dominates and wider shared simulation adds little value |
| Session/chapter progression | Bounded campaign resolution and clear group pacing | Mandatory opening hours conflict with the primary always-available experience | A creator deliberately selects a session-based world; never impose it on ordinary federation play |
| Caravans, fleets, mobile settlements | Community continuity through voluntary physical migration | Does not preserve every relationship and investment in cities left behind; cannot relocate everyone for load balancing | Mobile society itself is compelling authored content |
| Astral/dream social spaces | Shared social encounters across otherwise incompatible domains | Can become a disguised lobby; physical exports and shared history require explicit rules | Players need social contact without merging economy/time policies |
| Authorial scene director | Economical coherent group developments | Different independent-agency promise; secret leakage and human-consent risks require strict scope | A world explicitly chooses stronger narrative authorship and its guarantees are documented |
| Local time dilation | Can slow an overloaded but coherent encounter | Not unlimited capacity; cross-boundary effects, clocks, and commitments complicate fairness | A specific world accepts and tests the temporal coupling policy |
| Explicit isolated rollback tolerance | Potentially cheaper persistence for a casual isolated world | Not safe by default for exports, purchases, trade, or canonical campaign contributions | Measurements justify the tradeoff and rollback scope cannot leak external consequences |
| Noncanonical reenactments of famous battles | Access for late players to historical encounters | Must not fabricate another canonical defeat or undermine unique history | Players value historical play enough to warrant an explicitly separate mode |

## 3. Shortcuts not accepted

Do not merge contradictory timelines as if they were interchangeable saves. Do not clone one memory-bearing companion into unrelated simultaneous parties. Do not wake an entire city at maximum cognition because one player sees its gate. Do not freeze all unseen consequences while calling the world fully autonomous.

Do not replace unsupported mechanics with unconstrained retrospective LLM narration. Do not assume valid JSON makes a joint scene private, fair, or authoritative. Do not generate full transcripts for every ambient person merely to discard them later. Do not treat a high model price as proof of correct personality or rule compliance.

Do not use observer departure or logout to cancel an accepted attack. Do not satisfy background protection by placing someone in a fatal detailed-entry trap. Do not let a private creator's omnipotence bypass another human's privacy or a public economy's rules.

Do not assume a regional player cap handles dense interaction inside that region, or that another machine removes arbitrary all-to-all coupling. Do not reduce acknowledged durability as an unmeasured generic performance fix. Do not call a global villain canonical while quietly awarding every group an independent final death.

## 4. What changed during the discussion

Earlier reasoning favored preserving exact offscreen outcomes wherever possible. The accepted product rule is broader: declared coarse resolution may produce different outcomes while preserving established facts, scopes, permissions, and coherent continuation. Exact integration remains useful where available; it is not the only valid offscreen law.

Earlier clock examples coupled fast dates with proportionally faster mechanics and decision frequency. The desired authored-reality direction permits independent calendar pacing. Existing runtime speed semantics remain unchanged until an explicit feature implements that separation.

Earlier protection examples allowed background danger or an unresolved crisis while prohibiting death. The stronger accepted direction protects specified harms and the state inherited at detailed entry. Existing contested encounters are intentionally excluded from departure-based rescue.

Earlier model routing emphasized cheap tasks regardless of character. The refined direction permits a higher baseline quality tier for heavily scrutinized characters even on brief socially significant interactions, with empirical evaluation rather than an assumption that cost guarantees quality.

Chapter scheduling was considered as a broad world model. It is not the default: scheduled local campaign opportunities coexist with continuous ordinary play. Caravans remain optional world content, not an automatic answer to place-bound continuity.

## 5. Reconsideration process

State which principle or promise would change, the evidence that motivates it, the affected world/engine owners, player-facing consequences, economic implications, and a bounded experiment. Preserve prior useful reasoning. A fallback becoming attractive does not silently authorize changing current saves, authority, privacy, costs, or ongoing encounters.

The [technical design](../projects/product-scalability-tech-design.md) and [tracker](../maintainers/product-scalability.md) own staged work. This document is not an independent research backlog or a second list of implementation tasks.
