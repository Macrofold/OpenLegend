# Repertoire for behavior experiments

Use the observed failure to choose the next comparison. These are hypotheses and reversible controls, not prescriptions for every character or a checklist to exhaust. Keep requested Jev-only, no-generation, no-forced-goal or read-only constraints explicit; diagnostic permission is not permission to silently change final acceptance.

| Suspected missing connection                         | Useful experiment                                                                                                                                  | What it distinguishes                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The situation does not justify the expected choice   | Add or remove an easier alternative, change scarcity, risk, distance or access; inspect actual reachability                                        | A reasonable preference versus a missing decision capability; geometry or unavailable resources versus unwillingness                                 |
| The character does not understand its internal state | Compare numbers alone with authored labels, units, direction, consequences and urgency; test an actual threshold crossing                          | Missing meaning versus missing trigger; discomfort versus a condition that truly prevents action                                                     |
| Background or psychology conflicts with the target   | Compare the full persona with a concise equivalent, then vary relevant knowledge, competence, fear, trust, values or risk tolerance                | Distraction, contradiction or believable reluctance; a personality change is an intervention, not a neutral prompt repair                            |
| A need is recognized but no useful intention follows | Compare no goal, a broad freeform goal, an explicit objective and a diagnostic target-specific goal                                                | Need interpretation versus goal formation versus means selection; keep displayed goals, active goals and plan state consistent                       |
| The causal link is missing                           | Supply a concise truthful relationship between an observed resource, tool, action and consequence; compare explicit tags with natural descriptions | Missing world knowledge or relevance versus inability to execute; declare which relationship was authored rather than inferred                       |
| A useful action is absent or misleading              | Inspect exact options and bindings; temporarily offer the intended action, pre-equip a tool, move into reach or expose prerequisites               | Missing capability discovery, composition or navigation versus selection; a label cannot create a missing mechanic                                   |
| Action purpose or granularity is wrong               | Compare a low-level verb with its supported purpose and effects, or one step with an explicitly supported short sequence                           | Whether the model mistakes preparation for completion or cannot connect steps; describe costs, risks and what remains afterward                      |
| The question asks the wrong thing                    | Compare atomic questions, progress versus overall willingness, feasibility versus preference, independent ratings versus exclusive choice          | Different judgments and competing alternatives; inspect the exact candidate references, criteria, raw answers and selection rule                     |
| Context hides or contradicts useful evidence         | Compare the full request with a bounded minimal version; remove duplication, stale facts or irrelevant prose, then restore realistic context       | Omission, ordering, ambiguity, truncation, conflicting goals or noisy retrieval; never add private facts merely to win a choice                      |
| The provider is suspected of refusing                | Try a minimal task-appropriate positive control, truthful fictional framing when applicable, and a supported alternative                           | Actual refusal versus low rating, uncertainty, transport/decoding failure or unavailable execution; do not infer a blanket restriction from deferral |
| The choice is made but nothing happens               | Force one typed selection through normal admission, inspect rejection/cancellation, and verify the native action separately                        | Decision quality versus stale state, invalid references, missing resources, permission or execution defects                                          |
| Behavior starts but does not continue or recover     | Observe a real miss, completion, rejection or changed resource; inspect recorded outcome, recall, active plan and next opportunity                 | Lost feedback, memory omission, stale plan, cooldown/suppression, queued or in-flight work versus a considered change of mind                        |

For question changes, use the current shared rubrics and actual provider contract. A probability of agreement, confidence, action utility and calibrated correctness are different. Keep raw results and output failures visible; choose a question for the decision needed, not merely because its numbers are larger. A new experimental question is an explicit trial, not an automatic paid retry after failure.

## Removing assistance

A useful diagnostic progression is: prove the native action can work; show a forced typed choice reaches it; remove the forced answer and test real model selection under an explicit goal or simplified scene; then remove that remaining assistance and restore the real scene. Start at the boundary the evidence implicates rather than always performing every stage. Different failing stages imply different fixes.

For example, if “choose this weapon and target” works but the normal scene fails, that establishes no autonomous hunt. Remove the imperative, then the target-specific goal, and compare what the character actually knows about resources, tools and consequences. Supplying an action's food use can be legitimate authored knowledge; supplying “when hungry, attack this entity” has already chosen the policy. Record that distinction instead of calling both emergence.

When several edits are required together, test removing each from the successful combination; one-at-a-time additions can miss interactions. Then test the smallest justified combination through normal callers. Include a meaningful alternative or missing prerequisite, and a fresh snapshot/seed or changed incidental details where the requested reliability claim warrants it. Do not reject sensible alternative behavior merely because it differs from the target.

Retain the exact intervention and all outcomes, including deferrals. Keep exploration in the experiment record; reconcile durable conclusions, accepted content changes and unresolved generalization gaps in their existing owners. Do not turn this repertoire into a new agent framework, permanent target-specific controller or exhaustive benchmark suite.
