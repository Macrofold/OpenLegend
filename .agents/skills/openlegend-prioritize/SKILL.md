# Choose work by the experience it earns

Use this workflow when the user wants a recommendation about what to build next, a roadmap, a next batch of projects, or a comparison/reordering of future work. Interpret the conversational intent: “what’s next?” can request product priorities or merely the next step in an already chosen task. Only the former invokes prioritization. A typo containing those words, an implementation continuation, or writing prompts for an unchanged selected plan does not require this skill.

Honor the user's audience, focus, constraints and chosen priorities. This workflow recommends work; it grants no implementation, spending or publication authority. If the user also authorizes implementation, make the selection within that scope and continue under the root workflow without asking for approval again. Root [task scope](../../../AGENTS.md#task-scope-and-authorization), [boundaries](../../../AGENTS.md#boundaries), [verification](../../rules/verification.md) and [documentation ownership](../../rules/documentation.md) continue to apply. Do not reopen an agreed implementation assignment just because another feature sounds more exciting.

Apply [the developer-question rule](../../../AGENTS.md#resolve-developer-questions-before-dependent-work) before a recommendation or design depends on an unanswered developer choice. Complete independent evidence gathering and comparisons, then ask for the answers needed to finish the selection. Present options to help the developer decide; do not select an assumed answer or turn the uncertainty into a design-only task to bypass the question.

## Principles for choosing

1. **Begin with an experience somebody would value.** Name who benefits and what becomes worth doing, watching or creating: discovering a possibility, inventing something useful, getting to know a person, making a place, overcoming an enemy, gaining power, or enjoying the world. Then identify the behavior and mechanics it needs. A detailed schedule, realistic resource loop, flexible engine or new action does not justify itself. Include players who enjoy observing independent characters and creators who want to make a playable reality, not only direct character control.

2. **Reduce breadth while preserving the selected experience's promise.** Keep the relevant part of OpenLegend's premise real: useful invention, independent characters, persistent consequences and player initiative. One interesting resident and an actually invented useful object may suffice. Do not replace promised live invention or free choice with a hidden canned demonstration to claim completion. Each increment need not exercise every promise at once. A deliberately authored world or a reliable known recipe can be appropriate; it is not evidence of invention or independent choice.

3. **Deliver a complete worthwhile activity before expanding supporting systems.** Describe how someone enters it, makes a meaningful choice, reaches a result and experiences that result. Find the smallest end-to-end version with a payoff. Separate genuine dependencies from convenient implementation order: ordinary conversation need not wait for crowds, useful follow-through need not wait for unattended communities, and competence need not wait for generalized dice. Preserve needed integrity and engine/world seams without making a speculative framework a prerequisite. Prior document order, checklist position, sunk effort and proximity to recently edited code are not independent reasons for priority.

4. **Make complexity earn its cost in play.** Ask what a proposed mechanic adds: expression, strategy, discovery, mastery, tension, surprise or consequences. Compare it with a simpler version or omission. Detail that mainly adds bookkeeping, repeated instructions, waiting or recovery is a burden, not automatic depth. Conversely, keep a difficult constraint when working around it is part of the desired experience. A repertoire entry or an implementation gap identifies an option, not an obligation to fill it.

5. **Identify attraction before adding pressure to force participation.** Explain what the activity enables, expresses or improves. Making fire unreliable does not by itself establish that building shelter is compelling. Maintenance, scarcity, injury and loss can earn their place through valued stakes and choices; they are neither default priorities nor forbidden mechanics. Challenge, danger, conflict and hard-won relief are valid attractions alongside beauty, companionship and quiet observation. Do not manufacture chores solely to make another feature necessary.

6. **Respect choice, ordinary competence and different kinds of enjoyment.** Players may enter a prepared world, skip construction, prefer a dependable method or value a relationship over numerical progression. Characters retain their own goals and knowledge; a daily routine prescribed by code is not evidence of independent life. Ordinary supported actions should generally be competent, and learned expertise may become reliable; randomness and equal use of every option are not goals in themselves. These are design choices for the intended experience and authored world, not new universal engine laws or permission to weaken validation.

7. **Judge the whole benefit and the full burden.** Count implementation and integration, player setup/explanation/reading, latency, repeated model calls, retries, recovery and ongoing operation. Population, generated history and background simulation need a concrete additional benefit before their costs become permanent. Reliability, accessibility, privacy, save integrity, honest spending and necessary foundations protect the experience: confirmed serious failures can outrank new gameplay. Explain their actual consequence or named consumer; neither “infrastructure first” nor “only visible features matter” is sufficient. Address minimum necessary enabling work without making a tiny workload look like proof of scale.

8. **Treat the recommendation as a hypothesis.** Research, design completeness and correct calculations do not prove enjoyment. Identify the uncertainty that could change the choice and the smallest relevant evidence: existing player observations, a comparison, a walkthrough or an authorized focused play session. Reuse valid evidence under the verification policy; do not create mandatory new tests, analytics or a playtest bureaucracy. Reconsider when people cannot find a wanted activity, need repeated rescue, receive too little benefit for the added burden, or wait too long for the payoff. Recommend simplifying, deferring, omitting or revising an existing proposal when warranted; do not silently rewrite accepted requirements or erase useful work.

## Check repertoire priorities when selecting gameplay

For gameplay selection or an open-ended next-work recommendation that includes gameplay, read the current [priority policy](../../../docs/repertoires/gameplay-priorities.md) and [whole-game coverage](../../../docs/repertoires/coverage.md), then inspect the highest-priority relevant entries in the [canonical catalogues](../../../docs/repertoires/README.md#catalogue-map). Current priorities live beside each entry; use those maintained values and coverage rankings, not historical ranking snapshots or entry-ID order. Load relevant sections, not the entire library, and honor a user-restricted focus.

Identify the highest-priority unfinished experiences and entries. Check their actual remaining scope against maintainer trackers, implemented behavior, verification, active branches and dependencies before recommending work; a catalogue proposal is not evidence that its capability is missing. Cite the selected entry IDs/priorities and remaining gap, and explain why any relevant higher-ranked unfinished alternative is deferred. Rankings guide the choice alongside player value, urgent defects, prerequisites and the user's constraints; they neither override those considerations nor establish a quota of repertoire assignments.

## Plan around unfinished prerequisites

Valuable future work may depend on previous tasks or implementation that is still unfinished or not confirmed fully merged into `main`. That does not disqualify it from a recommendation or a new batch. Distinguish priority from readiness; do not replace important work with lower-value tasks merely to make every assignment immediately runnable.

For every suggested batch, explicitly list **open prerequisites** for each affected assignment. Identify the specific required deliverable and task ID/design/tracker, its current completion and merge status, the supporting branch/ref or evidence when known, and what it blocks: starting, a particular part of implementation, integration or final acceptance. State what can proceed independently. A dependency on an entire earlier batch is insufficient when only one of its deliverables is needed.

Treat the prerequisite as open unless the required scope is confirmed complete and fully merged into `main`, using the repository's meaning of that ref. Work completed on another branch, partially merged work and uncertain completion/merge status remain explicitly open; distinguish these states instead of guessing. An explicitly supplied alternative development base may make work available there, but does not establish its merge into `main`. Recheck actual required behavior/evidence before calling the dependency satisfied; a branch name or completed-plan label alone is not proof.

Carry each open prerequisite and the independent-work boundary into the affected copyable prompt. Pending implementation or merge can have a complete conditional assignment; an unanswered developer decision still follows [the root question rule](../../../AGENTS.md#resolve-developer-questions-before-dependent-work). Do not use this permission to invent a dependent design answer, duplicate the prerequisite's implementation or grant workers merge authority.

When producing copyable implementation/task prompts, include the [specific new-branch instruction](../create-parallel-tasks/references/task-prompts.md#name-a-new-branch-in-each-prompt) in every prompt. This specifies the future worker's branch; writing a recommendation does not create it.

## Apply the principles proportionally

- Establish the requested scope and current state. Reuse adequate supplied or already-loaded evidence; investigate only decision-relevant gaps or potentially stale claims through the [maintainer index](../../../docs/maintainers/README.md), owning plans, implementation and existing evidence. Distinguish proposals, owner-approved requirements, delivered behavior and verified outcomes. When a gameplay comparison needs broader direction not already in context, consult relevant sections of the [repertoire design foundation](../../../docs/repertoires/design-foundation.md) or [selection guidance](../../../docs/repertoires/selection-and-scale.md). Do not preload both documents or the whole catalogue for a narrow grounded question; the catalogue is not an implementation queue.
- Compare a small credible set of alternatives, including important defects or enabling work where relevant. For open-ended gameplay selection, include different possible sources of value rather than considering only additions adjacent to the last implementation. For a user-restricted request, compare within that focus; do not force combat, domestic play, invention or any other category into every batch.
- For each serious recommendation explain **the experience or failure addressed, why it matters, the smallest complete delivery, the main burden/tradeoff, why now and what evidence would change the recommendation**. A short answer can cover these in connected prose; a larger plan can use a compact comparison. Distinguish an existing accepted priority from your recommendation to change it, and cite the actual owning plan. Accepted sequencing deserves explicit reconciliation, not blind obedience to document order or silent disregard.
- Separate the desired result from indispensable enabling work and optional expansion. Explain why the selected work wins over the strongest alternative. Use qualitative reasoning supported by evidence, not invented numerical scores, fixed category quotas or a rigid ranking formula. Ease of implementation helps choose among valuable deliveries; it cannot supply the missing value.
- Match the requested deliverable. A question can receive a concise recommendation without creating documents or tasks. When plans, task definitions or implementation are requested, use the existing design/documentation and applicable assignment workflows. Keep their established authorization, worker independence, verification and completion rules; this skill adds no parallel process. Record changed accepted priorities in their owners when authorized, and state material unknowns rather than presenting preference as proof.
