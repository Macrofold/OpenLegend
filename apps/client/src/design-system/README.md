# Open Legend UI system

## Whole-interface hierarchy

The world remains the main view. The upper-left character summary names the controlled character and current location, shows the installed world's condition attributes and participation state, and opens Character at the requested condition. A separate collapsible Current work panel sits beneath it, behind other panels, without moving the centered launcher rails or resizing open workspaces. Its body scrolls within the available space. Current work is shown from the server's `hasWork` projection, including queued or paused work; a retained completed activity alone does not create a current-work strip. Save failures remain visible with the permitted recovery route.

The left launcher rail contains **Inventory**, **Character**, **Events** and **Character records**; **Speak** opens a compact text bar rather than a transcript window. The right rail contains **In view**, **Craft**, **Build**, **Create** and **Speak**. The labeled **Game** entry provides return to play, live-save status, **Checkpoints** when permitted, **Settings & help**, **Intelligence & allowance**, creation, authorized diagnostics, the separate **World operations** page when permitted, and Log Out. Create contains ordinary **Invent / Recipes / Requests** navigation. **World editor** and **Inspect world** are separate authorized Game entries; God mode does not replace the ordinary Create surface. Moving an entry does not grant its capability.

Events uses a wider reading panel with category filters, search, an on-demand game-time selector and an explicit expand control. It combines permitted speech, actions and observations; it never becomes an omniscient world log. Character records retains personal story, promises and known places separately from external occurrences and has its own launcher. Character groups traits with hover/focus descriptions; its condition and capability sections do not repeat an available-action catalogue. Navigation to primary destinations uses their launchers rather than unrelated cross-links. Character's condition, capabilities and equipped content still comes from native knowledge; there is no universal body-slot, progression, map or turn-budget model. [World-first presentation](../../../../docs/projects/player-experience-overhaul-feature-spec.md) and the [coverage map](../../../../docs/ui-ux/interface-coverage.md) distinguish delivered surfaces from remaining acceptance.

Settings & help is a wide retained workspace with **Appearance & reading**, **Camera & graphics**, **Controls & help**, and authorized **World permissions** tabs. Inactive tabs keep their local selection and drafts while their content is hidden from interaction. Device preferences explain that they apply immediately; world permissions keep their separate native authority and save behavior. The rendering-rate display is optional and off by default.

## Contextual actions and retained tasks

Routine native actions stay with the relevant object, belonging, recipe or shortcut; the complete known-action browser stays in Settings & help. **Describe an action** opens a focused optional task with the actor, exact named subject, original words and explicit commitment. It does not expose target/item/tool/recipient/amount fields as a default worksheet. Choosing a subject temporarily hides the open panels to expose the scene; a world click or the keyboard-accessible **In view** list binds that exact perceived subject without sending. Cancel returns to the prior task and subject. Losing sight of a selected subject retains its name and explains why it must be changed or removed.

The action task keeps its primary action and Cancel beside the possible interpretation-allowance notice, while its longer fields scroll. Scheduling choices appear only when actual work exists and distinguish queueing, pausing to resume, and replacement. **Stop all work** follows native cancellation availability and explains that paused work is also discarded. Cancel leaves the words available; it does not claim to stop an already submitted request.

Keep private task identity separate from fresh permission to read or act. The server's private-draft scope, world, timeline and character identify retained drafts and supported task selections through an allowed same-owner Resume. Current access/control scope still fences requests and asynchronous replies; readers use it to refresh permitted data and reject old completions. A changed private owner/session/grant, character or timeline invalidates private work. Hiding a panel, changing a reading tab or regaining the same permitted character is not a new owner. Never read earlier unscoped drafts as a compatibility path. [The Resume contract](../../../../docs/projects/completed/tab-resume-feature-spec.md) and [current architecture](../../../../docs/architecture.md) own the authority and persistence details.

## World presentation ownership

[World presentation](../../../../docs/world-presentation.md) owns the accepted continuous right-drag orbit/pitch, Shift + right-drag or middle-drag pan, mixed sprite/mesh depth, default illumination, projected shadows and configurable local character-visible read-through. These are presentation settings, not extra bodily perception. Follow that owner where older camera-binding or unlit-art directions conflict; SW18 retains visual qualification and further art work.

## Camera and spatial interaction

The [spatial owner](../../../../docs/spatial-world.md#picking-and-controls) defines meanings rather than another camera schema here. The compact camera toolbar keeps zoom, recenter, follow and range toggles visible. **Camera settings** expands rotation, tilt, projection, rotation lock and shortcut help, with keyboard access and Escape returning focus to its toggle; Shift + right-drag or middle-drag pans, right-drag orbits/tilts, wheel zooms, and focused-canvas arrows rotate/tilt, Shift + arrows pan and Home recenters. Floor selectors and Page Up/Page Down floor shortcuts are removed. Held Option (Alt) reveals already perceived subjects through scenery according to the [presentation owner](../../../../docs/world-presentation.md#local-read-through); it never expands character knowledge. P pauses/resumes the world; Shift + ] and Shift + [ step faster/slower through the same 0.5×, 1×, 3× and 8× choices as the time buttons. Projection switches through its camera button. Time shortcuts ignore held-key repetition, typing, composition, popups and disconnected states; pause/resume remains blocked during maintenance; a speed change never resumes a paused world. Pointer capture ends without action after a drag. The small renderer interface is plain TypeScript; PlayCanvas types stay in scene/art implementation. Multi-touch pinch and assistive-device qualification remain SW09/SW16 work.

Independent **Sight** and **Hearing** toggles remain in the common-controls row, with eye/ear icons, pressed states, hover/focus names and solid/dashed theme accents. Thin connected outlines and very faint area tints show the displayed reach; hover labels use short sight/speech wording. [Range guides](../../../../docs/spatial-world.md#perception-range-guides) own meaning and display approximations. The camera toolbar and speech-caption layout share the existing measured HUD exclusion area; desktop right docks reserve the stack, while open narrow sheets expose only the two range controls in a separate bottom strip. Tight sheets use the remaining viewport reading area. The app root clips overflow without programmatic scrolling when a descendant receives focus; panel/text scrollers retain their own scrolling. The scene's range hint is below world labels/HUD, pointer-transparent and subordinate to object picking.

Closed narrow views place camera controls and quick actions in one bounded lower region. The region scrolls vertically when necessary, and the shortcut row scrolls horizontally with room for visible keyboard focus. The separate horizontal launcher strip uses inset focus outlines so its edges cannot clip the shared ring. Shortcut configuration and exact-source review stay in the lower region; their child lists and shared tooltips use the existing portals. Subject picking collapses hidden quick controls, and caption avoidance measures the visible region while omitting hidden HUD elements. Desktop positioning and the open-sheet range strip retain their existing owners. [HC01](../../../../docs/limits/interface.md#hc01--narrow-hud-controls) records the bounds and observed scope.

This is the production port of the supplied September 20 design system. React owns the HUD and world-status markup. React Aria provides buttons, toolbars, radio groups, workspace tabs and delayed tooltips. PlayCanvas still renders the world and supplies screen coordinates; it never authorizes actions.

First scene startup yields between graphics allocation and applying the first permitted world view so the browser can handle HUD input between expensive stages. A fresh blocked tab waits for explicit Resume before allocating graphics. Existing scenes keep their suspended updates and private-scope resets. The scene refuses canvas commands before it has a view, while In view remains the readable interaction route; an inspected selection made there is reflected when graphics become available. [RF02](../../../../docs/limits/interface.md#rf02--first-scene-scheduling) records the measured startup tradeoff and remaining integration scope.

When enabled in **Settings & help → Camera & graphics**, the bottom-left FPS counter counts actual PlayCanvas render passes against monotonic real elapsed time, sampling about once per second. It is a local preference, off by default. Foreground stalls lower the measured rate; hidden-tab time is discarded and return starts a fresh sample. It measures application rendering throughput, not GPU execution time or a guarantee of monitor presentation. The small React counter updates independently of the full HUD and does not intercept world input.

Use semantic token names, a 4px spacing scale, the supplied display/UI/utility type roles, and accessible names on every icon button. Unknown content receives an initial-letter sigil. Do not use emoji or imply that generated artwork is being requested when no art job exists. Keep one primary gold action per panel; focus uses sage in action search. Game-icons.net art is CC BY 3.0, Lucide ISC, and fonts OFL; first-party code remains AGPL-3.0-only.

World/location/time labels, item/entity/action icons and grouped-shortcut labels come from installed world or native capability presentation. Resolve a supplied semantic icon through the trusted local catalogue; unknown keys keep a readable fallback. A client subtype name cannot invent a gather capability, preparation verb or wilderness label. Theme selection changes presentation, not the installed world or its laws.

Use `TextTooltip` for short hover/focus labels and `Explanation` for richer action details and facts. Both use `PointerTooltip`: an opaque, padded surface whose lower left or right corner anchors near the pointer, extending toward the side with more room. Flip below when necessary and clamp to the visible viewport; keyboard focus uses the focused control as its anchor. Portals escape clipping and remain above their originating modal. Placement work is coalesced only while a tooltip is open; scrolling, resizing and content changes update it without a game-state subscription. Supplementary help stays reachable on focus and while hovering its text. This replaces the earlier transparent, unpadded tooltip decision (October 16, 2026).

## Accepted adaptations

The attached reference instructions are design input. The user's explicit overrides govern this port:

- Work above characters is a continuous white bar, interpolated against the server's simulation rate and frozen while paused. It disappears on completion/cancellation. Keep at most three transient notices, separate from active work.
- Quick circles separate contextual **Suggestions** from three persisted **Shortcuts · 1–3**. Assignments remain stable when unavailable and expose a reachable reason instead of opening reassignment. Suggestions retain still-offered identities while hovered or focused, but replace a retired action immediately, such as Sleep becoming Wake up. A grouped shortcut reviews the exact resolved source before committing and refuses a changed source instead of substituting another. In the quick-action bar, rings are reserved for actual action cooldowns. Current actions have no cooldown contract, so no rings are shown there; never feed work progress into them.
- Pausing does not change clock width. Create, Events and Settings & help use wider task/reading space with reachable close controls. Events can expand without changing its record scope; Speak remains a compact text bar.
- Search retains “Search actions or invent something…” and an inline invention affordance for unmatched text. Enter opens an editable invention draft; only explicit Send dispatches. There is no separate invention row below the search.
- Look closer uses current server-projected descriptions. Hover facts use actual native work durations and material yields/costs. Installed world/native previews own those values; shared UI code must not restate bundled-world timing or cost rules.
- God actions always carry a visible **God mode** label. Ground **Add something** opens the reusable right-hand `Pullout` by hover, click or keyboard, then the Items, Actors and Environment categories. Leaf catalogues are searchable and show eight rows before scrolling. [Base-world items](../../../../docs/worlds/base/items.md) owns item creation, pile menus and drop quantities; player inventory uses the same installed-item catalogue as other-character editing. Person creation is a React Aria modal; trait search exposes descriptions and selected traits become removable tags. Character panels group applicable owner controls under **God mode**: **Revive**, **Grant cognition and speech**, **Edit Person** and **Inspect private mind**. Panel and context-menu revival/cognition visibility share one predicate; mutations use the same server handlers. Edit Person opens on Character with current statuses and applicable meters using authored labels, units, ranges and edit permissions. Fill meters stages only their applicable maxima. Inventory has searchable item-type selectors, whole-number quantities and add/remove controls. Identity contains name, description, personality, backstory, traits and goals. Edits across tabs remain drafts until Save. Narrow editor section controls retain visible icons and accessible names when their text labels collapse.
- Traits are saved domain data, sampled three without replacement from `packages/domain/src/worlds/base/config/traits.json`. They are descriptive starting dispositions, not earned bonuses. The character sheet shows the player's scoped memories and observed history; private NPC cognition stays behind god authorization.
- Speech and owner-chat inputs begin at one line and grow with wrapping or explicit newlines. Speak keeps request recovery beside its input; Events contains only actual perceived occurrences. Owner chat retains its own pending/error presentation. Provider stages and queue text never become in-world utterances.

Right-dragging rotates and tilts; Shift + right-dragging or middle-dragging pans. Primary dragging does not move the camera or act on release. A stationary secondary click opens actions, and a map click that dismisses a picker never walks. Keyboard shortcuts do not intercept text fields. Panels use opening order and collapse older panels when space is insufficient; narrow layouts share one sheet. The shared width-expansion button is hidden on narrow screens, where both widths have the same geometry; Close remains available and the desktop workspace preference is retained. Events uses an expanded reading sheet when needed; Speak remains a compact separate input with reachable volume and send controls. Other sheets at enlarged UI scales leave room for the launcher row and open-panel tabs; expanded sheets retain their close control and panel tabs. Theme/scale/reduced-motion preferences stay local and never alter game semantics.

Action menus show up to five contextual shortcuts, then direct rows for categories containing one action and counted right-opening submenus for larger categories, opening by hover, click or Right; Left or Escape returns to the category row. A singleton already present among the quick choices is not repeated. Submenus flip left at the screen edge and overlap their parent only when neither side fits. Search shows matching action rows directly across the complete permitted result. Larger categories and search results start with at most 40 choices; Scrolling within 80px of the end reveals another 40 choices without a focus jump; **Show more choices** remains a keyboard route and focuses the first added command. Valid refresh retains the reading window. Pickup uses the same commitment rows, with Take all first. Creation-menu groups use the same one/two-choice inline treatment and retain visible God mode labels. [Ordinary action discovery](../../../../docs/action-experience.md#ordinary-player-action-discovery) and [AP01](../../../../docs/limits/interface.md#ap01--ordinary-action-discovery) own the detailed behavior.

## Shared scrolling

The device's UI scale also applies to portaled dialogs, menus and explanations. The App publishes the setting on the document so those surfaces share it with the HUD. Modal content scales inside an unscaled viewport scrim; `.ol-portal-content` scales popup reading and controls inside React Aria's unscaled positioning boundary. Keep physical anchor measurements and viewport bounds in that outer coordinate system, and divide bounds used inside scaled content by the scale. Nested HUD editors already inherit HUD enlargement and must not scale a second time. Browser zoom remains a separate setting and qualification.

The base rules in `components.css` own scrollbar appearance for every `.ol-root` surface and its descendants, including portaled menus, lists, panels, editors and text areas. Scrollbars use a thin muted thumb and transparent track, with rounded thumbs where the browser supports styling. Containers own their dimensions and overflow; they do not repeat scrollbar appearance rules or need an extra React wrapper. Native wheel, keyboard, touch and thumb dragging remain available. Intentional hidden-scrollbar tab strips retain their local rule, and forced-color mode uses system scrollbar colors.
