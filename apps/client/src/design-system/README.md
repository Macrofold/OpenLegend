# Open Legend UI system

**Gildmoss** is integrated as a runtime theme beside Wilderness, Fantasy and Sci-fi. [`gildmoss/`](gildmoss/README.md) records its accepted visual specification; the shared tokens and components own runtime styling. Running-game qualification is still in progress in the [Gildmoss verification report](../../../../docs/verification/gildmoss-interface.md), so implementation here does not imply that every screen or viewport has passed.

## Whole-interface hierarchy

The world remains the main view. The shared shell follows the [accepted Gildmoss layout](../../../../docs/ui-design-brief.md): the character plate, perspective lock, minimap and daylight dial, hotbar, compact Speak/Act bars, narrator and Events quick view. Theme selection changes materials and typography, not the shell's destinations, permissions or the installed world's rules. The plate's frame remains swappable for Mike's corner artwork. Native work progress, interruptions, recovery and save failures keep their permitted presentation; there is no player work card.

The main workspace groups **Character · Skills · Journal · Map · Crafting**. Inventory belongs to Character; Invention belongs to Crafting. Journal composes the existing permitted readers, including Events and Story, without changing their evidence or private scope. In view and independent person/object inspectors remain reachable. The game menu retains live-save status, permitted Checkpoints, Settings & help, Intelligence & allowance, creation, authorized diagnostics, World operations and Log Out. World editor and Inspect world keep their separate capabilities. Moving an entry never grants its capability. The [coverage map](../../../../docs/ui-ux/interface-coverage.md) owns the surface inventory; the brief owns exact layout and keys.

Character's conditions, attributes and equipment places come from the installed world and native knowledge. The interface does not invent extra needs, body slots, progression or private facts about another person. The [world-first presentation contract](../../../../docs/projects/player-experience-overhaul-feature-spec.md) and the Gildmoss verification report distinguish implemented presentation from remaining acceptance.

Settings & help is a wide retained workspace with **Appearance & reading**, **Camera & graphics**, **Controls & help**, and authorized **World permissions** tabs. Inactive tabs keep their local selection and drafts while their content is hidden from interaction. Device preferences explain that they apply immediately; world permissions keep their separate native authority and save behavior. The rendering-rate display is optional and off by default.

## Contextual actions and retained tasks

Routine native actions stay with the relevant object, belonging, recipe or shortcut; the complete known-action browser stays in Settings & help. **Describe an action** opens a focused optional task with the actor, exact named subject, original words and explicit commitment. It does not expose target/item/tool/recipient/amount fields as a default worksheet. Choosing a subject temporarily hides the open panels to expose the scene; a world click or the keyboard-accessible **In view** list binds that exact perceived subject without sending. Cancel returns to the prior task and subject. Losing sight of a selected subject retains its name and explains why it must be changed or removed.

The action task keeps its primary action and Cancel beside the possible interpretation-allowance notice, while its longer fields scroll. Scheduling choices appear only when actual work exists and distinguish queueing, pausing to resume, and replacement. **Stop all work** follows native cancellation availability and explains that paused work is also discarded. Cancel leaves the words available; it does not claim to stop an already submitted request.

Inventory keeps one controller and one ordinary inventory command/receipt owner while [`BagWindow`](../ui/bag-window.tsx) supplies independent draggable reading shells. Each explicitly opened bag has one deduplicated, permitted collection reader with its own search, sort, filter and pagination; the character’s own collection is shared. Hiding Character keeps that controller mounted. Desktop bags raise and close independently; narrow layouts expose the active bag through a sheet with a window switcher and a route back to belongings. The active bag supplies the quick-move destination, while explicit item actions and cross-window drops preserve exact endpoints and quantities. Lost original-root access withdraws the contents until an explicit permitted reopen. The [Inventory contract](../../../../docs/ui-ux/inventory.md#open-the-object-and-keep-both-inventories-visible) owns transfer, scope and interaction details. Actual pointer, drag, resize-retention, focus-return and ground-container reach-withdrawal checks passed; the [Gildmoss report](../../../../docs/verification/gildmoss-interface.md#actual-running-game-observations) records that scope separately from broader permission variants and matrix qualification.

Keep private task identity separate from fresh permission to read or act. The server's private-draft scope, world, timeline and character identify retained drafts and supported task selections through an allowed same-owner Resume. Current access/control scope still fences requests and asynchronous replies; readers use it to refresh permitted data and reject old completions. A changed private owner/session/grant, character or timeline invalidates private work. Hiding a panel, changing a reading tab or regaining the same permitted character is not a new owner. Never read earlier unscoped drafts as a compatibility path. [The Resume contract](../../../../docs/projects/completed/tab-resume-feature-spec.md) and [current architecture](../../../../docs/architecture.md) own the authority and persistence details.

## Camera and spatial interaction

The [spatial owner](../../../../docs/spatial-world.md#picking-and-controls) defines controls rather than another camera schema here. Main-camera settings, including Sight/Hearing range toggles, projection, follow and rotation lock, live under **Settings & help → Camera & graphics**; the HUD keeps the accepted perspective-lock toggle and minimap-only zoom. Right-drag orbits/tilts, Shift + right-drag or middle-drag pans, and the wheel zooms. Focused-canvas keys retain their native bindings; text editing, composition and child popups take precedence. Speed controls remain God-mode debugging tools. Pointer capture ends without action after a drag. Floor selectors and floor shortcuts remain removed. Held Option (Alt) reveals only already perceived subjects through scenery under the [presentation owner](../../../../docs/world-presentation.md#local-read-through), never additional knowledge.

Sight and Hearing remain independent, named pressed-state controls with theme accents. [Range guides](../../../../docs/spatial-world.md#perception-range-guides) own their meaning and display approximations. The existing HUD geometry observer publishes visible reading-surface exclusions to speech captions and world labels, including portaled bag panels inside the play root. Hidden surfaces reserve no space. Bounded scrollers retain wheel, keyboard and focus access; the app root clips overflow without scrolling the entire interface when a descendant receives focus. [HC01](../../../../docs/limits/interface.md#hc01--narrow-hud-controls) and the Gildmoss verification report retain qualification limits. Multi-touch and assistive-device qualification remain SW09/SW16 work.

React owns the HUD, reading surfaces and world-status markup. React Aria supplies shared controls, tabs, overlays and delayed tooltips. PlayCanvas renders the world and supplies screen coordinates; neither presentation layer authorizes actions, and PlayCanvas types stay in scene/art implementation.

First scene startup yields between graphics allocation and applying the first permitted world view so the browser can handle HUD input between expensive stages. A fresh blocked tab waits for explicit Resume before allocating graphics. Existing scenes keep their suspended updates and private-scope resets. The scene refuses canvas commands before it has a view, while In view remains the readable interaction route; an inspected selection made there is reflected when graphics become available. [RF02](../../../../docs/limits/interface.md#rf02--first-scene-scheduling) records the measured startup tradeoff and remaining integration scope.

When enabled in **Settings & help → Camera & graphics**, the bottom-left FPS counter counts actual PlayCanvas render passes against monotonic real elapsed time, sampling about once per second. It is a local preference, off by default. Foreground stalls lower the measured rate; hidden-tab time is discarded and return starts a fresh sample. It measures application rendering throughput, not GPU execution time or a guarantee of monitor presentation. The small React counter updates independently of the full HUD and does not intercept world input.

## Shared scrolling

The device's UI scale defaults to **Auto**. Its existing preference owner also retains explicit 90%, 100%, 115% and 130% choices. Auto uses the smaller of the available CSS viewport's width/1920 and height/1080, clamped to 90–100%: 1920×1080 uses the authored size; smaller windows reduce the interface only to the existing 90% minimum, then use compact layouts and scrolling. Larger windows give room back to the world. Fixed choices bypass that calculation. [`ui-scale.ts`](ui-scale.ts) owns the values; [UXL14](../../../../docs/limits/ui-ux.md#uxl14) records the tuning and tradeoff. Window resizing derives the effective size without replacing the preference or remounting the task. Display pixel density is not a scaling input.

The effective size also applies to portaled dialogs, menus and explanations. The App publishes it on the document so those surfaces share it with the HUD. Modal content scales inside an unscaled viewport scrim; `.ol-portal-content` scales popup reading and controls inside React Aria's unscaled positioning boundary. Keep physical anchor measurements and viewport bounds in that outer coordinate system, and divide bounds used inside scaled content by the scale. Nested HUD editors already inherit HUD enlargement and must not scale a second time. Browser zoom remains separate: Auto can offset up to 10% as its CSS viewport changes, while a fixed size retains the full explicit enlargement. These size bounds are tuning, not an assistive-technology or readability qualification.

The base rules in `components.css` own scrollbar appearance for every `.ol-root` surface and its descendants, including portaled menus, lists, panels, editors and text areas. Scrollbars use a thin muted thumb and transparent track, with rounded thumbs where the browser supports styling. Containers own their dimensions and overflow; they do not repeat scrollbar appearance rules or need an extra React wrapper. Native wheel, keyboard, touch and thumb dragging remain available. Intentional hidden-scrollbar tab strips retain their local rule, and forced-color mode uses system scrollbar colors.

## Theme source and generation

The shared [`tokens/tokens.json`](tokens/tokens.json) owns the Wilderness, Fantasy, Sci-fi and Gildmoss palettes and semantic roles. [`themes.ts`](themes.ts) exposes that same inventory to Settings and characterless operations. Run `pnpm tokens:generate` after an authored token change; `pnpm tokens:check` verifies the generated stylesheet. Components consume roles rather than palette literals. Gildmoss lighting is selected with `data-lighting="night"` from the installed world, independently of the chosen theme. Its archived design tokens remain a reference artifact, while the shared token source owns runtime values.

Entry waits up to five seconds for the four Gildmoss font faces. When any face fails or remains unavailable, `data-interface-fonts="fallback"` selects the shared display/UI fallback roles for that document entry, including nested HUD and portaled surfaces. A later download does not change its typography; reloading tries the published faces again.
