# Art direction after round two — detail before technique

Reviewed September 19, 2026. Source: [saved round-two feedback](feedback-round-02.json), saved at 23:00:19 UTC; [reviewed snapshot](round-02-feedback-reviewed.json). All 20 references received feedback. The file was saved in the parent art-direction folder, not inside round-02; it has been read in place and preserved.

## What became clearer

**REPLACED remains the favorite; Tails Noir remains a strong second anchor.** You again liked every selected visual aspect of both. VirtuaVerse joins them as a strong positive for environmental design, color composition and detailed clothing. NORCO supplies a positive natural-environment/animal reference with a reservation about gloom. Tower 57 supplies a positive environment reference and an important reconsideration of character scale.

Your minimum is meaningful detail, not merely a higher-resolution image. You rejected arcade-like figures even where the pixel density was good (HUNTDOWN, The Last Faith and others). Whispers of a Machine supplied a particularly useful face/clothing example: the seated figure's overalls, hair and recognizable face appeal even though its broader style remains too cartoon-like. You liked ANNO: Mutationem's character detail while finding its environment visually inconsistent/uncanny. These are separate ratings of people and surroundings, not blanket endorsements.

Darkness is not synonymous with the atmosphere you want. Unavowed and NORCO earned environmental praise but mood reservations. Jubilee's landscapes appealed, with a request for still more density. Preserve daylight, natural color and life alongside strong cinematic lighting.

## Character detail at two scales

In R15 / Tower 57 you explicitly considered allowing a less detailed world avatar if the character profile supplies more face/body detail. Your remaining requirement is that clothing, gear and the results of what a character has done are readable in the world. This softens the earlier inference that every facial feature must be visible at the normal simulation camera. It does not eliminate the need for distinct people.

Proposed comparison: judge a world avatar and a profile portrait together. Record whether silhouette, clothing, equipment and skin/hair identity survive at normal play scale; judge expressive facial detail separately in the close view. No exact pixel dimensions or asset pipeline are decided yet.

## The Hades correction

You explicitly added **Hades and Hades II as games whose art you really like**. They should have been included earlier as adjacent visual references, even though they are not conventional pixel-art games. The earlier curation weighted the literal pixel-art category too strongly. The publisher describes Hades' environments as hand-painted; Hades II also belongs in the illustrated comparison set. [Hades publisher/store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1145360/Hades/) · [Hades II official page](https://www.supergiantgames.com/games/hades-ii/).

**Working inference:** the stronger requirement may be finely crafted, dimensional, legible art rather than a particular pixel technique. This remains a question, not a change to the selected renderer or an instruction to replace current game assets. Hades is itself strongly stylized; liking it suggests that “too cartoony” needs to be unpacked into proportions, material treatment, linework, palette and clarity rather than treated as a ban on stylization.
