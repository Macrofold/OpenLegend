## Should we buy Critter to reverse engineer it?

**Do not buy it solely on the assumption that we can clone or port it.** If a package supplies source and its terms permit the intended inspection and adaptation, source review can be technically useful. Purchase alone does not establish those permissions or prove that its renderer fits OpenLegend.

The official [Critter camera listing](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/camera/critter-3d-pixel-camera-263695) describes pixel-perfect rendering with smooth subpixel movement, limits those features to orthographic cameras, and lists web builds as unsupported. It lists built-in/URP support rather than HDRP. These are significant limits for OpenLegend's browser and free-camera requirements. The listing also says the marketing scenes are not included. The [toolkit bundle](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/game-toolkits/critter-3d-pixel-art-toolkit-asset-bundle-286677) is a separate purchase listing; do not assume every demonstration asset or source file comes with it.

The [Unity Asset Store terms](https://unity.com/legal/as-terms), as inspected, restrict reverse engineering in section 3.8(i) unless permitted, and include restrictions on using assets as inputs to AI/ML in section 3.8(v) and EULA 2.2.1.1(g). Modification rights elsewhere in the EULA are subject to the applicable conditions. Having a human license to a package does not automatically authorize giving its contents to an AI coding tool. The camera listing identifies an Extension Asset license, which also raises seat-assignment questions.

Before a purchase intended for this workflow, obtain the publisher's answers about included source, perspective and browser support, AI-assisted source inspection, adaptation into PlayCanvas, and redistribution of derived source in an AGPL repository. Ask about the actual intended use, rather than requesting a vague permission to “learn from it.” Do not publish purchased source or assets merely because they can be opened in an editor.

For now, the public MIT examples and independently described techniques give us enough material to investigate camera compensation, lighting, grass and outlines without this dependency. This is a research recommendation, not a claim that the commercial toolkit has no value for its supported Unity workflow.

## Technical access is separate from permission

A source package is easier to inspect than a compiled plugin, but source availability does not remove license conditions. A binary-only plugin may also make a useful port impractical. Conversely, an independently implemented camera-snapping equation or lighting technique does not require reproducing proprietary code.

Any eventual dependency decision should record the exact version, license, source/art distinction and intended distribution. This task supplies research and documentation only; it does not approve a paid purchase, proprietary dependency, AI upload, source publication or engine migration.
