# Character production: evidence and OpenLegend application

**Research date: October 31, 2026.** This note records the supplied tutorial and a focused review of professional character/game-animation practice and primary tool documentation. It is not a controlled provider comparison or a claim that one studio's method is universally best. No generation, purchase, asset upload, runtime integration or production benchmark was performed. The [execution recipe](../../../docs/production-workflows/character-production.md) owns the reusable procedure; the [3D feature](../../../docs/projects/3d-pixel-art-feature-spec.md) and [technical design](../../../docs/projects/3d-pixel-art-tech-design.md) retain product and runtime ownership.

## 1. What the supplied video actually supports

**Source:** [the character-production video supplied by Mike](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VeOU0FnTjjc), read through the complete transcript supplied in the conversation as `Pasted text(1).txt`. The transcript, rather than invented frame-by-frame observations, is the basis for this account. The creator discusses Tripo P2, Blender, AccuRIG, material finishing and Unreal Engine, and promotes credits and a course.

The demonstrations progress from a roughly three-minute generated model to a roughly one-hour assembled/rigged character and a reported roughly six-hour polished result. The creator generates important parts separately, adjusts their geometry complexity, assembles and repairs them, reworks UVs and materials, improves the eyes, starts with automatic body rigging, corrects rigid attachments, and adds secondary bones/cloth in Unreal. The timings and comparative quality judgments are the creator's report, not independently measured OpenLegend results. The polished workflow still contains substantial skilled manual finishing.

Two specific production options are worth carrying forward. First, separately produce an important feature when that gives more control without replacing already successful work; separation during production need not survive as runtime modularity. Second, refine a directly usable mesh rather than automatically rebuilding it through a high-detail source and retopology. Neither option removes the need to inspect shape, joint deformation, fitting, exported materials and actual game presentation.

Do not infer that P2 is the best provider, that every generated character takes six hours, that a large population of compatible characters is now inexpensive, or that all steps can run unattended. One successful body does not prove wardrobe interchangeability, facial acting, nonhuman rigs, arbitrary interactions or crowds. The Unreal portion demonstrates an end result and manual effort; it does not install equivalent cloth, collision or retargeting support in OpenLegend's PlayCanvas integration. The tutorial's character style is not a new OpenLegend art direction.

### Design judgment

The common useful pattern is not a universal tool sequence. It is a coherent intention, an early working prototype, feedback from the actual use, repair in the responsible source, and preservation of the successful result. For OpenLegend, the intention must support identifiable people, practical invention, persistent possessions and understandable consequences alongside immediate playability. This interpretation is ours; the cited studios are not endorsing OpenLegend's architecture.

A returning resident with recognizable features and a personally chosen repaired coat can matter more than another ornate hero. A clearly fitted invented tool can make the player's idea visible. An interrupted task that stops honestly can be more convincing than an elaborate generic animation that continues after the action failed. These are illustrative applications, not claims that a particular resident, outfit or action is already implemented.

## 4. Decisions carried into the procedure

The [character recipe](../../../docs/production-workflows/character-production.md) carries these decisions without creating a second runtime system:

- Preserve approved identity; generate or repair parts only where independent control helps. Continuous body surfaces, rigid attachments and fitted garments need different assembly decisions.
- Permit direct-mesh refinement, local reconstruction and optional high-detail baking as alternatives selected by the actual deficiency. No all-quad, per-part-polycount or high-poly-first rule.
- Prove silhouette, fit and movement early. Improve UV allocation, eyes and material regions locally; more fine detail is not automatically better pixel art.
- Keep rig compatibility, real equipment, required clips and exported source dependencies explicit. Auto-rigging and a familiar joint name do not prove those contracts.
- Add cloth/hair/tail motion selectively, with a stable fallback and actual consumer support. Avoid noisy perpetual motion and unqualified Unreal-to-PlayCanvas assumptions.
- Inspect the actual exported package and the actual gameplay presentation at separate stages. Preserve truthful action/custody/state results, ordinary scale, camera stability and the approved art direction.
- Demonstrate a meaningful revision or compatible variation before claiming reuse. Compare routes only to answer an unresolved production question, including all rejected attempts and finishing effort.

The broader comparison of Tripo, Meshy and an authored baseline remains an available experiment, not a mandatory three-provider project. One existing baseline plus a candidate that addresses a concrete bottleneck can be sufficient. A second compatible outfit is more relevant to wardrobe reuse than a large gallery of unrelated generated heroes.

## 5. What is not being adopted

No new style, altered anatomy, mandatory provider, engine migration, full cloth requirement, facial-animation requirement, runtime generator, universal body/wardrobe solver, new asset database or production-time promise is selected. The current [generative-art guidance](generative-pipeline.md) and production records already cover source rights, reusable rigs, manual finishing and game-context review; those are refined, not rediscovered as missing infrastructure.

The appealing possibility of an economically broader population remains a hypothesis. It requires evidence of repeated accepted designs, useful variation, fitting effort, animation coverage, rights and runtime cost. A one-character tutorial is encouraging evidence of an available method, not proof of that population claim.
